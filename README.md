# Reading Between the Lines: How People Perceive and Detect Cultural Erasure in AI-Retold Bengali Folktales

This repository contains the data, code, and evaluation artifacts for a BRAC University B.Sc. thesis (Department of Computer Science and Engineering, June 2026) that studies whether people can tell when generative AI has quietly erased the culture out of a folktale — and builds an automated pipeline to measure that erasure at scale.

**Authors:** Sidratul Montaha
**Supervisor:** Dr. Taiabul Haque, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, BRAC University

## What this project is about

Bengali folktales are an important repository of Bangladesh's intangible cultural heritage. As LLMs become a common way people encounter and retell such stories, this project asks: when an AI "retells" a Bengali folktale, does it quietly Westernize, Disneyfy, or flatten the culture out of it — and can readers actually notice when that happens?

The thesis makes three contributions:

1. **A verified, expert-annotated Reference Corpus** of 27 Bengali folktales (54 Bengali/English story pairs), each annotated for culturally non-substitutable lexical items, plot-point structure, socio-cultural behavioral traits, and Western narrative-form imposition.
2. **A multi-component automated evaluation pipeline** that goes beyond string-matching and embedding similarity to score cultural fidelity, including a novel **Structural Displacement Indicator (SDI)** based on a "Proppian Inversion" of Vladimir Propp's morphology of folktales — counting Western narrative functions injected into a retelling that don't exist in the source tradition. This pipeline evaluated 1,296 retellings generated by six LLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini, Llama-3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B, DeepSeek, Mistral) across four prompt-engineering strategies.
3. **A human verification study (n = 76)** testing whether people can detect AI-driven cultural distortion. Under blind reading, human accuracy was near chance due to overconfidence in surface fluency; when given the automated pipeline's structured feedback, detection accuracy rose to **86.0%**.

From these findings, the thesis derives six HCI design guidelines for building culturally accountable generative systems.

## The three evaluation designs

| Design | Approach | Measures | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Lexical Fidelity** | Fuzzy string matching (TheFuzz/Levenshtein) of annotated Bengali terms against the retelling | Whether culturally specific words are retained, anglicized, or erased | Precise but blind to plot/structure loss — a story can gut its culture at the plot level while keeping a few surface Bengali words and still score well |
| **B — Structural Alignment** | Cosine similarity between multilingual sentence embeddings (`paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2`) of source vs. retelling | Semantic/structural similarity of the narrative arc | Refuted — high embedding similarity can co-occur with complete cultural replacement |
| **C — Holistic Cultural Assessment** *(primary evaluator)* | Rubric-based LLM-as-judge (Gemini 2.5 Flash) with hard logical constraints, scoring Lexical Integrity, Narrative Fidelity, Socio-Cultural Marker Preservation, and the Structural Displacement Indicator (SDI) | Full-spectrum cultural fidelity across surface, structural, and semantic dimensions | Selected as the primary evaluator based on correlation with expert human judgments |

**Structural Displacement Indicator (SDI):** applies a "Proppian Inversion" — instead of using Propp's 31 narrative functions prescriptively, the pipeline uses them diagnostically. SDI counts Western narrative functions present in an AI retelling that are absent from the expert-annotated reference; a higher SDI means more imposed Western structure.

## Prompt strategies tested

| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| 1. Simple Control | Model given only the story's name |
| 2. Context-Rich | Name plus attributive/cultural context |
| 3. Constraint-Based | Name plus an explicit instruction to preserve cultural fidelity |
| 4. Fail-Safe (Source) | Full original source text supplied, asking for a retelling in the model's own words |

Across all six models, only the Fail-Safe (source-grounded) condition meaningfully improved cultural fidelity — mean lexical retention rose from roughly 0.11–0.12 (Prompts 1–3) to roughly 0.58–0.64 under Prompt 4 — though cultural distortion was reduced, not eliminated, even then.

## Key findings

- Standard AI retelling conditions (Prompts 1–3) produce systematic cultural compression toward Westernized/Disneyfied/Eurocentric storytelling, regardless of model.
- Supplying the original source text (Prompt 4) is the only intervention that substantially improves fidelity, but has low ecological validity (few real-world use cases hand a model the full source text).
- In blind reading, human participants performed near chance at detecting AI-distorted retellings, largely relying on superficial stylistic fluency and exhibiting overconfidence bias.
- When shown the automated pipeline's structured cultural-fidelity feedback, human detection accuracy rose to **86.0%** — evidence that automated cultural audits and human judgment are complementary rather than substitutes.

## Models evaluated

GPT-4o (OpenAI), Gemini 2.0 Flash, Llama-3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B, DeepSeek, and Mistral (via free-tier APIs/OpenRouter) generated the 1,296 evaluated retellings; Gemini 2.5 Flash served as the Design C judge model.

## Reproducing/using this repository

1. **Source material** — `Text Corpus/Books (pdf)` holds the original scanned collections (regional oral folktales sourced with in-person permission from the Bangla Academy, plus the public-domain *Thakurmar Jhuli*).
2. **Reference Corpus** — `Cleaned Bangla/English Stories` and `Gold_Standard_Annotations` together form the expert-validated ground truth used by all three designs.
3. **Retelling generation** — see `Code Files` for the generation scripts/notebooks; outputs land in `P2_s 10/Bangla Retellings` and `English Retellings`, organized by model and prompt strategy.
4. **Evaluation** — Design A, B, and C pipelines (also in `Code Files`) consume the retellings and reference annotations, producing the JSON/CSV/XLSX outputs under `Evaluation Results` and `Final Results`.

Total direct monetary cost of the project was $0 — all inference, embeddings, and translation used free-tier APIs and open-source tooling (TheFuzz, Sentence-Transformers, Tesseract OCR, the Python scientific stack).

## Ethics

This research was reviewed and approved by BRAC University's Institutional Review Board (IRB). All human study participants were consenting adult volunteers; no identifiable participant information was retained, and data was encrypted with restricted access. Source material was obtained with formal in-person permission from the Bangla Academy (regional oral folktales) or drawn from the public domain (*Thakurmar Jhuli*).
