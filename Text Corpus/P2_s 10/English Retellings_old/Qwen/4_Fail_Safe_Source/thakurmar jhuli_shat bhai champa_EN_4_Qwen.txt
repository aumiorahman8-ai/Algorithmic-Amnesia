A king had seven queens, but the youngest queen was the most gentle and loved by the king. However, the king had no children, which made him very sad. One day, the youngest queen was about to give birth, and the king declared that whoever could take as much as they wanted from the royal treasury could come and get it. The elder queens, who were jealous, tugged on a golden chain tied to the youngest queen's waist, but there was nothing. The king, angry, threatened to have all the queens beheaded if the chain was tugged again before a child was born. The elder queens, in turn, tugged the chain, but again, there was nothing. The youngest queen gave birth to seven sons and one daughter, who were like dolls made of moonlight and flower buds. The elder queens, in a fit of jealousy, tugged the chain, and the king arrived to see only tadpoles and baby mice. The king became furious and drove the youngest queen out of the palace. The elder queens, happy, began to live their lives in happiness. The youngest queen became a dung-gathering maid, and the king's heart and kingdom were filled with sorrow. One day, the gardener found seven perfect Champa flowers and one Parul flower blooming on the ash heap. The Parul flower called out to the Champa flowers, and they refused to give their flowers until the king's eldest queen came. The flowers continued to rise higher, and the king summoned the eldest queen, but the flowers refused to give their flowers until the king's youngest queen came. The king sent a palanquin to bring the youngest queen, who was covered in cow dung and torn clothes. The flowers glided down from the sky, and the youngest queen emerged with seven handsome princes and one princess. The king immediately ordered the elder queens to be buried in a pit of thorns, and he took the seven princes, his daughter Parul, and the youngest queen to the royal palace. The drums of victory sounded in the royal palace.
