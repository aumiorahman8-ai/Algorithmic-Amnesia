Khumpuiti agreed, and the snake began to help them by building a Jhum hut, clearing the jungle, and setting up a Wakhrab to scare birds. However, Khumpuiti's health declined as she fed the snake from her share of rice, and her father, Darbasing Ochai, became worried.

Eventually, Khumbarti told her father about the snake and how it helped them. Darbasing Ochai decided to kill the snake, but Khumbarti explained that they must first arrange for good food and water for the snake. Darbasing Ochai arranged for this, and Khumbarti cooked and arranged the food.

Khumpuiti, after finishing her work, returned home and found that neither her father nor her younger sister was there. She saw a severed piece of the snake and understood that it belonged to her husband, Dungui. She strung the pieces together into a garland and wore it around her neck. She then went to the riverbank and prayed for Dungui, asking him to take her with him.

She told Khumbarti to go to a banyan tree at a crossroads and sit on a branch. Khumbarti followed her sister's instructions and was later taken to the king as a queen.

However, the six queens conspired to make Khumbarti give birth to puppies and kittens, which they then replaced with puppies and kittens. No milk came from the six queens, but Khumbarti, dressed in torn rags, produced seven streams of milk.
