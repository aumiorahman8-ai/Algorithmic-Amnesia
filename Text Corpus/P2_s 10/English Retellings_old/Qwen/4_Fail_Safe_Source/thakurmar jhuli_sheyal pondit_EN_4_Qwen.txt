Once upon a time, there was a jackal who built a wall for his son. The jackal's son, who was also a jackal, decided to open a school in the Shanti forest. The school attracted many students, including insects, tortoises, millipedes, earthworms, scorpions, dung beetles, cockroaches, frogs, crabs, and spiders. The jackal's school was so popular that a crocodile, seeing the commotion, decided to send his seven sons to study there.

The crocodile was overjoyed when his sons returned, claiming they would become great scholars and master archers. However, the jackal pundit, who was secretly eating the crocodile's sons, only showed the last remaining son to the crocodile. The crocodile, thinking his sons had become great scholars, left happily.

The next day, the jackal pundit finished his final snack and dismantled the school. The crocodile, realizing the students weren't studying and the jackal pundit was not at home, understood the truth. The crocodile then hid in the canal, waiting for the jackal pundit to return.

The jackal pundit, unable to resist the sight of crabs on the sandbank, jumped into the water. The crocodile, with its many teeth, caught the jackal pundit's leg, leading to a struggle. The jackal pundit, using a reed, tricked the crocodile into biting a stick instead. Freed from the reed, the jackal pundit crossed the water in three leaps.

The crocodile, still alive, lay on the sandbank. The jackal pundit, suspecting the crocodile was not dead, tested for signs of death. The crocodile, with no ears, twitched its head and slapped its tail, fooling the jackal pundit. However, a group of cowherds, recognizing the crocodile, attacked it with sickles, sticks, bricks, and stones.

The jackal pundit fled, entering an eggplant field. While eating the eggplants, a thorn pierced his nose, causing him to bleed profusely. The jackal went to a barber's house, where the barber removed the thorn but cut off the jackal's nose in the process.

The jackal, with his nose cut off, went to a potter's house. The potter, needing a nail-clipper, examined the jackal's nail-clipper, which broke. The jackal, angry, demanded the potter replace the nail-clipper. The potter, unable to do so, gave the jackal a pot.

A wedding procession passed by, and a firecracker landed in the jackal's pot, causing it to shatter. The jackal, angry, demanded the groom give him the bride. The groom, flabbergasted, gave the bride to the jackal.

The jackal went to a drummer's house, demanding the drums for his wedding. The drummer's wife, chopping vegetables, accidentally cut the bride in half. The drummer's wife hid the pieces in a haystack.

The jackal returned to the drummer's house, demanding the bride. The drummer's wife, frightened, brought the dhol and bolted the door. The jackal, taking the dhol, climbed a palm tree and began to play and sing, celebrating his new dhol.
