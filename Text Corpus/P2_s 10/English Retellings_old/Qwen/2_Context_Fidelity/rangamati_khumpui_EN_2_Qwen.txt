The tale of "Khumpui" from Rangamati is a richly woven narrative that reflects the deep-rooted cultural fabric of the Chakma community. It is a story that speaks of the importance of family, the power of nature, and the consequences of human greed. Here is a retelling of the tale, preserving the original cultural nuances, kinship terms, and specific rituals:

In the heart of the Rangamati hills, where the forests whisper secrets to the mountains, there lived a Chakma village. The village was known for its lush greenery and the gentle flow of the river that ran through it. The village was led by a wise elder, named Khumpui, who was respected for his knowledge of the forest and the spirits that dwelled within.

Khumpui had a daughter named Kuma, who was as beautiful as the morning dew and as kind as the forest itself. Kuma was loved by all the villagers, but her heart was particularly close to her father's. She often accompanied him on his forays into the forest, learning the ways of the trees and the animals.

The stranger returned to the village, and the villagers were overjoyed to hear the songs of the spirits. They danced and sang, and the village was filled with joy. However, the stranger had a secret. He had fallen in love with Kuma, the daughter of Khumpui, and he wanted to take her with him to his land.

Khumpui, upon hearing the stranger's intentions, was deeply troubled. He knew that Kuma was his pride and joy, and he did not want to lose her. He called Kuma to him and told her of the stranger's intentions. Kuma, being a wise and kind-hearted girl, understood her father's concerns. She decided to go with the stranger, but not before she promised her father that she would return to the village as soon as she could.
