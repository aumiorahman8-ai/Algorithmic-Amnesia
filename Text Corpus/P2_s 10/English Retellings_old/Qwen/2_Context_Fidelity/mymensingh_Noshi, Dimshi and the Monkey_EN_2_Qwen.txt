The family was known for their kindness and their deep connection with the natural world around them. Noshi was the eldest, a woman of great wisdom and strength, while Dimshi, the youngest, was a child full of curiosity and wonder.

One day, as the sun was setting, casting a golden glow over the forest, Noshi and Dimshi were tending to their crops. They had planted a variety of vegetables and fruits, and the harvest was bountiful. As they worked, they noticed a group of monkeys gathering nearby, their chatter a symphony of sounds that filled the air. Among them was Chota, who had been their friend for as long as they could remember.

However, as the sun began to dip below the horizon, casting long shadows across the fields, Noshi and Dimshi realized that they had forgotten to prepare a meal for Chota. They felt a pang of guilt, knowing that Chota had been their constant companion and friend.

They were unsure if he would accept their offering, or if he would be offended by their lack of preparation. As they sat down to eat, they looked at each other, their eyes filled with worry.

Noshi and Dimshi were taken aback, but they quickly recovered and offered him a piece of their meal. As the night fell, Noshi and Dimshi decided to take Chota to a nearby temple, where they would perform a ritual to honor the spirits of the forest. They knew that Chota was a wild creature, but they also knew that he was a friend, and they wanted to show him their gratitude for his companionship. They performed the ritual, and as they did, they felt a sense of peace and contentment wash over them.

The next morning, as the sun rose, casting a warm glow over the forest, Noshi and Dimshi awoke to find Chota sitting by their side, his eyes filled with gratitude.
