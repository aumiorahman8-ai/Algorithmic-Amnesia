In the heart of Cox's Bazar, nestled between the lush greenery and the serene waters of the Bay of Bengal, there once lived a young woman named Kali. Kali was the daughter of a humble fisherman named Bhaba and his wife, Bhabu. The family lived by the river, where they would catch fish and sell them in the bustling markets of the town.

Kali was a beautiful young woman, with a kind heart and a gentle soul. She was known for her love of nature and her ability to communicate with the animals. One day, while she was out by the river, she noticed a peculiar sight. A snake, unlike any she had ever seen before, was basking in the sun. It was a large, majestic snake, with scales that shimmered in the sunlight. Kali approached the snake with curiosity and respect, and the snake, sensing her kindness, spoke to her in a language that only she could understand.

The snake told Kali that it was the prince of the snakes, and that it had been banished from its kingdom by an evil sorcerer. The sorcerer had cursed the prince, making him a snake, and had taken his kingdom for himself. The prince had been searching for a way to break the curse and return to his rightful place as the ruler of the snakes.

Kali was moved by the snake's story and offered to help. She knew that the prince was a kind and noble creature, and she wanted to help him. The snake, touched by Kali's kindness, agreed to marry her and help her find a way to break the curse.

The wedding ceremony was a grand affair, with the entire town of Cox's Bazar coming together to celebrate. The snake prince, now in his human form, dressed in a suit of shining silver, stood beside Kali, who was adorned in a beautiful white sari. The ceremony was filled with traditional Bengali rituals, including the exchange of rings, the feeding of the couple, and the lighting of the ceremonial lamp.

After the wedding, the snake prince and Kali set out on a journey to find the evil sorcerer and break the curse. They traveled far and wide, encountering many challenges and obstacles along the way. But Kali's kindness and the snake prince's noble nature helped them overcome each one.

Finally, they reached the sorcerer's castle, where they found the evil sorcerer, who was guarding the curse with all his might. The snake prince and Kali engaged in a fierce battle, but the snake prince emerged victorious. With the curse broken, the snake prince returned to his kingdom, and Kali returned to her life as a fisherman's daughter.

The story of Kali and the snake prince became a beloved tale in Cox's Bazar, passed down from generation to generation. It taught the people of the town the importance of kindness, courage, and the power of love.
