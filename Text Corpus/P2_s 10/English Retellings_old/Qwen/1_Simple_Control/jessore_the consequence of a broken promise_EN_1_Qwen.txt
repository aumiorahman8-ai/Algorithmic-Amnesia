The Bengali folktale "The Consequence of a Broken Promise" is a classic tale that teaches the importance of keeping one's word and the potential consequences of breaking a promise. Here's a retelling of the story:

Once upon a time, in a village nestled in the heart of Bengal, there lived a young man named Raja and a young woman named Sunita. They were deeply in love and had promised each other to marry. However, Raja's family was not pleased with Sunita's family, and they put pressure on Raja to break off the engagement.

Raja, feeling torn between his love for Sunita and the expectations of his family, decided to break the promise. He told Sunita that he could not marry her and that he would marry someone else. Sunita, heartbroken, accepted his decision and moved on with her life.

Years passed, and Raja married another woman, but he never forgot Sunita. He often thought about her and wished he had kept his promise to her. One day, while on a journey, Raja encountered Sunita, who was now an old woman. She was living in poverty and had no one to care for her.

Raja was filled with regret and guilt for breaking his promise. He approached Sunita and apologized for his actions. Sunita, though she had moved on, was touched by Raja's remorse and forgave him. She told him that she had always loved him and that she had never stopped hoping for his return.

Raja, moved by Sunita's forgiveness, decided to take care of her. He built her a new home and provided for her needs. Sunita lived happily with Raja for the rest of her life, and the villagers were amazed by their love and the consequences of a broken promise.

The moral of the story is that keeping one's word is essential, and breaking a promise can have severe consequences. It is always better to keep one's promises and to be true to one's word.
