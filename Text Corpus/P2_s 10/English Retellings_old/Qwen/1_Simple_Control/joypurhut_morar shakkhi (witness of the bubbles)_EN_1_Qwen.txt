The Bengali folktale "Morar Shakkhi" is a charming story that teaches the importance of truth and the consequences of lying. Here's a retelling of the tale:

Once upon a time, in a village by the banks of a serene river, there lived a young girl named Morar. Morar was known for her innocence and her love for nature. She spent most of her days playing by the river, watching the bubbles that danced on the water's surface.

One day, while Morar was playing, she noticed a group of children from a nearby village. They were playing a game of hide-and-seek, and Morar was curious to join them. However, the children were not very welcoming and made fun of her for not knowing the rules of their game. Morar felt hurt and decided to leave.

As she walked away, Morar saw a group of bubbles floating on the river. She picked up a stone and threw it into the water, causing the bubbles to burst. The children, who had been watching her, laughed and jeered at her, saying that she had destroyed their fun.

Morar, feeling upset, continued her walk. She came across an old man who was fishing. He noticed Morar's sadness and asked her what was wrong. Morar explained her situation and the children's laughter. The old man listened to her and then said, "Morar, you have a gift. You can see the truth in the bubbles."

Morar was confused. She asked the old man what he meant. The old man explained that the bubbles on the water's surface were like the words people say, and the truth is hidden beneath the surface. He told Morar that she could see the truth in the bubbles and that it was her gift.

Morar was amazed and thanked the old man. She decided to use her gift to help others. She went back to the children and told them the truth about their game. The children were surprised and realized that they had been lying to Morar. They apologized to her and asked for forgiveness.

From that day on, Morar became known as "Morar Shakkhi" or "Witness of the Bubbles." She used her gift to help others and to teach them the importance of truth. The children learned to be honest, and Morar became a beloved figure in the village.

The moral of the story is that truth is always hidden beneath the surface, and it is our responsibility to seek it out and tell the truth. Morar's gift of seeing the truth in the bubbles taught her and others the importance of honesty and the consequences of lying.
