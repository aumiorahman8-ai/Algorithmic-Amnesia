In the Bengali folktale "Shat Bhai Champa," a young boy named Champa, who is the youngest of 50 brothers, is often teased and bullied by his older siblings. Despite their cruelty, Champa remains kind and patient, always offering to help and sharing his meager possessions with them. One day, a famine strikes the village, and the older brothers, unable to bear the sight of their younger sibling's suffering, decide to leave him behind. Champa, however, refuses to be left behind and follows them, determined to stay with his family.

The older brothers, feeling guilty, decide to return to the village to find Champa. They find him, weak and hungry, but still with a smile on his face. They apologize for their actions and promise to be kinder to him in the future. Champa, being the forgiving and compassionate soul he is, forgives them and forgives himself for not being able to protect them from their own greed.

The moral of the story is that kindness and forgiveness can overcome even the most difficult situations. It teaches us that we should always be kind to others, even when they are unkind to us, and that forgiveness is a powerful tool that can heal even the deepest wounds. The story also highlights the importance of family and the bonds that we share with our loved ones, no matter how difficult the circumstances may be.
