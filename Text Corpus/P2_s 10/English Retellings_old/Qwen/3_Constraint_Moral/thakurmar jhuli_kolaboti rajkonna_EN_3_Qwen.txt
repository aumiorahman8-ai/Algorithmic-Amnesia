In the land of Bengal, there once lived a king named Rajkonna, who was known for his wisdom and fairness. One day, a poor fisherman named Kolaboti came to the king's court with a plea. Kolaboti had caught a large fish, but when he tried to sell it, he found that the fish was too big for the market. The fisherman was desperate and sought help from the king.

Rajkonna, being a just ruler, listened to Kolaboti's plight and decided to help him. He ordered his courtiers to measure the fish and found that it was indeed too large for the market. Rajkonna then had the fish cut into smaller pieces, which were then sold at the market. Kolaboti was overjoyed and thanked the king for his kindness.

The moral of the story is that even the smallest of people can be helped by a just ruler. It teaches us that we should always be kind and compassionate towards others, especially those who are less fortunate. It also highlights the importance of fair and just rulers who are willing to help their subjects in times of need.
