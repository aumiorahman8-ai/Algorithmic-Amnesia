In the village of Morar, there lived a young girl named Shakkhi. One day, while playing near the river, she noticed a group of bubbles floating on the water. Intrigued, she watched them closely. The bubbles, she observed, were formed by the wind and the water, and they would burst as soon as they touched the surface. Shakkhi was fascinated by their beauty and fragility.

One day, a wealthy merchant came to the village and asked Shakkhi to be his wife. Shakkhi, being a simple girl, was hesitant but eventually agreed. The merchant, however, was not satisfied with her simplicity and often criticized her for not being as refined as he expected. Shakkhi, feeling hurt, decided to leave him and return to her village.

On her way back, Shakkhi passed by the river where she had first seen the bubbles. She remembered the beauty and fragility of the bubbles and realized that just like the bubbles, her life was also fragile and could be easily burst by the wrong person. She understood that true happiness and contentment come from within and not from external validation.

Shakkhi returned to her village and lived a simple, happy life. She taught her children the importance of being true to oneself and not letting others define their worth. The moral of the story is that true happiness comes from within and not from external validation. It teaches us to be true to ourselves and not to let others define our worth.
