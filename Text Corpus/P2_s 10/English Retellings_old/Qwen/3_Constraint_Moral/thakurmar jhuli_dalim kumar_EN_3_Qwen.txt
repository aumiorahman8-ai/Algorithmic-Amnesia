In the heart of a lush, verdant forest, there once lived a young boy named Dalim Kumar. His parents were poor, and he was often hungry. One day, while wandering through the forest, he stumbled upon a magical tree that bore golden apples. These apples were said to grant the eater immortality. However, the tree was guarded by a fierce dragon who demanded a sacrifice of a golden apple for every one he wished to eat.

Dalim Kumar, driven by his hunger and the promise of immortality, decided to take the risk. He offered the dragon a golden apple, and in exchange, the dragon granted him the apples he desired. Over time, Dalim Kumar grew rich and powerful, but he forgot the lesson of the dragon: the price of immortality is often too high.

Years passed, and Dalim Kumar grew old and weak. He realized too late that the golden apples had not granted him immortality but had instead shortened his life. He learned that true immortality lies not in material wealth or power but in the love and respect of others, and in the legacy of good deeds.

The moral of the story is that true immortality is found in the love and respect of others, and in the legacy of good deeds. It teaches us that material wealth and power do not guarantee immortality, and that true happiness and fulfillment come from within, not from external sources.
