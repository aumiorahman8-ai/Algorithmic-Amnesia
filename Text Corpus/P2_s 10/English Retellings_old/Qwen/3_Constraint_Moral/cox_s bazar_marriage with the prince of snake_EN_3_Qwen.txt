In the land of Bengali folklore, there once lived a young woman named Kali. She was known for her beauty and intelligence, but her family was poor and struggled to provide for her. One day, a mysterious prince of snakes appeared in their village, offering to marry Kali. The villagers were skeptical, but Kali, driven by her desire to help her family, agreed to the marriage.

The prince of snakes led Kali to a hidden cave where he revealed his true form. He was a powerful and wise serpent, and he promised to bring wealth and prosperity to Kali's family. Kali was overjoyed and returned to her village, where she was celebrated as a princess.

However, the villagers soon realized that the prince of snakes was not as kind as he seemed. He demanded more and more from Kali, and her family's wealth began to dwindle. Kali, feeling guilty and desperate, sought advice from a wise old woman in the village.

The old woman told Kali that the prince of snakes was not a true prince but a deceitful serpent. She advised Kali to leave him and return to her village. Kali, with the old woman's guidance, left the cave and returned to her village, where she was welcomed back as a hero.

The moral of the story is that one should be cautious when making promises or agreements with strangers, especially those who seem too good to be true. It also teaches the importance of seeking advice and guidance from wise and experienced individuals before making important decisions.
