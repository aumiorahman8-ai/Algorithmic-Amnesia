Alright, let's delve into the Mymensingh folktale of Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey! Here's my retelling, attempting to preserve the original cultural nuances, kinship terms, and rituals as best as I can glean from common Bangla/Bengali folklore:

**Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey King**

Once upon a time, in a village not far from the Brahmaputra river, lived two sisters, Noshi and Dimshi. Noshi was the elder, calm and competent, a real *boudi-ma* (a dutiful daughter-in-law, though unmarried). Dimshi, the younger, was playful and loved to sing *baul* music. Their *Baba* (father) was a simple farmer, struggling to make ends meet. Their *Ma* (mother) was, alas, gone, having succumbed to a fever a few seasons past. Life was difficult, but the sisters shared a bond stronger than yarn spun on a *charkha* .

One scorching *boishakh* (summer month) day, Baba fell ill. His cough wouldn't cease, and he grew weaker with each passing *dopahar* (afternoon). Noshi, with her practical mind, knew a *hakim* (traditional healer) was their only hope. But the villages lacked the money and so she went to the deep forest to collect wood to sell at the local *haat* marketplace.

The forest was dense and ancient, the trees whispering secrets in the wind.  Deep within its heart, Noshi stumbled upon a clearing. And in the centre, sat a magnificent monkey! He wasn't just any monkey. He was enormous, dressed in silk dhoti and a mukut (crown)- a bona-fide *Bonoraj* (Monkey King)!

He spoke to her in a resonating voice, "Hey, O *Ma*! What troubles you so? You carry a worry as heavy as the monsoon clouds."

Noshi, though initially frightened, gathered her courage.  She told him about her Baba's illness and lack of money for a *hakim*.

The Monkey King listened patiently. When she finished, he chuckled. "Is that all, little *Beti* (girl)? I shall help you. Under this very banyan tree, you will find a pot filled with gold *mohur* (gold coins). Take what you need to heal your father. But remember, honesty is the truest wealth."

Noshi's eyes widened. She thanked him profusely, touching his feet as a sign of respect – a true *pronam* to a greater being. She took enough gold, promising to repay if possible, and rushed back home.

The *hakim* came, prepared a potent medicine with herbs and roots, and after days of dedicated nursing by Noshi and Dimshi Baba regained his strength. The village rejoiced to their *Bap-er* (father) good health.

However, greed twisted Dimshi's heart. Seeing the gold from Bonoraj she pressured Noshi to go back to the forest. Noshi reluctantly agreed, not wanting to disrespect Bonoraj by not using all the gold he had given. This time, Dimshi insisted on joining.

Upon reaching the clearing, Dimshi, rather than showing respect, demanded more gold. "Bonoraj; this time we want all the gold hidden here! We are the most honorable ladies."

The Monkey King glared at Dimshi. His voice, once warm, was now colder than the *poush* (winter month) wind. "You have broken your trust, little *Beti.* Honesty is the highest honor and you have none. Dimshi your greed will be your punishment."

With a snap of his fingers, the pot of gold turned into a heap of dry leaves. He pointed a long finger towards Dimshi who turned into a monkey! He then told Noshi, " I would have helped you, but your sister's actions tainted you both. If you want her healed you must pray to *Maa Durga* three times a day and take care of her as a true sister. Only then will she gain back her human form."

Noshi, heartbroken but determined, obeyed. She took Dimshi, now a shivering little monkey, back to their home.  She looked after her day and night, fed her the best fruits, protected her from the cruel village kids. She did her daily *puja* (worship) to *Maa Durga* and prayed to get her sister back. Soon Dimshi began to change she began to listen to Noshi, no longer being an unruly monkey.

Slowly, after much praying Noshi had done, Dimshi finally turned back into a girl. The experience changed Dimshi deeply. She understood the value of honesty, gratitude, and the unwavering love of her *bon* (sister).

From that day on, Noshi, Dimshi, and their Baba lived a life in honest labor and with respect for the natural world and *Devi Durga.* They never forgot the lesson learned from the Monkey King of the forest, a tale whispered among the villagers of Mymensingh for generations to come – a reminder that true wealth lies in honesty, and that the bond between sisters is stronger than any magic.
