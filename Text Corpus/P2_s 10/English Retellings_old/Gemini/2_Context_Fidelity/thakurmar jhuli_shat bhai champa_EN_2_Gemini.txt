**The Story of Shat Bhai Champa (Seven Brothers Champa)**

Once upon a time, in a kingdom draped in silks and scented with sandalwood, lived a king deeply devoted to his seven queens. But alas, none bore him a son. Discouraged, he finally married a humble woman, Rani Durba, known for her kindness and gentle heart.

Soon, Rani Durba’s belly swelled with promise. The other queens, devoured by jealousy ( *rishiri bhare dushho bhare* ), plotted her downfall. They whispered venom in the king’s ear, claiming she was not with child at all, but only faking. Sadly, the king, weakened by their constant accusations, started doubting his Rani's pure heart.

As the time drew near, Rani Durba, unaware of the treachery brewing, was secretly helped by her faithful *dai ma* (midwife). On a dark, stormy night, she gave birth to not one, not two, but seven beautiful sons! The *dai ma*, overjoyed, carefully bundled the *saptaputro* (seven sons) to show to the king.

But the jealous queens intercepted the *dai ma*. Cruelly, they stole the seven *narashishu* (newborn boys) and replaced them with seven puppies. Horrified, the *dai ma* could only weep silently as they then presented the puppies to the despairing king. The scheming queens then buried the seven innocent princes alive in the *upaban* (royal garden), under a flourishing champak tree.

Rani Durba was devastated. Forced to present her “offspring” to the King and witnessing his disappointment, she was branded a fraud and banished to the *ghordaal* (stable), forced to live a life of shame and servitude. She spent her days sweeping and tending to the horses, her heart heavy with an unspoken sorrow.

Meanwhile, in the *upaban*, a miracle occurred. From the earth, rose seven beautiful champak buds, one for each prince. Every morning, Rani Durba, forced to clean the stables beside the *upaban*, noticed the magnificent *champa* blossoms. She felt inexplicably drawn to them, unable to understand the depth of her connection with them. She'd caress the delicate petals, and whisper soft lullabies, *“Aai, aai chonda, mama tor kotha…*”, the same songs she would have sung to her babies.

One day, a *sadhu baba* (holy man) passed through the kingdom. He sat under the champak tree to meditate. He instantly recognized the unusual energy emanating from the blossoms.  He observed Rani Durba’s daily visits and her deep affection for the flowers.  Intuition told him something was amiss. He called for her and gently questioned her, sensing her hidden grief. Rani Durba, after much hesitation, confided her tragic story to the revered *sadhu baba*.

The *sadhu baba* listened intently, his eyes filled with compassion. He instructed Rani Durba to perform a special *puja* (prayer ritual). He procured seven small clay bowls and told her to fill them with *amrito jol* (ambrosial water) and to smear the base of the *champa* tree with *alta* (red dye, traditionally used by women).

Following the *sadhu baba's* instructions to the letter, Rani Durba performed the *puja*. As she poured the *amrito jol* and painted the tree with *alta*, she chanted, " *Sat Bhai Champa, keu ki acho? Tumi ki amar putra ho?* (Seven Brothers Champa, is anyone there? Are you my sons?)"

Miraculously, as the last drop of water touched the ground, the seven *champa* buds transformed into seven beautiful princes!  Dressed in radiant robes, they embraced their mother, their tears of joy mixing with her own.

The truth quickly unraveled. Word reached the king who, upon hearing the *sadhu baba’s* testimony and witnessing the miraculous transformation, realized the extent of his queens’ treachery.  Filled with remorse, he rescued Rani Durba from the *ghordaal*, adorned her in jewels, and welcomed her back to the palace with immense respect. The guilty queens were punished justly (usually exiled or worse, depending on the telling), and Rani Durba and her seven sons brought prosperity and joy to the kingdom. The boys, known as Shat Bhai Champa, grew up to be wise and valiant princes, always cherishing the memory of their early struggle and their mother's enduring love. They understood that the power of love can overcome the darkest treachery and turn even flowers back into princes.

**Cultural Nuances Preserved:**

*   **Kinship Terms:** The relationships are carefully defined using terms like *dai ma* (midwife), *rani* (queen), *narashishu* (newborn child), *saptaputro* (seven sons), *sadhu baba* (holy man).
*   **Rituals:** The significance of *puja*, *amrito jol* (ambrosial water), and *alta* (red dye) are maintained, reflecting their role in Bengali cultural practices. The lullaby *"Aai, aai chonda, mama tor kotha…"*, embodies a mother's love and connection to her child.
*   **Themes:** Emphasis on the destructive power of jealousy (*rishiri bhare dushho bhare*), the importance of unwavering maternal love, and the triumph of good over evil.
*   **Setting:** The story is situated in a traditional Bengali kingdom with specific locations like the *upaban* (royal garden) and *ghordaal* (stable) mentioned.
*   **Language:** Certain phrases, like the chant "*Sat Bhai Champa, keu ki acho? Tumi ki amar putra ho?* ", are used to retain the authentic Bengali flavor.

This retelling aims to capture the essence of the original folktale, maintaining its cultural context and emotional depth. The specific details may vary slightly depending on the version from Thakurmar Jhuli, as with all oral traditions. However, the core narrative and its underlying themes remain consistent, offering a glimpse into the rich storytelling heritage of Bengal.
