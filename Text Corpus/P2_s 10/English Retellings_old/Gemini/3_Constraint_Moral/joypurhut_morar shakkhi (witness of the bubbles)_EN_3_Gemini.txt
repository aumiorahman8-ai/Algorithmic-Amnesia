A wealthy, dying merchant had accumulated his riches through deceit. Knowing death was near, he summoned two witnesses: a poor barber and a powerful king, and entrusted them to divide his massive fortune equally between his two feuding sons. After he died, the king, consumed by greed, influenced the barber to lie, awarding the elder son almost everything.

The younger son, destitute, wandered disconsolate. He built a pyre to end his life, but before lighting it, he threw rice into a nearby pond, creating bubbles. A wise old woman, witnessing this, inquired. He explained his plight. She instructed him to summon the king and barber back to the pond, claiming the bubbles, 'morar shakkhi', witnessed his father's will.

The king, terrified and superstitious, confessed his deception. The barber, overwhelmed by guilt, corroborated. Thus, the bubbles restored justice, righting the wrong done.

The tale teaches the virtues of honesty and integrity, contrasting them with the destructive power of greed and deceit. It also highlights the existence of a higher power, represented by the bubbles, capable of exposing wickedness and restoring balance when earthly justice fails. Ultimately, it underscores the importance of righteousness and the inevitable consequences of corruption.
