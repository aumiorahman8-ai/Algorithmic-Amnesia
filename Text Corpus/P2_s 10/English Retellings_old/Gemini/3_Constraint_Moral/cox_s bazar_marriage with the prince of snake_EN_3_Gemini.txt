Chandrabati, a beautiful maiden, was renowned for her piety and devotion to the goddess Manasa. One day, while offering prayers, she discovered a snake prince, injured and seemingly lifeless. Her compassion overwhelmed her, leading her to nurse him back to health. Unknown to her, the snake prince, Chandradhar, was deeply moved by her kindness and fell in love. He eventually revealed his true form and proposed marriage.

Chandrabati, torn between societal expectations and her own feelings, hesitated. Snakes were considered inauspicious, and marriage to one was unheard of. Her fear of societal judgment and what it would bring was great. However, Chandradhar pleaded, emphasizing his genuine love and devotion. Witnessing his sincerity, Chandrabati chose love and compassion over societal norms, agreeing to marry him.

The folktale imparts the importance of compassion, empathy, and judging others based on their character rather than outward appearances or societal prejudices. Chandrabati teaches us to challenge preconceived notions and prioritize genuine love and intention while showing the importance of the strength to act on it.
