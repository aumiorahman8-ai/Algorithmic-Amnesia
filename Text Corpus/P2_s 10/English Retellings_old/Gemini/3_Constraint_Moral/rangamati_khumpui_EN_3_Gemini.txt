A greedy and proud Brahmin, obsessed with fame, undertook a dangerous journey to collect a magical mushroom named 'Khumpui' believed to grant immense knowledge. Despite warnings, he sneered at the simple villagers, relying on his intellect alone. He found the elusive fungus deep in a perilous forest, guarded by spirits.

His arrogance made him disobey the spirits' instructions: to cook and eat the Khumpui immediately, alone and in silence. He planned to share it with royalty, hoping to gain even greater renown. Delighted with his perceived cleverness, he carried the mushroom back.

However, on his return, Khumpui began to decompose, its power fading. By the time he reached the court, it was useless, and the Brahmin was left empty-handed and humiliated. He learned a harsh lesson: pride comes before a fall, and ignoring the wisdom of those who possess local knowledge leads to failure. Khumpui teaches us the value of humility, respecting tradition, and appreciating the present rather than chasing future glory. It's a tale about listening to seasoned voices and understanding that knowledge is not always meant to be hoarded or exploited for personal gain.
