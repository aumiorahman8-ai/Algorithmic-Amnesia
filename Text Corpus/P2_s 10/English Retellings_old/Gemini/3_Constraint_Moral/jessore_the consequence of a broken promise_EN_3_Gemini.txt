Once in a Bengal village, lived a woodcutter named Ram, known for his honeyed words but weak promises. He vowed to offer the first fruit of his mango tree to the village deity, but when luscious mangoes grew, greed overpowered him. He ate them all, forgetting his promise.

Soon after, Ram fell terribly ill. No medicine worked. An old woman, wise in the ways of the village, recognized the curse of a broken vow. She advised Ram to recreate the mango grove in miniature, entirely from sugar, and offer it to the deity, truly repenting his deceit.

Ram, humbled and weak, painstakingly sculpted the sugar grove, pouring his remorse into each sugary mango. He offered it to the deity with tears in his eyes. Slowly, his health recovered.

The tale teaches that reneging on a promise, especially one made to a higher power or community, breeds consequences. It emphasizes the importance of honesty and integrity. While tempting to prioritize immediate desires, the story reminds us that actions have repercussions, and true repentance and restitution can often heal the breach created by broken trust. It warns against the dangers of greed and the enduring power of keeping one's word.
