Queen Lalana and Queen Kamalini, though married to the same king, were drastically different in fortune. Lalana, blessed with ten sons, was envied by the childless Kamalini. Jealousy festered, and Kamalini, fueled by a wicked maid, plotted against Lalana. Each time Lalana gave birth, Kamalini replaced the babies with puppies and had them buried alive.

But fate intervened. From beneath the soil, ten Champa saplings sprouted, transforming each day into ten beautiful boys, known as 'Shat Bhai Champa' (Seven Brothers Champa). A wise old woman, privy to their secret, prophesied their eventual reunion with their mother.

Years later, a magical river transported the ten brothers to the king's court where their beautiful singing and demeanor charmed everyone. When the king heard their melodious song about their true mother’s suffering, he saw through Kamalini's deceit.

Kamalini was punished, and Lalana was reunited with her long-lost sons. The tale teaches the dangers of jealousy and the power of truth. It also highlights the unwavering bonds of family and the eventual triumph of good over evil, even when faced with cruelty and injustice. It reinforces the belief that truth will always prevail and bring justice.
