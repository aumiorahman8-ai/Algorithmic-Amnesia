Sheyal Pondit, the clever fox, boasted of his wisdom to a tiger. The tiger, impressed, asked Sheyal to teach him. Sheyal agreed, but fearing the tiger's strength, devised a plan.

He led the tiger to a farmer's field where they were seemingly stuck to a buffalo scarecrow tied with rope. Sheyal claimed it was a test of strength and intelligence. He then 'freed' himself by feigning strain and cleverly untying his rope, leaving the tiger bound.

The farmer, seeing the tiger trapped, thrashed him mercilessly. Sheyal, watching from afar, laughed. The tiger, finally free, understood Sheyal's deception and vowed revenge.

The moral of the story is two-fold. Firstly, it highlights the dangers of boasting and being easily flattered. The tiger's vanity blinded him, making him vulnerable to Sheyal's trickery. Secondly, it demonstrates the importance of real wisdom over superficial intelligence. While Sheyal was clever, his deceitful actions ultimately revealed his lack of genuine understanding of respect and honesty, illustrating that true strength lies not just in power but in integrity. Relying solely on cunning can backfire.
