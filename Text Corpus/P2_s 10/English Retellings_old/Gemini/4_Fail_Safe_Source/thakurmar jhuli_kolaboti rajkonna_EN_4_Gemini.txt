Once upon a time, a king with seven queens – the Eldest, Second, Third, Fourth, Fifth, Unfavored, and Youngest - was unhappy because none had given him a child.

A hermit gave the Eldest Queen a root, promising beautiful sons if they shared it. The queens agreed to share, with each preparing a portion of the meal. At the kitchen, the Eldest, Second, Third, Fifth, and Unfavored Queen did tasks; the Fourth drew water; the Youngest cleaned fish near an ash heap. The Eldest Queen had the Unfavored Queen grind the root. The Unfavored ate a piece, then gave its paste to the Eldest Queen. The Eldest Queen ate some then passed it around, with each other queen taking a portion– the Fourth Queen found only a tiny bit. The Youngest Queen received nothing and fainted as a result. The other queens blamed each other. The fourth queen suggested the youngest queen try drinking water runoff from the grinding stone. The others agreed mockingly.

Ten months and ten days later, the Eldest, Second, Third and Fifth queens each gave birth to beautiful sons. (Hira, Manik, Moti, Shankha, and Kanchan) To the shame of the Fourth Queen she had an owl (Bhutum), and the Youngest, a monkey (Buddhu). The kingdom rejoiced at the birth of 5 royal heirs, and mourned that of the unfortunate owl and monkey.

The Fourth and Youngest Queens were shunned, becoming a maid in the menagerie and a servant collecting dung cakes.

As the princes and their sons grew, the five princes behaved badly and caused general distress. As children, Bhutum and Buddhu played on the bakul tree outside of their mother's hut. Bhutum fed birds and Buddhu brought fruits to their mothers in the forest and lived humble lives.

One day, the princes found Bhutum and Buddhu. They were captured for pets, and taken to the palace.The menagerie maid and dung-cake-collecting servant mothers were devastated. Discovering their mothers' identities, the princes cast Bhutum and Buddhu out. Bhutum and Buddhu realized they are also king’s sons!

The princes left on Mayurpankhi boats for Princess Kalabati’s kingdom to win her hand, unaware the princess was unattainable. Buddhu and Bhutum were refused Mayurpankhi Boats for the same journey. The wives mockingly slap Buddhu. Buddhu and Bhutum built boats made of areca-nut sheaths and set sail for Kalabati’s kingdom themselves. Bhutum and Buddhu’s mothers, weeping on the bank, sent small areca-nut boats afloat in hopes that the princes would come home safe.

The princes were captured and eaten by Three Old Crones. Arriving after, Buddhu and Bhutum freed them from the crones’ stomachs through their noses. The princes continued the journey.

The royal boats were then lost in the Red River’s magical, bankless and shoreless waters. Buddhu and Bhutum, in their canoes, guided them to clearer waters where the others revived. Out of greed and cruelty, the princes threw Buddhu and Bhutum overboard.

Because of its magic, the boats suddenly sank. Buddhu dove and found more magic causing the boats to sink with the princes, soldiers and crewmembers into another world. Once he rescurd them he brought the princes, boats and Princess Kalabati back.

After securing the princess the princes once again abandoned Bhutum and Buddhu back in the river as they returned home as heroes.

While visiting their mothers, people were amazed by the magical effects Buddhu and Bhutum’s presense created (market, fruit, and armed guard). The return started the events to restore the unfortunate fourth and youngest queens.

A wedding was arranged between the princess and one of the princes but Princess Kalabati was magically bound to whom had the magical dhol-dogor which Buddhu owned. The prince owners were challenged.

Princess Kalabati and his father the King were restored the fourth and youngest queens to the royal family.

Although Buddhu and Bhutum helped them travel to the kingdom of Princess Kalabati, they shut themselves in to block them from entering.

The Fourth and Youngest Queens discovered the secret of Buddhu and Bhutum's appearance: monkey and owl skins. When those special skins were burned, both princes took their former true appearance as handsome sons of the king.

Buddhu and Bhutum, now known by their new names, Budhkumar and Rupkumar, lived happily as noble princes with their loving wives, the other queens and princes never to be seen again.
