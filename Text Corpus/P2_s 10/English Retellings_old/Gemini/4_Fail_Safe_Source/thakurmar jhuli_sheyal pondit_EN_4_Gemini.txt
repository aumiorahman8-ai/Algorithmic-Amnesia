Once, a jackal, wanting to be like his father who built a wall, opened a school in the Shanti forest. Insects and other small creatures became his pupils, filling the forest with noise.

A crocodile, not wanting his sons to be uneducated, brought his seven sons to the jackal's school. The jackal promised to make them master archers in a week. Each day, the jackal ate one of the crocodile's sons as a snack.

On the sixth day, the crocodile, excited to see his learned sons, went to the school. The jackal, hiding the last son, convinced the crocodile to leave him for one more day to become a master archer. The foolish crocodile agreed.

The next day, the jackal ate the last crocodile son, closed his school, and fled. The crocodile discovered the truth and vowed revenge.

He hid in a canal, waiting for the jackal. Driven by hunger, the jackal eventually jumped into the canal to catch crabs. The crocodile caught the jackal's leg, but the jackal tricked him into biting a reed instead, and escaped.

Later, the crocodile pretended to be dead on the canal bank. The jackal, wary, said that a dead creature's ears must twitch and tail must slap. The crocodile, trying to prove his death, twitched his head and slapped his tail, revealing himself to nearby cowherds who attacked him. The jackal fled again.

Seeking food, the jackal ate eggplants in a field, where a thorn pierced his nose. He sought help from a barber, who accidentally cut off his nose. The jackal, in exchange for walking away without a further problem, forced the barber to give him his nail-clipper as recompense.

He then traded the nail-clipper for a pot from a potter. The pot was then broken by a firecracker at a wedding. The jackal demanded the bride as compensation.

He left the drummer in charge of the bride, promising to return with a priest. While alone, the bride fell on a knife and split in two. The drummer's wife hid the remains, and the jackal, instead of taking the bride, then took the drummer's drum.

Climbing a palm tree, the jackal played the drum and sang about his misfortunes and gains until a slip caused him to fall.
