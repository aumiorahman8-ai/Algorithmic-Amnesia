In a village called Naran, lived Darbasing, a busy priest, and his two daughters, Khumpuiti and Khumbarti. While Khumbarti was known for her beauty, both sisters worked hard in their Jhum fields, enduring tough conditions to cultivate crops. One day, Khumpuiti, overwhelmed by their struggles and seeking help, wished for assistance, even from an animal. A multi-colored snake then appeared, transforming into a man who offered to help with the Jhum work under the condition that Khumpuiti never reveal his true form and feed him rice daily.

The snake helped build a Jhum hut, cleared the field, and built bird-scarers to protect their crops. Meanwhile, Khumpuiti's health declined as she shared her rice with the snake. Her father noticed her declining health and questioned Khumbarti for the reason. After initial hesitations, Khumbarti revealed her sister's pact with the snake. Darbasing, fearing the snake and its drain on his daughter, plotted to kill it. He tricked Khumbarti into luring the snake with a feast. When the snake arrived, Darbasing ambushed and killed it with a sword.

Heartbroken, Khumbarti collected the severed pieces of the snake and placed them in places her elder sister frequented. Upon discovering the fate of her snake-husband, Khumpuiti, in deep sorrow, strung the pieces into a garland and drowned herself in the river, but before doing so, she instructed her younger sister to wait for the king's hunters at the Crossroads and follow their instructions.

Following her sister's final advice, Khumbarti reached the banyan and sat on one of its branches. True to her sister's vision, the hunters spotted her and notified the king. The king arrived, charmed by her beauty, and made her his queen. The king soon had a child, during her pregnancy the old queens hatched a plan to make Khumbarti look as if she gave birth to puppies and kittens by throwing the babies into the river and replacing them at birth. The king banished the youngest queen, Khumbarti, by the riverside to herd ducks.

Local people kept hearing a lullaby by the shore. The lullaby mentioned the babies wanted a mother who would relieve Khumbarti's hardship, herding ducks. People spread the word about the babies who appeared and disappeared. The king had all the fishermen cast nets, but no one caught the babies.

One day, after growing old enough, the seven children from the river talked to their aunt Khumpuriti, who told them what had happened to their mother and that their task was to go to the banyan where the hunters were and, just like their mother, answer that they are mortal. But that they could only reveal the identity of their mother if and only if the woman could stand on the ground, squeeze her breast, and make seven streams of milk reach their mouths.

This again, true to prophecy, happened just like that, and the king was once again notified and was there himself to witness it. The queen proved she was their mother and was once again held as one of the family and the six original queens cast out.
