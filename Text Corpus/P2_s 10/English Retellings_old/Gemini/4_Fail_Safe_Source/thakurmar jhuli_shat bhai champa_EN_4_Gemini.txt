A king with seven queens favored the youngest because she was kind. He was childless and sad. When the youngest queen became pregnant, he rejoiced and opened the treasury to the people. The jealous elder queens waited for the birth and falsely signaled the king with a golden chain he'd tied to the youngest queen. Twice they summoned him prematurely. Angered, he threatened to behead them if they did it again before the birth.

The youngest queen bore seven sons and a daughter, but the elder queens hid the children in earthen pots and buried them in an ash heap. They showed the king tadpoles and baby mice, claiming the queen had given birth to those things. The king, enraged, banished the youngest queen, who became a dung-gathering maid.

The kingdom suffered. One day, the gardener found seven Champa and one Parul flower on the ash heap. When he tried to pick them, the Parul flower called to the Champas, who refused to be picked unless the king himself came. Then they refused unless the first queen came, and then the second, and so on. Finally, the flowers said they would only be picked by the dung-gathering maid.

The king brought the youngest queen, and the flowers immediately turned into seven princes and a princess, who embraced their mother. The king saw the truth, buried the wicked queens in a pit of thorns and welcomed his youngest queen and children back to the palace.
