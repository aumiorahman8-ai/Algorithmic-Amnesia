Once upon a time, in a kingdom far away, lived a king and queen who longed for a child. Desperate, they consulted a wise old fakir. He advised them to eat a special pomegranate, but warned them that the queen could only eat *one* seed. Tempted by its delicious aroma and hopeful for a son, the queen devoured the entire pomegranate.

Soon after, she gave birth, not to one son, but to twelve! Eleven handsome princes emerged, but the twelfth was a small, inanimate doll. The king, disappointed and ashamed, ordered the doll to be thrown into the river.

The river gently carried the doll downstream, eventually resting it on the roots of a Dalim tree (Pomegranate tree) near a washerwoman's house. The washerwoman, who was childless, found the doll. Feeling pity, she took it home and cared for it, bathing it regularly.

One day, as she bathed the doll, it miraculously transformed into a radiant young boy. Overjoyed, she named him Dalim Kumar – the Pomegranate Prince. Dalim Kumar grew up strong, brave and intelligent, loved by the washerwoman as if he was her own.

Meanwhile, the king and queen were constantly visited by their eleven sons. They were selfish, cruel, and spent their days hunting and tormenting the people. The king, growing weary of their behavior, yearned for a son who was kind and righteous.

One day, the king's soldiers were causing havoc near the washerwoman's hut. Dalim Kumar, witnessing their cruelty, intervened andSingle-handedly defeated them. Impressed by his courage and righteousness, the villagers praised Dalim Kumar to the king.

The king, curious, summoned Dalim Kumar to the palace. Upon seeing him, the king was struck by his noble demeanor and intelligence, unaware of the boy's true identity. He challenged Dalim Kumar to a test: to spend the night alone in a haunted room rumored to steal lives.

Dalim Kumar accepted the challenge. Inside the room, he found a giant rakshasa (demon). Instead of fighting, Dalim Kumar cleverly befriended the beast, offering it food and comforting words. He learned that the rakshasa was lonely and misunderstood, forced to guard a chest containing a deadly spell.

The next morning, Dalim Kumar emerged unharmed, narrating his encounter. The king, astonished, pressed him for the secret of his survival. Dalim Kumar revealed the rakshasa's plight and the dangerous spell in the chest. He destroyed the spell, freeing the rakshasa from its torment.

The king, deeply moved by Dalim Kumar's compassion and bravery, realized the boy's true worth. He noticed a striking resemblance between Dalim Kumar and his eleven sons. After further questioning, the washerwoman revealed the truth about the pomegranate and the doll.

The king was overjoyed to find his lost son, Dalim Kumar. Realizing his past mistake, he publicly acknowledged Dalim Kumar as his rightful heir. The eleven princes, jealous and bitter, plotted to kill Dalim Kumar.

They arranged a hunting trip, lured Dalim Kumar into the forest, and pushed him into a deep well. However, a group of kind woodcutters discovered him and rescued him.

Learning of their treachery, Dalim Kumar returned to the kingdom. He didn't seek revenge. Instead, he exposed their cruelty and selfishness, showing the king and the people their true nature. Ashamed and repentant, the eleven princes were banished from the kingdom.

Dalim Kumar, the Pomegranate Prince, ruled wisely and justly. He brought peace and prosperity to the land, always remembering the kindness of the washerwoman and the lonely rakshasa, ensuring that justice and compassion guided his every decision. He was loved by all, a testament to the fact that true worth lies not in birth, but in character and deeds. And so, Dalim Kumar, who started as an inanimate doll, became the most beloved ruler the kingdom had ever known.
