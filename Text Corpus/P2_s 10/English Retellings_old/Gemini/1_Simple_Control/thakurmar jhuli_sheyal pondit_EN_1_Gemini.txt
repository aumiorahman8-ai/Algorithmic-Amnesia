Okay, let's retell the Bengali folktale of 'Sheyal Pondit' (The Clever Jackal). There are many variations, but here's a common and engaging version:

**The Clever Jackal (Sheyal Pondit)**

Once upon a time, in a lush green forest, lived a jackal. Now, this wasn't just any jackal. This jackal fancied himself a scholar, a 'Pondit' as they say. He was known for his cunning and quick wit, but more so for his immense ego – he was convinced he was the smartest creature in the entire jungle.

One day, the jungle animals, tired of the Sheyal Pondit's boasting and trickery, decided to teach him a lesson.  A wise old monkey, known for his calm demeanor, suggested a plan.

He proposed a contest. "Sheyal Pondit," the monkey called out, "we challenge you to prove your intelligence! We will give you a task. If you succeed, we’ll acknowledge your superior intellect. But if you fail, you must promise to stop your boasting and playing tricks on us."

The Sheyal Pondit, never one to back down from a challenge (especially one where he thought he'd undoubtedly win), readily agreed.

The animals huddled and came up with the task. High atop a very tall, slippery mango tree grew the ripest, juiciest mangoes imaginable. The task: the Sheyal Pondit had to bring a single mango down from the highest branch. No help allowed.

The Pondit was overjoyed! “So simple!” he thought. He swaggered over to the tree. The monkeys, the deer, the rabbits, even the slow-moving tortoises gathered to watch.

He started to climb. He used his claws and teeth, but the bark was smooth and slippery. He slipped, he slid, he struggled. The animals suppressed their laughter. The Sheyal Pondit huffed and puffed, his fur getting covered in sap. He got a little bit higher, but always slid back down, and he continued trying for many hours. He tried different angles.

Finally, exhausted, bruised, and covered in sticky sap, he collapsed at the base of the tree.  He looked up at the tantalizing mangoes, a picture of defeat. One of his strategies involved calling on the Monkeys for help, but the Monkey's told him that he had to do it on his own.

The monkey, still calm, approached him. "Well, Sheyal Pondit," he said kindly, "it seems you were unable to complete the task."

The Sheyal Pondit, humbled and ashamed, had to admit his failure.  "I... I was wrong," he mumbled, wiping mango sap from his eyes. "I am not as clever as I thought."

From that day on, the Sheyal Pondit was a changed jackal. He stopped his boasting and his tricks. He learned that true intelligence wasn't just about cleverness, but also about humility, perseverance and that sometimes it's okay to ask for help. He even started helping the other animals in the forest, using his quick wit for good.  He became known not for being 'Sheyal Pondit' the boastful, but 'Sheyal Pondit' the helpful.

And so, the forest lived happily ever after, all thanks to a single mango and a lesson learned by the previously boastful jackal. The animals also, learned a lesson about judging others based on one experience.
