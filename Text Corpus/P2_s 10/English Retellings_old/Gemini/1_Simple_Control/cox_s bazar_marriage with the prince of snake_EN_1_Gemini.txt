The land of Bengal simmered under the hot summer sun. In a small village, lived a beautiful maiden named Behula, known for her piety and kindness. Her father, Chand Saudagar, was a wealthy merchant, devoted to Lord Shiva. However, Chand stubbornly refused to worship the serpent goddess, Manasa, despite her repeated pleas. This infuriated Manasa, who vowed to bring ruin to Chand's life.

Chand had six sons, all of whom were killed tragically by Manasa's snakes. Each death brought immense sorrow, but Chand’s faith in Shiva remained unwavering. Finally, his seventh and youngest son, Lakhindar, was born. Everyone doted on him, especially Behula, who was betrothed to him at a young age.

As Lakhindar grew, Chand sought every precaution to protect him from Manasa's wrath. He consulted astrologers, and they predicted that Lakhindar would die on his wedding night from a snakebite. Despite the dire warning, Chand didn't break the wedding arrangement with Behula. He decided to build an iron house specifically to protect Lakhindar from snakes during their first night.  The impenetrable structure was constructed, hoping to foil Manasa's curse.

The wedding day dawned with joyous celebration. However, Manasa was relentless. She enlisted the help of the skilled sculptor, Sitha, to carve a tiny hole in the iron house, cleverly disguised and almost invisible. She then summoned Kulnagini, the most venomous snake of all, to fulfill the prophecy.

On the wedding night, despite the iron walls, Kulnagini slipped into the room through the almost imperceptible hole.  Behula, who had been meticulously watchful, saw the shadow of the snake slithering towards her beloved.  She threw herself over Lakhindar, trying to shield him, but Kulnagini struck, injecting its deadly venom into Lakhindar's hand.  He died instantly.

Behula was devastated, but her spirit remained unbroken.  Traditional customs demanded cremation, but Behula vehemently refused to leave her husband.  She declared she would take Lakhindar's body to the Ganga River and pray until he was resurrected. Ignoring the grieving protests of her family, she bravely embarked on a raft, with her husband's lifeless body laid beside her.

The long and arduous journey down the Ganga began. Behula survived by foraging for food, facing storms, and battling her own despair.  She prayed constantly to the gods, her unwavering faith a beacon in the darkness. Along the way, she encountered others who knew of Manasa's power and the suffering she inflicted.

Eventually, her raft reached the abode of the gods.  There, she danced before the court of Indra, the king of the gods, with such grace, skill, and devotion that even the gods were moved. Her performance, filled with sorrow and unwavering love, depicted the tragic story of her and Lakhindar.

Impressed by her dedication, Indra asked her what she desired.  Behula pleaded for the life of her husband.  Indra, touched by her devotion and faith, offered her a deal.  He would revive Lakhindar and the six sons of Chand Saudagar if, and only if, Chand Saudagar agreed to worship Manasa.

Behula agreed, knowing the difficult task that lay ahead. With Lakhindar revived, they returned to the village. She recounted her journey and the deal she had struck with the gods. Chand, stubborn as ever, initially refused.  He abhorred Manasa and refused to yield to her demands.

However, Behula, along with the pleas of the villagers who had suffered under Manasa's wrath, eventually wore down Chand's resistance. He finally relented and offered a small puja to Manasa, with the left hand rather than the right, symbolizing his continued reluctance.

This act, though begrudging, was enough.  Manasa, though still holding a grudge, was appeased.  The curse was lifted, prosperity returned to the village, and Behula's unwavering love and devotion had saved her husband and brought peace to the land. The tale of Behula's journey and sacrifice became a lasting legend, a testament to the power of love, faith, and the ultimate reconciliation between opposing forces. The story serves as a reminder that even the most powerful curses can be overcome with unwavering devotion and a willingness to find common ground, even when it seems impossible.
