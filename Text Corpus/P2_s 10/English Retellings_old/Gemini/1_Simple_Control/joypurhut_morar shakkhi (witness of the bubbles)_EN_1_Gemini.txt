The tale of 'Morar Shakkhi' or 'Witness of the Bubbles' is a classic Bengali folktale often told to showcase wit, righteousness, and the power of simple observation. Here's a retelling:

Once upon a time, in a small Bengali village, lived two brothers, Ram and Shyam. Ram was wealthy and influential, a man who commanded respect and wielded power. Shyam, on the other hand, was a humble farmer, honest but struggling to make ends meet.

One day, a priceless necklace belonging to the village headman, or “Pradhan,” went missing. Suspicion immediately fell upon Shyam. After all, he was poor, and the whispers quickly grew into accusations. Ram, eager to further his own standing in the village, joined the chorus of accusers, even adding lies and exaggerations to the narrative. He proclaimed Shyam greedy and dishonest, claiming to have seen him near the Pradhan's house that very day.

Shyam vehemently denied the theft, but no one believed him. The Pradhan, influenced by Ram’s fabricated evidence, ordered Shyam to be arrested and brought to trial. Overwhelmed by despair, Shyam knew he had no chance against his powerful brother.

An old and wise woman, the "Didima" of the village, known for her sharp intellect and fair judgment, noticed the injustice. She intervened and suggested a trial by ordeal, the "Morar Shakkhi," or "Witness of the Bubbles."

Here's how it worked: The accused and the accuser were to each immerse themselves in the village pond. They would need to shout their innocence (or in Ram's case, affirm Shyam's guilt) while submerged. The person who could stay underwater the longest would be deemed truthful, as the gods would favor the honest soul.

Ram, confident in his strength and believing in his manipulative skills, readily agreed. Shyam, though anxious, trusted Didima's wisdom. He knew that truth was on his side.

The entire village gathered at the pond. Ram, puffed up with pride, went in first. He bellowed accusations against Shyam, his voice echoing across the water before he went under. Everyone watched, impressed by his seeming endurance.

Then it was Shyam's turn. He waded into the water, his heart heavy with fear but firm in his conviction. He closed his eyes, took a deep breath, and instead of shouting, he simply sat still. He sat, and sat, and sat.

Ram, meanwhile, had already surfaced, gasping for air and congratulating himself. He demanded that Shyam be declared guilty. But the villagers, eyes wide with astonishment, pointed towards the pond.

Shyam was still underwater! He hadn't surfaced, and bubbles were slowly rising to the surface of the pond. He seemed to be able to stay submerged indefinitely. The villagers gasped. This was clearly divine intervention!

But then, Didima stepped forward. She pointed not at Shyam, but at a bundle of reeds floating near the surface. "Look!" she exclaimed. "Shyam is breathing through the reeds! He has cleverly used them to stay underwater, showcasing his intelligence and honesty!"

Indeed, Shyam had used his knowledge of the pond and the reeds to trick everyone. He hadn't lied; he hadn't claimed supernatural powers. He had simply used his wits.

The truth dawned on everyone. Shyam had not stolen the necklace. More importantly, Ram had knowingly lied and tried to frame his own brother. The Pradhan, ashamed, immediately released Shyam and ordered Ram to find the real thief.

Under pressure, Ram confessed that he had stolen the necklace himself, hoping to frame Shyam and increase his own wealth and influence.

Ram was punished for his treachery, and Shyam, exonerated and praised for his cunning and integrity, was rewarded. The stolen necklace was recovered, and the village learned a valuable lesson about the importance of truth, justice, and not being swayed by appearances or the power of the wealthy.

And so, the tale of 'Morar Shakkhi' continues to be told, reminding everyone that even in the face of overwhelming odds, honesty and wit can triumph over deceit and oppression. The bubbles didn't lie, but the true witness was the cleverness of Shyam, guided by the wisdom of the Didima, which ultimately brought the truth to the surface.
