The folktale spins around a poor woodcutter named Rupchand who lived a humble life with his wife in a small village nestled beside a dense, ancient forest. Rupchand, though poor, was known for his honesty and kept his word above all else.

One day, venturing deeper into the forest than usual, Rupchand stumbled upon a shimmering lake. The water was crystal clear, reflecting the deep blue sky like a mirror. As he knelt to drink, a voice boomed from the center of the lake, startling him.

Looking around, Rupchand saw a magnificent golden fish, its scales gleaming in the sunlight. The fish spoke, "Rupchand, I am the King of the Fish. I have been observing you. You are an honest and hardworking man. I will grant you a wish."

Rupchand was stunned. He thought for a moment and replied, "Great King, I wish for my wife and I to be free from poverty."

The Golden Fish nodded. "Your wish is granted. Return home. You will find your hut transformed. But remember this: you must never forget your humble origins. Do not become arrogant or greedy. And most importantly, you must never reveal the source of your good fortune to anyone, not even your wife. Promise me this, Rupchand."

Rupchand, overwhelmed with gratitude, readily promised. "I promise, Great King. I will keep your secret."

He rushed home and found his dilapidated hut transformed into a magnificent mansion. Servants bustled, and shelves overflowed with delicious food and fine clothes. His wife, Lakshmi, gasped at the sight. Rupchand, remembering his promise, just smiled mysteriously and told her that fate had brought them this good fortune.

Life was good. They feasted, wore fine clothes, and enjoyed the comfort of their new life. Rupchand, however, diligently continued his woodcutting, reminding himself of his past.

Lakshmi, though enjoying the luxurious life, grew increasingly curious. Where did this wealth come from? She badgered Rupchand relentlessly, nagging him day and night. She pleaded, she cried, she even threatened to leave him if he didn't reveal the truth.

Rupchand resisted for as long as he could, remembering his promise to the Golden Fish. But Lakshmi's constant pressure eventually wore him down. One fateful evening, weak and exhausted from her endless questioning, he broke. He confessed everything, revealing the secret of the Golden Fish and his promise.

As soon as the words left his lips, a violent tremor shook the earth. The mansion began to crumble, and the servants vanished into thin air. The fine clothes turned to rags, and the overflowing shelves emptied. In a blink, they were back in their original dilapidated hut, poorer than before.

Rupchand, filled with remorse, rushed back to the lake. The Golden Fish was gone. The once clear water was now murky and disturbed. He called out for the King of the Fish, but only the echo of his voice answered.

He understood. He had broken his promise, and the consequences were devastating. They were not just back to poverty, but had also lost the trust and blessing of the Golden Fish. From that day on, Rupchand and Lakshmi lived a life of even greater hardship, a constant reminder of the devastating consequence of a broken promise, a testament to the importance of honoring one's word, even when faced with immense pressure. The tale served as a lasting lesson to the villagers and all who heard it, emphasizing the importance of integrity and the lasting damage caused by dishonesty and breaking a sacred pact.
