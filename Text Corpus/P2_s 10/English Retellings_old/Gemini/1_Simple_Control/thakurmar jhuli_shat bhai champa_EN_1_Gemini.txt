Okay, here's a retelling of the Bengali folktale of Shat Bhai Champa (Seven Brothers Champa), incorporating its key elements and themes:

In the heart of ancient Bengal, a king had two wives. The first wife, Soraani (or Surani), was beautiful and kind, but barren. The second wife, Dourani, was jealous, spiteful, and eager to secure her place as queen. When the king announced his plan to seek blessings from a certain baba to have children, Dourani saw a golden opportunity.

Before the king even left, Dourani secretly bribed the royal physician, ensuring that when Soraani eventually conceived, she would claim the queen had birthed puppies and kittens.  As fate would have it, Soraani conceived and, due to the Baba's blessings, miraculously gave birth not to the animals Dourani plotted, but to seven beautiful, golden *champa* (a type of fragrant flower) buds.

Dourani, consumed by spite, carried out her twisted plan. She had the palace guards hide Soraani away and present seven puppies and kittens to the king. She also secretly disposed of the *champa* buds into a nearby pond, using her cunning and lies to paint Soraani as cursed. The king, heartbroken and ashamed, was left to believe his beloved Soraani had born beasts. Soraani was cast out and humiliated, left to live a life of hardship and sorrow.

Unbeknownst to everyone, the *champa* buds, once immersed in water, transformed into seven beautiful, golden-skinned boys, the Shat Bhai Champa, or Seven Brothers Champa. They lived a hidden life by the pond, raised by a kind old woman who had witnessed Dourani's treachery. The old woman kept their existence a secret, caring for them and telling them the story of their mother.

The brothers grew up strong, wise, and exceptionally talented. They had a magical gift - when the sun rose, they transformed to human form, and when the sun set, they reverted back to the lovely champa flowers.  They spent their human days hunting, learning, and helping the villagers around the pond. Their goodness and bravery earned them the love and respect of everyone they encountered.

One day, the king, during a hunting trip, stumbled upon the pond and witnessed the boys' magical transformation. He was astonished by their beauty and skill with their bows and arrows. Curious, he questioned them, and eventually, they revealed their true identity and the tragic tale of their mother, Soraani.

The king, overcome with grief and remorse, raced back to the palace and confronted Dourani. He condemned her cruelty and treachery. The royal physician, filled with guilt, confessed his role in the plot. Dourani, exposed for the liar and manipulator she was, was punished (details vary depending on the version, sometimes exile, sometimes imprisonment). The king then searched for Soraani and brought her back to the palace with great honor.

Finally, the brothers remained in human form, now revealed as princes, and were reunited with their mother. They lived happily ever after, restoring justice and prosperity to the kingdom. Soraani was reinstated as the queen, and The Shat Bhai Champa became beloved princes.
The tale reminds us that truth prevails, and justice exists for people who are good.
The End.
