### **Shat Bhai Champa (The Seven Brothers and Champa)**
Once, in a village, there lived seven brothers who were deeply devoted to each other. Their parents had passed away, leaving them to care for one another. The eldest brother, **Dada**, took on the role of the family head, while the youngest, **Chhoto**, was the most beloved.

One day, the brothers decided to marry. They said, *"We will all marry the same girl, and she will be our shared wife."* They searched far and wide until they found a beautiful girl named **Champa**, the daughter of a poor farmer. Her father, hesitant at first, agreed when he saw the brothers' unity and love.

The wedding was held with great joy. The brothers performed the **Sindoor Daan** (vermilion ceremony) together, and Champa wore the **Loha** (iron bangle) as a symbol of her marriage. The village celebrated with **Dhunuchi Naach** (dance with incense burners) and **Sankha-Pola** (conch shell and bangle rituals).

### **The Test of Loyalty**
After the marriage, the brothers worked hard in the fields while Champa managed the household. One day, the eldest brother, Dada, fell seriously ill. The others tried every remedy, but nothing worked. Finally, a **Kabiraj** (traditional healer) said, *"Only the heart of a human can save him."*

The brothers were horrified. How could they kill an innocent person? But Champa, hearing this, said, *"If my heart can save Dada, take it."* Before anyone could stop her, she took a knife and ended her life.

### **The Miracle**
As Champa’s blood touched Dada’s lips, he opened his eyes, fully healed. The brothers were grief-stricken. They buried Champa with great sorrow, performing the **Shraddha** (ancestral rites) for her soul.

That night, a divine voice spoke to them: *"Your love and sacrifice have moved the gods. Champa shall return to you."* The next morning, Champa stood before them, alive and radiant. The brothers rejoiced, and from that day on, they lived in peace, their bond stronger than ever.

### **Moral of the Tale**
The story teaches the power of **selfless love** and **sacrifice** in Bengali folklore. Champa’s devotion and the brothers' unity are celebrated as ideals of familial love.
