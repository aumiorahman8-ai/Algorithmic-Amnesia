### **Kolaboti Rajkonna (The Princess Who Turned into a Pumpkin)**

Once upon a time, in a far-off kingdom, there lived a kind and beautiful princess named **Kolaboti**. She was the beloved daughter of the **Raja** (king) and **Rani** (queen), and the people adored her for her gentle nature.

One day, the Raja and Rani had to leave the palace for an important journey. Before departing, they called Kolaboti and said, *"Beta (child), we must go away for some time. Stay here with your **Thakuma** (grandmother) and obey her words. Do not open the door for anyone, no matter what they say."*

Kolaboti nodded and promised to follow their instructions.

After the Raja and Rani left, a wicked **Dakini** (witch) disguised herself as an old beggar woman and came to the palace gates. She knocked and called out in a weak voice, *"Baba (child), please open the door. I am hungry and thirsty. Give me some food."*

Kolaboti, remembering her parents' warning, refused. But the Dakini was clever. She kept pleading, *"Beta, I am just a poor old woman. Your parents would want you to help the needy."*

Finally, Kolaboti’s heart softened, and she opened the door. The moment she did, the Dakini cast a terrible spell on her, turning her into a **kolaboti** (pumpkin)!

The Dakini then took Kolaboti’s form and sat on the throne, waiting for the Raja and Rani to return.

When the royal couple came back, they were shocked to see their daughter behaving strangely—she was rude, greedy, and nothing like their sweet Kolaboti. The Raja, sensing something was wrong, called his **Purohit** (priest) to perform a **mantra** (sacred chant) to reveal the truth.

The Purohit chanted holy verses, and the Dakini’s disguise melted away. The Raja and Rani were horrified to see the witch in their daughter’s place. They demanded, *"Where is our Kolaboti?"*

The Dakini laughed wickedly and said, *"She is gone forever! I turned her into a pumpkin!"*

The Raja and Rani wept in despair. But the Purohit assured them, *"Do not lose hope. If we perform a **yagna** (sacred fire ritual) and offer prayers to **Ma Durga**, she may restore your daughter."*

The royal family conducted the yagna with great devotion. As the flames rose, the goddess Durga appeared in a divine vision and said, *"Your daughter is trapped in the form of a pumpkin in the palace garden. Cut it open, and she will be free."*

The Raja rushed to the garden and found a golden pumpkin glowing under the moonlight. With trembling hands, he cut it open—and out stepped Kolaboti, as beautiful and kind as ever!

The Dakini, seeing her magic undone, fled in terror, never to return. The kingdom celebrated, and Kolaboti was reunited with her parents. From that day on, she never opened the door to strangers again.

And so, the tale of **Kolaboti Rajkonna** teaches us the importance of obedience, wisdom, and the power of divine grace.

### **Cultural Nuances Preserved:**
- **Kinship Terms:** *Beta* (child), *Thakuma* (grandmother), *Baba* (affectionate term for child).
- **Rituals:** *Mantra* (sacred chant), *Yagna* (fire ritual), invocation of *Ma Durga*.
- **Folklore Elements:** The Dakini’s deception, the pumpkin transformation, and divine intervention.
