### **Sheyal Pondit (The Fox Priest)**
*(From Thakurmar Jhuli)*

Once, in a village, there lived a clever fox named **Sheyal Pondit** (Sheyal the Priest). He was known for his cunning ways and often tricked others. One day, he decided to play a prank on his friend, **Kakadwip** (Crow Island), a wise old crow.

Sheyal Pondit went to Kakadwip and said, *"Dada (elder brother), I have heard that you are very learned. Why don’t you become a priest and perform rituals?"*

Kakadwip, flattered, agreed. Sheyal Pondit then said, *"But first, you must observe **brata** (a vow) and fast for three days. Only then can you perform the **puja** (worship)."*

Kakadwip, eager to prove himself, began his fast. On the third day, weak from hunger, he waited for Sheyal Pondit to bring him food. But the sly fox had no intention of feeding him. Instead, he brought a **muri** (puffed rice) and **chira** (flattened rice) offering, saying, *"This is your **prasad** (blessed food). Eat it only after the puja."*

Just as Kakadwip was about to eat, Sheyal Pondit shouted, *"Wait! You must first take a bath in the pond!"*

Kakadwip flew to the pond, but as soon as he dipped his beak into the water, Sheyal Pondit pushed him in! The poor crow struggled, but the fox laughed and said, *"Now you are truly purified!"*

Kakadwip, drenched and humiliated, realized he had been tricked. He flew away, vowing never to trust Sheyal Pondit again.

From that day on, whenever someone acts too clever for their own good, people say, *"Just like Sheyal Pondit—tricking others but getting caught in the end!"*

---

### **Cultural Nuances Preserved:**
1. **Kinship Terms:** *"Dada"* (elder brother) is used respectfully.
2. **Rituals:** *Brata* (vow), *puja* (worship), and *prasad* (blessed food) are key elements.
3. **Food Items:** *Muri* (puffed rice) and *chira* (flattened rice) are traditional offerings.
4. **Moral Lesson:** The tale warns against blind trust in cunning individuals.

This retelling keeps the essence of *Thakurmar Jhuli*—simple, moral-driven, and rich in Bengali folk culture.
