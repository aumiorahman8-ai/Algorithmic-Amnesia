Certainly! The folktale of **"Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey"** is a beloved story from the **Mymensingh region** of Bangladesh, rich in cultural nuances, kinship terms, and rural Bengali traditions. Below is a retelling that preserves its original essence:

### **Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey**
*(A Mymensingh Folktale)*

In a small village near the **Brahmaputra River**, there lived two sisters—**Noshi** (the elder) and **Dimshi** (the younger). Their parents had passed away, leaving them to fend for themselves. Noshi was kind and hardworking, while Dimshi was lazy and cunning.

One day, as Noshi was cooking rice in an **earthen pot (hāṇḍi)** over a **chula (clay stove)**, a **monkey (bāndor)** crept into their kitchen. The monkey, clever and mischievous, snatched a handful of rice from the pot. Noshi, seeing this, scolded it gently:

> *"O bāndor, why do you steal? If you’re hungry, I’ll share my food with you."*

The monkey, understanding her kindness, bowed its head in gratitude and left.

Dimshi, who had been watching, scoffed. *"Why feed a thief? Next time, I’ll teach it a lesson!"*

The next day, as Noshi went to the **bāzār (market)** to sell vegetables, Dimshi stayed back. The monkey returned, hoping for food. But Dimshi, instead of offering rice, threw a **burning coal (gāchh)** at it. The monkey yelped in pain and fled, but not before vowing revenge.

That evening, as Noshi returned, she found Dimshi crying. *"The monkey stole all our rice!"* she lied. Noshi, trusting her sister, believed her.

The following morning, Noshi went to the **dhān kheṭ (paddy field)** to work. The monkey, still angry, followed Dimshi as she went to fetch water from the **kuā (well)**. As she bent down, the monkey pushed her into the well!

Hearing Dimshi’s screams, the villagers rushed to help. They pulled her out, drenched and shivering. When Noshi returned, she was horrified. *"What happened?"* she asked.

Dimshi, still trembling, confessed: *"I was cruel to the monkey, and it punished me."*

Noshi sighed. *"You should never harm those who seek kindness. Even animals remember cruelty."*

From that day on, Dimshi changed her ways. She helped Noshi in the fields, shared food with the poor, and even left a little rice for the monkey near the **tulsi maṇḍap (sacred basil plant)**.

The monkey, seeing her repentance, forgave her. And so, the sisters lived in peace, their bond stronger than before.

### **Cultural Nuances Preserved:**
1. **Kinship Terms:** *Noshi (elder sister), Dimshi (younger sister)*—common in rural Bengali naming.
2. **Household Items:** *Hāṇḍi (earthen pot), chula (clay stove), gāchh (burning coal), kuā (well).*
3. **Rural Life:** *Dhān kheṭ (paddy field), bāzār (market), tulsi maṇḍap (sacred space).*
4. **Moral Lesson:** The tale emphasizes **karma, kindness, and repentance**, common in Bengali folklore.
