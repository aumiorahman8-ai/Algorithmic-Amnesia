### **Morar Shakkhi (The Witness of the Bubbles)**

Long ago, in a village near **Joypurhut**, there lived a poor but honest farmer named **Ramu**. He had a small piece of land, a loyal wife named **Lakshmi**, and a young son, **Gopal**. Despite their hardships, they lived peacefully.

One day, Ramu’s wealthy neighbor, **Dhaniram**, accused him of stealing his prized cow. Dhaniram was a cunning man who wanted Ramu’s land and saw this as an opportunity to frame him. He dragged Ramu to the **panchayat** (village council) and demanded justice.

The **panchayat** was led by the wise **Moulavi Sahib**, who listened to both sides. Dhaniram presented false witnesses, but Ramu had no one to vouch for him. Just as the Moulavi was about to declare Ramu guilty, Ramu’s young son, Gopal, stepped forward.

**"Father is innocent!"** Gopal cried. **"The real thief is Dhaniram himself!"**

Dhaniram laughed. **"A child’s word means nothing! Where is your proof?"**

Gopal, though small, was clever. He said, **"I have a witness—** **Morar Shakkhi (the bubbles in the water)!** **If you throw a stone into the pond, the bubbles will tell the truth!"**

The villagers were puzzled, but the Moulavi agreed to the test. They all went to the village pond. Gopal took a stone and threw it into the water. As the bubbles rose, he said:

**"If my father is guilty, let the bubbles rise straight. If Dhaniram is the thief, let them swirl in circles!"**

The bubbles swirled wildly, and the villagers gasped. Dhaniram’s face turned pale. The Moulavi declared, **"The bubbles have spoken! Dhaniram is the liar!"**

Shamed and exposed, Dhaniram confessed. He had hidden the cow in his own barn. The panchayat punished him, and Ramu’s name was cleared.

From that day on, the villagers spoke of **"Morar Shakkhi"**—the witness of the bubbles—as a symbol of truth prevailing over deceit.

### **Cultural Nuances Preserved:**
1. **Panchayat System** – The traditional village council where disputes were settled.
2. **Kinship Terms** – Use of **"Baba" (father), "Ma" (mother), and "Moulavi Sahib" (respected elder).**
3. **Ritual of Truth** – The belief that nature (like bubbles in water) can reveal the truth, a common motif in Bengali folktales.
4. **Moral Lesson** – The story emphasizes honesty and cleverness over brute power.

This tale remains a beloved part of **Joypurhut’s** oral tradition, teaching that even the weakest can triumph with wisdom.
