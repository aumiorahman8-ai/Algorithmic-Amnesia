### **The Consequence of a Broken Promise**
*(A Folktale from Jessore, Bangladesh)*

Long ago, in a village near Jessore, there lived a poor but honest farmer named **Ramu Kaka** (uncle Ramu). He had a young daughter, **Maya**, who was as kind as she was beautiful. One day, while Maya was fetching water from the pond, she saw a **snake (sap)** with a golden hood resting on a lotus leaf. The snake spoke in a soft, melodious voice:

*"Maya, dear child, I am no ordinary snake. I am the guardian of this pond. If you promise to marry me, I will grant your family wealth beyond measure."*

Maya, though frightened, remembered her father’s struggles. She hesitated but finally said, *"I promise, but only if my father agrees."*

The snake nodded and vanished, leaving behind a **golden bangle (sonar churi)** in the water. Maya took it home, and when Ramu Kaka saw it, he was overjoyed. That night, their hut filled with gold coins, fine silk, and jewels.

But Ramu Kaka was troubled. *"How can I give my daughter to a snake?"* he thought. He consulted the village **purohit (priest)**, who warned, *"A promise to a divine being cannot be broken lightly. The consequences will be severe."*

Yet, Ramu Kaka, blinded by greed, decided to betray the snake. He arranged Maya’s marriage to a wealthy **zamindar’s (landlord’s) son** instead. On the wedding day, as the **boron (bride’s procession)** was about to leave, a terrible storm erupted. The sky darkened, and the snake appeared in its true form—a **Naga (serpent deity)** with fiery eyes.

*"You broke your promise,"* it hissed. *"Now, you will face the curse of the Naga!"*

Suddenly, the gold turned to dust, the silk rotted, and the zamindar’s son fled in terror. Maya, realizing her father’s folly, fell to her knees and begged forgiveness. The Naga, moved by her sincerity, spared her but cursed Ramu Kaka:

*"You will wander as a beggar, and your wealth will turn to ashes in your hands."*

From that day, Ramu Kaka was reduced to poverty, and no matter how much he earned, it slipped away like sand. The villagers whispered that this was the **Naga’s shrap (curse)** for breaking a sacred vow.

Maya, however, lived humbly, and in time, the Naga, seeing her pure heart, blessed her with a simple but happy life.

**Moral:** *A promise, once given, must be kept—especially to the divine. Greed brings ruin, but honesty and humility bring grace.*

### **Cultural Nuances Preserved:**
- **Kinship Terms:** *Ramu Kaka* (uncle), *Maya* (affectionate name for a girl).
- **Rituals & Customs:** *Boron* (bride’s procession), *purohit* (priest’s role in advising).
- **Supernatural Elements:** *Naga* (serpent deity), *shrap* (curse).
- **Regional Context:** Jessore’s rural setting, pond worship (common in Bengali folklore).
