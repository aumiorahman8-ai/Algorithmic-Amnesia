Certainly! The folktale of **Khumpui** is a cherished story from the **Chakma community** of **Rangamati**, Bangladesh, and parts of Mizoram, India. It is a tale of love, betrayal, and the supernatural, deeply rooted in the cultural and spiritual beliefs of the Chakma people. ### **The Tale of Khumpui**

Long ago, in the lush hills of Rangamati, there lived a beautiful young woman named **Khumpui**. She was the daughter of a respected **Chakma chief (Raja)**, and her beauty was so radiant that it was said even the **forest spirits (Jhumma deities)** admired her.

One day, while Khumpui was bathing in the **Karnafuli River**, a handsome young man named **Duluchampa** saw her from afar. He was the son of a powerful **spirit king (Jhumma Raja)** from the underworld. Enchanted by her beauty, Duluchampa decided to take her as his bride.

Using his supernatural powers, he transformed into a **golden deer** and lured Khumpui into the forest. When she followed the deer, he revealed his true form and declared his love. Despite her fears, Khumpui was captivated by his charm and agreed to marry him.

### **The Wedding and the Broken Promise**
Duluchampa took Khumpui to his **underworld kingdom (Jhumma Desh)**, where they lived in great splendor. However, he made her promise never to reveal his true identity to her family. If she did, he warned, he would be forced to leave her forever.

For many years, Khumpui lived happily with Duluchampa, and they had a son named **Bongthai**. But as time passed, she grew homesick. One day, she begged Duluchampa to let her visit her parents. Reluctantly, he agreed but reminded her of her promise.

### **The Betrayal and the Curse**
When Khumpui returned to her village, her family was overjoyed. However, her **elder brother (Dada)** noticed something strange—Khumpui’s son, Bongthai, had **supernatural strength** and could lift heavy stones with ease. Suspecting that Khumpui’s husband was not an ordinary man, he pressured her to reveal the truth.

Torn between love and loyalty, Khumpui finally confessed that Duluchampa was a **spirit king**. The moment she spoke the words, a great storm erupted, and Duluchampa appeared in a fury. "You have broken your promise!" he cried. "Now I must leave you forever."

Before vanishing, he cursed her: **"You will turn into a stone, and only when the river waters touch your feet will you be freed."**

### **The Stone Maiden and the Ritual of Liberation**
Heartbroken, Khumpui turned into a **stone statue**, standing motionless near the riverbank. Her son, Bongthai, grew up under the care of his grandfather, the Chakma chief. Years later, when Bongthai became a young man, he learned of his mother’s fate.

Determined to free her, he performed a **sacred ritual (Pujari’s chanting)** and prayed to the **Jhumma deities**. Then, with great effort, he carried the stone statue to the river, where the waters touched Khumpui’s feet. Miraculously, she returned to life!

### **The Reunion and the Lesson**
Khumpui was reunited with her son, but Duluchampa never returned. The tale of Khumpui serves as a cautionary story in Chakma folklore, teaching the importance of **keeping promises** and the consequences of betrayal.

To this day, in some Chakma villages, a **stone maiden (Khumpui’s statue)** is worshipped, and women perform rituals to honor her, seeking blessings for marital fidelity and protection from supernatural forces.

- **Rituals:** *Pujari’s chanting* (priest’s prayers), *stone maiden worship*.
- **Moral Lesson:** The consequences of breaking promises in Chakma tradition.
