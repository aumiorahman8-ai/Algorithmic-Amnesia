The Bengali folktale **"Morar Shakkhi" (The Witness of the Bubbles)** is a clever and humorous story about a poor but wise man who outsmarts a greedy king. Here’s a retelling of the tale:

---

### **The Witness of the Bubbles**

Long ago, in a village in Bengal, there lived a poor but intelligent man named **Gopal Bhar**. He was known for his wit and quick thinking. One day, the king of the land, who was both arrogant and unjust, decided to test Gopal’s intelligence.

The king summoned Gopal to his court and said, *"Gopal, I hear you are very wise. Let me see if you can solve this riddle: If I were to drown in the river, who would be my witness?"*

Gopal, understanding the king’s trick, replied, *"Your Majesty, if you were to drown, the bubbles rising from the water would be your witness!"*

The king, amused but not convinced, said, *"Prove it!"*

Gopal then asked the king to accompany him to the river. When they reached the bank, Gopal pushed the king into the water! As the king splashed and struggled, bubbles rose to the surface.

Gopal calmly said, *"See, Your Majesty? The bubbles are indeed your witness!"*

The king, now drenched and humbled, realized Gopal’s cleverness. He laughed and admitted defeat, rewarding Gopal for his wisdom.

From that day on, the king treated Gopal with respect, and the tale of **"Morar Shakkhi" (The Witness of the Bubbles)** became a famous example of how intelligence can outwit power.

---

### **Moral of the Story:**
The tale teaches that wisdom and quick thinking can triumph over arrogance and brute force. It also highlights the importance of humility, even for those in power.
