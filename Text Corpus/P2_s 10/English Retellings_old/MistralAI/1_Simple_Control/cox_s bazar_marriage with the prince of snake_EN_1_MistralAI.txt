Certainly! The Bengali folktale **"Marriage with the Prince of Snakes"** (or **"Sapurey Badshar Biye"**) is a popular tale about love, magic, and transformation. Here’s a retelling of the story:

---

### **The Marriage with the Prince of Snakes**

Once, in a small village, there lived a kind and beautiful girl named **Champa**. She was the youngest of three sisters, and while her elder sisters were cruel and lazy, Champa was gentle and hardworking.

One day, while fetching water from the pond, Champa saw a **golden snake** floating helplessly in the water. Feeling sorry for it, she carefully picked it up and placed it on the bank. To her surprise, the snake transformed into a **handsome prince**—the **Prince of Snakes**!

The prince thanked her and said, *"You have saved my life. In return, I will marry you."* Before Champa could respond, he disappeared, leaving behind a **golden bangle** as a token of his promise.

When Champa returned home, her sisters were furious. They demanded to know where she had gotten the bangle. When she told them about the snake prince, they mocked her, calling her a liar.

That night, the **Snake Prince** appeared in Champa’s dreams and said, *"Come to the pond at midnight, and I will take you to my palace."* Though frightened, Champa went to the pond as instructed. There, the prince appeared in his true form—a **magnificent serpent**—and took her to his **underwater kingdom**, where he lived in a palace of gold and jewels.

Champa was amazed by the beauty of the snake kingdom. The prince treated her with love and respect, and she lived happily as his queen. However, she missed her family and often thought of her village.

One day, the prince allowed her to visit her home, but he warned her, *"Do not stay past sunset, or you will never return to me."* Champa promised and went to see her sisters.

But her sisters, jealous of her wealth and happiness, tricked her into staying late. When Champa realized the sun was setting, she rushed back to the pond, but it was too late—the prince had vanished, and the pond had dried up.

Heartbroken, Champa wandered the forest, calling for her husband. Finally, an old **sage** appeared and told her, *"The Snake Prince has been cursed. To break the spell, you must find the **magic herb** that grows on the **Mountain of Serpents**."*

With great courage, Champa journeyed to the dangerous mountain, facing many trials. At last, she found the herb and used it to break the curse. The Snake Prince regained his human form permanently, and they returned to his kingdom, where they lived happily ever after.

As for Champa’s cruel sisters, they were turned into **stone statues** as punishment for their deceit.

---

### **Moral of the Story:**
- **Kindness is rewarded**—Champa’s compassion saved the prince.
- **Jealousy leads to ruin**—her sisters’ greed brought them misery.
- **True love conquers all**—Champa’s devotion broke the curse.
