### **The Tale of Dalim Kumar**

Long ago, in a small village in Bengal, there lived a kind and brave young man named **Dalim Kumar**. He was known for his intelligence and fearlessness. One day, as he was walking through the forest, he heard the cries of a beautiful woman trapped in a tree. The woman begged for his help, claiming she was a princess cursed by a wicked witch.

Dalim Kumar, being noble-hearted, climbed the tree to free her. But as soon as he touched her, the woman revealed her true form—a terrifying **witch (or demoness, often called a *Daini* or *Rakkhashi*)**! She cackled and said, *"Now you are mine! You must carry me on your back to my home, or I will eat you!"*

Dalim Kumar, though frightened, kept his wits about him. He agreed but set a condition: *"I will carry you, but you must not look back, no matter what you hear."*

The witch agreed, and Dalim Kumar began the long journey through the forest. As they walked, he started singing a silly song:

> *"Dalim Kumar carries a witch,
> The witch is old, her teeth are loose,
> Her hair is tangled, her nose is long,
> She stinks like rotten fish!"*

The witch, furious at being mocked, couldn’t resist looking back. The moment she turned her head, Dalim Kumar threw her into a deep ravine, where she fell to her doom.

But the witch was not so easily defeated. She returned as a **giant serpent** and coiled around Dalim Kumar, squeezing him tightly. Thinking fast, Dalim Kumar pretended to be amused and said, *"Oh, what a fine belt you’ve made for me! But it’s too loose—tighten it more!"*

The serpent, fooled by his trick, squeezed even harder—until it burst into pieces!

Still not giving up, the witch took the form of a **fierce tiger** and pounced on Dalim Kumar. Quick-thinking, he grabbed a handful of red chili powder from his pouch and blew it into the tiger’s eyes. The beast roared in pain and fled into the jungle, never to return.

Finally, the witch appeared as a **beautiful maiden** once more, weeping and begging for mercy. But Dalim Kumar, having learned his lesson, saw through her deception. He took out his sword and struck her down, freeing the forest from her evil forever.

From that day on, Dalim Kumar was celebrated as a hero, and his story was told to children as a lesson in **courage, cleverness, and never trusting strangers blindly**.

---

### **Moral of the Story**
The tale teaches that **evil can take many forms**, and one must stay alert and use intelligence to overcome danger. It also warns against blind trust and highlights the power of quick thinking in difficult situations.
