The Bengali folktale **"Shat Bhai Champa"** (শত ভাই চম্পা) is a charming and moralistic tale about unity, kindness, and the power of collective effort. Here’s a retelling of the story:

---

### **The Tale of Shat Bhai Champa**

Long ago, in a small village in Bengal, there lived a poor but kind-hearted woman named **Champa**. She was a widow with **one hundred sons** (Shat Bhai, meaning "a hundred brothers"). Despite their large number, the brothers were very close and always worked together.

One day, the village faced a terrible drought. The crops failed, and the people suffered from hunger. Champa, worried about her sons, prayed to the gods for help. That night, she had a dream where a divine voice told her:

> *"Go to the forest, and you will find a magical tree. Its fruits will feed your sons, but you must share them with others."*

The next morning, Champa and her sons went deep into the forest and found a **wondrous tree** laden with golden fruits. The brothers plucked the fruits and shared them with the entire village. The fruits were so magical that no matter how many were eaten, the tree never ran out.

However, a **greedy merchant** from a nearby town heard about the tree and wanted it for himself. He went to the king and lied, saying:

> *"The hundred brothers have stolen a magical tree from your kingdom! They are keeping its fruits for themselves."*

The king, believing the merchant, sent his soldiers to arrest the brothers. When the soldiers arrived, the brothers stood together, holding hands in a circle around the tree. No matter how hard the soldiers tried, they could not break the brothers' unity.

Seeing this, the king realized the truth—the brothers were innocent and generous. He punished the greedy merchant and rewarded Champa and her sons for their kindness.

From that day on, the **Shat Bhai Champa** became a symbol of **unity and selflessness**, teaching everyone that **togetherness is strength**.

---

### **Moral of the Story:**
- **Unity is power** – The brothers' bond made them unstoppable.
- **Kindness is rewarded** – Their generosity brought them blessings.
- **Greed leads to downfall** – The merchant’s lies backfired.

This folktale is often told to children in Bengal to teach the values of **brotherhood, honesty, and sharing**.
