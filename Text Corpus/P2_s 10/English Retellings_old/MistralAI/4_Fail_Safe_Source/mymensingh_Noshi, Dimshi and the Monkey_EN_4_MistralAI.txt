Here’s a retelling of the story based strictly on the source text:

Two sisters, Nasi and Dimsi, were walking along a deserted road when they heard a faint voice calling out to them. At first, they ignored it, but the voice persisted, saying, *"Hey Nasi, hey Dimsi, will you marry me?"* The younger sister remarked that someone seemed to be speaking to them, but they saw no one around. The voice repeated itself, and when they looked carefully, they spotted a monkey following them, jumping from tree to tree and playing tricks.

The monkey kept trying to tempt them, promising gifts like a water pot if they married him. Eventually, the sisters reached a wide, deep canal they couldn’t cross. Desperate, they struck a deal with the monkey: if he helped them cross, one of them would marry him. The monkey eagerly agreed. He swam across first to test the waters, then carried each sister one by one on his back to the other side.

Once safely across, the sisters laughed and said, *"Who would marry a monkey like you? We only said that to get across!"* The monkey was heartbroken but refused to give up. As they continued walking, the sisters saw a woodpecker’s nest high in a tree and heard baby birds chirping. They suddenly wanted the chicks and told the monkey, *"If you get us the baby birds, we’ll really marry you this time."*

The monkey climbed the tree and stuck his hand into the nest’s small hole. The sisters waited impatiently, but he didn’t come down. When they asked why, he replied, *"Let me count the chicks first."* After a long time, he still hadn’t returned, muttering, *"This one is for Nasi, this one is for Dimsi."* The sisters grew annoyed and decided to leave, saying they no longer wanted the birds.

Panicked, the monkey tried to pull his hand out, but it was stuck. He yanked with all his strength until his arm tore off, leaving it trapped in the hole while he fell into the thick mud below. No matter how hard he struggled, he couldn’t escape, and the sisters walked away, leaving him behind.
