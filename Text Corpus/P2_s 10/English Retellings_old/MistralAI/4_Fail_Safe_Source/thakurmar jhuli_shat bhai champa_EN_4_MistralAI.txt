**Retelling of the Story:**

A king had seven queens, but he loved the youngest one the most because she was gentle, while the elder queens were arrogant. For a long time, the king had no children, which made him very sad. When the youngest queen finally became pregnant, the king was overjoyed and announced a grand celebration, offering treasures to all.

The elder queens, burning with jealousy, tricked the king by tugging a golden chain tied between him and the youngest queen, making him rush to the birthing room only to find nothing. After warning them not to deceive him again, the youngest queen gave birth to seven sons and one daughter—beautiful children who filled the room with light. But the cruel elder queens lied, claiming she had given birth to animals instead. They then buried the children in pots under an ash heap and showed the king tadpoles and mice, causing him to banish the youngest queen in anger.

The youngest queen, now a dung-gathering maid, lived in sorrow. Meanwhile, the kingdom suffered—flowers withered, and the king’s worship was neglected. One day, the gardener found rare flowers blooming on the ash heap: seven Champa flowers and one Parul flower. When he tried to pick them, the flowers spoke, refusing to be taken unless the king himself came. Even then, they demanded each of the elder queens appear, one by one, but still would not yield.

Finally, the flowers declared they would only be picked by the "dung-gathering maid." When the youngest queen arrived, the flowers transformed into her seven sons and daughter, who joyfully embraced her. The king, realizing the truth, punished the elder queens by burying them in thorns and restored the youngest queen and her children to the palace, where celebrations erupted.
