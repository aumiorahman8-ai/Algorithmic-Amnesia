At the place where the Mathabhanga river met another, there was a long-standing tradition. Every merchant boat passing through had to offer some of its goods to the goddess. Whether the boat carried eggplants, radishes, jackfruits, or wood apples, a portion was always given. One day, a merchant refused to give anything and even insulted the goddess. As punishment, his boat, full of cargo, sank into the deep river.

Divers were called to recover the boat. They first tried to measure the river’s depth by lowering ropes, but the rope kept running out without ever reaching the bottom. No matter how much more rope they used, the riverbed remained unfound. No diver was brave enough to go down—until one man volunteered. He was equipped with ropes and diving gear and descended into the depths.

There, he saw a golden boat with two fairies sitting on either side of its deck. They were pulling on the rope, which had piled up into a huge heap. The man, frightened, said, *"Our boat has sunk, so I have come looking for it. Please forgive me, mother."* The fairies replied, *"You will get your boat back, but only if you promise not to tell anyone about this. If you do, you will vomit blood and die. Go to the Kazis' ghat, and you will find the boat there."*

The boat was indeed found at the Kazis' ghat. But the man could not keep the secret—he told his wife what had happened. As soon as he spoke, he vomited blood and died.
