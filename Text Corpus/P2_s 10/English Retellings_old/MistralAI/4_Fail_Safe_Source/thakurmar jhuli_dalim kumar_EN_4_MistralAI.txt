**Retelling of the Story:**

Once, there was a king who ruled with his queen and their prince. The queen’s life was tied to a pair of dice, a secret known only to an ogress hiding in the palace’s palm tree. One day, while the king was hunting, the prince played dice with his friends. The ogress, disguised as a beggar, tricked him into giving her the dice. With a breath, she sent them far away, causing the queen to faint. The ogress then devoured the queen and took her form.

The prince noticed something was wrong—his "mother" drooled like a beast. He left in silence, keeping his suspicions to himself. Years passed, and the king had seven more sons. The prince observed the palm tree withering, but said nothing.

When the seven brothers grew up, they prepared to travel the world. The king summoned the eldest prince, who joined them on winged horses. The ogress, realizing the prince was gone, panicked. She consulted a magical snake, Sutashankha, who revealed that the prince’s life was hidden in a pomegranate seed. She sent the snake to Pashabati’s kingdom with a message to marry her seven daughters to the ogress’s sons and destroy the prince.

The ogress hid the pomegranate seed under the palace steps, blinding the prince in the forest. His brothers rode ahead, unaware, and were later eaten by Pashabati’s sisters when they lost a dice game. The prince, now blind, was carried by his winged horse to a cursed kingdom where a princess killed kings each night. He defeated the snake inside her, regaining his sight and breaking the curse.

Learning his brothers were in danger, he rode to Pashabati’s city. He tricked the sisters into returning his brothers by using a kitten to kill the mouse controlling the dice. The ogress’s spell broke, revealing the sisters as worms. The prince returned home with his brothers, restoring the true queen. The cursed kingdom followed him, and the palm tree—where the ogress had hidden—collapsed, ending her power.

The kingdom rejoiced, and the prince’s family was reunited.
