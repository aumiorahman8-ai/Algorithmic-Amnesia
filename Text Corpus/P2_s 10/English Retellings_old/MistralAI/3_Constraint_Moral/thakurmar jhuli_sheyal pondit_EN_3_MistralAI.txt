**The Tale of Sheyal Pondit (The Fox Scholar)**

In a Bengali village, a clever fox named Sheyal Pondit tricked animals into believing he was a wise scholar. He wore a turban, carried a book, and spouted nonsense verses, fooling even the tiger, the king of the jungle.

One day, the tiger demanded Sheyal prove his wisdom. The fox, terrified, claimed he could bring the moon down from the sky. The tiger, eager to see this, followed Sheyal to a lake. The fox pointed at the moon’s reflection, saying, *"See, I’ve captured it!"* The tiger, amazed, bent down to drink—and Sheyal pushed him into the water, escaping.

Later, the tiger sought revenge. Sheyal, trapped, pretended to read a "divine message" warning that the tiger would die if he ate a fox. The superstitious tiger spared him.

**Morals:**
1. **Cunning overcomes brute strength** – Sheyal’s wit saved him from the mighty tiger.
2. **Blind faith in false wisdom is dangerous** – The animals’ gullibility made them easy prey.
3. **Arrogance leads to downfall** – The tiger’s pride in his own intelligence was his weakness.

The tale warns against deception but also celebrates cleverness in the face of oppression.
