**Kolaboti Rajkonna: A Bengali Folktale of Wit and Justice**

In a village, a poor but clever girl named Kolaboti lived with her mother. One day, the king, impressed by her intelligence, married her. However, the jealous queen mother plotted to kill Kolaboti by sending her to fetch a magical *shingara* (horn) from a demon.

Kolaboti outsmarted the demon by tricking him into revealing his weakness—a hidden hair that held his life. She burned the hair, killing him, and returned with the horn. The queen mother, furious, then demanded Kolaboti bring a *sonar kath* (golden stick) from a tiger’s nest. Again, Kolaboti used her wit, luring the tiger away with food and escaping with the stick.

Finally, the queen mother ordered Kolaboti to retrieve a *raj-hans* (royal swan) from a distant lake. Kolaboti befriended the swan, who carried her back safely. Realizing Kolaboti’s brilliance, the king exposed the queen mother’s cruelty and banished her. Kolaboti became the beloved queen, ruling wisely.

**Moral Lessons:**
1. **Intelligence triumphs over brute force** – Kolaboti’s cleverness saves her, proving wit is mightier than strength.
2. **Justice prevails** – Evil schemes fail when faced with truth and wisdom.
3. **Kindness and resourcefulness** – Kolaboti’s empathy (toward the swan) and quick thinking help her overcome challenges.

The tale celebrates the power of a sharp mind and moral integrity over deceit and oppression.
