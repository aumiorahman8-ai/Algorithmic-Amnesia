**The Witness of the Bubbles (Morar Shakkhi)**

In a Bengali village, a poor farmer named Gopal borrowed money from a greedy moneylender. Despite repaying the loan, the moneylender falsely accused him of defaulting and demanded more. To settle the dispute, they went to the king’s court.

The moneylender bribed the judge, who ruled against Gopal. Desperate, Gopal prayed to Goddess Kali, who appeared in his dream and told him to bring a pot of water to court the next day.

In court, Gopal claimed the bubbles in the water were his witnesses. The judge mocked him, but Gopal insisted. The moneylender, fearing divine intervention, confessed his lie. The king, realizing the corruption, punished the moneylender and returned Gopal’s money.

**Moral Lessons:**
1. **Truth Prevails** – Even when powerful people conspire against you, honesty and faith can triumph.
2. **Divine Justice** – The story reinforces the belief that higher powers protect the innocent.
3. **Exposing Corruption** – It highlights how cleverness and courage can uncover dishonesty in authority.

A timeless tale reminding us that justice, though delayed, will not be denied.
