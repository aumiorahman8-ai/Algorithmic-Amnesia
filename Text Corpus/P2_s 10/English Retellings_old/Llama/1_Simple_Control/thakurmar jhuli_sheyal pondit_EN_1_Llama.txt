'Sheyal Pandit' is a popular Bengali folktale that revolves around the life of a clever and cunning Brahmin named Sheyal Pandit. The story is a testament to the power of wit, intelligence, and cleverness.

Once upon a time, in a small village in Bengal, there lived a poor Brahmin named Sheyal Pandit. Despite his poverty, Sheyal was known for his exceptional intelligence, wit, and cunning nature. He was often called upon by the villagers to settle disputes and offer advice on various matters.

One day, the king of the land announced that he would give his beautiful daughter's hand in marriage to anyone who could answer his three riddles. The king's daughter was renowned for her beauty, and many suitors from far and wide came to try their luck. However, none of them could answer the king's riddles, and they were all sent back disappointed.

Sheyal Pandit, hearing about the king's challenge, decided to take on the task. He set out for the king's palace, despite being poor and not having the means to afford the journey. On his way, he met a group of travelers who were also on their way to the palace to try their luck. Sheyal cleverly joined their group and traveled with them, sharing their food and expenses.

When they arrived at the palace, the king presented his three riddles to Sheyal and the other suitors. The first riddle was: "What is it that is blind, yet sees; silent, yet speaks; and is born, yet dies?" Sheyal thought for a moment and then replied, "The answer is a river. A river is blind, yet it sees the path it flows; it is silent, yet it speaks through the sound of its waters; and it is born, yet it dies when it meets the sea."

The king was impressed with Sheyal's answer and presented the second riddle: "What can be broken, but never held? What can be given, but never sold?" Sheyal thought for a moment and then replied, "The answer is a promise. A promise is something that can be broken, but it can never be physically held in one's hands. A promise is something that can be given, but it can never be sold like a commodity."

The king was even more impressed with Sheyal's answer and presented the third and final riddle: "What is it that goes around the world, yet stays in a corner?" Sheyal thought for a moment and then replied, "The answer is a stamp. A stamp goes around the world on letters and packages, yet it stays in the corner of an envelope or package."

The king was delighted with Sheyal's answers and declared him the winner. As promised, the king gave his beautiful daughter's hand in marriage to Sheyal Pandit. Despite being a poor Brahmin, Sheyal's intelligence, wit, and cunning nature had won him the heart of the princess and the respect of the king.

Sheyal Pandit's story has been passed down for generations in Bengal, and it continues to inspire people to value intelligence, wit, and cleverness. The tale reminds us that with determination and clever thinking, even the poorest and most unlikely person can achieve great things.
