Dalim Kumar, also known as the "Pomegranate Prince," is a classic Bengali folktale that has been passed down through generations. The story goes like this:

Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a kind and gentle prince named Dalim Kumar. He was the son of a mighty king and queen, who ruled over a prosperous kingdom. The prince was loved by all, and his parents were extremely fond of him.

One day, a wicked and powerful demon, named Chand Saudagar, cast a spell on the prince, turning him into a pomegranate (dalim in Bengali). The demon's intention was to keep the prince captive and prevent him from growing up to become a powerful king.

The pomegranate-prince was then sold to a fruit vendor, who took him to a marketplace to sell. A poor old woman, who was a devotee of the goddess Durga, bought the pomegranate, unaware of the prince's true identity.

As the old woman was about to cut the pomegranate, Dalim Kumar, still trapped inside, prayed to the goddess Durga to save him. The goddess, moved by the prince's devotion, appeared before the old woman and revealed the truth about the pomegranate.

The old woman, filled with compassion, decided to keep the pomegranate safe and care for it. As time passed, the pomegranate began to grow and ripen, and the prince's spirit inside started to regain his strength.

Meanwhile, the wicked demon, Chand Saudagar, was searching everywhere for the pomegranate-prince, intending to destroy him once and for all. However, the goddess Durga, determined to protect Dalim Kumar, created a series of illusions to mislead the demon and keep him away from the old woman's house.

Eventually, the pomegranate ripened, and Dalim Kumar emerged from it, restored to his true form as a handsome prince. The old woman, overjoyed, took him to the palace, where he was reunited with his overjoyed parents, the king and queen.

The prince, now freed from the demon's spell, grew up to become a wise and just ruler, loved by his people. The wicked demon, Chand Saudagar, was punished for his evil deeds, and the old woman was rewarded for her kindness and devotion to the goddess Durga.

And so, the story of Dalim Kumar, the Pomegranate Prince, has been passed down through generations, reminding us of the power of kindness, devotion, and the protection of the divine.
