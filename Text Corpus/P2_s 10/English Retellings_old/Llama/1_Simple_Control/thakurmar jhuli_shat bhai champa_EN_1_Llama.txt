What a delightful tale! "Shat Bhai Champa" (Seven Brothers and Champa) is a classic Bengali folktale that has been passed down for generations. Here's my retelling of the story:

Once upon a time, in a small village in Bengal, there lived seven brothers who were known for their kindness, bravery, and unwavering dedication to one another. The brothers were extremely close, and their bond was unbreakable. They lived with their mother, who was a wise and loving woman.

One day, while the brothers were out working in the fields, a beautiful and kind-hearted girl named Champa arrived in the village. She was an orphan, and the villagers, taking pity on her, offered her shelter and food. The seven brothers, upon meeting Champa, were immediately drawn to her warmth and generosity. They decided to take her under their wing and treat her like a sister.

As time passed, Champa became an integral part of the brothers' lives. She would help their mother with household chores, cook for the family, and even assist the brothers with their farm work. The brothers, in turn, protected and cared for Champa, making sure she was safe and happy.

However, not everyone in the village was pleased with the brothers' affection towards Champa. The village headman's son, a cruel and power-hungry man named Raghuram, had been eyeing Champa for himself. He began to spread rumors and lies about the brothers, trying to turn the villagers against them.

Raghuram's plan was to marry Champa by force, and he eventually convinced the village headman to arrange the wedding. The seven brothers, determined to protect Champa, came up with a plan to outsmart Raghuram and his father.

On the day of the wedding, the brothers pretended to agree to the marriage, but they had a secret plan in store. As the wedding procession approached Champa's doorstep, the brothers, one by one, created a series of distractions, each one more clever and hilarious than the last.

The first brother, a skilled musician, began to play a mesmerizing tune on his flute, entrancing the wedding party. The second brother, an expert wrestler, challenged Raghuram to a wrestling match, which he easily won. The third brother, a master of disguise, dressed up as a ghost and scared the wedding party, causing them to flee in terror.

The fourth brother, a skilled archer, shot an arrow that severed the rope holding the wedding canopy, causing it to collapse. The fifth brother, a talented cook, prepared a dish so spicy that it made everyone's eyes water, including Raghuram's. The sixth brother, a clever magician, made Raghuram's horse disappear, leaving him to walk to the wedding on foot.

Finally, the seventh brother, the most clever of all, disguised himself as the village priest and performed a fake wedding ceremony, declaring Champa to be the bride of the seven brothers collectively, rather than Raghuram.

The villagers, realizing they had been outsmarted, couldn't help but laugh at the absurdity of it all. Raghuram, humiliated and defeated, slunk away, never to be seen again. The seven brothers, Champa, and their mother lived happily ever after, their bond and cleverness having saved the day.

The story of "Shat Bhai Champa" has been retold and adapted in various forms of Bengali literature, theater, and film, and remains a beloved tale of sibling love, cleverness, and the power of unity in the face of adversity.
