What a delightful tale to share. 'Noshi, Dimshi and the Monkey' is a traditional Bengali folktale from the Mymensingh region, which I'll retell while preserving the original cultural nuances, kinship terms, and specific rituals.

Once upon a time, in a small village in Mymensingh, there lived two sisters, Noshi and Dimshi. They were the daughters of a poor but kind-hearted farmer, known as 'Baba' or father. Their mother, 'Ma', had passed away when they were young, and their father had remarried. The stepmother, 'Step-Ma', was cruel and treated the sisters poorly.

One day, while the sisters were out collecting firewood in the forest, they stumbled upon a mischievous monkey. The monkey, known as 'Bandar', was known for its cunning and thieving ways. Noshi, being the elder sister, warned Dimshi to be careful around the monkey, but Dimshi, being the more naive and kind-hearted one, decided to befriend the Bandar.

As they spent more time with the monkey, it began to help them with their chores, such as collecting fruits and vegetables from the forest. The sisters would often share their meals with the Bandar, and it would sit with them, eating from a separate plate, as was the custom in Bengali culture.

However, their stepmother, Step-Ma, was not pleased with the sisters' new friendship. She would often scold them, saying, "What's the point of befriending a thieving monkey? It will only bring trouble to our doorstep." But Noshi and Dimshi ignored her warnings and continued to care for the Bandar.

As the days passed, the monkey's antics became more and more troublesome. It would steal food from the neighbors' houses, and the villagers would complain to Baba. The sisters would apologize and promise to keep the Bandar under control, but it seemed to have a mind of its own.

One day, the Bandar stole a valuable item from the village priest's house, a 'Shanka' (conch shell), which was used in Hindu rituals. The priest, 'Panditji', was furious and threatened to curse the family if the item was not returned.

Noshi and Dimshi were devastated. They begged the Bandar to return the Shanka, but it refused. The sisters decided to perform a 'Puja' (prayer ritual) to appease the gods and seek their help in resolving the situation. They prepared a 'Bhoger Khichuri' (a traditional Bengali dish made with rice, lentils, and vegetables) and offered it to the gods, along with some 'Dhup' (incense sticks) and 'Diya' (earthen lamps).

As they performed the Puja, they prayed to the goddess 'Durga' to help them retrieve the stolen Shanka and restore peace to their family. Miraculously, the Bandar appeared, holding the Shanka in its hand, and returned it to the priest's house.

The villagers were amazed, and the priest, Panditji, was grateful. He blessed the sisters and their family, saying, "Your kindness and devotion to the gods have been rewarded. May your family always be blessed with peace and prosperity."

From that day on, the Bandar became a reformed monkey, and the sisters continued to care for it. Their stepmother, Step-Ma, realized the error of her ways and began to treat the sisters with kindness. The family lived happily ever after, and the story of Noshi, Dimshi, and the monkey was passed down through generations as a reminder of the power of kindness, devotion, and the importance of preserving traditional rituals and cultural nuances.

In Bengali culture, the story highlights the significance of respecting and caring for all living beings, including animals, and the importance of performing rituals and seeking the blessings of the gods in times of need. The tale also showcases the strong bond between sisters and the importance of family values, such as kindness, empathy, and forgiveness.
