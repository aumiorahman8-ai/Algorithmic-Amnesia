What a delightful tale to retell! "Kolaboti Rajkonna" is a beloved story from the iconic Bengali folktale collection, Thakurmar Jhuli (Grandmother's Bag of Tales). I'll do my best to preserve the original cultural nuances, kinship terms, and specific rituals.

Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a kind and gentle girl named Kolaboti. She was the daughter of a humble farmer, Kaka (father's younger brother), and her Mami (aunt, father's sister). Kolaboti's parents had passed away when she was very young, and her Kaka and Mami had raised her with love and care.

Kolaboti was a hardworking and obedient girl, always helping her Kaka and Mami with household chores and farming tasks. She was especially skilled in weaving and spinning, and her beautiful fabrics were renowned in the village.

One day, while Kolaboti was out collecting firewood in the forest, she stumbled upon a hidden clearing. In the center of the clearing stood an enormous, ancient tree, its trunk twisted and gnarled with age. As she approached the tree, a soft, melodious voice called out to her, "Kolaboti, Kolaboti, come and sit with me."

Kolaboti was both startled and intrigued. She looked around, wondering who was speaking to her, and that's when she saw a magnificent Rajkonna (king's daughter) sitting on a throne-like seat, carved into the trunk of the tree. The Rajkonna was dressed in exquisite attire, with intricate jewelry and a dazzling crown on her head.

The Rajkonna introduced herself as the guardian of the forest and invited Kolaboti to sit with her. As they conversed, Kolaboti learned that the Rajkonna was a just and kind ruler, loved by all the creatures of the forest. However, she was also a victim of a cruel curse, which forced her to remain in the forest, separated from her kingdom and her people.

The Rajkonna explained that the curse could only be broken if a selfless and pure-hearted person, like Kolaboti, would agree to help her. Kolaboti, being a kind and compassionate girl, readily agreed to assist the Rajkonna.

The Rajkonna instructed Kolaboti to return to her village and prepare a special offering of Khichuri (a traditional Bengali dish made with rice, lentils, and vegetables) and Paayesh (a sweet rice pudding). Kolaboti was to bring the offering to the forest, along with a handful of Ganga jal (holy water from the Ganges River), and perform a specific ritual to break the curse.

Kolaboti returned to her village, prepared the offerings, and collected the Ganga jal from the nearby river. She then returned to the forest, where the Rajkonna guided her through the ritual. As they performed the puja (worship), the curse was lifted, and the Rajkonna was finally free to return to her kingdom.

The Rajkonna, overjoyed and grateful, thanked Kolaboti for her selflessness and kindness. As a reward, she granted Kolaboti a single wish. Kolaboti, being a humble and simple girl, wished for the prosperity and happiness of her Kaka, Mami, and their village.

The Rajkonna, pleased with Kolaboti's selfless wish, granted it and also bestowed upon her the gift of weaving the most exquisite fabrics, which would bring wealth and prosperity to her family and village. From that day on, Kolaboti's fabrics were renowned throughout the land, and her Kaka, Mami, and the entire village prospered.

As the story concludes, it is said that Kolaboti's kindness, selflessness, and devotion to her family and community were rewarded, and she lived a happy and fulfilling life, always remembering the magical encounter with the Rajkonna in the forest.

In this retelling, I've tried to preserve the original cultural nuances, kinship terms, and specific rituals, such as the use of Khichuri, Paayesh, and Ganga jal, which are an integral part of Bengali culture and traditions. The story highlights the importance of kindness, selflessness, and the strong bonds of family and community, which are core values in Bengali society.
