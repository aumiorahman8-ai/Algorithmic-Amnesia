Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a kind and gentle woman named Champa. She was the daughter of a humble farmer and had six brothers, whom she lovingly called "Dada" (াা), a term used to address older brothers. Champa's family lived a simple life, working hard to till the land and tend to their crops.

As the story goes, Champa's Dadas were extremely fond of her and would do anything to make her happy. They would often tease her and playfully call her "Champa Bou" (া্া ), a affectionate term used to address a younger sister. Champa, in turn, would lovingly address her brothers as "Dada" and take care of their needs, often helping their mother with household chores.

One day, the seven brothers decided to go on a journey to find their fortune, leaving Champa behind with their mother. Before departing, they promised to return with wealth and prosperity, and Champa, with tears in her eyes, bid them farewell, saying "Dada-ra, tomra ki koro?" (াা-রা, রা ি র?) or "Brothers, what will you do?"

As the brothers traveled far and wide, they encountered various challenges and faced numerous hardships. Meanwhile, back in the village, Champa's mother fell ill, and Champa took on the responsibility of caring for her. She would often pray to the goddess Durga, saying "Ma Durga, amar Dadas ke dekho" (া র্া, া াা ে ে) or "Mother Durga, please take care of my brothers."

Months passed, and the brothers finally returned home, successful and wealthy. They were overjoyed to see their mother recovering and Champa, who had taken care of her with love and dedication. The family was reunited, and they celebrated with a grand feast, preparing traditional Bengali dishes like "luchi" (ি) and "kosha mangsho" (া া).

As they sat down to eat, Champa's Dadas praised her, saying "Champa Bou, tumi amader jonno ekta maa" (া্া , ি াের ন্ া া) or "Champa, you are like a mother to us." Champa, filled with joy and love, replied "Dada-ra, amar jonno tumi sabai" (াা-রা, া ন্ ি া) or "Brothers, you are everything to me."

The story of "Shat Bhai Champa" highlights the importance of sibling love, respect, and care in Bengali culture. The tale showcases the strong bond between Champa and her brothers, as well as the significance of traditional values like "dai" (া) or respect for one's elders. The story also emphasizes the role of women in Bengali society, depicting Champa as a caring and responsible individual who takes care of her family's needs.

In Bengali culture, the relationship between siblings is considered sacred, and the term "Dada" is used to address older brothers with respect and affection.
