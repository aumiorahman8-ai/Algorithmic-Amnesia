In the Bengali folktale, "The Consequence of a Broken Promise," a poor farmer promises to offer his most precious possession to the goddess if she blesses him with a bountiful harvest. When his crop flourishes, he reneges on his vow, thinking the goddess won't notice. However, the goddess appears, reminding him of his promise. The farmer tries to deceive her, offering a meager substitute, but she sees through his trickery.

As punishment, the goddess curses the farmer, causing his crops to wither and die. The farmer realizes too late the gravity of breaking his promise. The story teaches the importance of keeping one's word and honoring commitments. It highlights the consequences of dishonesty and the value of integrity. The moral lesson is that breaking a promise can lead to severe repercussions, not just from others, but also from one's own conscience.

The tale also emphasizes the idea that one should not try to deceive or cheat, as it can ultimately lead to one's downfall. The story promotes honesty, trustworthiness, and accountability, encouraging listeners to uphold their promises and respect the trust placed in them. By keeping one's word, individuals can build strong relationships, earn respect, and maintain a clear conscience.
