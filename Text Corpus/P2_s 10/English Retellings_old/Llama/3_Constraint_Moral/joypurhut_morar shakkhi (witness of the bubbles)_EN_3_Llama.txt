In the Bengali folktale "Morar Shakkhi" or "Witness of the Bubbles," a poor but honest farmer is falsely accused of stealing. The king, determined to uncover the truth, calls upon the farmer to prove his innocence. The farmer, with faith in his own honesty, agrees to undergo a test. He is asked to throw a stone into a river and then retrieve it, but when he does, the stone is not found. The king's men claim the farmer has hidden the stone, further implicating him.

However, the farmer suggests that the bubbles rising from the river be considered as witnesses to his actions. As the bubbles rise and then disappear, the farmer explains that just like the bubbles, the truth will eventually surface. The tale ends with the real thief being caught, and the farmer's innocence being proven.

The moral of "Morar Shakkhi" emphasizes the importance of patience, honesty, and faith in the truth. It teaches that even in the face of adversity and false accusations, one should remain steadfast and have confidence that the truth will ultimately prevail. The story also highlights the value of cleverness and creative thinking in resolving complex situations.
