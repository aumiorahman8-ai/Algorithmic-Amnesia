In a far-off kingdom, a king, queen, and prince lived in a royal palace. The queen's life was tied to a pair of dice, and an ogress, disguised as a beggar woman, tricked the prince into giving her the dice. The ogress then ate the queen and took her form. The prince suspected something was amiss when he saw his "mother" drooling while serving food.

Years passed, and the king had seven more sons. When they grew up, they decided to travel the world. The eldest prince joined them, and they set out on their journey. The ogress-queen, fearing the prince's return, sent a snake named Sutashankha to find out where the prince's life was hidden. The Sutashankha discovered that the prince's life was inside a pomegranate seed, which the ogress then hid beneath the thousand steps of the royal palace.

The seven younger princes arrived at the city of Pashabati, where they were challenged to a game of dice. They lost and were eaten by Pashabati and her sisters. The eldest prince, meanwhile, had become blind and was carried by his winged horse to a city where a princess was eating kings. The prince killed the snake that was eating the kings and regained his sight.

The prince read a missive found in the snake's belly, which revealed that his brothers had been eaten by an ogress. He decided to rescue them and set out on a journey. He encountered his winged horse, which had turned to stone, and revived it. Together, they crossed treacherous landscapes, including a mountain of cowrie shells and a mountain of bones, to reach the city of Pashabati.

The prince challenged Pashabati to a game of dice and initially lost. However, he brought a kitten that chased away a mouse that was helping Pashabati cheat. The prince then won the game and reclaimed his winged horse. He continued to play and won back his seven brothers, their horses, and Pashabati's kingdom.

Finally, the prince obtained the dice and killed the mouse that was helping Pashabati. The kingdom disappeared, and the prince returned home with his brothers. The queen, who had been in a magical sleep, awoke, and the palace was once again filled with life and celebration. The prince also reunited with the princess from the night-king's land, who brought her entire court and kingdom to join him. The story concluded with the destruction of the palm tree in the royal palace, symbolizing the end of the ogress's evil spell.
