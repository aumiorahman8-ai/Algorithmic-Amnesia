Once upon a time, there was a king with seven queens, but none of them had borne him a child, leaving the king and his kingdom in sorrow. One day, a hermit gave the Eldest Queen a tree root, instructing her to grind it and share it among the seven queens, promising that they would bear sons as beautiful as the golden moon.

The queens followed the hermit's instructions, but the Unfavored Queen ate a piece of the root while grinding it, and the Youngest Queen was left with nothing. The five main queens gave birth to sons, each as beautiful as the golden moon, while the Fourth Queen gave birth to an owl named Bhutum, and the Youngest Queen gave birth to a monkey named Buddhu.

The king, unaware of the true parentage of Bhutum and Buddhu, banished the Fourth Queen and the Youngest Queen, forcing them to live as a maid and a servant, respectively. Bhutum and Buddhu grew up, and one day, they discovered their true identities as the king's sons.

Meanwhile, the five princes, the sons of the five main queens, set out to find Princess Kalabati, who had sent a message saying she would marry the prince who could bring her the flower of pearls and pass through various challenges. Bhutum and Buddhu, determined to prove themselves, built their own boat and set out to follow their brothers.

Along the way, the five princes were captured by the three old crones and later by a quilt-crone, but Bhutum and Buddhu rescued them. However, the princes, ungrateful and cruel, threw Bhutum and Buddhu into the water. Buddhu, determined to succeed, continued on his journey, using his cunning and bravery to overcome various obstacles, including a python and a magical marketplace.

Buddhu eventually reached Princess Kalabati's palace, retrieved the flower of pearls, and won her hand in marriage. He also rescued his brothers and brought them back to the kingdom. However, the princes, still ungrateful, tried to steal Princess Kalabati from Buddhu.

In the end, the truth was revealed, and the king, realizing his mistake, welcomed the Fourth Queen and the Youngest Queen back to the palace. Buddhu married Princess Kalabati, and Bhutum married Princess Hirabati. The five queens and princes who had wronged them were punished, and the kingdom celebrated the happiness of the king, the two queens, and their sons, Buddhu and Bhutum, who were revealed to be princes in disguise, named Budhkumar and Rupkumar, respectively. The kingdom lived happily ever after.
