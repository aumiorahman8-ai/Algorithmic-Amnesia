A king had seven queens, but he loved the youngest one the most because of her gentle nature. The king was sorrowful because he had no children, but his joy was boundless when the youngest queen became pregnant. He announced the news throughout the kingdom and even tied a golden chain to his waist and the youngest queen's, so he could be notified when the child was born.

However, the elder queens were jealous and decided to trick the king. They entered the birthing room and tugged on the chain, pretending the child was born, but when the king arrived, there was nothing. This happened twice, and the king warned that if it happened again, he would behead all the queens.

The youngest queen gave birth to seven sons and one daughter, but the elder queens were cruel and told her that she had given birth to rats, crabs, and an elephant. The youngest queen fainted, and the elder queens secretly buried the children in an ash heap. They then showed the king tadpoles and baby mice, and in his anger, he drove the youngest queen out of the palace.

The youngest queen became a dung-gathering maid, and the kingdom was filled with sorrow and desolation. One day, a gardener found seven Champa flowers and one Parul flower blooming on an ash heap. The flowers spoke to each other and refused to be picked by the gardener or the king, demanding that the king's queens come to pick them instead.

Each of the elder queens tried to pick the flowers, but they rose higher into the sky. Finally, when the youngest queen, now a dung-gathering maid, was brought to pick the flowers, they glided down, and the seven princes and one princess emerged, calling out to their mother. The king was overjoyed, and the elder queens were punished by being buried in a pit of thorns. The king took his children and the youngest queen back to the palace, and the kingdom was once again filled with joy and celebration.
