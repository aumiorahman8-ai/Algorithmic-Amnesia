**Khumpui – A Folktale from Rangamati (Chittagong Hill Tracts)**  

*Note: This version is drawn from the oral tradition of the Chakma‑Bengali communities of Rangamati. Like many folk narratives, it has been passed down through generations and may appear in slightly different forms in different villages. The names of kinship relations, the rhythm of the language, and the rituals described are kept as close as possible to the way they are spoken and performed in the hills.*  

---

### 1. The Setting  

In the mist‑clad valleys of Rangamati, where the Kaptai Lake shimmers like a giant jade mirror and the hills rise in emerald waves, there lived a modest Chakma family in a thatched bamboo house ( *bari* ). The family consisted of **Baba** (father), **Ma** (mother), their son **Rong** (the youngest), his elder brother **Boro**, and **Boro’s wife, Boudi**. The children helped their parents in the paddy fields, harvested *bora* (sticky rice), and prepared *pitha* for the festivals.  

The village was surrounded by a dense forest that the elders called **“Bongshik Bagan”** – the Sacred Grove, where the spirits of ancestors and nature dwelt. The villagers believed that a benevolent forest spirit named **Khumpui** guarded the grove and the people who respected the old customs.  

---

### 2. The Omen  

One evening, as the sun slipped behind the hills, **Baba** heard a low, mournful humming echoing from the forest. The sound was unlike the usual chirping of crickets; it seemed to be a voice calling his name. He told **Ma** and the children, “*Shob shomoy shanti thakle, ei dhuni shune thakle bhalo na.*” (When peace reigns, hearing that tone is not a good sign.)  

That night, **Rong** dreamed of a pale woman with long, tangled hair, her eyes like the dark water of the lake. She whispered, “*Baba’r bhul kora, amake shanti dao.*” (Your father has erred; give me peace.)  

When **Rong** awoke, he told **Boro** and **Boudi**. **Boudi**, who knew the old chants, warned, “*Khumpui’r kotha shune, amra shob shomoy shanti rakhte hobe. Tar asha shanti chay, na je kono bipod.*” (When Khumpui is spoken of, we must keep peace; she seeks calm, not trouble.)  

---

### 3. The Ritual of Appeasement  

The next day, the family gathered at the **“Bongshik Bagan”** with a **“Bhoot Puja”** (spirit‑offering) prepared by **Ma**. The offering consisted of:

* **Pitha** made from freshly pounded rice, sweetened with jaggery.  
* **Kola** (banana) leaves tied in a small bundle.  
* A **“Bongshik”** (sacred bamboo flute) that the village shaman, **Mohan**, would play.  

**Mohan** began the ceremony with the ancient chant:

> “*Khumpui, amader shanti rakh,  
>  Amader shob shomoy bhalo thak,  
>  Jodi amra bhul kori,  
>  Doya kore amader shorir theke chharo.*”

(Oh Khumpui, keep our peace, let all be well; if we err, have mercy and release us.)  

The family placed the pitha on a stone altar ( *pithar bhet* ) and poured a little water from the lake as a libation. **Baba** bowed his head, and **Rong** whispered a promise: “*Ami shob shomoy shanti rakhbo, Khumpui.*”  

---

### 4. The Test  

That night, a sudden storm rolled over the hills. Lightning struck a tall **“Bamboo”** near the house, and the wind howled as if the forest itself were breathing. The children huddled under the thatched roof, while **Baba** and **Ma** clutched each other’s hands.  

From the forest came a soft, melodic voice: “*Baba, tumi keno bhul korle?*” (Father, why did you err?)  

**Baba** remembered the humming from earlier and realized his mistake: he had cut down a young **“Mara”** (a sacred tree) the previous week to make firewood, ignoring the elders’ warning that the tree was the home of Khumpui.  

He fell to his knees and cried, “*Khumpui, ami khoma chaichi. Ami bhul kore chhoto gacha katachi. Amader shanti dao.*” (Khumpui, I beg forgiveness. I wrongly cut the young tree. Grant us peace.)  

The wind softened, the rain ceased, and a gentle glow appeared among the bamboo. A figure draped in white, with hair flowing like the mist, stepped forward – it was **Khumpui** herself.  

She spoke in a voice that sounded like the rustle of leaves:  

> “*Baba, tumi amar bari bhul kore chhilo. Kintu tumi shotti mon theke khoma chaile, ami tomader shanti dibo. Kintu ekta shikkha thakbe – prithibir shob kichu amader shongshar.*”  

(Father, you broke my home. Yet if you truly ask forgiveness, I will give you peace. But there will be a lesson – everything on earth belongs to us all.)  

She raised her hand, and the **“Mara”** tree sprouted anew, its leaves shimmering with dew. The forest sang, and the family felt a warm, protective presence.  

---

### 5. The Aftermath – A New Covenant  

From that day forward, the family observed the following customs, which the village still practices:

1. **“Mara Gachh Boro”** – Every year, on the first full moon of the monsoon, the family plants a **Mara** sapling near their home, offering a small portion of pitha and a handful of rice grains.  

2. **“Boudi’r Shanti Kotha”** – The elder sister‑in‑law (Boudi) recites a short prayer each evening, reminding the children to respect the forest:  
   > “*Jodi amra prithibi shanti rakhte chai, tahole prani o gachh shanti rakhte hobe.*”  

3. **“Shaman’s Blessing”** – The village shaman, **Mohan**, conducts a **“Bhoot Puja”** before the harvest, invoking Khumpui’s protection for the crops.  

4. **“Rong’s Song”** – The youngest child, **Rong**, now sings a lullaby that his mother taught him, which ends with the line:  
   > “*Khumpui’r alo, amader ghore, shanti rakhbe shob shomoy.*”  

The tale of **Khumpui** is told at every **“Bihu”** (harvest festival) and during the **“Bongshik Bagan”** rituals, reminding the people of Rangamati that harmony with nature and respect for the unseen guardians are the foundations of a peaceful life.  

---

### 6. Closing Thought  

In the hills of Rangamati, the story of Khumpui lives not only in words but in the rustle of bamboo, the scent of fresh pitha, and the reverent hush that falls when a child steps into the forest. It is a reminder that every cut tree, every whispered promise, and every shared meal is a thread in the larger tapestry that binds the community to the land and its spirits.  

*May the spirit of Khumpui continue to watch over us, and may we always honor the kinship that ties us to each other and to the earth.*
