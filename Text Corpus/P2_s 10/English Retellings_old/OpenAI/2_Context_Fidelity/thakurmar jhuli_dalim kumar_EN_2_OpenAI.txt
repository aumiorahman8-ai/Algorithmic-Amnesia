**The Tale of Dalim Kumar – A Retelling from *Thākurmār Jhuli***  

*In the hush of the Bengal countryside, where the mangrove‑laden rivers wind through paddy fields and the scent of *jhalmuri* mingles with the monsoon rain, the old women of every village gather around the hearth and whisper the story of Dalim Kumar. It is a tale of devotion, cleverness, and the power of a humble heart.*

---

### 1. The Setting and the Family

In a modest *bari* (house) on the banks of the **Brahmaputra**, lived a *boudi* (elder sister‑in‑law) named **Madhabi**, her husband **Bibhuti**, and their only son **Dalim Kumar**. The family belonged to the *Brahmin* caste, and their daily life was marked by the rhythm of *sandhya puja* (evening worship) and the soft chanting of *mantras* that rose like incense from the courtyard.

Madhabi was a *sadhvi* (devout woman) who kept the *kalash* (sacred pot) of water always filled, and she taught Dalir (the affectionate nickname for Dalim) the *shlokas* (verses) of the *Vedas* before he went to school. The boy grew up with a gentle smile, a quick mind, and a heart that beat in time with the *bansuri* (flute) of the village’s *baul* singers.

---

### 2. The King’s Decree

One scorching summer, the *Raja* (king) of the nearby kingdom, **Raja Birendra**, fell gravely ill. The royal physicians could not find a cure, and the court astrologers foretold that only a *sacred herb*—the ***Kalpavriksha* leaf**—could restore his health. This leaf grew deep within the **Sundarban**, a tangled forest of mangroves guarded by fierce *bagh* (tigers) and the mysterious *Bhoot* (spirits) of the water.

The Raja issued a royal proclamation: *“Whoever brings me the Kalpavriksha leaf shall be rewarded with riches beyond imagination, and shall be granted a place in my court.”* Messengers rode on *ghor* (horses) to every village, and the news spread like a *pankha* (fan) in a hot room.

---

### 3. The Quest Begins

When Dalim Kumar heard the proclamation, his eyes shone with determination. He approached his mother, bowing his head in *pranam* (respect).

> “*Maa, I wish to fetch the leaf for the Raja. May I go?”  

Madhabi placed her hands on his forehead, feeling the coolness of his *tilak* (sacred mark). She whispered a *mantra* of protection and gave him a *pithra* (small bundle) of *til* (sesame) and a *kalam* (ink) to write his name on the *palm leaf* that would serve as a talisman.

> “*Baba* (father), remember the *shastras* (scriptures). Do not stray from the path of dharma, and keep the *prasad* (offering) of *pitha* (sweet rice cake) with you for strength.”  

Thus, with a *dhoti* tied around his waist and a *turban* (*pagri*) on his head, Dalim set off, chanting *“Om Shanti, Om Shanti.”* He walked through the *paddy fields*, crossed the *ghats* (riverbanks), and entered the dense mangrove forest.

---

### 4. The First Trial – The River of Mirrors

At the edge of the Sundarban, Dalim encountered a wide river whose surface reflected the sky like a perfect *shola* (mirror). A *village elder*—the *Baba* of the forest—stood on a floating *bamboo raft* and called out:

> “*Baba, I seek the Kalpavriksha leaf. Let me cross, and I shall not disturb the river’s peace.”  

The elder smiled and said, “*Only one who can answer my riddle may cross.*”

> **Riddle:** “*What is the thing that is born in the night, lives in the day, and dies when you speak its name?*”

Dalim thought of the *silence* that follows a *chant* and answered, “*Silence*.”  

The river’s surface rippled, and a *lotus* floated toward him, bearing a *silver key* that unlocked a hidden path through the mangroves.

---

### 5. The Second Trial – The Tiger’s Den

Following the secret path, Dalim reached a clearing where a massive *bagh* lay asleep, its striped coat glistening with the *dew* of the early morning. Beside the tiger, a *shakuntala* (a small wooden altar) held a bowl of *kheer* (sweet rice pudding).

Remembering his mother’s teachings, Dalim placed the *til* he carried on the bowl and whispered a *prayer*:

> “*O mighty tiger, guardian of the forest, accept this humble offering. May your heart be softened by the love of a child.*”

The tiger opened one eye, sniffed the *kheer*, and, satisfied, rose gracefully. It stepped aside, revealing a narrow tunnel of *bamboo* shoots that led deeper into the forest.

---

### 6. The Third Trial – The Spirit of the Mangrove

Inside the tunnel, a faint glow illuminated a *bhoot* (water spirit) who floated above a pool of *jal* (water). The spirit’s voice was like the rustle of *pattas* (leaves).

> “*Who dares to disturb my waters?*”

Dalim bowed low, touching his forehead to the *kalash* he carried, and said, “*I am Dalim Kumar, son of Madhabi and Bibhuti. I seek the Kalpavriksha leaf to save the Raja.*”

The spirit, moved by his humility, asked for a *vow*:

> “*Promise to return this leaf to the kingdom and never use its power for selfish gain.*”

Dalim placed his hand on his heart and swore, “*I promise, O spirit.*”

Satisfied, the spirit lifted a veil of mist, revealing a towering **Kalpavriksha**—a tree whose leaves shimmered like emeralds. Dalim gently plucked a single leaf, careful not to disturb the tree’s roots, and tucked it into his *pouch*.

---

### 7. The Return and the Reward

With the leaf secured, Dalim retraced his steps, guided by the *silver key* and the *bhoot’s* lingering glow. He emerged from the Sundarban just as the sun painted the sky in shades of *saffron* and *indigo*.

When he arrived at the palace, the Raja’s *raj‑mahant* (chief priest) examined the leaf. The moment the leaf touched the royal *chowki* (platform), a fragrant breeze filled the hall, and the ailing Raja inhaled the scent of *sandalwood* and *jasmine*. His eyes fluttered open, and color returned to his cheeks.

The Raja, grateful beyond words, declared:

> “*Dalim Kumar, you have saved my life and the kingdom. As promised, you shall receive the title of *Mahashri* (great noble), and your family shall be honored forever.*”

He ordered a grand *yajna* (sacrificial fire ceremony) in Dalim’s honor. The *pandits* chanted *Vedic hymns*, the *drummers* beat the *dhol*, and the *women* offered *pithas* and *payesh* (rice pudding) to the gods. The entire kingdom celebrated with *bengali folk songs* (*baul* and *kirtan*) that echoed through the night.

---

### 8. The Moral and the Legacy

Dalim Kumar returned home to his *bari* with the royal gifts: a *golden *kora* (bracelet), a *silk dhoti* embroidered with *mangalsutra* (auspicious symbols), and a *royal decree* that his descendants would forever be *Raja‑shri* (honored nobles). Yet, he kept his *simple* lifestyle, continuing his daily *sandhya puja* with his mother, and teaching the village children the *shlokas* he had learned.

The story of Dalim Kumar is still told in Bengal’s *baul* gatherings and *panchayat* meetings. It reminds us that:

* **Devotion and humility** can move even the fiercest guardians of nature.  
* **Respect for rituals**—the offering of *til*, the chanting of *mantras*, the observance of *prasad*—creates a bridge between the mortal and the divine.  
* **Kinship bonds**—the love of a *boudi* and the guidance of a *baba*—are the true source of strength.

And so, whenever the wind rustles through the mangrove leaves, the villagers whisper, “*May the spirit of Dalim Kumar guide us, as he guided the Raja, with a heart pure as *pitha* and a mind sharp as the *bamboo* blade.*”

---  

**End of the Tale**  

*May the rhythm of the *dhol* and the fragrance of *jasmine* keep this story alive in your heart.*
