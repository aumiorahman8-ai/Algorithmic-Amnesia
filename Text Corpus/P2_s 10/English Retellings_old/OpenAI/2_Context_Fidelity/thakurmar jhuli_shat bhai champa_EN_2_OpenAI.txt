**Shat Bhai Champa – A Retelling from *Thākurmār Jhuli***  

*The tale is taken from the beloved 1907 collection *Thākurmār Jhuli* (Grandmother’s Bag of Stories), compiled by Dakshinaranjan Mitra Majumder. The story itself belongs to the oral tradition of Bengal and is in the public domain. Below is a faithful retelling that keeps the original cultural colour, kinship terms, and ritual details.*

---

### 1. The Six Brothers and Their Mother

In a modest *bari* (house) on the banks of the **Brahmaputra**, lived a widowed mother, **Mātā**, and her six sons. The sons—**Bābā**, **Bābā**, **Bābā**, **Bābā**, **Bābā**, and **Bābā**—were all called *bhāi* (brother) by the villagers, for they were inseparable and worked together as one.

Mātā was a devout Hindu woman. Every **Sankranti** (the solar transition day) she would wake before sunrise, light a *diya* (oil lamp) in the *shri* (shrine) of **Saraswati**, and chant **“Om Saraswati Namah”** while offering *pāni* (water) and *prasad* (sweet rice cakes). The brothers, in turn, helped her with the daily chores—*bhāte* (cooking rice), *pāni* (drawing water from the well), and *kheti* (tilling the paddy field).

---

### 2. The Gift of the Magic Tree

One monsoon evening, after the rain had ceased, the brothers went to the forest to collect *tāla* (bamboo) for a new *chāra* (fence). While cutting a thick stalk, they heard a soft voice:

> “*Bābā, bābā, bābā…*”

It was the **Kalpavṛksha**, a mystical tree that grew only once every hundred years. The tree, with leaves that shimmered like emeralds, spoke in a gentle rustle:

> “*If you each take one leaf and plant it at the foot of your mother’s house, you shall receive a boon that will protect your family forever.*”

The brothers obeyed. Each placed a leaf in a neat row before the *shri* of Saraswati. The next morning, the leaves had sprouted into six slender saplings, each bearing a single golden fruit.

---

### 3. The Arrival of Champa

A few weeks later, a beautiful young woman named **Champa** arrived at the village. She was a *paribārik* (relative) of the *pādā* (village head) and had come to seek a *vivāha* (marriage) alliance for her brother. Champa’s eyes were as dark as the night sky, and her voice sang like the *bānsuri* (flute) of the river.

When the brothers saw her, they were struck by her grace. The eldest brother, **Bābā**, bowed and said:

> “*Devi, we would be honoured if you would stay with us as a sister.*”

Champa, moved by their humility, agreed to stay. She took up residence in the *bhāi*’s *chhāyā* (courtyard) and helped Mātā with *pāni* (water) and *prasad* (offerings). The household rang with the sound of *dhak* (drum) during festivals, and the scent of *pāṭṭa* (sweets) filled the air.

---

### 4. The Evil *Bāḍa* (Witch) and the Curse

One night, a *bāḍa* (witch) named **Kālīrani**—who lived in the dense *jungle*—spied on the happy home. Envious of the brothers’ prosperity, she plotted to destroy them. She disguised herself as a *sādhū* (holy man) and entered the *bari* during *Mahāshivaratri*.

She whispered a curse while the family performed the *abhisheka* (ritual bathing) of the *Shiva lingam*:

> “*May the golden fruits wither, and may the brothers lose their bond.*”

The next dawn, the golden fruits turned to ash, and a cold wind blew through the *chhāyā*. The brothers felt a strange heaviness in their hearts; arguments began to sprout like weeds.

---

### 5. Champa’s Wisdom and the Ritual of *Pañcāṅga*

Seeing the discord, Champa remembered an old *pāṭha* (lesson) from her grandmother: the **Pañcāṅga** (five‑step ritual) that could cleanse a curse. She gathered the brothers, Mātā, and the whole village under the *pāṇi* (large banyan tree) and announced:

1. **First Step – *Sankalpa* (Resolution):** Each brother must declare his love for his siblings aloud.
2. **Second Step – *Prāṇāyāma* (Breath):** They would breathe together, inhaling the scent of *sandalwood* and exhaling the curse.
3. **Third Step – *Puja* (Worship):** Offer *pāni* (water) and *prasad* (sweet rice) to the *Shri* of **Saraswati** while chanting *“Om Saraswati Namah”*.
4. **Fourth Step – *Homa* (Fire Offering):** Light a *homa* (sacred fire) with *ghee* and throw the ash of the cursed fruits into it, asking the fire to devour the evil.
5. **Fifth Step – *Anjali* (Blessing):** The eldest brother would place his hands on the heads of his siblings, invoking the *Vedic* mantra *“Om Shanti Shanti Shanti”* for peace.

The villagers sang *bhajans* (devotional songs) while the *dhak* beat a steady rhythm. The fire crackled, and the ash rose like a dark cloud, then vanished.

When the ritual ended, the six brothers felt a warm glow in their chests. Their bond, once frayed, was now stronger than ever. The *golden fruits* reappeared on the saplings, this time shining brighter than before.

---

### 6. The Marriage and the Blessing of the Gods

Grateful for Champa’s wisdom, the brothers asked her to become their *bāi* (sister) forever. Champa, however, wished to return to her own family. The *pādā* (village head) arranged a *vivāha* for Champa’s brother, **Raghav**, with a *bride* from a neighboring *gram* (village). The wedding was a grand *sādhāraṇ* (traditional) affair:

- **Mangal Sutra** (sacred thread) was tied around the bride’s neck.
- **Sindoor** (vermilion) was applied to the groom’s forehead.
- The *shashti* (sixth day) *puja* was performed to invoke **Lakshmi** for prosperity.

During the *kanyādāna* (giving away of the bride), Champa placed a *mangalsutra* on the bride’s neck, symbolizing the sisterly bond she had forged with the six brothers. The *pandit* (priest) recited the *mantra*:

> “*Om Sarve Bhavantu Sukhinah, Sarve Santu Niramayah.*”

The whole village celebrated with *pāṭṭa* (sweet rice), *pitha* (steamed rice cakes), and *jhalmuri* (spiced puffed rice). The *shri* of Saraswati shone with a golden light, blessing the families for generations.

---

### 7. The Moral

The story of **Shat Bhai Champa** teaches that:

- **Family unity** (*ekatā*) is a divine gift that can overcome any curse.
- **Wisdom and devotion** (*buddhi* and *bhakti*)—as shown by Champa—can restore harmony.
- **Rituals** (*puja*, *homa*, *prāṇāyāma*) performed with sincere heart have the power to cleanse negativity.

Thus, the six brothers lived happily ever after, their home forever fragrant with the scent of *sandalwood* and the sound of *dhak* echoing through the fields. And every year, on the day of the *Pañcāṅga* ritual, the villagers gather under the banyan tree to remember the tale of unity, love, and the blessed sister, Champa.
