At a place where the Mathabhanga River meets another waterway, every merchant ship that passed was expected to give a portion of its cargo to the river’s goddess. Whether the boat carried eggplants, radishes, jackfruits or wood‑apples, a share was always offered.

One merchant broke this custom. He not only kept his entire load but also insulted the goddess. As punishment, his heavily laden boat sank straight into the river’s depths. When the authorities sent divers to retrieve it, they first tried to measure how deep the water was. They let out a rope, but it ran out before reaching the bottom. They let out more rope, and still the riverbed was nowhere to be seen. Even after adding even more rope, the depth could not be determined, and no diver was willing to go down.

At last a brave man volunteered. He was equipped with ropes and the usual diving gear and was lowered into the river. In the darkness he saw a golden boat. On its deck sat two fairies, one on each side, pulling at a rope that had become a huge tangled heap. The man, trembling, said, “Our boat has sunk, so I have come looking for it. Please forgive me, mother.”

The fairies answered, “You will get your boat back, but on one condition: you must never tell anyone about this. If you do, you will vomit blood and die. Go to the Kazis’ ghat, and there you will find the boat.”

Following their instruction, the man recovered the boat at the Kazis’ ghat. However, he could not keep the secret; he told his wife what had happened. The moment he spoke, the warning came true—he vomited blood and died.
