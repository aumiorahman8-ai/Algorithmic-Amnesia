A man once killed his enemy and, after the murder, dug a shallow pit not far from his own house where he buried the body. Years passed, and because no evidence ever linked anyone to the crime, the villagers gradually forgot that the killing had occurred.

During a heavy monsoon season the man sat on his veranda, stripping jute fibers, and stared straight out the door toward the spot where he had hidden the corpse. The body had long since decomposed, and bubbles were now rising from the ground where it lay. Seeing the bubbling earth, the man began to smile to himself.

His wife noticed his repeated smiles and, when she asked why he was laughing, he gave a vague answer that there was “no reason.” Unsatisfied, she pressed him for an explanation. At last he confessed, saying, “They say ‘Sin never stays hidden,’ but that’s false. Look at those bubbles gurgling up from the ground over there—that’s where I killed [Name] and buried him. It’s been several years; no one has ever caught me as the killer. When I think of that, I can’t help but laugh.”

Later the couple had a fierce argument. In a burst of anger the wife shouted, “You are a wicked man! You murdered [Name]!” Her accusation spread rapidly through the village. The relatives of the murdered man reported the matter to the authorities, and a police investigation was opened. The police arrested the man, and he was eventually tried and punished for his crime.
