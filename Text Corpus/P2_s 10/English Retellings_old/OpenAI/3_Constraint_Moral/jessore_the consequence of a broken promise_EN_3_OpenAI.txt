**The Consequence of a Broken Promise – A Bengali Folktale (250 words)**  

In a quiet village by the Hooghly River lived a humble potter named Gopal. One monsoon, a wealthy merchant, Raghav, visited his workshop, admired a beautiful clay lamp, and begged Gopal to lend it for his daughter’s wedding. Gopal, fearing loss, promised to give the lamp **only if** Raghav swore to return it untouched after the ceremony. Raghav solemnly swore, and the lamp was taken.

The wedding night arrived, and the lamp’s soft glow enchanted all guests. Overcome with pride, Raghav’s son, Arjun, lifted the lamp, admiring its intricate design, and accidentally knocked it over. The lamp shattered, its shards scattering across the floor. Raghav, ashamed, rushed back to Gopal, confessing the mishap.

Gopal listened, his eyes heavy with disappointment. He reminded Raghav of the oath they had made, explaining that a promise is a bond stronger than any material gift. “When a promise is broken, trust erodes; the loss is felt far beyond the broken object,” he said. He forgave Raghav but kept the broken pieces as a reminder that **words spoken must be honored**, for they shape a person’s honor.

**Moral Lessons**  
1. **Integrity over convenience** – A promise, even to a stranger, must be kept; otherwise, respect and trust crumble.  
2. **Responsibility for one’s actions** – Carelessness, especially after a vow, magnifies the damage.  
3. **Consequences extend beyond the immediate loss** – Broken trust harms relationships and reputation more than any physical loss.  

Thus the village remembered the tale, teaching children that a spoken word is a lifelong pledge, and breaking it invites lasting regret.
