**Kolaboti Rajkonna – A Short Retelling (≈230 words)**  

In a small Bengal village lived a humble weaver named Raghav and his clever wife, Kolaboti. Their modest home was always filled with laughter, though they owned little. One day, a royal messenger arrived, announcing that the king sought a bride for his son, the prince, who would marry only a woman of extraordinary virtue and wit.  

Kolaboti, hearing the news, decided to test the kingdom’s values. She crafted a simple yet beautiful garland of wildflowers and set out for the palace, carrying only a basket of rice and a tiny brass bell. At the palace gate, the royal guards scoffed at her plain appearance, but she rang the bell politely and offered the rice as a token of goodwill.  

The prince, curious, invited her in. He asked her three riddles:  

1. *What is more valuable than gold yet cannot be bought?*  
2. *What grows when shared but shrinks when hoarded?*  
3. *What can bind a kingdom without rope?*  

Kolaboti answered: **Love**, **Kindness**, and **Truth**. Impressed by her wisdom and humility, the prince declared her the true queen, for she embodied the virtues the kingdom needed.  

**Moral Lessons**  

- **Humility triumphs over ostentation**: Kolaboti’s simple garland and rice win respect where riches would fail.  
- **Wisdom and virtue outweigh wealth**: The riddles show that love, kindness, and truth are the kingdom’s real foundations.  
- **Compassion opens doors**: Her polite bell‑ring and sharing of food turn hostility into hospitality.  
- **True leadership is service**: By choosing a modest weaver’s wife, the prince learns that a ruler’s strength lies in the moral character of those beside him.  

Thus, “Kolaboti Rajkonna” reminds us that inner goodness, not outward splendor, crowns a true queen—and a true leader.
