**Morar Shakkhi – The Witness of the Bubbles**

In a small Bengal village lived a miserly old man named Bhandu, who hoarded every grain, coin, and piece of cloth he could find. He never shared with neighbors, even when a famine threatened. One night, while counting his treasure, a sudden storm flooded the river, and the water rose to swallow the fields. The villagers begged Bhandu for food, but he shut his door, saying, “My store is my own; I’ll die before I give it away.”

That night, as Bhandu slept, a strange dream visited him. He found himself in a vast, dark sea of bubbles, each bubble reflecting a different life. A tiny, trembling bubble floated close and whispered, “I am the soul of the hungry child you refused.” The bubble burst, releasing a sigh that echoed through the sea. One by one, more bubbles burst—each a life he had ignored: the old widow, the sick farmer, the laughing children. With every pop, the sea grew darker, and the dream‑self felt a cold emptiness spreading through his heart.

When he awoke, the flood had receded, but the village lay in ruin. Bhandu’s store was washed away, and he was left with nothing but the memory of the bubbles’ lament. He realized that wealth hoarded selfishly becomes a hollow shell, and that compassion is the only true treasure.

**Moral/Lessons**

1. **Compassion outweighs material hoarding** – Sharing in times of need builds community and safeguards one’s own well‑being.  
2. **Every life matters** – Ignoring the suffering of others creates a chain of loss that eventually returns to the selfish.  
3. **We are witnesses to the consequences of our actions** – Like the bubbles, our deeds reflect back on us; kindness leaves a lasting, bright imprint, while greed leaves emptiness.  

Thus, “Morar Shakkhi” reminds us that the true witness of our deeds is not a distant god, but the very lives we touch—or neglect—every day.
