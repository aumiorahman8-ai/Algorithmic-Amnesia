In a remote Bengali village a poor girl named Lila was famed for her kindness and humility. One monsoon night a hungry snake slithered into her hut, and instead of fearing it, Lila offered it rice and milk. The snake, grateful, revealed itself as Nagar‑Raja, a prince cursed to live as a serpent until a pure‑hearted human broke the spell.

He promised Lila that if she kept his secret, he would marry her and lift the curse. Lila agreed, and the next day the snake vanished, leaving a glittering necklace as proof of his promise. Word spread, and jealous neighbors tried to tempt Lila with riches to betray the snake, but she refused, remembering the value of loyalty and honesty.

When the moon rose full, the snake returned, now in human form, radiant and regal. He married Lila, and the curse broke forever. The couple ruled the village with fairness, sharing their wealth with the poor and teaching that compassion transcends appearances.

**Morals**

1. **Kindness to all beings**—even the lowliest—can bring unexpected blessings.  
2. **Loyalty and integrity** outweigh material temptations; true wealth lies in character.  
3. **Patience and humility** open doors to transformation and harmony.  
4. **Respect for nature** reminds us that every creature may hold hidden wisdom.  

Thus the tale of the Princess and the Prince of Snakes endures as a reminder that love, truth, and compassion are the greatest powers of any kingdom.
