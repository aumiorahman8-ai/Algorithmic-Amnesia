**The Tale of Shat Bhai Champa (The Hundred Brothers and the Champa Flower)**  

A king once ruled a prosperous kingdom. He was blessed with a hundred sons, each as brave as the next, but the palace was crowded and the king feared that his children would grow idle and quarrelsome. To teach them humility, teamwork and the value of a single purpose, he set a simple yet impossible‑seeming task: *“Bring me the rare Champa blossom that blooms only on the far‑away hill of Shikhar.”*  

The brothers set out together, but the king had not told them to travel as a single group. Eager to prove themselves, they split into many small parties, each boasting that they could reach the hill faster. The first group hurried alone, got lost in the forest, and returned empty‑handed. The second tried to cheat by buying a fake flower, only to be exposed when the king recognized the counterfeit.  

Meanwhile, a lone brother, the youngest, remembered his father’s words: *“A single thread is weak, but a rope of many strands is unbreakable.”* He gathered his siblings, reminded them of their shared blood, and they formed a single, orderly caravan. They helped one another across rivers, shared food, and kept watch through the night.  

When they finally reached Shikhar, the Champa bloomed only for those who approached with pure hearts and united purpose. Together they plucked the flower, and the king welcomed them home, proud of their newfound cooperation.  

**Moral(s):**  

1. **Unity outweighs individual ambition.** A collective effort turns a daunting task into a reachable goal.  
2. **Humility and listening to wise counsel** prevent prideful shortcuts that lead to failure.  
3. **Brotherhood (or any community) thrives when members support each other**, sharing strengths and covering weaknesses.  

Thus, the story of *Shat Bhai Champa* endures as a reminder that true success is woven from cooperation, humility, and love for one another.
