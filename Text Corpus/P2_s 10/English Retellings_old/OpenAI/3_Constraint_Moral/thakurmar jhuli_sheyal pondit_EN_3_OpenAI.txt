**Sheyal Pondit – The Fox Priest**

In a village of Bengal, a clever fox named Sheyal pretended to be a learned priest. He wore a saffron robe, carried a bamboo stick, and recited verses from the holy books, though he never truly understood them. The villagers, eager for guidance, sought his counsel on every matter—marriage, harvest, disputes, even the proper way to pray.

Sheyal would answer with riddles and grandiose sayings, often flattering the questioner or sowing doubt in rivals. When a poor farmer asked how to increase his yield, Sheyal advised “plant the seeds at midnight, when the moon is full, and chant the name of the moon‑god.” The farmer, trusting the priest, wasted his season. When a wealthy merchant begged for a secret to wealth, Sheyal whispered, “Give away all you own; the gods will reward you.” The merchant lost his fortune in charity, only to find his wealth never returned.

Soon the villagers noticed that every advice led to loss, loss, loss. They confronted Sheyal, who, cornered, fled into the forest, shedding his robe. The people learned that a title or appearance does not guarantee wisdom; true knowledge comes from humility, experience, and sincerity.

**Moral:** Do not mistake showy authority for genuine wisdom; question pretenders, seek truth through reason and humility, and remember that empty words cannot replace real understanding.
