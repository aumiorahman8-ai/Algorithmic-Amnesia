**The Tale of “Shat Bhai Champa” (The Hundred Brothers and the Champa Tree)**  

*Note: This is a traditional Bengali folk story that has been passed down through generations. The version below is a retelling meant to capture the spirit of the original oral narrative.*

---

### 1. The Unusual Birth

In a small village nestled among the emerald fields of Bengal, lived a humble farmer named **Bikram** and his wife **Madhuri**. They were childless and spent their days praying to the village deity, hoping for a miracle. One moon‑lit night, as the couple slept under a thatched roof, a soft, fragrant breeze drifted through the open window, carrying the scent of **champa** (magnolia) blossoms.

When they awoke, they found a tiny bundle of golden‑hued infants swaddled in a leaf‑woven blanket beside a blooming champa tree. The children’s cries were as sweet as the flower’s perfume. The village elder, hearing the news, declared that the gods had answered their prayers: **one hundred brothers** had been born at once, each a gift of the champa spirit.

---

### 2. Growing Up Together

The hundred brothers—each named **B** (for “Bikram”) followed by a number—were inseparable. They learned to walk, speak, and work side by by, their laughter echoing across the paddy fields. Because there were so many of them, the village soon called them **“Shat Bhai”** (the Hundred Brothers).

Madhuri, though overwhelmed, cared for them with boundless love. She taught them the art of weaving, the rhythm of folk songs, and the wisdom of the **“panchayat”** (village council). The brothers, in turn, helped the farmer tend the fields, plow the soil, and harvest the rice. Their combined strength turned a modest plot into the most prosperous farm in the region.

---

### 3. The Envy of the King

Word of the miraculous brothers spread far beyond the village. The **king of the neighboring kingdom**, a proud and jealous ruler, heard of the Shat Bhai and coveted their strength. He sent a messenger to the village with a golden invitation:

> “Come to my palace and serve as my royal guards. In return, you shall receive riches beyond imagination.”

Madhuri, wary of the king’s motives, consulted the village elder. The elder warned that the king’s heart was clouded by greed, and that the brothers should stay true to their humble roots. Yet the brothers, eager to see the world beyond their fields, decided to visit the palace—each promising to return before the sun set.

---

### 4. The Test of Loyalty

At the palace, the king welcomed the hundred brothers with pomp and ceremony. He assigned each a task: some guarded the gates, others tended the royal gardens, and a few were asked to lift heavy chariots. The brothers performed flawlessly, their unity making them seem like a single, unstoppable force.

But the king’s true test came at night. He ordered the chief guard to **steal the sacred champa blossom** that grew in the palace courtyard—a blossom said to grant eternal health to anyone who possessed it. He whispered, “If you bring me that flower, I will make you the richest men in the land.”

The brothers, remembering the champa tree that had given them life, felt a tug at their hearts. They gathered in secret, and the eldest brother, **B‑1**, spoke:

> “Our mother’s love and the village’s trust are more precious than any gold. We must not betray the spirit that birthed us.”

Together they decided to **refuse** the king’s demand. Instead, they returned the stolen blossom to its place, letting its fragrance fill the night air. When the king discovered the betrayal, his fury boiled over.

---

### 5. The King’s Wrath and the Miracle

Enraged, the king summoned his army and ordered them to **capture the Shat Bhai**. The brothers, though many, were outnumbered. As the soldiers advanced, the champa tree that stood outside the palace began to tremble. Its branches swayed, and a gentle rain of golden petals fell upon the battlefield.

Each petal that touched a brother turned into a **protective shield of light**. The soldiers, blinded by the brilliance, stumbled and fell. The king, seeing his forces defeated by a simple flower’s magic, fell to his knees and begged for mercy.

Madhuri, who had followed her sons to the palace, stepped forward. She placed a single champa blossom in the king’s palm and said:

> “Let this flower remind you that true strength lies not in domination, but in humility and gratitude.”

The king, humbled, promised never to misuse his power again. He released the brothers and ordered that a **champa grove be planted** at the village’s edge, so that the spirit of the flower could protect the people forever.

---

### 6. Return to the Village

The Shat Bhai returned home to a jubilant celebration. The villagers sang songs of their bravery, and the champa grove blossomed, its fragrance forever mingling with the scent of fresh rice fields. The brothers continued to work the land, but now they also served as **guardians of the village**, teaching the children the values of unity, honesty, and respect for nature.

Madhuri, now revered as the mother of a hundred heroes, lived out her days under the shade of the champa tree, listening to the rustle of its leaves—each whisper a reminder that **the greatest miracles are born from love, humility, and the simple gifts of the earth**.

---

### 7. Moral of the Story

- **Unity is Strength:** The hundred brothers could achieve what a single person could not.
- **Respect for Nature:** The champa tree, a symbol of purity, protects those who honor it.
- **Humility over Greed:** The king’s downfall came from his covetous heart; redemption came through humility.
- **Loyalty to One’s Roots:** No matter how far they traveled, the brothers never forgot the village that raised them.

---

**Thus ends the tale of “Shat Bhai Champa,” a story that continues to be told in Bengali households, reminding each generation that true power lies in togetherness, reverence for nature, and a humble heart.**
