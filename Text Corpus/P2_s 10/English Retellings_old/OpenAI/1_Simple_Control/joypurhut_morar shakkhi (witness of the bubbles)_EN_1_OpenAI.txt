**Morar Shakkhi (মরার শাক্কি) – “The Witness of the Bubbles”**  
*An old Bengali folktale, retold for today’s ears*

---

### 1. The Setting

In a quiet village tucked along the banks of the Hooghly River, there lived a modest potter named **Biren**. He worked in a small, thatched workshop, shaping clay into earthen pots, water‑carriers, and the occasional decorative figurine. Though his hands were skilled, Biren’s life was simple and his fortunes modest.

Beyond the village, deep in the mangrove‑laden wetlands, there was a mysterious pond known to the locals as **“Morar Pukur”** – the *Pond of Mirrors*. The water there was so still and clear that, on moonlit nights, it reflected the sky like a perfect mirror. The villagers whispered that the pond was a place where wishes could be heard, but only if one approached it with a pure heart.

---

### 2. The Trouble

One scorching summer, a severe drought struck the region. The river receded, the fields cracked, and the village wells ran dry. Biren’s clay, once abundant, became hard and brittle, and his pots began to break before they could even be sold. The villagers grew anxious, and a sense of helplessness settled over the community like a heavy fog.

One evening, as Biren sat on the threshold of his workshop, an old woman—known to everyone as **Maya**, the village’s wise‑woman—came to him. She carried a small, earthen jar that seemed to glow faintly in the fading light.

> “Biren,” she said, “the pond of mirrors will show you a way, but you must first prove that you can see beyond what is obvious.”

She placed the jar on the ground and, with a gentle smile, whispered, “When the moon rises, go to Morar Pukur. Let the water hear your heart’s true wish. But remember—only the bubbles that rise from the bottom will be your witnesses.”

Biren, though skeptical, felt a spark of hope. He thanked Maya and set out for the pond, his mind swirling with thoughts of his failing craft and the starving villagers.

---

### 3. The Night at Morar Pukur

That night, the moon was a silver crescent, its light spilling over the still water of Morar Pukur. Biren arrived, his feet sinking into the soft, muddy bank. He knelt, placed his palms on the cool surface, and closed his eyes.

He whispered his wish: *“May the clay be soft again, may my pots hold water, and may my village never thirst again.”* As he spoke, a gentle breeze rustled the reeds, and tiny bubbles began to rise from the pond’s depths.

The bubbles were ordinary at first—small, translucent spheres that floated upward, popping silently at the surface. But then, something strange happened. Each bubble seemed to carry a faint, shimmering hue—green, amber, and deep blue—like tiny lanterns.

Biren watched, mesmerized. The bubbles rose in a rhythm, one after another, forming a silent procession. When the last bubble reached the surface and vanished, a soft, echoing voice seemed to emanate from the water itself:

> “The witness of the bubbles is the patience of the earth. To coax the clay to soften, you must first soften your own heart.”

Biren opened his eyes. The pond was still, but the words lingered in his mind like a mantra.

---

### 4. The Lesson

Returning to his workshop at dawn, Biren reflected on the pond’s message. He realized that his frustration and haste had hardened his own spirit, just as the drought had hardened the clay. He decided to change his approach:

1. **Patience in the Kiln** – Instead of firing his pots quickly, he lowered the temperature and let the fire breathe, allowing the clay to dry slowly.
2. **Gentle Hands** – He began to massage the clay with warm water, speaking soft verses to it, treating each lump as a living thing.
3. **Community Care** – He shared the little water he had with his neighbors, offering his pots for storing the precious rainwater they collected.

Days turned into weeks. The first rain finally fell, a gentle drizzle that soaked the parched earth. The river swelled, the wells filled, and the clay in Biren’s workshop regained its pliability. His pots, now crafted with calm hands, held water without leaking, and the villagers rejoiced.

---

### 5. The Return to the Pond

Months later, when the village celebrated the end of the drought with a small festival, Biren walked once more to Morar Pukur. He placed a freshly made earthen jar on the water’s edge and whispered his gratitude.

This time, the bubbles rose again, but they were different. They swirled together, forming a delicate pattern that resembled a lotus blooming on the surface. As they burst, a faint, melodic chime echoed, as if the pond itself were singing.

> “You have become the witness of the bubbles, Biren. As you listened, you learned to listen to the earth, to the water, to the breath of life. May your heart always be as clear as this pond.”

Biren bowed, feeling a deep peace settle within him. He understood that the true magic was not in the pond’s water, but in the humility and patience he had cultivated.

---

### 6. The Moral

The tale of **Morar Shakkhi**—the “Witness of the Bubbles”—has been passed down through generations in Bengal as a reminder that:

- **Patience softens even the hardest clay.**  
- **Listening to nature’s quiet signs can guide us through hardship.**  
- **True wishes are fulfilled when we first heal our own hearts.**

In villages today, when children see bubbles rising from a pond or a river, elders often tell them this story, urging them to watch, to wait, and to learn the quiet wisdom that lives in every ripple.

---

**End of the tale.** May the bubbles of your own life rise gently, carrying with them the wishes of a patient heart.
