**The Tale of Dalim Kumar – A Bengali Folktale Retold**

*Setting the Scene*  
In a small, sun‑kissed village on the banks of the Hooghly River, life moved to the rhythm of the monsoon rains, the rustle of paddy fields, and the soft chants of the village priest. The people were simple folk—farmers, weavers, and fishermen—who lived together in harmony, sharing joys and sorrows alike. Among them lived a modest family: a widowed mother, her clever little son Dalim, and an old, faithful dog named Bholu.

*The Boy Who Loved Stories*  
From a very early age, Dalim was fascinated by the stories his mother told him at night—tales of brave princes, wise sages, and mischievous spirits. He would sit on the low bamboo stool, eyes wide, and imagine himself as the hero of those adventures. Though his family was poor, Dalim’s imagination was rich, and his quick wit often helped the village solve everyday problems.

*The King’s Decree*  
One scorching summer, a royal messenger arrived in the village with a proclamation from the king of the nearby kingdom: “All subjects must bring a golden mango to the palace within three days, or they shall pay a heavy tax.” The golden mango was a mythical fruit said to appear only once a year, glowing like the sun and tasting like honey. It was a test of loyalty, but also a clever way for the king to gather wealth.

The villagers were bewildered. No one had ever seen a golden mango, and the forest around the village was thick with wild mango trees that bore ordinary fruit. Panic spread—how could they meet the king’s demand?

*Dalim’s Plan*  
While the adults fretted, Dalim sat under the shade of a banyan tree, listening to the murmurs. He remembered an old legend his mother once told him: “When the moon is full and the night is still, the spirit of the forest, *Bhootnath*, appears and grants a single wish to those who speak to him with a pure heart.”

That night, under a luminous full moon, Dalim slipped into the forest with Bholu trotting beside him. He found a quiet clearing, knelt, and whispered his wish: “Great spirit of the forest, our village cannot bring a golden mango to the king. Please help us, so we may not suffer.”

A gentle breeze rustled the leaves, and a faint, golden glow shimmered among the trees. From the shadows emerged a slender figure—*Bhootnath*, the forest spirit—his eyes like polished amber, his voice like the rustle of leaves.

“Child of the village,” the spirit said, “your heart is pure, and your wish is humble. I shall grant you a single golden mango, but you must promise to share it with all who need it, and never use it for selfish gain.”

Dalim bowed deeply and promised. With a wave of his hand, the spirit produced a single mango that glowed like sunrise. It was warm to the touch, and its skin shimmered with a golden hue. The spirit vanished as silently as he had appeared, leaving Dalim clutching the miraculous fruit.

*The Clever Delivery*  
The next morning, the village awoke to find Dalim returning with the golden mango cradled in a woven basket. The villagers gasped in awe, but Dalim had already thought ahead. He split the mango into many thin slices, each piece still glowing faintly. He wrapped each slice in a leaf and gave them to the villagers, saying, “Take these to the palace. Let the king see that we have honored his command.”

The villagers, though skeptical, trusted Dalim’s wisdom. They set out, one by one, to the royal palace, each carrying a slice of the golden mango.

*The King’s Surprise*  
When the royal courtiers saw the villagers arriving with glowing mango slices, they were bewildered. The king, a stern man with a reputation for harshness, ordered the mangoes to be placed on his throne. As he lifted a slice, the golden light bathed the hall, and a sweet fragrance filled the air. The king tasted the fruit and felt a sudden warmth in his heart—a feeling he had not known in years.

He called his ministers and declared, “Let this be a lesson. The true wealth of a kingdom lies not in gold or taxes, but in the generosity and unity of its people.” He ordered that the tax be lifted for the village and that the golden mango be shared among all the kingdom’s subjects during the upcoming harvest festival.

*The Aftermath*  
The village celebrated with music, dance, and feasting. Dalim’s mother wept with pride, and Bholu barked joyously. The story of Dalim Kumar’s cleverness and pure heart spread far and wide, becoming a favorite tale told around hearths and in schoolyards.

*The Moral*  
The folktale of Dalim Kumar teaches us that:

1. **Ingenuity and courage can turn impossible challenges into opportunities.**  
2. **A pure heart and selfless intention attract help—sometimes from the most unexpected places.**  
3. **True leadership is about compassion and sharing, not about demanding tribute.**  

And so, whenever the moon shines full over the Hooghly, the villagers whisper thanks to the forest spirit and remember the brave boy who turned a golden mango into a golden lesson for all.
