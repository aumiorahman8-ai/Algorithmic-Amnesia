**The Tale of the Girl Who Married the Prince of Snakes**  
*(A Bengali folk story, retold for modern ears)*  

---

### 1. The Setting

In a quiet village tucked between the emerald paddy fields and the winding banks of the Ganges, lived a modest family of weavers. Their daughter, **Moyna**, was known throughout the hamlet for her gentle voice, bright eyes, and a heart as soft as the cotton she spun. Though the family was poor, they were rich in love and in the simple blessings of the river’s bounty.

Beyond the village, deep in the tangled mangrove forest, there lay a hidden pond where the water shimmered like liquid silver. The locals whispered that the pond was the home of **Nagar‑Brahma**, the king of the serpents—a being who could shed his scaly skin and appear as a handsome prince when the moon was full.

---

### 2. The Omen

One monsoon night, a fierce storm battered the village. Lightning split the sky, and a great flood surged through the fields. In the chaos, Moyna’s younger brother slipped into the swollen river and was swept away. The family prayed desperately, but the waters kept him hidden.

The next morning, a mysterious old woman arrived at the weaver’s door, her eyes glinting like polished stones. She offered a single, silver‑scaled snake and said, “Place this on the riverbank and chant the ancient prayer of **Mrittika**. The child will be returned, but you must keep a promise to the serpent that comes at night.”

Moyna, desperate and trusting the old woman’s words, placed the snake on the bank and sang the prayer. As the last note faded, the river’s roar softened, and a small, trembling boy emerged from the water, clutching a handful of reeds. The family rejoiced, believing the miracle was complete.

---

### 3. The Night Visitor

That night, as the moon rose high and silvered the pond, a soft rustle echoed from the mangrove. A sleek, emerald‑green snake slithered into Moyna’s modest hut. Its eyes glowed with an uncanny intelligence. When it reached the foot of Moyna’s cot, the snake shed its skin in a flash of light, revealing a tall, regal young man dressed in a silk dhoti of gold thread.

He bowed low and spoke in a voice that sounded like the rustle of leaves:  

> “I am **Nagar‑Brahma**, king of the serpents. You have freed my brother, the river’s child, with your pure heart. In return, I ask for your hand in marriage. I shall become a man when the moon is full, and a snake when the sun rises. Will you accept?”

Moyna, though startled, felt a calm settle over her. She remembered the old woman’s warning about a promise, and she saw the sincerity in the prince’s eyes. With a steady voice, she answered, “I accept, but only if we live together in truth and love, no matter the shape I see.”

The serpent‑prince smiled, and the moonlight turned his scales to a soft, golden hue. He promised to protect Moyna and her family, and to bring prosperity to the village.

---

### 4. Life in Two Forms

For many months, Moyna lived with her husband in the duality of his nature. By night, when the moon was full, he was a handsome prince, sharing stories of the hidden world beneath the water and teaching her the secret songs that could calm storms. By day, he was a sleek snake, coiling around the hearth to keep the fire warm, and slipping into the fields to guard the crops from pests.

The villagers, initially fearful, soon saw the blessings that followed. The paddy yielded a richer harvest, the river’s fish multiplied, and the children grew strong. Moyna’s kindness, combined with the serpent‑prince’s subtle magic, turned the once‑struggling hamlet into a thriving community.

---

### 5. The Test of Faith

One year, a jealous neighboring chieftain coveted the village’s newfound wealth. He sent a band of thieves to steal the golden amulet that the serpent‑prince wore—a talisman that kept his two forms in harmony. The thieves broke into the hut at night and snatched the amulet, fleeing into the forest.

When Moyna awoke, she found her husband trembling, his scales dull, his eyes clouded with pain. “Without the amulet,” he whispered, “my two selves will clash, and I will be lost forever.”

Moyna’s heart pounded, but she remembered the old woman’s lesson: **courage is the fire that forges destiny**. She gathered the village women, and together they set out into the mangrove, following the faint scent of the amulet—an aroma of sandalwood and river mud.

After a night of searching, they found the thieves camped near a fire, the amulet glinting on a stone. Moyna stepped forward, her voice steady, and sang the prayer of **Mrittika** once more. The chant resonated through the trees, and the thieves, overwhelmed by a sudden sense of shame, dropped the amulet and fled.

Moyna reclaimed the talisman and returned it to her husband. As she placed it around his neck, a brilliant light burst forth, and the serpent‑prince’s two halves merged into a single, radiant being—half‑man, half‑snake, whole in spirit.

---

### 6. The Blessing

The next full moon, the village gathered by the pond to celebrate. The serpent‑prince, now fully human in appearance but still bearing a faint, iridescent pattern on his skin, thanked Moyna and the villagers. He declared:

> “Because of your faith, love, and bravery, the river will forever protect this land. Whenever the monsoon threatens, the waters will recede at your call. And whenever you need guidance, look to the stars reflected in the pond, for they are the eyes of the serpents watching over you.”

Moyna and the prince married under a canopy of lanterns, their vows echoing the ancient prayer that had saved her brother. The old woman, who had set the events in motion, appeared one last time, smiling. She placed a single silver scale on Moyna’s hair—a token that the bond between humans and the hidden world would never be broken.

---

### 7. The Moral

The tale of **Moyna and the Prince of Snakes** is told in Bengali villages to remind listeners of three timeless truths:

1. **Compassion can bridge worlds** – A pure heart can reach even the most mysterious of beings.  
2. **Promises made in desperation must be honored** – True courage lies in keeping one’s word, even when the path is unclear.  
3. **Faith and unity turn adversity into blessing** – When a community stands together, even the fiercest storms can be calmed.

And so, whenever the moon rises high over the mangrove pond, the villagers whisper a soft song, thanking the serpent‑prince for his protection, and they remember the brave girl who married a snake and turned a humble village into a place of wonder.  

---  

*May the story inspire you to see the magic hidden in everyday kindness, and to trust that love—no matter its shape—can transform the world.*
