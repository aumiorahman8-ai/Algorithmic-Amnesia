**The Tale of Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey**  
*(A Bengali folk story, retold for a modern audience)*  

---

### 1. Setting the Scene  

In a quiet village tucked among the emerald fields of Bengal, there lived two inseparable friends: **Noshi**, a clever little girl with bright eyes and a quick smile, and **Dimshi**, her faithful companion—a plump, orange‑spotted cat who loved to nap in the warm sun. Their modest home was a thatched‑roof cottage at the edge of a mango grove, and the village market bustled nearby with traders, storytellers, and the occasional wandering animal.

One sweltering summer afternoon, as the cicadas sang and the scent of ripe mangoes hung in the air, a mischievous monkey named **Bhalu** (meaning “bear” in Bengali, a nickname the villagers gave him for his size) swung down from the trees and landed in the village square.

---

### 2. The Monkey’s Trick  

Bhalu was known for his clever tricks and his love of stealing food. He eyed the market stalls, especially the one where a plump, golden **pitha** (a sweet rice cake) was displayed. With a swift flick of his tail, he snatched the pitha and darted away, laughing a high‑pitched, chattering laugh.

Noshi, who was helping her mother carry a basket of fresh vegetables, saw the monkey’s daring theft. She frowned, not because she wanted the pitha for herself, but because the monkey’s antics often caused trouble for the whole village. Dimshi, perched on the basket’s edge, twitched his whiskers and let out a low, warning growl.

“Let’s get that pitha back,” Noshi whispered to Dimshi. “And maybe we can teach Bhalu a lesson he won’t forget.”

---

### 3. The Plan  

Noshi remembered an old rhyme her grandmother used to sing:

> *“If you want a monkey to stay, give him a treat, then lead him away.”*  

She decided to use the monkey’s love for sweets against him. She gathered a handful of **jaggery‑coated peanuts**—the kind that melt in the mouth and stick to the tongue. Dimshi, ever the opportunist, nudged the peanuts into a small bamboo basket.

Noshi then set a trap: she placed the peanuts on a low branch of a mango tree, just out of reach, and tied a thin rope to the branch. The other end of the rope was tied to a sturdy wooden plank that rested on the ground, ready to slide when pulled.

---

### 4. The Monkey’s Return  

Bhalu, still feeling the thrill of his earlier theft, returned to the square, his eyes scanning for more loot. The sweet scent of the jaggery peanuts drifted on the breeze, and his nose twitched. He followed the smell straight to the mango tree.

Seeing the peanuts dangling temptingly, Bhalu tried to reach them with his long fingers, but they were just out of his grasp. He hopped, stretched, and finally, with a clever little jump, managed to snag one. As he pulled the peanut, the rope tightened, and the wooden plank slid forward, knocking the monkey off balance.

Bhalu tumbled down, landing with a soft thump on a pile of fresh mangoes. The mangoes burst open, splattering sweet juice everywhere. The monkey, now covered in sticky mango pulp and jaggery, squealed in surprise.

---

### 5. The Lesson  

Noshi and Dimshi emerged from behind the market stalls, laughing. Noshi held out the original pitha, now slightly crumbled but still delicious.

“Here, Bhalu,” she said gently. “Take this back. But remember—stealing from others brings trouble, and sometimes a little kindness can turn a prank into a friendship.”

Bhalu, his fur dripping with mango juice, stared at the girl and the cat. He sniffed the pitha, then, with a shy grin, took a bite. The sweet taste softened his mischievous heart.

From that day on, Bhalu visited the village not as a thief but as a playful guest. He would swing from the mango trees, share the mangoes he found, and sometimes even help Noshi carry water from the well. Dimshi, ever watchful, kept a lazy eye on the monkey, ready to pounce if mischief resurfaced.

---

### 6. Moral of the Story  

The villagers still tell the tale of **Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey** to remind children that:

1. **Cleverness can solve problems** – Noshi used her wits instead of force.  
2. **Kindness can change a heart** – Offering the pitha turned a thief into a friend.  
3. **Friendship bridges differences** – A girl, a cat, and a monkey learned to live together in harmony.

And whenever the mango trees sway in the summer breeze, the villagers swear they can hear a faint, cheerful chattering—Bhalu’s thank‑you echoing through the grove.  

---  

*May this story bring a smile to your day, just as it has brightened many evenings in the villages of Bengal.*
