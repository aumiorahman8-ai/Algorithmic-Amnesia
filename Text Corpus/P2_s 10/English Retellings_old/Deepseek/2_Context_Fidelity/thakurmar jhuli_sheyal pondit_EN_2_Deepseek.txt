Here is a faithful retelling of the Bengali folktale "Sheyal Pônḍit" (শিয়াল পণ্ডিত) from Dakshinaranjan Mitra Majumder's *Thakurmar Jhuli*, preserving cultural nuances, kinship terms, rituals, and phrasing:

**The Tale of Sheyal Pônḍit (The Fox Scholar)**

Long ago, in a village hemmed by emerald paddy fields and whispering bamboo groves, lived a poor family: an old mother called *Dida* (Grandmother), her daughter *Chhelema* (literally "Boy," often used affectionately for daughters), and Chhelema's husband, the farmer.

They led a simple existence, harvesting rice chaff (*khoḍa*) and gleaning fallen grains (*nikri*). One harsh winter, famine gripped the land. Their stomachs growled emptiness. In desperation, Dida sighed, "Ore *Bow-ma* (Daughter-in-law), what can we feed our boy and *Damaḍa* (Son-in-law)? Not a grain remains." Chhelema, thoughtful, replied, "*Jetha-moshai* and *Jethi* (Father-in-law & Mother-in-law), fret not. Our ancestor, the *Maa Go* (Mother Cow), stands tethered. She who blesses our prosperity must help us now. Prepare her for market: wash her hooves, adorn her horns with turmeric paste (*haldi*), braid her tail with vermillion streaks (*alta*). We shall sell her."

Touched by their piety, Goddess Lakshmi blessed the cow. As they led her through a dense forest, ablaze with *Palash* flowers, they encountered **Sheyal Pônḍit** – Brother Fox – perched on a ridge. Known throughout Bangla and Bardhaman for his cunning, his sharp eyes appraised the plump cow. A wicked scheme hatched in his mind.

He slithered down, pressed his paws together in a profound *Pranam* (obeisance), and recited in flowing Sanskrit, "*Bor Jetha-moshai! Bor Jethi-moshai! Bor Bow-major!* (Great Father-in-law! Great Mother-in-law! Great Auntie-in-law!) *Shashto Pranam!* (Six-fold salutations!) My humble abode lies nearby. The Sun's fury scorches. Won't you grace my *ashon* (seat/homestead) for a simple *pari-panta* (cold fermented rice - a humble meal)?"

Amazed by his reverence and Sanskrit, their farmer-Damaḍa exclaimed, "Dida, Chhelema! Such a learned Pônḍit honours us! We *must* accept!" They followed him, trusting deeply. He led them to his den – a hole under a *Bot* tree (Banyan) – and vanished inside. Moments later, he returned with shamefaced paws: "*Aei re! Mor shunday-lok re!* (Alas! Woe is my fate!) Last night, wretched rats gnawed through every pot. Not a grain remains! But stay! Blessings are upon us. Give me Maa Go's rope, I'll tether her by succulent grass while you rest."

Fooled by his scholarly charade and elaborate courtesy, they handed him the rope. With gleeful treachery, Sheyal Pônḍit dragged the frightened cow deep into the forest... straight into his belly! Only horns and hooves remained – offered mockingly to propitiate the ancestors (*Pitr*).

Returning tearfully with horns and rope, the trio wailed, "Sheyal Pônḍit *Bhagō*! (Fox-Pundit Ran!) He ate Maa Go!" Their cries echoed through *Baro-Borobari* (neighbourhoods). Enraged villagers – the mighty *Bagha Raja* (Tiger King), *Kukur* (Dog), *Sagh* (Jackal) – assembled.

"*Kothaey gelé sheyal?*" (Where did the fox go?) roared Bagha Raja. "He went *utō*!" (There!) the villagers pointed. Driven by fury and injustice, the chase began: Bagha Raja loping, Kukur barking, Sagh snarling, joined by *Kukura* (more dogs), *Baghyas* (Hyenas), even *Beral* (Cat).

Cornered beneath a sprawling *Boru* tree (a type of Banyan?), Sheyal trembled. But cunning beat fear. Swiftly, he brushed dung onto the *Dhekergach* (Fern Tree - likely giant fern) trunk, whispering a deadly scripture: "*Ei boru gachéy je ŗebéy, shak béshéy joré marbéy!*" (Whoever climbs this Boru tree, Ferns will sprout & strangle them!).

As Bagha Raja arrived, Sheyal cried, "*Hai Hai Mahātma-rājā!* (Alas, Great Soul-King!) Run! Flee! This tree is cursed! See the mark?" He showed the smeared dung. "Run South! Protect your valor!" The majestic tiger abandoned the chase, vanishing toward Udoygiri hills.

Next came Kukur. Sheyal pointed again: "*Hai Hai Duto-Chokhe-Kurra-janjāl!* (Alas, Two-Eyed Danger-Chain!) Flee! See this cursed tree? Run South!" The dog obeyed.

Sagh approached. Sheyal pleaded, "*Hai Hai Amāero Chhōt Bhāiye!* (Alas, My Own Little Brother!) Save yourself! This tree destroys Sadhs! Go South!" Confused, Sagh followed.

Sheyal grinned, "Fools! All fooled!" He crept towards the Padma River to drink, whispering *"Tok-broot nibhé!"* (May bitterness be subdued!).

But unbeknownst to Sheyal, crafty Birāl Mama (Uncle Cat) hid under river reeds (*khāg*). As Sheyal leaned down, *"Chhabāo!"* (Snap!) Birāl sprang, sinking claws deep.

"*Hai Hai Māgo!* (Alas Mother!) Birāl Mama!" cried the fox. "Release me! I'll dazzle you!" Rolling, writhing, Sheyal shed fur but Birāl held firm. "*Chhēlé-go*? (Son?)" mocked Birāl, "*Tor ki éka Chadmopuri*? (Is this your lone trick?)" He dragged Sheyal out. Seeing their prey unmasked, Bagha Raja, Kukur, Sagh, rallied, tearing the *Chhōlkāl* fox (Trickster-Dishonest fellow) to pieces.

And so, Sheyal Pônḍit's greed and treachery met a fitting end. Justice echoes near **Ganga-Sagar**, proving: Trickery breeds destruction, while humility bows to the divine Surya-Narayan.

**Preserved Nuances:**

1.  **Kinship Terms:** *Dida* (Grandmother), *Chhelema/Damaḍa* (Daughter/Son-in-law), *Jetha-moshai/Jethi* (Father-in-law/Mother-in-law), *Bow-major/Bow* (Aunt/Married Woman), *Bor Jetha-moshai* (Exaggerated reverence), *Bhāgē!/Bhagō* (Ran!), *Mahātma-rājā* (Majestic Tiger), *Chhōt Bhāiye* (Little Brother), *Birāl Mama* (Uncle Cat), *Chhēlé-go* (Son? - sarcastic).
2.  **Rituals & Customs:**
    *   Preparing Maa Go for sale: Washing feet, turmeric/germinated rice (*Haldi-tégu*) on horns, *Alta* on tail – acts of reverence before a significant transaction involving a sacred animal.
    *   Offering *Pari-pānṭā*: Symbol of Bengali village hospitality, especially towards guests.
    *   *Pranam* & Sanskrit recitation: Shows extreme reverence in traditional hierarchy.
    *   Offering horns/hooves to *Pitr*: Ritual appeasement of ancestors, twisted mockingly by the fox.
    *   "*Tok-broot nibhé*": Folk magic incantation to ward off bitterness (literally "submerge the bitterness seeds").
    *   River as boundary: Crossing Padma/Ganga symbolizes crossing into danger/mortality.
3.  **Cultural Elements:**
    *   Famine reliance on cow: Reflects agrarian dependence.
    *   Use of *Khoḍa* (rice bran/broken bits) & *Nikri* (gleaned grains): Heightens desperation.
    *   Forest imagery: Palash flowers, Bot/Boru/Dhekergach trees evoke Bengal's landscape.
    *   **Ganga-Sagar:** Sacred confluence, emphasizing the tale's moral & divine justice.
    *   Animal Hierarchy: Tiger King as ruler, Jackal/Brother Fox tricksters.
    *   Folk sayings/imprecations: "*Ei Boru Gachéy je ŗebéy...*" (Created magic); "*Tor ki éka Chadmopuri?*" (Is this your only trick?), "*Chhōlkāl*" (Scoundrel/Trickster).
    *   **Structure:** Classic chase & trickster downfall with poetic justice and closing wisdom.

This retelling aims to capture the oral storytelling rhythm, the specific Bengali lexicon, and the embedded cultural worldview found in *Thākurmār Jhūli*.
