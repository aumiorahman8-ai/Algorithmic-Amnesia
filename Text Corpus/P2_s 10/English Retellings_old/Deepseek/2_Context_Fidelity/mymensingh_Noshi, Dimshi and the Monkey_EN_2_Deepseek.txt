Here is the authentic retelling of the **'Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey'** folktale from Mymensingh, preserving the cultural nuances, kinship terms, rituals, and local flavor:

**_The Tale of Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey-Friend_**

In a *Mouza* (village settlement) nestled beside one of Mymensingh’s mighty *haors* (wetland marshes), lived two brothers-in-law - **Noshi** (the elder brother-in-law) and **Dimshi** (the younger brother-in-law). Dimshi was married to Noshi's sister, making Dimshi Noshi's *'Shaala'* (শালা - brother-in-law, specifically wife's younger brother/country bumpkin), a term often used with affectionate annoyance.

One sticky *Joishthyo* (summer month) afternoon, Noshi decided to teach his somewhat slow *Shaala* a lesson. He said to Dimshi: *“Haire Shaala! Shob din gorom, ajke shikar-e jawa jaaye? Bhaatkapor-er khoj neyi?”* (Hey Brother-in-law! Isn't a hunt perfect on such a hot day? Need a challenge?). *Bhaat-Kapor* (ভাত-কাপড়) - literally 'rice and clothes' - here signifies sustenance, prosperity, a gift/prize.

Now, Dimshi, ever a bit dim but trusting, readily agreed. As they entered the deep forest fringing the *haor*, Noshi pointed dramatically at a dense patch: *“Ey barir parke Ugro Chhere! Dekho tomar bhaatkapor!”* (Look near that Sal tree! There lies your prize!). Then, whispering conspiratorially, he bet Dimshi couldn't sneak up silently and jump on this elusive target blindfolded.

Dimshi, blinded by a *gamchha* (checked cotton towel), leaped with gusto... crashing squarely onto an ancient, bony *Bagher-Mama* (বাঘের মামা - Tiger’s Uncle, a bold euphemism for a fierce *old tiger*!), startling it awake! Terrified, Dimshi froze. Noshi, grinning mischievously from a safe *Gamar-gachh* (Gamhar tree), yelled: *“Kaallo Dimshi! Tikkaanno koro na! Turr de!”* (Run Dimshi! Don't hesitate! Bolt!). Dimshi scrambled away wildly, blindfold forgotten, leaving Noshi chuckling atop his perch.

Wandering bruised and distraught deep into the jungle, Dimshi stumbled upon a troop of monkeys. A large, seemingly wise monkey approached. Dimshi poured out his woes: *“Bhai! Amar Shaala!’y amar shonge shokto chadhdha korechilo!”* (Brother! My *Shaala* set me up horribly!). The Monkey, adopting Dimshi's rustic Bangla, patted him: *“Ki shombrom Chhelebaba! Chinta koro na. Ami tomar bondhu. Cholo Amar bagan-e.”* (Don't worry, Young Sir! Walk with me to my garden). Seeing Dimshi's torn clothes, the Monkey offered him clothes fit for a *Zamindar*'s *Naior* – fine silk with pin-stripes (*Gaja-saaj*).

Thus began Dimshi’s new life. He ate *Shetkaler Bhaat* (mountain rice), juicy *Kul* fruits (Indian Jujube), slept under fragrant *Chapa-phool* trees (Plumeria). But Dimshi dearly missed his village *Bari* (home) and sister. He confided in Monkey: *“Bandhu, ami hridoye Parayesh kinna hoitesi! Gyachhe choley jaboon!”* (Friend, I'm homesick! I need to go back!). Monkey lovingly agreed: *“Thik achhey Bondhu. Kintu ekbaar tomar daargu bukey shapat dilay jaben?”* (Alright, but swear by your elder brother?).

Dimshi, avoiding any oath mentioning Noshi directly, swore passionately: *“Na Bandhu! Mane gele to khub boro Dosh hobey! Bandhu tomar naam-ei shapath khaiya koichhi!”* (No way, Friend! That'd be a huge sin! I swear *by your name and honor*!). Satisfied with this vow (*Shapath*), Monkey helped Dimshi pack *Chitoi* baskets laden with treasures: *aada-gura* (ginger-jaggery sweets), *narikel* (coconuts), fragrant *Chambeli* flowers.

Thrilled, Dimshi started his journey. Seeing the laden *Chitoi*, villagers buzzed: *“Ei je Dimshiy-er Naiorey khabar! Shaala Noshi tomaarey toh she janpatay!”* (Look at Dimshi the Aristocrat! Brother-in-law Noshi tried to finish you!). Noshi arrived greedily: *“Ki Shaala! Chaau-laa ta tomar jethhay giyechhilo? Dekhi tomay ekbaar…”* (Where'd you go *Shaala*? Show me…). Dimshi explained his Monkey saviour.

Noshi’s ears perked up. Tricking Dimshi into revealing Monkey’s location, he immediately sought the garden. Approaching Monkey, Noshi whined dishonestly: *“Mukhtyar-bondhu! Ami Noshi – Dimshiy-er mohashshoi-bhai! Shei na bhujhey amar gaaye jaaphaat kaal!”* (Dearest Friend-Mediator! I'm Noshi – Dimshi’s respected elder brother! I've been frantic!). Monkey, cautious but hospitable, proclaimed: *“Jandeen chaad hoilo toh Achhaa Maanuush!”* (Alright, Enter good sir!). Noshi stayed, smirking inwardly at Dimshi's 'foolishness' in leaving.

But Noshi couldn't hide his greed. He broke utensils, complained about the food (*Lal-morich dilen na keno? Enog-eyer eshbo ta kokhon?* - Why no red chilli? When's that aristocrat perfume coming?), and worst of all, constantly eyed Monkey’s lush tail. One moonlit night, drunk on fermented *shiuli* flowers, Noshi calculated his escape route.

The next morning, pretending affection while combing Monkey’s fur, Noshi suddenly lunged! *“Hairey Mukhtyar-maari Bakshaash koro!”* (Forgive me Mediator-Lord!), he yelled... **GRABBING MONKEY'S TAIL!** He leapt wildly towards the tallest *Koromcha* tree (Sour Cherry), screaming insults learned from the *Jaamadar*: *“Lorkottoka!* (ভারী গালি - Extremely obscene insult implying maternal relations/infant death) *Bagher Bhaat Khaowa Shorshashur!"* (Tiger-food-eating Father-in-Law!). This was the ultimate betrayal disguised as kinship betrayal.

Monkey, in agony and fury, shrieked chillingly: *“EYEEEEEEE!”*. The jungle trembled. From nowhere, the ancient *Bagher-Mama* appeared, awakened again. Seeing Noshi dangling scandalously from Monkey's tail amidst the abuse, the *Bagher-Mama* judged instantly. Noshi wasn't just *Bokha* (a liar/thief); he was an *"ABHAAG"* (উপজাতি নয় - অত্যন্ত অসৎ ও নীতিবর্জিত লোক) - a morally bankrupt cheat who twisted trust. In a flash of teeth and fur, the *Bagher-Mama* delivered grim jungle justice.

Dimshi lived peacefully thereafter, forever haunted by his *Bhaat-Kapor*-hungry Shaala’s deceitful heart and the echoing *Mukhtyar-bandhur* mournful cry: *“EYEEEEEEE!”*. Folks say the monkeys near Mymensingh flinch at the name 'Noshi' and favour the truly gentle-hearted like Dimshi ever since.

**Cultural Nuances Preserved:**

1.  **Kinship Terms:** *Shaala* (শালা), *Mohashshoi-bhai* (মহাশয় ভাই - respectful elder brother), *Naior* (নাইওর - wealthy aristocrat/landed gentry).
2.  **Social Structures:** *Mouza*, *Bari* (household), *Bhaatkapor* (symbolizing livelihood/gift/prize), servant (*Adhnai*), village *Jaamadar* (headman).
3.  **Locale:** Mymensingh's *Haor* wetlands, sal/gamhar/koromcha trees, kul fruit.
4.  **Beliefs & Rituals:** *Shapat* (Swearing sacred oath), invoking *Daargu Bhai* (Elder Brother) in oaths.
5.  **Insults/Language:** Heavy native slang - *Shorshashur* (father-in-law), *Chachbora* (fish guts), extreme obscenity (*Lorkottoka*), *Bagher Bhaat-Khaowa*. Emphasis on rustic dialects.
6.  **Status Symbols:** *Pagri* (turban), *Gaja-Saaj* clothes, *Chitoi* baskets, agricultural goods as wealth (*Aada-Gura*, rice).
7.  **Characters:** Trickster Shaala (Noshi), Popat-Para* character (Dimshi), Animal mediator/judge (Monkey), Avenging force (Bagher Mama).
8.  **Outcome:** Moral justice delivered by the forest/jungle itself upon betrayal of hospitality and kinship bonds. Reflexive punishment for greed (*"ABHAAG"*).

*(Popat/Popat-Para* - এবেলা ওবেলা বোকাদের লোক বা গালি - Naive village fool, simpleton).
