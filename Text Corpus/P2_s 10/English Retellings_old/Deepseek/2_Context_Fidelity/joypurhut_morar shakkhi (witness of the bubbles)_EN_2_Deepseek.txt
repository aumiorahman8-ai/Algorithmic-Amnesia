Here is the retelling of the Bengali folktale "Morar Shakkhi" from Joypurhat, preserving cultural elements, kinship terms, and rituals as requested:

### The Tale of Morar Shakkhi (The Witness of the Bubbles)

In the ancient times of the kingdom of **Joypurhut**, ruled by the mighty yet forgetful **Raja Ramjibon**, lived a desperately poor farmer couple: **Gobardhan Gop** and his wife, **Savitri Dasi**. Their existence was a constant battle against hardship. The earth was parched, their **khottaan** (bullocks) weak, the bamboo frame of their **mosool** (plough) brittle, and their **dheki** (foot-operated rice pounder) long silent for lack of grain. Their lean bodies, **kalo kalo** (darkened) by the relentless sun, were clad only in **dhuti-gamcha** (lungi & coarse towel) and **shari bostro** (patched sari scraps).

One scorching **Jaishtha** afternoon, Raja Ramjibon travelled past Gobardhan's humble **chhan** (shack). Seeing the King's procession – the glittering **chhatradhon** (grand parasol), mounted soldiers, and **wazir** (vizier) – Gobardhan dared to prostrate himself, touching his forehead to the dusty earth (**pronaam kore**). He pleaded, "O Maharaj, **Thakur Budhodeb** (Lord Buddha) preserve Your Majesty! Your humble servant begs for just **eshoto dhan** (this much rice) and **eshoto go** (this much cow)! Grant me this *kripa* (grace), and I swear by your sacred feet, I shall repay you manifold when my fortunes turn!"

Perhaps moved by sincerity or momentarily pious, Raja Ramjibon gestured vaguely. "Ho-i-jo" (So be it), he mumbled distractedly to his **Mokaddam** (steward/overseer), "Ensure this man gets his measure." Then, enticed by the birdsong from deeper gardens, the Raja wandered off. As expected, his **bekar** (useless) Mokaddam, Haatichaaran, dismissed Gobardhan harshly: "Think the Maharaja feeds every begging crow? Off with you, **Moshai**!" (A respectful-but-distancing term meaning 'Sir').

Months dragged on. **Ash-Dorja** (Month of Ashwin/Durga Puja) brought heavy **barsha** (monsoon rains). The kingdom rejoiced! Fields became green, **jhora** (small canals) overflowed. Fisherfolk cast their **khapla jaal** (casting nets), joyous **gaan waalas** (folk singers) wandered singing **bhatiali**, and relatives exchanged **pithapulis** (festive sweets).

One day, Raja Ramjibon toured his flooded lands near Gobardhan's field. He saw the farmer diligently ploughing with strong bullocks and a fertile cow grazing nearby. Instantly, greed overcame him! He summoned Gobardhan: "Dhangshi Gaachhi!" (Lesser-term: You rustic!). "Why haven't you repaid my rice and cow? Have you forgotten the royal grace?"

Gobardhan trembled, palms joined (**jukto haat**), "Mahaprabhu! Thakur! The Mokaddam Saheb gave me nothing! Not a grain, not a hoof!"

The Raja roared, "Liar! Thief! Take him!"

Guards bound Gobardhan. Savitri ran, tears mixing with rain, pleading, "Dhorba na Shona! Dhorba na!" (Don't take him, beloved!). But the Raja decreed grimly: "Tomorrow, before the **Nagar-debi'r** (Town Goddess) altar, you swear oath! False testimony means your head!"

Back in soggy Joypurhut fort prison, Gobardhan despaired. Who would testify against the King? Savitri wept hysterically. Suddenly, trapped rain filled a shallow depression in their cell floor. Fat raindrops fell: **dhoop... dhap...**. Each drop created a shimmering **moira** (bubble) that floated briefly before bursting. Savitri stared, transfixed. A memory surfaced – something her mother mentioned about drought-time rituals...

At dawn, the **daak-dhatri** (sweetly chirping magpie) song echoed. Everyone gathered at the temple courtyard: Raja on his throne under a crimson **chal-chhatri** (square oilcloth parasol), **purohit** (priest), **karatadhari** (royal guard), and terrified villagers (**praja**). Gobardhan stood alone.

The purohit began drawing the sacred oath-circle (**sapath o-kroucher**) on the earth. Raja sneered, "Name your witness **Pagla**" (Madman), pointing at Gobardhan.

Gobardhan, instructed by Savitri’s vision, took a deep breath. His voice, though trembling, carried: "Shomoshchaan Maharaja! Thakur Budhodeb! **Aamar Shakkhi:** ***Morar Shakkhi!*** (My Witness: The Witness of the Bubbles!)"

Confusion rippled. The priest stuttered, "Moira? Fofo-bùbù? Who can *bear* such a witness?"

Suddenly, Savitri stepped forward. She raised her hands towards the storm-dark sky where **kal-boishakhi** clouds gathered: "Hear me, Shomoshchaan! The bubbles formed when rain falls upon thirsty earth – **Shombhu** (Shiva) sheds tears witnessing injustice! Their ephemeral glow holds truth! Even the King cannot defy **Shrishter Daata** (The Creator)! Call upon **Morar Shakkhi**! They testify!"

Before anyone reacted, the sky **forag-lag** (cracked)! Lightning split the heavens! Thunder like **Nandi's** roar shook the fort. A torrential downpour poured forth! Raindrops splashed onto the dusty courtyard instantly... forming thousands of shimmering bubbles **foi-foich-che**!

The Raja gasped. Seeing the countless **moiras** reflected in every puddle, shimmering like celestial eyes, his hubby collapsed. He interpreted the storm as divine wrath (**Ishwarer Gaj**). "Stop!" he cried, shielding his head. "Lo-gai-ni! Lo-gai-ni!" (I accept defeat/I withdraw!). "Manush Maaria! The debt is forgiven! Release him! Give back his livestock **Taratali** (Immediately)!"

Victorious, Gobardhan and Savitri returned home. Raja Ramjibon, shaken to his core, ordered his Mokaddam Haatichaaran flogged for negligence. The tale echoed through Joypurhat's **aa-mer jal-kuli-khoi-khaali** (winding canals), whispering: Believe not just kings and parchments, but the tears of the earth. For even a fragile **moira**, born from heaven's justice, can bring down tyranny. Thus, it became the legend of **Morar Shakkhi**.

*(Recorded originally by Rabindranath Tagore in 'Lokashahitya')*

**Key Cultural Nuances Preserved:**
1.  **Kinship/Honorifics:** Thakur (God/Royalty), Maharaja/Mahaprabhu (Your Majesty/Lord), Moshai (Sir), Dhangshi Gaachhi/Pagla (derogatory rustic/madman), Shriaster Daata (Creator God).
2.  **Rituals:** Pranam Koro (Prostration), Sapath O-Kroucher (Oath-circle drawing), Nagar-debi'r altar (Town Goddess shrine), invoking Thakur Budhodeb.
3.  **Material Culture:** Dhoti-Gamcha, Shari Bostro (patched clothes), Khottaan (bullocks), Mosool (plough), Dheki (rice pounder), Khapla Jaal (net), Chal-Chhatri (parasol).
4.  **Environment:** Barsha (monsoon), Jhora (canal), Kal-Boishakhi (early-summer storm), Kadam-Kolongcha trees (imagery).
5.  **Social Structure:** Raja, Wazir, Mokaddam (steward), Purohit (priest), Karatadhari (guards), Praja (citizens), Gaan Waala (folk singer).
6.  **Language & Expressions:** Ho-i-jo (Casual assent), Jukto Haat (folded hands), Dhorba na Shona (Don't take, beloved!), Lo-gai-ni (I yield!), Taratali (Immediately), Foi-foich-che (onomatopoeia for bubbling), Ammer jal-kuli-khoi-khaali (winding waterways).
7.  **Divine Concepts:** Shomoshchaan (Omnipresent/God), Shombhu (Shiva), Ishwarer Gaj (God's thunder).
8.  **Folklore Motifs:** Divine retribution using natural phenomena (rain/bubbles), Justice for the poor against powerful injustice, Binding nature of oaths, even misunderstood ones.
