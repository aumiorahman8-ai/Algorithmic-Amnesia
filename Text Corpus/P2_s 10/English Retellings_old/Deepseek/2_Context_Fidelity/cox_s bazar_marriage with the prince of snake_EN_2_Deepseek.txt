Here is a retelling of the Bengali folktale "Biyer Belai" or "Snake Husband," specifically from the Cox's Bazar tradition, preserving its core cultural elements:

**The Tale of Bidya and Nag Kunwar (The Snake Prince) in the Land of Cox's Bazar:**

In a coastal village near **Bagha Kona** (Tiger's Corner - specific to Cox's Bazar lore), lived a poor **Krishto** (Farmer) named **Budhu Mian** with his gentle-hearted daughter, **Bidya**. Their life was simple but difficult, dependent on uncertain harvests and the mercy of the Bay of Bengal.

One blisteringly hot Baishakh afternoon, Bidya ventured deep into their bamboo grove (**Bansh Bon**) to collect fuel sticks (**kathi**). Unknown to her, she disturbed a large, magnificent snake resting in the cool shade near a **Nag Chauras** (sacred snake-ant hill). Startled, the snake bit Bidya on her sunburnt ankle. Bidya cried out in terror and pain.

Heard from afar, Budhu Mian rushed to find his beloved daughter limp and pale beneath the bamboo. Seeing the trail of the snake entering the Chauras, consumed by grief and fear at the thought of losing his **kanna** (young daughter/beloved child), Budhu Mian cried out in desperation:

> **"Hai Allah! Jo iccha (snake) tomar, tomar jat-er shathe tomar beya! Jaanno ke tumi -- tumi ekjon Debta! Ami tomar shonge biye diyechhi amar meyeke! Tomar ichhe hoy, ashben sheye jonno kar bari-e?"**
*(Translation: "Oh Allah! Whatever snake you are, marry someone of your own kind! I know you must be a Deity! I've promised you my daughter in marriage! If you wish, come to collect her at her groom's house?")*

This desperate promise, uttered aloud near the sacred Chauras, echoed exceptionally in that mystical land, reaching unseen ears.

Bidya miraculously recovered swiftly, almost as if unharmed. Days passed. One twilight, after Maghrib prayers, Budhu Mian heard a loud knocking (**ja-ja-kor thok-thok**) on their **uden** (verandah). Outside stood a tall, strikingly handsome **Saheb**, dressed in impossibly fine Dhakai **jamdani**. His eyes held an uncanny depth. He addressed Budhu Mian with profound respect:

> **"Salaam Alaikum, Baba. Abba'r kach theke ami aashchi."**
*(Translation: "Peace be upon you, Father. I come from your Father-in-Law.")*

Budhu Mian was confused. "Father-in-Law? Mine? Who?"

The young man replied, his voice soft but resonant: **"Huzur, mehrbani korey amar shungo ashben? Shakole mon mene niben je. Jhokkol!"**
*(Translation: "Sir, kindly come with me? All will understand. Fear not!")*

Underneath his humble protestations, Budhu Mian felt a strange compulsion. He followed the Saheb deep into the darkest part of the Bansh Bon, near the Nag Chauras. The young man tapped thrice rhythmically (**tinbar taal**) on the earth. The mound shimmered. Then, with a soft sigh like sand loosening, an ornate doorway appeared.

Inside, Budhu Mian was astounded. It was not a dank hole but a glittering **Rajbari** (royal palace)! Bright chambers open to unseen skies (**goyar asman**), walls encrusted with jewels resembling **chokla-fota** gemstones, shimmering curtains woven of coral webs. At the center sat the majestic **Nag Raaj** on a throne of woven pearls, wearing a **pagdi** studded with **nadi-foa** moonstones from Teknaf. By his side sat a serene **Rani**, radiant in jewels. Before them stood the young Saheb.

The Nag Raaj spoke, his voice resonating like distant waves (**duror dohor**):
> **"Kenna Budhu Mian, tumar kanna ke chokkhe dekhechhi. Amar chhoto chheleh holo ei Nurani Shaheb. Tomar obhishekkotha shuni tomar kakhon-er daowa ehhan shothyo kortei hobey. Amar chhele tomari meyeke chay. Tin diner moddhye biyer jonno hamra ashbo tomader airee."**
*(Translation: "Dear Budhu Mian, I have seen your daughter with my own eyes. This is my youngest son, Nurani Shaheb. Hearing your oath sworn at the Chauras, we must make it truth. My son desires your daughter. We will come to your dwelling in three days for the wedding.")*

Stunned and awed, Budhu Mian could only nod. As he returned, clutching the heavy **dolfira** (snake treasure basket filled with gold **mohurs**, rare sea-pearls, and silk), the vision vanished. He was left standing near the Chauras under the starlight.

Three frantic days passed. Budhu Mian knew he could not refuse the **Debtonir** (Deity's) decree. On the appointed evening, eerie lamps lit a path toward their hut. The mist-shrouded wedding procession emerged: finely dressed **Sahebs** and **Bibis** (courtiers/ladies), musicians playing the **kashi-dhol** (shell drum), bearers carrying incredible dowry (the **jor-bhari**, laden with gold, coral strands, starfish brocades). The Nag Kunwar himself arrived, breathtakingly handsome. Bidya, initially terrified, became entranced. Under the guidance of unseen **Kazi** (priest), but performed by the village elders with trembling hands, the **nikah** took place under a **najaal** star ornament symbolizing celestial blessings. The groom garlanded Bidya with a necklace of fragrant **narikel-thotha phool** (coconut blossom), unique to Cox's Bazar lore. Essential offerings (**bhog**) were made to Nag Devata: boiled rice (**bhath**), fragrant milk (**kheer**), dried fish (**sorpati shutki**), vermilion (**sindoor** by Bidya under specific instructions), local **hodoga** sweets, and specially prepared eggs (**khoor-dyar dim**) placed near the household hearth (**chulo**) and **nitantir kon** (the shadowed corner).

Before dawn cracked the sky, the procession started its return. Bidya followed her serpent-husband towards the Chauras. Budhu Mian wept uncontrollably as Bidya turned for a final glimpse, whispering:
> **"Baba, chinta korio na. Amar bor-joyee amader dik-ey attho. Hamra kobujei thakbo, kintu tumar chokk-er samney-e. Pani dewey kandanu kobul koro shey diney..."**
*(Translation: "Father, worry not. My husband's family shelters eternally. We will live hidden, yet right before your eyes. Water the Chauras daily, and accept my tears on that day...")*

Bidya vanished into the Chauras. True to her word, signs appeared: Budhu Mian's fields flourished like coastal monsoon greenery (**barsater shaj**). His hut overflowed with inexplicable abundance. Yet, Budhu Bhalobashar Bidya remained unseen. Daily, with immense devotion (**bhakti**), Budhu Mian performed the **Meghla** ritual: pouring fresh milk (**taratari dudh**) mixed with **turmeric** (**hualdi**) and **sandal paste** (**sandar door**) onto the Nag Chauras before casting fresh blossoms **(bak-phool/narikel-thotha**). And annually, on the Nag Deepaan day (**Nag Panchami**), villagers witnessed the Chauras weep shimmering tears—Bidya's tears—mingling with the offerings, proving her presence and blessing the entire **Para** (neighborhood) with protection from snakebites and abundant rains (**barsha-shampod)**. Bidya became revered as **Chauras-er Bou** (The Bride of the Snake-Mound), worshipped annually along with the Nag Kunwar himself, forever merging the human world and the realm of the Serpent Prince beneath the Cox's Bazar soil.

**Key Preserved Nuances:**

1.  **Kinship Terms:** Baba (Father), Abba (Father-in-law), Kanna/Bety (Young Daughter), Chhele (Son), Bor-Joyee (Husband's Family), Bou (Bride/Daughter-in-law).
2.  **Honorifics & Address:** Huzur (Respectful Sir), Shaheb/Saheb (Lord/Gentleman), Krishot (Farmer), Debta (Deity/Spirit), Kazi (Priest).
3.  **Regional Specifics:** Cox's Bazar setting ("Bagha Kona", coastal imagery "Bay of Bengal", Teknaf moonstones), Chakla folk gemstones ("Chokla-fota"), Rohingya influences ("Nurani Shaheb" hinting at light-skinned/fair), Coastal/Bura Gauria specific plants ("narikel-thotha phool", Bak-phool).
4.  **Rituals & Offerings:** Significance of Nag Chauras, Promise sworn at Chauras, **Meghla** ritual (daily milk offering), Specific **Bhog** (kheer, shutki, kosha dim/khoop-dar dim, sindoor offering), **Nikah** performed ambiguously showing Muslim influence, Nag Panchami observances with literal tears, Use of turmeric/sandalwood/vermillion.
5.  **Spiritual Concepts:** Transformation of snake to human prince, Underground palace (**Rajbari**) accessible through magical means, Binding power of a father's oath sworn to a deity, Coexistence of visible/invisible realms, Allegiance to Nag Devata. Emphasis on **bhakti** (devotion) and **shampodo** (blessing) through proper rituals.
