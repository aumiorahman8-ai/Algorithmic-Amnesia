Based solely on the source text provided, here is the retelling of the story:

*   At the meeting point of the Mathabhanga river, there was a long-standing custom: every merchant vessel passing by was required to offer part of its cargo to the goddess. Regardless of the goods – whether eggplants, radishes, jackfruits, or wood apples – a portion was given.
*   Once, however, a particular merchant not only failed to make any offering but also insulted the goddess. As a direct consequence, his laden boat sank deep into the river.
*   To recover the sunken boat, divers were summoned. They began by lowering ropes to gauge the river's depth. Astonishingly, even after letting out a vast length of rope, they could not reach the bottom. They released more rope, repeatedly, but still couldn't find the riverbed. The immense depth baffled everyone, and no diver volunteered to descend.
*   Finally, one brave man agreed to go down. He was equipped like a diver with ropes and gear.
*   Descending deep into the river, he encountered a remarkable sight: a golden boat. Seated on its deck were two fairies, one on each side, actively pulling on the rope he was attached to, causing it to coil into a huge pile.
*   Fearfully, the diver addressed them: "Mother, I came searching because our boat has sunk. Please forgive me."
*   The fairies responded: "You will get your boat back. But under one condition: you must not tell anyone about what you've seen here. If you reveal this secret, you will vomit blood and die."
*   Additionally, they instructed him: "Go to the Kazis' ghat; you will find your boat there."
*   Indeed, the merchant's boat was later discovered at the Kazis' ghat.
*   Despite the grave warning, the diver couldn't resist telling his wife about his extraordinary experience. The moment he spoke the secret, the curse took effect: he vomited blood and died.
