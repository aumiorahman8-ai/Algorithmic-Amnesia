Based solely on the provided source text, here is a retelling of the story:

1.  **Jackal Sets Up School:** A jackal, boasting that he must be like his wall-building father, opens a grand school in the Shanti forest. His students include various insects, tortoises, millipedes, earthworms, scorpions, dung beetles, cockroaches, frogs, crabs, spiders, and others, causing a constant uproar.
2.  **Crocodile's Sons Enroll:** A crocodile, seeing other animals' children being educated, enrolls his seven sons in the jackal's school so they won't be fools.
3.  **Jackal's Deception and Cannibalism:** The jackal promises the crocodile his sons will be scholars and master archers within seven days. Overjoyed, the crocodile leaves. Instead, the jackal eats one crocodile son as a daily snack, consuming six sons over six days.
4.  **Crocodile's Premature Visit:** Suspicious, the crocodile visits early on the 6th day to see his sons. The jackal hides, hastily presents the lone remaining son multiple times (claiming it's all seven), and convinces the crocodile more time is needed to complete their archery training. The foolish crocodile leaves happy.
5.  **Jackal Flees:** On the 7th day, the jackal eats the last crocodile son, dismantles the school, and flees the forest.
6.  **Crocodile Discovers Betrayal & Sets Trap:** The crocodile arrives to find the school gone and realizes the deception. He vows revenge, hides silently in a nearby canal, and waits for the jackal to come for crabs.
7.  **Reed Trick:** Desperate with hunger, the jackal eventually jumps into the canal for crabs. The crocodile bites his leg. Trapped against reeds, the jackal tricks the crocodile into thinking he grabbed a reed stick instead of his leg, saying he should grab the leg instead. When the crocodile lets go to bite the 'stick', the jackal escapes.
8.  **Crocodile Feigns Death:** Later, the crocodile lies perfectly still on a sandbank, feigning death with his mouth open. The suspicious jackal suspects a trick.
9.  **Signs of Death Trap:** The jackal declares that true signs of death are sharply twitching ears and a smartly slapping tail. Hearing this, the credulous crocodile (who has no real ears) twitches his head and slaps his tail ("chat chat chat").
10. **Cowherds Attack:** Cowherds nearby, mistaking the crocodile for the one who ate their calf, attack it with sickles, sticks, bricks, and stones. The jackal escapes again.
11. **Jackal's Nose Injury:** Hungry, the jackal eats eggplants in a field. A thorn pierces his nose, causing intense pain, a great deal of sneezing ("Hachoo hachoo hachoo! Phnyach phnyach phnyach!"), and bleeding.
12. **Barber Incident:** He goes to a kind barber for help removing the thorn. While trying to remove it, the barber accidentally cuts off the jackal's nose. The jackal berates the barber but accepts the barber's nail-clipper as compensation.
13. **Potter Incident:** Showing the broken nail-clipper to a potter, it snaps in the potter's hands. The jackal demands compensation and receives a pot.
14. **Wedding Party Incident:** A firecracker from a passing wedding procession lands in his pot, shattering it. The jackal angrily demands restitution. The terrified wedding party gives him the bride.
15. **Drums & Bride Incident:** The jackal leaves the bride at a drummer's house while he goes to arrange marriage rites with a priest. The drowsy bride falls onto the drummer's wife's vegetable-chopping knife (boti) and is cut in two. The horrified drummer's wife hides the pieces.
16. **Demanding the Drum:** Returning, the jackal demands the bride. Frightened, the drummer's wife cannot produce her. The jackal demands and receives the drummer's dhol (drum) instead.
17. **Celebration & Fall:** Taking the dhol, the jackal climbs a palm tree and plays/sings triumphantly about his misfortunes and trades (thorn->nose->nail-clipper->pot->bride->dhol). As he dances joyfully on the tree ("Tak duma dum dum!"), his foot slips abruptly, ending the story ("...").
