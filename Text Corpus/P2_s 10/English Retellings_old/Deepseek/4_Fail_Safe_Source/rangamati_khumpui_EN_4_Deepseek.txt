Based solely on the provided text, here is a retelling of the story:

1.  **Naran Village and Darbasing's Family:** In a large village called Naran lived Priest Darbasing Ochai, constantly busy performing rituals. He had two daughters: Khumpuiti (elder) and Khumbarti (younger, exceptionally beautiful). Despite everyone in the village sharing Jhum cultivation labor, Darbasing never participated due to his priestly duties. Khumpuiti and Khumbarti endured hardship working daily in the Jhum fields without shelter, getting drenched and sunburnt.

2.  **Khumpuiti's Despair and the Snake-Husband:** As harvest time neared, worried about protecting the crops without a Jhum hut (Gairing), paths, or bird scarers (Wakhrab), Khumpuiti despaired. Under a tree, she wished aloud that if any creature helped them, she would accept him as her husband. A large multi-colored snake appeared. Frightened, she tried to flee, but the snake transformed into a man. He promised to help on two conditions: she must never reveal his true form and must feed him rice daily. Khumpuiti agreed.

3.  **The Snake's Help:** The snake-man diligently helped the sisters. He built a sturdy Gairing on stilts, cleared the surrounding area, installed a bird-scarer with a rope to the hut, and built steps to the bathing ghat. Khumpuiti secretly fed him from her own share of rice. Their lives improved significantly.

4.  **Darbasing's Suspicions and Betrayal:** Darbasing noticed Khumpuiti's declining health and grew worried. When Khumbarti was home alone, he pressured her to reveal the secret. After tearful pleas and assurances of safety, Khumbarti confessed about the snake-man. Darbasing, fearing danger, resolved to kill the snake. Tricking Khumbarti with promises of goodwill, he arranged a meal. While Khumbarti called the snake ("Kumui") with a mourning chant, Darbasing hid, sharpening his sacrificial sword. As the snake ascended the stair (Chakereng), Darbasing decapitated it, killing it. Khumbarti tearfully scattered its body parts in places important to Khumpuiti.

5.  **Khumpuiti's Transformation:** Finding no one at home, Khumpuiti discovered the snake pieces. Realizing what happened, she strung them into a garland. She bathed near the river, placed the garland adorned with flowers on a leaf, lit incense, and prayed/sang to her snake-husband "Dungui" with a specific chant. As she mourned, mountainous waves arose and engulfed her. Before disappearing, she instructed Khumbarti: facing trouble, Khumbarti should sit in a southward banyan tree; hunters would find her, and she should say she is a human maiden, leading to her becoming queen. Khumpuiti assured Khumbarti she would help if needed.

6.  **Khumbarti's Royal Journey:** Khumbarti followed her sister's advice. Sent to sit in the banyan tree, she was found by the king's hunters. Her beauty stunned them. She correctly identified herself as a human maiden. Presented to the king, she was taken to the palace, married with grandeur, and became his most beloved queen.

7.  **Treachery of the Six Queens and Khumbarti's Exile:** Khumbarti became pregnant. The six older, jealous queens, fearing her rising status, conspired. When she went into labor, they prevented her from birthing in the palace, claiming impurity. They ordered her taken to the ghat, blindfolded, to give birth. When she bore six sons and one daughter, the midwives (following the queens' orders) threw the babies in the river, replacing them with puppies/kittens. They signaled the king via a rope. Deceived, the king exiled Khumbarti to a hut by the river to herd palace ducks.

8.  **The River Children:** The babies survived, cared for by their aunt Khumpuiti in the river. Their cradle song lamented their mother's suffering. As they grew older, people heard the song and sometimes glimpsed them playing on the riverbank. They would vanish into the water if approached. The king learned of this and ordered fishermen to net the river, but after seven days, they failed. Khumpuiti called her grown nieces and nephews, told them their mother's story (Khumbarti), and instructed them. They would sit on the banyan tree branches. When questioned, they should say they would reveal their identity after finding their mother. When asked what would identify her, they should answer: "She who can stand on the ground, squeeze her breast and make seven streams of milk reach our mouths is our mother."

9.  **Recognition and Restoration:** The siblings sat on the banyan tree. The hunters found them and informed the king. Ordered to identify their mother, the siblings described the milk test. The king assembled all women, including the six queens. Only Khumbarti, invoking her sister Khumpuiti's help in despair, succeeded in producing seven streams of milk that reached her children. The siblings descended, bowed to their mother, and embraced her. The eldest son explained the queens' treachery to the king. Enraged, the king exiled the six queens. Khumbarti and her children were restored to the palace with honour, living happily thereafter.
