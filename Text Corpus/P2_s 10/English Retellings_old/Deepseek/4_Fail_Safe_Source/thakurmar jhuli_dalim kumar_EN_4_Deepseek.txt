Based solely on the source text, here is a retelling of the story:

A king lived with his queen and prince. The queen's life resided inside a pair of dice. An ogress dwelling in the palace's palm tree knew this secret but lacked an opportunity. When the king went hunting and the prince was playing dice with friends, the ogress disguised herself as a beggar woman and asked the prince for the dice. Unaware, the prince gave them to her. The ogress blew on the dice three times, banishing them to an unknown place, causing the queen to faint. The ogress then ate the queen and assumed her form.

Upon the king's return, the impostor queen acted normally. Only the prince noticed a drop of saliva fall from her tongue while serving food, chilling him. He stopped eating and silently left.

Years passed. The king and the false queen had seven more sons. Only the prince noticed the palm tree withering and avoided by birds. As the younger princes grew up and underwent rites, they decided to travel. The king summoned the eldest prince. He joined them, and all eight brothers set off.

The ogress-queen, seeing them leave, panicked. She consulted Sutashankha, a snake in her casket, demanding to know where the eldest prince's life resided. Sutashankha revealed it lay within a pomegranate seed. The ogress instructed Sutashankha to fly with a message to Pashabati demanding her seven daughters for her seven sons and to devour the prince if he came for the dice. Sutashankha flew off, stopping exhausted in a garden fruit. A princess ate the fruit, swallowing Sutashankha and the message.

The ogress then cast a spell to send her winged horse (Pakshiraj) to Pashabati's kingdom. She sealed the pomegranate seed containing the prince's life beneath a crack in the palace's thousand steps. Simultaneously, this caused the eldest prince travelling with his brothers to cry out that a scorpion had bitten him, blaming his blindness. In the ensuing storm, he vanished.

The seven brothers reached Pashabati's city. Pashabati challenged them to dice: winners would marry her and her sisters, losers would be eaten. The brothers claimed not to be demons and lacked any missive (unbeknownst to them, inside the princess). They played, lost, and were eaten by Pashabati's sisters.

The blind prince, carried by Pakshiraj, fell separated from the horse. He regained consciousness in a strange city experiencing daily horror: a ceremonial elephant chose a new king each day (crowned amid celebration), only for him to vanish overnight, leaving only bones in the princess's chamber. The state elephant chose the blind prince as king. That night, he stayed awake. As the princess slept, a hair-thin snake emerged from her nose, growing into a huge, roaring python. The prince, swinging his sword blindly, struck and killed it (Sutashankha), regaining his sight in the process. Simultaneously:
*   The thousand palace steps crumbled.
*   The prince's life-force grew into a golden pomegranate tree.
*   The palace shook violently.
*   The ogress fled as a mouse.
*   The real queen's body reappeared.

The people, finding the Sutashankha's pieces and the message inside, understood it had eaten past kings. The prince, upon reading the message about his brothers, left immediately on Pakshiraj (which revived when he touched it on a mountain).

He crossed perilous lands (tangible darkness, stone wind, cowrie mountain, bone mountain, river of blood) to reach Pashabati's city. Playing dice with Pashabati, he recognized the dice as his own and saw a mouse manipulating them. He lost once, forfeiting Pakshiraj (which was eaten). Returning with a kitten the next day, the mouse fled upon seeing it. The prince won using an incantation. He won back Pakshiraj, then played repeatedly, winning back his seven brothers, their horses, the kingdom, the house, the city, and the dice. Finally, demanding the mouse, he released the kitten, which caught and ate the mouse. The lamp extinguished, the seven sisters turned into dead earthworms, and the kingdom vanished.

Taking the dice, the eight princes returned home. The awakened real queen asked for her prince. The triumphant princes returned, greeted her, and restored joy. Meanwhile, the people from the night-king's land found their king (the prince) and moved their court to his kingdom.

Finally, the withered palm tree fell and shattered.
