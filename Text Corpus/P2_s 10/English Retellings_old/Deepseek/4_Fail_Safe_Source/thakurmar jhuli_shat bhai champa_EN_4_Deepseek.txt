Based solely on the provided source text, here is a retelling:

A king loved his youngest queen best among his seven queens because she was gentle, unlike the arrogant elder queens. Despairing over having no children to inherit his vast kingdom, the king rejoiced when the youngest queen became pregnant. He opened his treasury for public celebration and tied a golden chain binding himself to the queen, instructing her to tug it when the child was born so he could come.

Consumed by jealousy, the elder queens volunteered to attend the birth. Before any child was born, they twice falsely tugged the chain, angering the king, who threatened to behead them if tugged again without a birth. The youngest queen gave birth to seven sons and a daughter, beautiful "like dolls made of moonlight." When she asked to see them, the elder queens mocked her, claiming she birthed vermin instead. They caused her to faint.

The elder queens did not tug the chain. They secretly placed the infants in earthen pots, buried them in an ash heap, and *then* tugged the chain. When the king arrived with celebration, they showed him tadpoles and baby mice. Enraged, the king banished the youngest queen. She became a dung-gathering maid wandering the streets.

The king and his kingdom grew desolate. Months later, the king's gardener reported blooming flowers fit for worship on Champa and Parul trees growing on the ash heap. When the gardener approached, the Parul flower called out "Seven Champa brothers, wake up!" The Champa flowers then refused to let the gardener pick them, saying they would rise higher unless the king came personally.

The king arrived. Again, the flowers refused him, demanding the eldest queen. One by one, each elder queen was summoned, but the flowers refused them all, rising higher each time. Finally, the flowers demanded the "dung-gathering maid."

Brought back in a palanquin, the youngest queen, filthy and ragged, reached for the flowers. Instantly, the flowers floated down from the sky. From them emerged seven princes and a princess, calling "Mother!" and clinging to her. Everyone was stunned. The king wept. The elder queens trembled in fear.

The king ordered the elder queens buried in a thorn-filled pit. He then took the princes, princess Parul, and the youngest queen back to the palace amidst victory celebrations.
