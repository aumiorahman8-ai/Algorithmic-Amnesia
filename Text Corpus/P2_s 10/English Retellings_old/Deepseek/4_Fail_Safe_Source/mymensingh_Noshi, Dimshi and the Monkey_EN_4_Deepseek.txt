Based solely on the source text, here is the retelling of the story:

Two sisters, Nasi and Dimsi, were walking along a deserted road. They heard a faint voice calling out but ignored it and kept walking. After going a little further, they heard it again: "Hey Nasi, hey Dimsi, will you marry me?" Dimsi remarked that someone seemed to be talking to them. They looked around but saw no one.

They heard the question repeated twice more as they walked. After the third time, they spotted a monkey jumping from tree to tree, following them and playing tricks while hiding. They paid little attention to the monkey.

The monkey continued to follow them, trying to tempt them with promises like "If you marry me, I'll give you this, I'll give you that, I'll give you a water pot."

Eventually, the sisters came to a wide, deep canal they couldn't cross. Worried, they told the monkey, "If you can get us across this canal, one of us will marry you."

Delighted, the monkey swam across to test it and declared he could do it, but they must cross one at a time. He instructed one sister to climb on his back and hold tight while he swam her across, then return for the other. He succeeded in carrying both sisters across with great effort.

After crossing, the sisters reneged on their promise, telling the monkey, "Who would marry a monkey like you? We only said we'd marry you to get across the canal." The monkey's hopes were dashed, but he persisted in following them.

Later, seeing a woodpecker's nest high in a tree and hearing chicks inside, the sisters wanted the baby birds. This time they promised the monkey they would marry him for certain if he retrieved them. The monkey asked for confirmation ("Really?") and received it ("Really.").

The monkey climbed the tall tree. Reaching the nest hole, he stuck his hand inside to get the chicks. When he delayed coming down, the sisters asked why. The monkey replied he needed to "count the chicks first." After a long wait, they inquired again. Still counting "This one is for Nasi, this one is for Dimsi," he delayed longer.

Growing impatient and annoyed at the continued delay as the monkey kept allocating chicks ("This one for Nasi, this one for Dimsi"), the sisters gave up and said, "Forget it! We don't need the baby birds anymore. We're leaving."

Distressed at hearing they were leaving and desperate to prevent his marriage dream from failing, the monkey forced his hand out of the hole. However, it was caught tight. He pulled forcefully, but it wouldn't budge. Exerting all his might with extreme force, his arm tore off from his body.

The severed arm remained stuck in the hole. The monkey himself was flung down from the tree and landed in thick mud below. Once in the mud, he found himself trapped, unable to get out no matter how hard he tried.
