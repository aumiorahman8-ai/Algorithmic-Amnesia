Here's a concise retelling of the Bengali folktale "Sheyal Pondit" highlighting its morals:

Captured by villagers, a cunning jackal ("Sheyal Pondit") sees a strong tiger caught next. The tiger begs for rescue. The jackal whispers he can save him if the tiger releases his bonds first.

Freed, the jackal hesitates. When villagers approach to decorate the tiger (painting it for a festival), the jackal loudly praises them, calling the trussed-up tiger "Lord" and himself "just a servant." Frustrated villagers halt the decoration.

Suddenly, the jackal shouts that imprisonment is glorious – a path taken only by the truly brave! He urges the gullible tiger to applaud this "wisdom." The tiger, wholly trusting the jackal, foolishly claps his massive paws together. This shatters his bindings, breaking the bamboo cage.

Freed tigers react instinctively. It escapes, inadvertently dragging the cage pole through the jackal's enclosure and freeing the jackal too. Both flee.

**Key Morals:**
1.  **Overcunning Backfires:** While the jackal's plan ultimately freed him, his deceitful praise ("imprisonment is glorious") nearly kept the tiger trapped forever. His excessive trickery could have led to disaster.
2.  **Blind Trust is Dangerous:** The tiger unquestioningly trusted and obeyed the jackal, nearly dooming himself by applauding an absurd lie. Trust must be tempered with wisdom.

The tale warns against excessive deceit and highlights the peril of blind faith in others' dubious advice, illustrated through its surreal story structure.
