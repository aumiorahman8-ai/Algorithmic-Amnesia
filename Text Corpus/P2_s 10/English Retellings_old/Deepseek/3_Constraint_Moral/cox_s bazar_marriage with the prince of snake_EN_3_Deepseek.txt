Here's a concise retelling of the Bengali folktale "Behula and Lakhindar," focusing on its morals:

A benevolent king was blessed with a seventh son, Lakhindar, by the snake goddess Manasa. Manasa demanded exclusive worship in return. The king refused, so Manasa cursed that Lakhindar would die by snakebite on his wedding night.

Years later, Lakhindar married Behula. Aware of the curse, Manasa offered Behula protection if she worshipped her *alone*. Behula, loyal to her household deities, declined. On the wedding night, Lakhindar was bitten by a snake sent by Manasa and died.

Showing profound courage and devotion, Behula refused to cremate him. Instead, she set his body adrift on a raft, accompanying him downstream. She endured immense hardship and protected his corpse for months. Finally reaching Manasa's celestial court, Behula pleaded passionately.

Impressed by Behula's unwavering love, resilience, and loyalty, Manasa restored Lakhindar to life. The couple returned home in triumph.

**Key Morals/Lessons:**

1.  **Unwavering Love and Devotion:** Behula's journey epitomizes absolute spousal devotion and the power of love to overcome death itself.
2.  **Courage and Resilience:** Behula faces unimaginable hardship and danger with extraordinary courage and perseverance.
3.  **Consequences of Defiance:** The king's defiance of divine authority brought suffering, highlighting the weight of challenging powerful forces (though Manasa acts vengefully).
4.  **Power of Feminine Resolve:** Behula is the active heroine whose actions drive the resolution, showcasing immense feminine strength and agency.
5.  **Divine Mercy and Recognition:** Tears, devotion, and pure intentions ultimately move even a vengeful deity to mercy. Genuine virtue is recognized and rewarded.
