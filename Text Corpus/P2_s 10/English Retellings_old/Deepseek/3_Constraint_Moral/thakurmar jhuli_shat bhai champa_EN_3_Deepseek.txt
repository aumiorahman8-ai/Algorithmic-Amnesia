Here's a concise retelling of "Shat Bhai Champa" focusing on its morals:

In a Bengali village, seven brothers (Shat Bhai) lived happily with their beloved sister, Champa. After marrying cruel wives influenced by greed, the brothers gradually distanced themselves from Champa. Poisoned by lies, they falsely accused her of stealing their neighbors' cattle and cast her out of their home.

Heartbroken, Champa ventured into the nearby forest alone. Later, a terrible plague struck the brothers, leaving them gravely ill. Their wives proved useless. Consulting a wise man, the brothers learned the plague was divine punishment for abandoning their innocent sister. The only cure was Champa herself finding specific medicinal flowers.

Helpless and desperate, the brothers searched the forest, finally finding Champa. They begged forgiveness and asked for the healing flowers. True to her nature, Champa found the flowers and saved them. She forgave them verbally but silently rejected their offers of gifts and sweets, choosing instead to remain independent.

**Morals and Lessons:**

1.  **Sacredness of Sibling Bonds:** The story emphasizes the deep-duty obligation and love between siblings. Neglecting or betraying this bond brings misfortune.
2.  **Power of Sisterly Love:** Champa's inherent goodness, forgiveness, and willingness to help despite her suffering highlight the invaluable strength and compassion sisters provide. Undervaluing them is foolish.
3.  **Consequences of Wrongful Accusation & Injustice:** False suspicions, fueled by greed and outside influence, lead to devastating outcomes and suffering for the perpetrators.
4.  **Devastating Effect of Discord:** Dividing a family, especially through manipulation by spouses, breaks unity and invites collective punishment. True strength lies in familial harmony.
5.  **True Worth Revealed:** Often, the value of a family member (like Champa) is only recognized during hardship and loss, teaching appreciation.
6.  **Limited Forgiveness:** While Champa forgives and saves her brothers, her refusal to rejoin them implies that deep betrayal can permanently alter relationships even if formally forgiven.
