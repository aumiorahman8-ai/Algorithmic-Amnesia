Here's a concise retelling of "Kolaboti Rajkonna" focusing on its morals:

A kind yet impoverished young woman finds a magical grain basket. When hit with a stick, it summons *Kolaboti Rajkonna* – a magnificent goose who lays golden feathers daily. Instructed never to pluck a feather, the woman gathers the freely shed feathers, sells them, and achieves prosperity. Her secretive nature, however, breeds envy in her greedy stepmother/sister-in-law (*Nanad*).

Spying, the vain *Nanad* witnesses the goose and demands it. Jealous, she disobeys the golden rule. Thinking she'll gain immense wealth faster, she forcefully plucks a feather from the sleeping Kolaboti. Instantly, the plucked feather turns worthless, the goose vanishes forever, and the greedy woman receives neither gold nor blessings. The original kind woman retains her hard-earned gains but loses her magical helper due to another's selfishness.

**Key Morals/Lessons:**

1.  **Heed Wise Instructions:** Disobeying clear rules leads to downfall (The *Nanad* loses everything by plucking).
2.  **Kindness Brings Reward:** Humility and patience are rewarded with sustainable blessings (Gathering shed feathers).
3.  **Greed Destroys:** Ruthless ambition to possess more destroys good fortune (Plucking ruins everything).
4.  **Sustain, Don't Exploit:** Respecting sources of prosperity ensures their longevity; exploiting them ensures their loss. True wealth comes through care and moderation.
5.  **Envy is Destructive:** Jealousy leads to harmful actions that backfire on everyone involved.

The story contrasts sustainable generosity with destructive greed, showing how patience and respecting boundaries lead to lasting good, while envy and exploitation inevitably lead to ruin. **(Word Count: 230)**
