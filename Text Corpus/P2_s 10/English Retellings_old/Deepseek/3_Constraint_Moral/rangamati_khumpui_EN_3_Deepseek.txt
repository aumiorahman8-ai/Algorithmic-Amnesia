Here's a concise retelling of the Bengali folktale "Khumpui" focusing on its moral lesson:

Khumpui, a beautiful but arrogant princess, constantly belittled her stepmother for stealing small items like grains of rice or lentils—quirks Khumpui deemed unbecoming of a queen. The stepmother bore this silently. One day, palace treasures began vanishing. Khumpui, quick to accuse her stepmother despite a lack of proof, convinced her father, the king, to exile the queen.

Years later, Khumpui, now queen herself in another kingdom, discovered her gardener secretly uprooting valuable flowering plants overnight. When confronted, the bewildered gardener confessed he suffered from an uncontrollable compulsion to steal plants he admired, unaware of his actions. This revelation struck Khumpui like lightning. She finally understood her stepmother's earlier "thefts" weren't malicious acts of greed or poor character, but a similar, involuntary condition—an uncontrollable need.

**Key Lessons/Morals:**
1.  **Avoid Hasty Judgment:** Don't condemn others' flaws or differences without understanding the *reason* behind their actions. Khumpui judged her stepmother harshly based on appearances and perceived weakness.
2.  **Compassion Over Contempt:** Show kindness and empathy towards people's struggles, especially those they cannot control. Mocking or punishing someone for an inherent compulsion is cruel.
3.  **The Danger of Arrogance:** Khumpui's pride blinded her. She assumed her stepmother stole out of base desire, failing to consider deeper, hidden causes.
4.  **Regret of Premature Action:** Acting rashly based on prejudice leads to irreversible consequences and deep regret, as Khumpui experienced upon realizing her error.

The core message is **empathy and understanding triumph over judgment and punishment**, urging us to look beyond surface flaws with compassion before passing sentence.
