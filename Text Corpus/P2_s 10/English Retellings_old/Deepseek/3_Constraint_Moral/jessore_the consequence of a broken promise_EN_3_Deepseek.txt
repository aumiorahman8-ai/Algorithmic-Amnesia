Here's the Bengali folktale "The Consequence of a Broken Promise" (<250 words), focusing on the lessons:

A poor fisherman lived humbly with his wife. One day, he released a huge fish caught in his net after she pleaded, revealing herself as a magical fish-mother. Deeply grateful, she granted him wealth secretly: gold coins would appear daily at home while he pretended to fish. Her condition: **absolute secrecy about her existence.**

Life became abundant. However, consumed by greed and unable to bear secrecy, his wife relentlessly demanded the source of their fortune. Finally, worn down, the fisherman revealed the truth about the fish-mother – **breaking his sacred promise.**

Instantly, the spell shattered. The gold vanished. The fisherman returned to sea, catching nothing but the very fish-mother whose trust he betrayed. She wept, stating: _"Abbadhani dile mile dhan! Abadhani korile khan!"_ (For keeping your promise, riches flowed. For breaking it, penance you'll know!).

He knew then: **trust broken cannot be regained, and greed destroys blessings.** Reduced to lifelong poverty for his betrayal, his fate served as a stark warning. The tale teaches:

1.  **The Sanctity of Promises:** Breaking a vow, especially one born of gratitude, invites dire consequences.
2.  **Trust is Paramount:** Betraying trust destroys relationships and blessings.
3.  **Greed Begets Loss:** Unsatisfied greed leads to the destruction of existing prosperity.
4.  **Contentment Over Covetousness:** The wife's inability to be content with abundance directly caused their downfall.
