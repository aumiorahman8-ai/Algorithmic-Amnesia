Here's a concise retelling of "Morar Shakkhi," focusing on its core morals:

A lazy boy, tasked with protecting a vegetable field from birds, repeatedly cries "Morar Shakkhi!" ("Witnesses are coming!") just for the thrill of seeing people rush to his aid unnecessarily. He does this multiple times, enjoying the commotion his false alarm causes and sometimes tricking adults into giving him sweets.

When a *real* thief finally comes and plunders the field, the boy desperately shouts the alarm, genuinely needing help. However, the villagers, having been tricked so many times before, ignore him, dismissing his cries as another prank. They sarcastically tell him to call upon the "bubbles" (*shakkhi* sounds like the Bengali word for bubbles, *shakki*) as his false witnesses. The distraught boy realizes his mistake too late as the thief escapes with the harvest.

**Key Morals/Lessons:**

1.  **The Peril of Lies:** Repeated lying destroys trust. Once you become known as a liar, people won't believe you even when you finally tell the truth.
2.  **Consequences of Crying Wolf:** Creating false alarms for fun or gain trivializes real dangers and exhausts the goodwill and readiness of others to help.
3.  **Responsibility and Reliability:** Failing in your duties through laziness or deception harms not only yourself but also those relying on you.
4.  **Trust is Fragile:** Building trust takes time and integrity, but it can be easily shattered by dishonesty, leaving you isolated in times of genuine need. The mocking reference to "bubbles" underscores how empty and worthless falsehoods ultimately are.
