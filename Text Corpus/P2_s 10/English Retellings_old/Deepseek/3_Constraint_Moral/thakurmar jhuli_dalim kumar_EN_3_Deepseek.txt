Here's the core story and morals of the Bengali folktale "Dalim Kumar" in under 250 words:

**Summary:**
Prince Dalim Kumar offends a sage who curses him to live among the *Chandals* (traditionally considered outcasts) and forbids uttering the word "mango." Years pass. Dalim suffers silently, enduring harsh conditions and prejudice without complaining or revealing his royal past. One scorching day, filled with thirst and exhaustion, he finally cries out, "If only I had a *mango*!" Instantly, the curse breaks. His magnificent royal robes reappear. Panicked, the Chandals beg forgiveness. Dalim Kumar, revealing his true identity, pardons them kindly before returning to his kingdom.

**Key Morals/Lessons:**

1.  **The Power of Endurance & Acceptance:** Dalim Kumar's primary merit is silently enduring his undeserved suffering without bitterness or complaint. His stoic acceptance is crucial to his ultimate redemption.
2.  **Obedience to Commands/Warnings:** The story emphasizes the absolute importance of adhering to prohibitions laid down by powerful figures (sages/deities). Disobedience brings immediate consequences.
3.  **Patience in Suffering:** His long wait demonstrates that suffering, endured patiently, can be a transformative test leading to eventual liberation or reward. Quick fixes weren't an option.
4.  **Consequences of Breaking Vows:** The curse activated instantly upon uttering the forbidden word, showing the strict penalty for breaking restrictions.
5.  **Grace & Forgiveness:** Despite his hardships, Dalim Kumar pardons the Chandals upon his restoration, highlighting compassion even for those who unknowingly contributed to his suffering.

The folktale serves as a lesson in humility, patience in adversity, strict adherence to vows, and the value of forgiveness. Suffering, it suggests, can be a necessary trial leading to liberation if borne with unwavering fortitude.
