Here's the Bengali folktale "Morar Shakkhi" (Witness of the Bubbles), emphasizing Shiva's playful wisdom and the triumph of faith:

**The Core Conflict:**
In a rural Bengali village, neighbors named **Makardhwaj** (greedy and cunning) and **Premanand** (poor, pious, and gentle) shared the rights to a precious village pond ("dera"). Makardhwaj, driven by avarice, concocted a cruel plan to steal Premanand's share entirely.

**The Theft & Fake Deed:**
One night, when Premanand was away, Makardhwaj secretly removed Premanand's marking stake and boundary stones ("ganja"). Crucially, Makardhwaj also stole Premanand's valid deed proving his rights. To replace it, he forged a new deed naming himself as the sole owner.

**Premanand's Plight:**
Premanand returned to find his share usurped and his deed missing. Devastated and helpless, he approached the village council ("panchayat") for justice. Makardhwaj confidently presented his forged deed as proof of sole ownership. Premanand had no written evidence left. The panchayat, swayed by the document, seemed poised to rule against the honest man.

**The Mysterious Milkman:**
In his utter despair and injustice, Premanand cried out fervently to Lord Shiva, his protector. Just then, a tall, ash-smeared **sadhu** appeared, seemingly a rustic milkman selling curds (**madar**). This unassuming figure was none other than Lord Shiva himself in disguise. He approached Premanand.

**Shiva's Intervention & The Plan:**
The disguised Shiva listened kindly to Premanand's woes. He then revealed a stunning claim: **He was Morar Shakkhi** – an eyewitness (*sakshi*) to the boundaries of the pond (*mor*)! Furthermore, he declared he had *seen* Premanand's rightful share before it was stolen.

Shiva instructed Premanand to inform the panchayat that he had an eyewitness ("morar shakkhi") ready to testify.

**The Outrageous Strategy & The Test:**
When brought before the panchayat, "Madan" (Shiva) confidently asserted:
1.  He was indeed "Morar Shakkhi", witness to the pond.
2.  He had personally *seen* Premanand's boundary.
3.  Crucially, he argued that the water of the pond itself ("pukur-jol") had **witnessed** the boundary and the theft.

He proposed a seemingly ludicrous plan: To prove his testimony, let Premanand stand by the pond holding the **blank** page that was meant to be his deed (despite having his original deed stolen, perhaps another copy or the *holder* existed), while Makardhwaj holds his forged deed. Then...

**The Miracle:**
Shiva/Madan declared: "Dip both documents into the pond's water! The genuine claim will float; the false one will sink! Let the water, the eternal witness, decide!"

Naturally, Makardhwaj, confident in the physical deed he held, quickly agreed, certain Premanand's blank paper could never "win". Premanand, acting purely on blind faith in the holy man, dipped his blank sheet into the water. Makardhwaj dipped his forged deed.

**Bubbles of Truth:**
As Makardhwaj's forged deed touched the water, it instantly bubbled and sank like a stone! Premanand's blank paper, however, floated lightly on the surface. The panchayat recognized the impossible event as divine intervention. They investigated and discovered Makardhwaj's theft and forgery.

**Justice & Moral:**
The verdict was swift:
1.  Premanand was restored his rightful share of the pond.
2.  Makardhwaj was exposed as a thief and fraudster, facing disgrace and punishment.
Shiva vanished shortly after, leaving both faith and justice affirmed.

**The Significance of "Morar Shakkhi":**
*   **Shiva's Playfulness:** It highlights Shiva's "Atharva" nature – appearing as a rustic milkman and using water bubbles ("mor") for justice. Truth doesn't always need conventional proof; divine justice works mysteriously.
*   **Faith Test:** Premanand's seemingly hopeless faith ("shraddha") in prayer and the holy man was rewarded miraculously.
*   **Truth Prevails:** Though false evidence temporarily dominates, ultimate truth ("satya") prevails unexpectedly.
*   **Natural Justice:** Water ("jal"), considered sacred and truthful, became the agent of justice, witnessed by bubbles ("mor").
*   **Reward & Punishment:** Goodness guarded (Premanand), evil exposed (Makardhwaj).

The tale endures as a beloved reminder that faith and truth, even when seemingly unsupported, have divine protectors capable of orchestrating surprisingly simple miracles against overwhelming odds.
