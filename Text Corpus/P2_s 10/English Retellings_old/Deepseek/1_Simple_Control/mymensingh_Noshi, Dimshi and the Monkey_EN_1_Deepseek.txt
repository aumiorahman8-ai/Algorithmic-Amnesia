Here's the classic Bengali folktale of **Noshi, Dimshi, and the Monkey**, capturing its essence of cleverness and trickery:

**The Tale:**

1.  **Grandma's Boast:** Once upon a time, there lived an old grandmother named **Noshi** and her sharp-witted little grandson, **Dimshi**. One day, Noshi was cooking something, perhaps rice or lentils. Dimshi, ever curious and observant, commented, "Grandma, why are you cooking so little? It hardly seems enough!"
2.  **Noshi's Claim:** Perhaps slightly offended or just playful, Grandma Noshi retorted, "You silly child! Do you think I cook only this tiny bit? Oh no! Today I am cooking *so* much that the cooks won't even be able to climb down from the stove!" She described an impossibly huge feast prepared by divine cooks appearing magically! ("*Aaj ami eto khana ranna korbo je rannyra chulathe uthe parbe na!*")
3.  **Dimshi's Doubt:** Dimshi, being the clever boy he was, wasn't fooled easily. He frowned skeptically and demanded proof: "Hee! Really, Grandma? Tell me the truth! Are you telling lies?"
4.  **The Setup:** Smiling knowingly, Noshi leaned in conspiratorially. "Alright, clever boy! If you want proof, come outside to the big tree near the water pump *(Chhakibandar dusta gachh)* tonight. Hide yourself very carefully and watch. Just before midnight, *all the cooks* will come down from the cooking pot here and go to wash their hands at that pump! You'll see how many there are!"
5.  **The Trap:** What Noshi *didn't* tell Dimshi was her secret plan. She knew the household's pet monkey, a notoriously greedy creature, was especially fond of boiled chickpeas *(chhola bhaja)*.
6.  **Baiting the Monkey:** Just before the appointed time, Noshi took a pot of freshly boiled, piping hot chickpeas. She crept outside and poured them all over the trunk and roots of the large tree near the water pump.
7.  **The Monkey Takes the Bait:** Soon after, the hungry monkey, smelling the irresistible aroma, came scrambling towards the tree. Seeing the scattered chickpeas, it eagerly started grabbing handfuls. But the chickpeas were scalding hot! "Oh! Hot! Hot!" The monkey would cry out, drop them quickly ("*Aah! Garam garam!*"), shake its burnt hand ("*Haath phele dile*"), flap its ears ("*Pa na phirye dile*"), and even hop around in pain. Yet, unable to resist the tasty treat, it would dive back in immediately for more, repeating the frantic cycle: grab, scream, drop, shake, flap, hop, grab again...
8.  **Dimshi's Mistaken Observation:** Hidden in the shadows, Dimshi watched this frantic scene unfold in the moonlight. All he saw was rapid, chaotic movement – quick flashes of a figure grabbing, tossing, jerking, moving erratically around the base of the tree and water pump. From his hiding spot in the dark, amidst steam or dust kicked up, it looked *exactly* like a whole group of busy cooks tossing things down, washing hands wildly, and bustling about!
9.  **"Proof" Accepted:** With eyes wide in amazement, Dimshi whispered to himself, "Ohh! Grandma wasn't lying! Look at all those cooks! So many! They truly can't stay on the stove – they're washing their hands frantically! Hurry up down the tree!" He saw his "proof" clear as day: a multitude of cooks bustling at the pump.
10. **The Revelation & Wisdom:** Dimshi rushed back to Noshi, bursting with newfound respect. "Grandma! Grandma! You were absolutely right! More cooks than I could count came down and were washing their hands wildly! I saw them!" Noshi chuckled deeply, her eyes twinkling with mischief and wisdom. "You see, Dimshi Kata?" she said thoughtfully. "Don't ever underestimate old people... or monkeys! Remember, *sometimes a single monkey looks like an army of cooks... and a clever old woman can trick even the sharpest little boy! Buri na gele purano kotha jai na, Dimshi balta!*"

**The Moral:** The tale celebrates Noshi's resourceful wit. It highlights how perception can be deceived, especially in the dark, and underscores that wisdom and cleverness (often the domain of elders in folktales) can outsmart youthful skepticism. It's a reminder not to underestimate anyone, especially grandmothers! The phrase "*Buri na gele purano kotha jai na*" (Without the old woman, the old tales are lost) often concludes stories featuring Noshi, emphasizing the importance of elders' wisdom and storytelling traditions.
