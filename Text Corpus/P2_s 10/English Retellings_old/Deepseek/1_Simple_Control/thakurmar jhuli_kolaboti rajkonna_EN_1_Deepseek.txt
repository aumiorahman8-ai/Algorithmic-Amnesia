The Bengali folktale of **Kolaboti Rajkonna** (The Jackfruit Princess) is a classic story rich with themes of temptation, promises, consequences, and redemption. Here's a retelling:

1.  **The Queen's Craving:** A long time ago, in a certain kingdom, the pregnant Queen experienced an intense craving for ripe jackfruit (*kola*). It wasn't the season for them, and no jackfruit could be found anywhere in the kingdom, despite the King's frantic searches.
2.  **The Mysterious Offer:** An ancient, forgotten jackfruit tree was found deep within the palace garden. Miraculously, despite the season, it bore one large, perfect, fragrant jackfruit. As the Queen reached greedily to pluck it, a hidden Demoness (*Rakkhosh* or *Shamlai Rano/Bonbibi* - names vary) appeared from within the tree.
3.  **The Fateful Bargain:** The Demoness offered the coveted jackfruit to the Queen but demanded a terrible promise in return: "You may have the jackfruit, but you must give me the child growing inside your womb."
4.  **Temptation and Betrayal:** Overwhelmed by her craving and thinking she might outwit the Demoness later (or believing the promise wasn't serious), the Queen agreed. She devoured the jackfruit with great satisfaction. After the Queen gave birth to a beautiful baby princess, she completely forgot about her promise, lost in the joy of motherhood.
5.  **The Demoness Claims Her Due:** Years later, on the princess's seventh birthday (or around then), the Demoness suddenly appeared in the court. She reminded the stunned King and Queen of the broken promise and demanded her prize - their beloved daughter.
6.  **Despair and Abduction:** Despite the King's pleas and attempts to offer wealth or another child, the Demoness was resolute. She accused the Queen of treachery. Ultimately, overpowering the royal guards, the Demoness snatched the terrified princess, Kolaboti, and vanished back to her dark realm deep within the forest.
7.  **The Loyal Servants:** Heartbroken and desperate, the King and Queen offered a reward to whoever could rescue Kolaboti. An old, loyal servant couple stepped forward. Seeing their sincerity and courage, the King entrusted them with the dangerous mission.
8.  **The Journey and Disguise:** Guided perhaps by divine intervention or rumors, the old couple travelled deep into the perilous forest controlling the Demoness's lair. Knowing they couldn't fight her, they devised a plan. The old woman disguised herself as a *sadhu* (holy man), while her husband acted like her disciple or attendant.
9.  **Gaining Access:** Disguised as ascetics seeking alms, they approached the Demoness's dwelling. Hungry wayfarers were rare and intriguing in that desolate place. The Demoness, surprised and perhaps charmed by the holy visitors, welcomed them cautiously but generously.
10. **The Rescue Plan:** Within the lair, they saw the mournful Kolaboti. Discreetly communicating through signs or coded language understood only by Kolaboti (perhaps a lullaby or phrase known from the palace), the rescuers conveyed their plan.
11. **The Escape:** They devised a moment to act:
    *   One evening, they requested a ceremonial bath after accepting royal hospitality. They asked Kolaboti to draw water for them.
    *   As Kolaboti brought water, the old woman whispered instructions.
    *   At the crucial moment, they threw sacrificial ash (*vibhuti*) or enchanted water towards the Demoness (or simply seized Kolaboti), declaring, "Demoness! Our holy vows forbid us from witnessing this form!" Creating chaos, they grabbed Kolaboti and fled.
12. **The Chase and Cleverness:** Enraged, the Demoness pursued them. Near a large river, cornered by the Demoness, the old couple used cleverness:
    *   They secretly placed Kolaboti inside a hollow bamboo trunk drifting downstream.
    *   They sacrificed themselves willingly to the Demoness, satisfying her immediate fury and buying crucial time.
13. **Kolaboti's Journey:** The bamboo trunk carrying Kolaboti floated safely down the river, eventually getting caught near the palace gardens or a village downstream.
14. **Homecoming:** She was discovered (often by her own parents or palace guards who recognized her). Recognizing the princess by a special birthmark, jewel, or simply her resemblance, they brought her back to the palace.
15. **Mourning, Reunion & Reverence:** While overjoyed at Kolaboti's return, the King and Queen deeply mourned the loyal servants who sacrificed their lives to save their daughter. A grand shrine (or annual prayers/festivals) was erected in honor of the brave old couple, who became revered figures for their ultimate devotion and sacrifice. Kolaboti lived the life she was meant to, forever grateful to her rescuers.

**Key Themes & Variations:**

*   **Promises:** The tale warns against making rash promises, especially under the influence of greed or temptation, and emphasizes the sanctity of keeping one's word.
*   **Consequences:** Every action has consequences; the Queen's moment of weakness led to years of suffering.
*   **Loyalty & Sacrifice:** The ultimate devotion and sacrifice of the servants contrast sharply with the Queen's earlier betrayal and highlight true virtue.
*   **Cleverness vs. Brute Force:** The rescuers succeed through wit and disguise, not strength.
*   **Seasons & Supernatural:** Jackfruit out of season represents unnatural temptation linked to dangerous spirits.
*   Variations exist: Sometimes Kolaboti is hidden in a clay pot instead of bamboo; the rescuers might kill the Demoness trickily; Kolaboti's tears might turn to gold pearls as a sign of her royal purity.

The story of Kolaboti Rajkonna remains a poignant reminder of responsibility, loyalty, and the price of broken vows, cherished across Bengal.
