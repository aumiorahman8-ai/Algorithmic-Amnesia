The Bengali folktale **"Dalim Kumar"** (sometimes spelled *Dalimkumar*) is a story of betrayal, transformation, magic, disguise, and ultimately, vindication and cleverness. Here's a retelling capturing its essence:

1.  **The Wealthy Merchant's Son:** The story begins with Dalim Kumar, a handsome and kind-hearted young man, the son of a very wealthy merchant. They lived a life of comfort and prosperity.

2.  **The Fatal Journey:** One day, Dalim Kumar undertook a long trading journey by sea with several other merchants. During the voyage, a storm wrecked their ships. While Dalim Kumar clung to a piece of driftwood, his companions drowned.

3.  **The Treacherous Merchant:** However, one fellow merchant managed to survive and reached the same shores as Dalim Kumar. Seeing Dalim Kumar weak and vulnerable, this jealous merchant attacked him, robbed him of his remaining valuables, and threw him into a deep well, believing him dead.

4.  **The Mysterious Escape & Transformation:** Dalim Kumar didn't die. Deep in the well, he was rescued and taken to a hidden kingdom inhabited by spirits (*Bhut*, *Preta*) or powerful supernatural beings. They saved his life but cursed (or transformed) him due to their own mystical laws – they turned him into a **Dalim Tree** (Pomegranate Tree). They told him he must remain in this form for exactly **seven years**.

5.  **The Bone Flute Gift:** Before sending him back to the human world disguised as a tree (planted near a King's garden), the spirits gave him one crucial gift: a small flute carved *from his own bones* that he had shed during his transformation. They instructed him to play it if he ever found a truly pure-hearted person able to recognize the magic within.

6.  **Life as the Pomegranate Tree:** Dalim Kumar endured years as the magnificent Dalim tree in the royal garden. The king prized it greatly because it bore incredibly sweet pomegranates (*dalim*) year-round.

7.  **The Princess and the Pomegranate:** The king had seven daughters. The six elder princesses were haughty, but the youngest princess was kind, gentle, and pure-hearted. One day, while playing near the tree, the youngest princess plucked a pomegranate. To her amazement, a drop of blood oozed from the stem instead of sap. The tree seemed to subtly react to her presence.

8.  **The Magic Revealed:** Intrigued and sensing something magical, the youngest princess secretly visited the tree each day. One day, driven by instinct or a promise Dalim Kumar conveyed silently, she gently blew into the bone flute hidden near the roots (or sometimes carried by a bird associated with the tree).

9.  **The Transformation Back:** Upon hearing the pure sound of the flute, the seven-year curse ended. Dalim Kumar instantly transformed back into his handsome human form while still wearing the distinctive royal gold anklets (*nupur*) he wore the day he was attacked.

10. **Explaining His Tale:** Dalim Kumar revealed his shocking story to the astonished princess – how he was the merchant's son, the shipwreck, the betrayal by his companion, his rescue by spirits, the transformation into the tree, and the significance of the bone flute recognizing her pure heart.

11. **Recognition and Betrayal Exposed:** The princess brought Dalim Kumar to her father, the king. They confirmed his identity, especially through the unique gold anklets. Dalim Kumar named his treacherous companion.

12. **Summoning the Traitor:** By royal command, the partner-turned-usurper merchant (who had likely used Dalim Kumar's stolen wealth to become influential) was brought before the king. He was asked about Dalim Kumar's fate. Thinking Dalim was long dead, the merchant lied, recounting a story of Dalim drowning.

13. **The King's Test:** The King devised a clever ruse. He declared he would marry his youngest princess to the treacherous merchant as a reward... *but only if* he proved his loyalty by jumping into the very well where he had tried to kill Dalim Kumar.

14. **The Traitor's Downfall:** Blinded by greed and ambition, the merchant readily jumped into the deep well, confident he could scramble out again. He never emerged. The well consumed him, delivering poetic justice.

15. **The True Reward:** Recognizing his nobility and suffering, the King happily married his youngest daughter, the kind princess who recognized his true nature, to Dalim Kumar. They lived happily ever after, restoring Dalim Kumar's rightful place and wealth.

16. **The Panchu Element (Alternative Ending):** In some versions, the spirits give Dalim Kumar an additional magic trick: a **Panchu** (a small, wizened, homunculus-like spirit or a vial/powder). After transforming back but *before* confronting the King, Dalim Kumar uses the Panchu. He asks it to take one of his gold anklets and hide it in the treacherous merchant's house deep within a box of grain. Later, when the merchant denies Dalim Kumar's story, the King orders his house searched. Finding the anklet hidden impossibly deep within the merchant's own grain stash serves as irrefutable proof of his treachery and Dalim Kumar's truth, leading to the merchant's punishment and Dalim Kumar's vindication. The Panchu then disappears after fulfilling its task.

**Key Themes:**

*   **Betrayal and Deception:** The central act by the jealous merchant.
*   **Endurance and Suffering:** Dalim Kumar's seven years trapped as a tree.
*   **Pure Recognition:** The youngest princess's ability to see beyond appearances.
*   **Magical Transformation:** Core to the plot.
*   **Poetic Justice:** The betrayer falling into his own trap.
*   **Reward for Kindness/Purity:** The princess finds true love.
*   **Clever Resolution:** The King's test or the Panchu trick exposing the lie.

The tale offers suspense, magic, moral justice, and ultimately, a satisfying triumph of good over treachery. The name "Dalim Kumar" signifies both his original name and, in the tale, his connection to the pomegranate tree (*Dalim* tree).
