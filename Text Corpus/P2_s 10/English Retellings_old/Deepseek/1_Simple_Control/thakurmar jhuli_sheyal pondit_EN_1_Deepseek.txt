The tale of **"Sheyal Pandit"** (The Jackal Scholar) is a classic Bengali folktale satirizing hypocrisy, greed, and blind allegiance to tradition. Here's the core story:

1.  **The Feast:** A Jackal ("Sheyal") stumbles upon a grand, untouched feast laid out for a wedding party deep in the forest. He's overcome with greed but knows he can't eat it all alone or defend it against larger animals.

2.  **The Cunning Plan:** To secure the feast, the Jackal transforms himself into a revered "Pandit" (scholar/priest). He loudly announces he has established a holy "school" nearby and needs offerings for his sacred students.

3.  **Manipulating Hierarchy:** The Jackal-Pandit finds various animals:
    *   **The Tiger ("Bag(tiger)shah/Bagh)**: He offers the Lion's share, inviting the Tiger as chief guest to symbolize power. The Tiger, flattered, accepts.
    *   **The Leopard:** Offered a portion as the "Commander-in-Chief" representing strength after the Tiger.
    *   **The Fox:** Offered a role as the "Minister" symbolizing cunning.
    *   **The Cat/Hyena:** Offered scraps as lowly "students."
    The arrogant Jackal-Pandit excludes smaller, "unworthy" animals entirely.

4.  **The Snub & The Fury:** Crucially, the Jackal **deliberately ignores** another Tiger/Bag(h) living deep within the forest ("Gacher Bag(h)"). This Tiger hears about the feast and the disrespect of being excluded.

5.  **The Dry River:** Furious, the deep-forest Tiger storms towards the feast site. Seeing him approach, the terrified Jackal-Pandit hatches another lie. He points to a dry riverbed and declares, "Great Guardian Tiger! We were *just* discussing who should inform you! But alas, crossing this mighty river proved impossible!"

6.  **Insult to Injury:** The Jackal-Pandit grovels, begging forgiveness, blaming the treacherous "river." Unable to contain his fury at the insult and obvious lie, the deep-forest Tiger roars:

7.  **Public Humiliation:** The Tiger forces the Jackal-Pandit into his ultimate humiliation:
    > **"Dhakka diye bhango e bartan!**
    > **Ar poro na Sheyal Panditer gaan!"**
    >
    > *(Translation:)*
    > **"Break all these pots with a mighty kick!**
    > **And sing no more of Sheyal Pandit's trick!"**

8.  **The Poetic Justice:** The Tiger *forces* the Jackal-Pandit to sing this verse mocking himself. The Jackal, terrified, sings it loudly while frantically kicking over the pots holding the feast. He destroys everything he coveted and exposed his deceit and vanity publicly. Shamed and ruined, he flees into the forest.

## Key Themes & Meaning

*   **Satire:** Mocks those who put on false airs of scholarship/piety ("Pandit") for selfish gain.
*   **Hierarchy and Hypocrisy:** Critiques the arbitrary exclusion/inclusion based on flattery and false status.
*   **Cunning Undone:** Shows how excessive greed and arrogance lead to self-destruction.
*   **Blind Tradition:** Highlights the folly of blindly following supposed authorities (like the Jackal's self-proclaimed school).
*   **Poetic Justice:** Humiliation is fitting – having to publicly proclaim one's own shame.

The variant "**Dhakka diye bhango e bartan / Ar poro na Sheyal Panditer gaan**" crystallizes the tale's moral – the downfall of pretentiousness and greed through forced confession and destruction of ill-gotten gains.
