Here's a retelling of the classic Bengali folktale "Broken Promise" (often called "Bhangi Pore" or similar titles), highlighting the devastating consequence of betraying trust:

## The Story

1.  **The Barren Times:** Once, in a village beside a mighty river, a terrible drought struck. The rains vanished, crops withered, and the river itself began to shrink drastically. Fisherfolk found their nets coming up empty for days and weeks. Hunger gripped the village.
2.  **The Mysterious Encounter:** Desperate, a group of fishermen took their boat out one stark, moonlit night, hoping for a miraculous catch. As they drifted silently, a luminous figure emerged from the shimmering water – it was **Ganga Maiya (Mother Ganga)**, the goddess of the river herself, radiating sorrow.
3.  **The Divine Plea & Promise:** Ganga Maiya addressed them gently, "My children, I see your suffering. The famine pains me deeply. I offer you a solution: **Promise me faithfully** that you will perform a specific ritual *(often described as throwing a unique offering back into the river after a bountiful catch, or sacrificing one special fish)*. If you swear this vow and keep it, I promise your nets will overflow tonight."
4.  **The Moment of Greed:** The starving fishermen eagerly swore the sacred promise, moved by the goddess's compassion and the prospect of food. They cast their nets. Miraculously, they hauled in an astounding catch, far more fish than they'd seen in years! Their boat overflowed. Joy replaced despair.
5.  **The Broken Vow:** The fishermen rowed back to shore, celebrating their incredible luck. Greed quickly overtook gratitude. Seeing the massive haul, some fishermen argued: "Why waste this precious bounty? Who knows if Ganga Maiya truly expects the offering? Let's keep *all* the fish! We are so hungry!" Despite reminders of their solemn oath, fear and greed prevailed. They kept every single fish, neglecting the ritual utterly, **breaking their promise to the goddess.**
6.  **The Terrible Consequence:** As dawn broke and the villagers rejoiced at the catch, a dreadful silence fell upon the river.
    *   The next morning, fishermen returned to the river... and found it **completely dry.** Not a drop of water remained. Overnight, **Ganga Maiya vanished**, taking the entire riverbed with her, leaving only parched, cracked earth. All life in the river – fish, plants – was gone.
    *   The promised relief turned to utter devastation. The drought became absolute. Without the river, true famine set in, worse than before. The village was doomed.

## The Moral & Core Theme

*   **Sacred Trust:** The promise made to a deity (representing nature, trust, or cosmic justice) is sacred.
*   **Dire Repercussions:** Breaking such a promise isn't just rude; it's a cosmic violation inviting catastrophic, irreversible punishment.
*   **Greed vs. Integrity:** Short-sighted greed destroys long-term security and blessings. Keeping one's word, especially solemn vows, is paramount.
*   **Nature's Wrath:** The story personifies the river as a goddess, powerfully illustrating how betraying a pact with nature leads to profound loss and hardship.

## Why it Endures

This folktale remains powerful because:

1.  **Memorable Imagery:** The vivid picture of the overflowing nets transforming into a stark, dry riverbed.
2.  **Universal Theme:** The danger of broken promises and greed transcends culture.
3.  **Emotional Resonance:** The shift from desperate hope to miraculous relief to crushing despair and ruin.
4.  **Cultural Identity:** Emphasizes the deep reverence Bengalis hold for the Ganga/Hooghly/Padma rivers as life-giving goddesses.

The "Consequence of the Broken Promise" serves as a timeless Bengali warning: Honor your vows, respect divine/natural forces, beware greed, or face utter ruin.
