The Bengali folktale **"Shat Bhai Champa"** (Seven Brothers and Champa) is a beloved story **exploring sacrifice, sisterly love, resilience, and transformation**. Here's a retelling incorporating its core elements:

1.  **The Happy Family:** Once upon a time, there lived **seven brothers (shat bhai)** and their **one beloved sister, Champa**. They were orphans but lived happily together in peace and harmony, deeply devoted to each other. Champa was the heart of the household.

2.  **The Threat:** Tragedy struck when a **wicked merchant (or sometimes, a king or zamindar)** saw the beautiful Champa and decided he must have her for himself. He demanded she be given to him.

3.  **The Flight:** Knowing his intentions were evil and desperate to protect their sister, the seven brothers took Champa and **fled deep into the forest** to escape the merchant's clutches.

4.  **Hiding and Discovery:** They found a relatively safe place and built a small shelter. However, the relentless merchant tracked them down in the jungle.

5.  **The Sacrifice:** With no escape route and the merchant's men closing in, the brothers faced an impossible choice. Showing immense love and sacrifice, they decided to **transform themselves to hide Champa and themselves**. They instructed Champa:
    *   "Dear sister, stand before us."
    *   "Herd the ants together into a pile." (They needed an anthill).
    *   When the anthill was formed, they told her: **"Hide inside this anthill.** Whatever happens, *do not come out* until it's completely silent and safe."

6.  **The Transformation:** As Champa hid trembling inside the anthill, her seven brothers performed a powerful act of love and magic. They uttered a mantra or simply willed their transformation:
    *   **We, the seven brothers, become Champa trees!** ("**Amra shat bhai hoye jai champa gach**!")
    *   Instantly, the **seven brothers transformed into seven beautiful Champa trees**, fragrant and blossoming, surrounding the anthill where their sister hid.

7.  **The Frustrated Villain:** The merchant and his men arrived at the spot. They found only a cluster of lovely Champa trees and an anthill. Despite searching high and low, **they found no trace of the brothers or Champa**. Frustrated and thwarted, the merchant eventually gave up and left the forest.

8.  **Champa's Vigil & Devotion:** Champa remained faithfully hidden inside the anthill for a **long time**, waiting for the silence that signaled safety. Finally emerging, she found her brothers gone and only the seven Champa trees standing there. Heartbroken but intuitively understanding what had happened, she devoted herself to them.
    *   She tended to the trees daily, watering them and caring for them.
    *   Every day, she **massaged the trees with fragrant pastes and bathed them with milk**, honoring them as her beloved brothers who sacrificed themselves to save her. She talked to them, pouring out her love and sorrow.

9.  **The Miracle:** Champa's unwavering love, devotion, and purity of heart became a powerful force. Her daily rituals and profound sisterly love **worked a miracle**. One day, as she performed her milk bath, the trees began to shimmer and transform.
    *   **The seven Champa trees changed back into her seven brothers!** They were restored to their human forms, alive and well.

10. **Reunion and Happy Ending:** Overjoyed, Champa rushed to embrace her brothers. They celebrated their reunion, their bond now stronger than ever. They lived happily ever after, safe in the knowledge that their profound love for each other had overcome the evil that sought to destroy them.

**Core Themes:**
*   **Selfless Sacrifice:** The brothers willingly gave up their human forms to save Champa.
*   **Sisterly/Unconditional Love:** Champa's unwavering devotion and care for the trees (her brothers) is the central force that breaks the curse/transformation.
*   **Purity & Virtue Triumphing Over Evil:** Champa's goodness contrasted sharply with the merchant's greed and lust.
*   **Resilience:** Champa's patience and enduring faith while hidden and in caring for the trees.
*   **Transformation:** Man to nature (trees), and nature back to man, signifying deep connection and impermanence of imposed states.
*   **Sibling Bond:** The ultimate power of the sibling relationship.

The tale remains a poignant reminder of familial love and sacrifice, particularly the special bond between siblings, and the enduring belief that pure love possesses transformative power. The fragrant Champa flower (Magnolia champaca) serves as a beautiful symbol of that enduring bond.
