Here is a retelling of the classic Bengali folktale **"Behula and Lakhindar"** (also known as **"Manasamangal"**), the story famously involving a marriage to the Prince of Snakes:

## The Tale of Behula and Lakhindar

1.  **The Mighty Merchant and the Defiant Devotee:** The story centers around **Chand Saudagar**, a fabulously wealthy merchant immensely devoted to Lord Shiva. His immense pride and prosperity made him arrogant. He utterly refused to worship the goddess **Manasa**, the deity of snakes, poison, and fertility, considering snake worship beneath him. Manasa, needing worship to solidify her place among the gods, became infuriated by Chand's repeated insults and refusal.

2.  **Manasa's Vengeance:** To humble Chand and force his worship, Manasa devised a cruel plan. She would systematically destroy his happiness by killing his sons. One by one, using her serpentine powers, she orchestrated the deaths of Chand Saudagar's six eldest sons.

3.  **The Sheltered Seventh Son – Lakhindar:** Chand Saudagar now had only one son left, his beloved seventh son, **Lakhindar** (also known as Lakhai). Terrified of Manasa's wrath, Chand took extraordinary measures. He built a magnificent, hermetically sealed wedding chamber entirely of iron (**Lohar Kuthi or Monihar Kuthi**). No crack, no hole, no gap existed. Inside this fortress-like room, Lakhindar was to spend his wedding night, safe from Manasa's serpents.

4.  **Finding a Bride – Behula:** Despite the danger, Chand Saudagar sought a bride for Lakhindar. Through divine manipulation (often attributed to Manasa herself), he chose **Behula**, the exceptionally beautiful and virtuous daughter of another wealthy merchant, Saha. Behula, sometimes forewarned or blessed by celestial beings, remained resolute in her destiny.

5.  **The Fateful Wedding Night:** On Lakhindar and Behula's wedding night, they entered the iron chamber. Chand believed his son was safe. However, Manasa's will was supreme. She sent the tiniest, most potent snake imaginable. Concealed as a speck of dust *inside* a special sesame seed (**Til**) brought in for ritual purposes unseen by anyone, the serpent gained entry. That night, as Behula slept, the snake emerged and fatally bit Prince Lakhindar.

6.  **Behula's Incredible Resolve:** Chand Saudagar was devastated, but Behula, filled with shock and immense grief, made a startling vow. Refusing cremation, she declared she would **float down the river on a raft** bearing her husband's corpse. Her unwavering goal? To appease Goddess Manasa, convene the gods, and **plead for the restoration of her husband and brothers-in-law**.

7.  **The Perilous Journey:** Behula placed Lakhindar's body on a large raft and embarked on an epic, solitary journey down the tumultuous river. The journey was arduous and filled with supernatural dangers:
    *   Monstrous creatures and demons attacked.
    *   Manasa herself, disguised in various forms (as an old woman, bandit, fisherman), continuously tested Behula's devotion, strength, and character. She tried to drown the raft, break Behula's spirit, and deter her.
    *   Throughout, Behula's unwavering courage, sharp wit, profound devotion to her husband, and incredible faith prevailed. She navigated rapids, placated spirits, and passed every test.

8.  **Reaching Heaven (Netherworld/Celestial Abode):** After seemingly floating endlessly for six months, Behula's raft miraculously reached the exclusive bathing ghat (**Shesh Netar Ghat**) belonging to **Netidhar**, the celestial washerman of the gods. Through divine intervention or stealth (using her beauty and intelligence), Behula gained an audience with the gods themselves in the heavenly court (**Indra's Sabha**).

9.  **The Challenge and the Plea:** Behula demonstrated her extraordinary skills – singing celestial songs and dancing with unparalleled grace – to entertain and placate Lord Shiva and the assembled deities. Shiva, impressed and moved by her suffering, devotion, and beauty, agreed to grant her a boon.

10. **Manasa's Demand:** Shiva summoned Manasa. Behula got her chance to plead her case. Manasa, witnessing Behula's incredible devotion and suffering, agreed to resurrect Lakhindar and his six brothers... but **only on one condition**: Behula must guarantee that Chand Saudagar would finally worship Manasa upon their return.

11. **The Restoration and The Final Submission:** Bringing Lakhindar and his brothers back to life, Shiva sent them, along with Behula, back to Chand Saudagar's kingdom. Upon their miraculous return, overwhelmed with joy and humbled by Manasa's power and Behula's impossible feat, **Chand Saudagar finally relented**. He offered puja (worship) to Goddess Manasa with his left hand (a sign of reluctance but acceptance), fulfilling her ultimate demand. He established her worship firmly among humans.

**Themes of the Tale:**

*   **Power of Devotion & Perseverance:** Behula's unwavering love and her harrowing journey showcase extraordinary feminine strength, loyalty, and determination.
*   **Divine Wrath & Requirement for Worship:** Manasa's relentless pursuit of worship highlights a central theme of folk deities needing human devotion for recognition.
*   **Pride Before a Fall:** Chand Saudagar's downfall stems entirely from his hubris and defiance of the goddess.
*   **Triumph over Adversity:** Behula's journey symbolizes overcoming impossible odds through faith and resilience.
*   **Enduring Cultural Impact:** This folktale forms the core of the "Manasamangal" literary tradition and underpins the Bengali snake goddess Manasa's worship, especially during the rainy season festivals like Manasa Puja/Manasa Thakurani Puja.

Behula remains an iconic figure in Bengali culture, revered as the epitome of a devoted wife (**Pativrata Nari**) whose love conquered death itself.
