The Bengali folktale of **Khumpui** (often set in the Chittagong Hill Tracts or Mizo-influenced regions) is a classic story contrasting kindness and cruelty, showcasing natural justice. Here's the essence of it:

1.  **The Tragic Beginning:** Once, there lived a poor, elderly widow with her two daughters. The elder was named **Nirosa** (meaning "dark-hearted" or "unkind"), and the younger was named **Khumpui** (meaning "golden-hearted" or "kind-hearted"). After the widow died, leaving the girls orphans, their uncles reluctantly took them in.

2.  **Cruelty and Abandonment:** Nirosa, lazy and spiteful, quickly turned her uncles against her gentle sister, Khumpui. They berated Khumpui constantly, forcing her to do all the hard work while Nirosa lounged. Deeming Khumpui a burden, Nirosa cunningly convinced the uncles to take the sisters deep into the forest under some pretext (like visiting relatives or gathering special food). Once deep in the wilderness, Nirosa told the uncles to abandon Khumpui while they pretended to look the other way. They heartlessly left the kind girl alone and terrified in the dense jungle.

3.  **A Magical Encounter:** Lost and weeping, Khumpui wandered until she found a curiously large and beautiful flattened lump of cow-dung (`gobar`/`gora`). To her surprise, the cow-dung spoke! Addressing her with affection as "Mother," it questioned why she cried. Khumpui poured out her sad tale.

4.  **Provision and Direction:** Moved by her kindness and plight, the magical cow-dung fashioned a tiny, intricate hut for her to sleep in safely. It also provided her with delicious cakes wrapped in leaves (`Shidol`) and instructed her to follow the path worn by wild animals working like cattle (`Sai`). It warned her not to stray but strictly follow that path.

5.  **A Kingdom Found:** Following the animal track diligently, Khumpui eventually emerged from the deep jungle into a magnificent kingdom. Disoriented but resourceful, she sought work.

6.  **The Kindness Rewarded:** The Queen of that land noticed the exhausted but gentle girl and took pity on her. Impressed by Khumpui's inherent goodness and diligence, the Queen adopted her as her own daughter! Khumpui found love, luxury, and security in her new royal life. She grew into a beautiful young woman cherished by her adoptive family.

7.  **The Reckoning:** Back home, Nirosa and the uncles faced hardship and famine. Hearing rumors of a kingdom ruled by a kind Queen with a beloved adopted daughter whose origin story was mysterious, Nirosa's suspicious nature clicked. She guessed it was Khumpui.

8.  **Imitation and Failure:** Driven by greed and envy, Nirosa decided to reclaim Khumpui's fortune or create her own. She convinced the uncles to abandon her in the *same* spot deep within the jungle.

9.  **No Magic for the Cruel:** Like her sister, Nirosa encountered the magical cow-dung slab. However, when it addressed her as "Mother" and asked why she cried, Nirosa reacted with disgust and contempt, kicking and insulting the humble offering. Enraged by her cruelty and arrogance, the cow-dung cursed her instead of helping.

10. **A Cruel Punishment:** The cow-dung told Nirosa she would find nothing ahead but suffering. Unlike Khumpui, Nirosa chose her own path arrogantly, ignoring guidance. She eventually stumbled into the kingdom—but in a shocking and humiliating state. Legends say:
    *   A cursed necklace of hard, abrasive **canes** appeared tightly locked around her neck.
    *   Alternatively, she emerged bearing heavy loads or unnatural burdens symbolizing her wickedness.
    *   She was afflicted with grotesque deformities or unbearable pain as fitting punishment.
    *   Her arrival horrified the people and the Queen.

11. **Justice Served:** Recognizing Nirosa's malice and cruelty (and likely confirming her identity as Khumpui's wicked sister), she was driven out of the kingdom in disgrace. Some versions imply she dies from her afflictions or lives as a terrified outcast. Meanwhile, Khumpui lived happily ever after in the royal palace, her kindness and resilience ultimately rewarded.

**Core Themes:**

*   **Karmic Justice:** Goodness and humility are rewarded exponentially; cruelty and arrogance are punished severely.
*   **Kindness Triumphs:** Khumpui's inherent goodness attracts miraculous help and a happy fate.
*   **Respect for Humble Things:** The magical cow-dung helping Khumpui symbolizes that blessings and wisdom can come from unexpected and seemingly lowly sources – mocking them brings curses.
*   **Sibling Rivalry & Jealousy:** Nirosa's envy drives the plot and her downfall.
*   **Nature as Helper/Judge:** The forest hosts both the magical aid and the trap; natural forces administer justice.

This poignant tale resonates as a powerful reminder of the virtues of kindness and humility and the inevitable downfall of cruelty and arrogance, deeply rooted in Bengali oral tradition, particularly from the southeastern hill regions.
