A long, long time ago, in a certain country, a couple lived with their only beloved daughter, Bilkis Banu. Their dear daughter was incomparably beautiful. The family consisted of three: the mother, the father, and the daughter. In their family of three, there was no lack of anything. As the days passed, the girl grew older. And as she aged, her beauty increased day by day. The brilliance of her beauty was like a flash of lightning. No lamp was needed to see her in a dark room. As the girl grew up, the couple became restless day and night, thinking of getting her married. However, they could not find a suitable groom to marry her off to. Then, without any warning, the man of the house passed away one day. The neighbors came and arranged for his burial. After Bilkis's father's death, the mother and daughter became very helpless. Now, it was just a household of mother and daughter. After her husband's death, Bilkis's mother became even more anxious to get her married. But no suitor was coming forward. Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, was of an extremely greedy nature!

Bilkis's family lived in a hilly area. Their house was on the edge of a hill surrounded by a dense jungle. It was a winter morning. Towards the end of the month of Poush, the cold is naturally more severe. In the pre-dawn hours, the intense cold was enough to make one's hands and feet go numb. In the morning, the mother and daughter were sitting in the courtyard, trying to warm themselves in the sun's heat. Just then, a huge python approached them. Having come down from the hills on that cold night, the python had taken shelter in their courtyard. The snake had been in the bushes. Feeling refreshed by the sun's warmth, the python saw the mother and daughter and began to move towards them. However, the mother and daughter did not notice the python.

The python was extremely hungry. As the sunlight fell on the python's body, it reflected and scattered in all directions, making the courtyard even brighter. This startled the mother and daughter. The next moment, regaining their senses, they saw a massive python coming towards them. Terrified, the mother and daughter ran inside the house for safety and bolted the door. The python lay stretched out at the door. It did not move. Meanwhile, the mother and daughter remained trapped inside the house. Morning passed, and even the afternoon was slipping away. There was no sign of the python moving from its spot. Pythons do not usually bite people. Unlike other snakes, especially cobras, which carry venom in their fangs, pythons have no venom in their mouths. However, when hungry, they can swallow whole humans, cows, goats, or deer.

As mentioned before, the woman was very greedy and cunning. The woman thought that since the python had been lying motionless at their door all day, it must not be a snake. If it were a snake, it would have left by now. Some prince must have come to their house in the guise of a snake. Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, was not willing to let this opportunity slip away. Sona Banu decided to marry her beautiful daughter to the snake. No sooner thought than done. Eventually, Sona Banu dressed up her daughter Bilkis and sent her to the Snake Prince. The snake was extremely hungry. Consequently, upon getting Bilkis, it began to swallow her. The snake first started to swallow Bilkis's two feet. At this, Bilkis screamed at the top of her lungs and, in a song-like tone, told her mother that the Snake Prince was swallowing her feet and that they were hurting. Her mother, Sona Banu, then reassured her daughter Bilkis by singing a reply. The swallowing of the daughter Bilkis by the snake, and this conversation between the mother and daughter, is given below:

When the Snake Prince began to swallow the daughter Bilkis's two feet, the daughter cried out:
"Oh mother, my feet are hurting."
Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, replied:
"Oh daughter, no, no, he is putting anklets on your feet."

After swallowing her feet, when the Snake Prince reached her waist, the daughter Bilkis said to her mother, Sona Banu:
"Oh mother, my waist is hurting."
Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, comforted her daughter, saying:
"Oh daughter, no, no, he is putting a golden waist-chain on you."

After that, the Snake Prince began to swallow Bilkis's hands. Then Bilkis told her mother:
"Oh mother, my hands are hurting."
Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, consoled her daughter:
"Oh daughter, no, no, he is putting golden bangles on you."

The Snake Prince continued to swallow Bilkis. At one point, the Snake Prince
began to swallow Bilkis's neck. At this time, the daughter Bilkis told her mother, Sona Banu:
"Oh mother, my throat is hurting."
Then Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, comforted her daughter, saying:
"Oh daughter, no, no, he is putting a necklace on you."

The daughter Bilkis told her mother, Sona Banu:
"Oh mother, my ears are hurting."
Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, comforted her daughter, saying:
"Oh daughter, no, no, he is putting golden earrings on you."

The daughter Bilkis told her mother, Sona Banu:
"Oh mother, my head is hurting."
Bilkis's mother, Sona Banu, reassured her daughter, saying:
"Oh daughter, no, no, he is putting a head ornament on you."

Eventually, the Snake Prince swallowed the daughter Bilkis completely. The mother thought that the Snake Prince (her son-in-law) was feeling content after swallowing her daughter. Perhaps he would later bring her out of his belly, and the Snake Prince himself would appear in human form. The hungry Snake Prince, having filled its belly with the daughter Bilkis, lay motionless. The daylight faded. The Snake Prince remained lying on the house's veranda. Meanwhile, the mother, Sona Banu, waited anxiously for the moment the Snake Prince would release her daughter Bilkis from his belly. Her eyes would fill with joy upon seeing her daughter's gold jewelry. Her son-in-law, the Snake Prince, would also reveal himself in human form. Daylight faded and night fell. But the Snake Prince did not stir. Even late into the night, the Snake Prince remained motionless. At some point, the woman fell asleep. Meanwhile, once the daughter Bilkis was digested in its belly, the python heaved a sigh of relief, regurgitated her clothes, and disappeared into the deep jungle before dawn. When the woman woke up at dawn and hurriedly opened the door, she saw that the Snake Prince was gone. Her daughter Bilkis was also gone. Only the clothes her daughter Bilkis had been wearing were there. The greedy Sona Banu finally understood that this was not a prince or a man in the guise of a python. It was a real snake. Then she began to lament, weeping her heart out for her daughter Bilkis. Before, at least she had her daughter Bilkis with her. Now, Sona Banu was all alone. Consequently, Sona Banu also left her home, went to a populated area, and took shelter in the courtyard of a house.
