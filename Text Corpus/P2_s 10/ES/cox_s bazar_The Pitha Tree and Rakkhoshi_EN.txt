Having lost his father at a young age, the only son lived a life of hardship with his mother. From childhood, the boy loved pithas. Every day, his mother had to make pithas for him, otherwise, the boy would become terribly upset. But for the sorrowful mother, this became increasingly impossible day by day.

On the last day she made a pitha, wondering how she would feed him pithas in the future, she planted one in a corner of the courtyard. A few days later, a sapling sprouted there. Leaves grew, and branches spread. Thus, like a dream, a strangely beautiful tree grew up. Flowers bloomed within a short time. And there, hanging from every branch, were pithas. The mother and son stared in amazement.

One day, the boy climbed the tree and plucked a pitha. It tasted wonderful. Oh, what joy the boy felt! His days passed in happiness and peace, eating the delicious pithas.

But within a few days, trouble began. An Ogress, in the guise of a woman, would come to stand under the pitha tree. She would try to entice the boy with sweet words. She would ask the boy for a pitha. If he threw a pitha to the Ogress from a distance, she would make various excuses. She would say, "This one has dirt on it," or "This one is broken". "Give it right into my hand". When he tried to hand it to her directly, she would try to grab him.

In this way, using all sorts of alluring words, the Ogress plotted to capture the boy. Sometimes, she would nearly catch him. When grabbed, the boy would scream and shout and manage to free himself.

One day, under the pretext of taking a pitha, the Ogress truly caught the boy. Then she bound his hands, feet, and mouth, stuffed him into a sack, and started carrying him away. On the way, the Ogress went into the jungle by the roadside to answer nature's call. The boy, struggling inside the sack, suddenly tore the knot at the sack's opening. The boy slowly climbed out of the sack.

After escaping, the boy cleverly filled the sack with clods of earth, tree leaves, thorns, and a bamboo tube filled with water, then tied the mouth of the sack shut and fled home. Meanwhile, the Ogress returned, saw the sack looking as it was before, and hoisted it onto her shoulder to continue on her way.

After walking a short distance, water from the bamboo tube trickled down onto the Ogress's body. The Ogress thought the boy was urinating on her. So, every time water trickled down, the Ogress would say: "Hmph. You are wetting me now. I am going to eat you".

When a thorn pricked her, she would say: "Hmph. You are pricking me with thorns. I will teach you a lesson". "Hut-put, oh human's son, I will take revenge for what you've done".

When she was hurt by a clod of earth, she would say: "You are kicking me. I will deal with you". "Hut-put, oh human's son, I will take revenge for what you've done".

In this way, burning with rage, the Ogress reached her own home. "Hut-put, oh human's son, I will take revenge for what you've done".

Shouting this, she tore open the sack. And when, instead of the human boy, she saw clods of earth, a bamboo tube, thorns, and leaves inside the sack, she became furious and began to scream bizarrely.

From the next day on, she again began to coax the boy from the pitha tree in various ways. When she was trying to persuade him, the Ogress spoke so sweetly that anyone's heart was bound to melt. And so, just like before, the Ogress captured the boy one day and took him away.

As she walked, the Ogress fumed with rage, remembering the boy's previous escape. She reached home thinking about how she would cut the boy into pieces and eat him.

"Wife, wife, Ghutila!" she called out, shouting, "Quickly, cut this son of man into pieces and cook him". The wife sent word to everyone at her father's house through her husband. Taking the sack to the kitchen, the wife opened its mouth and took the boy out. And with the others, she began preparations to cook him.

The boy, looking around, saw a huge cleaver. Without delay, he chopped off the head of the Ogress's daughter-in-law, Ghutila. Then he quickly put on Ghutila's clothes and disguised himself as the wife. Pulling the veil down low, he cooked the Ogress's daughter-in-law.

Everyone was present, eager to eat. The Ogress sat down to eat with her daughter-in-law's mother, father, and sister. While eating, the wife's sister exclaimed, "Mother, mother, look, this hand is just like my sister's hand". Everyone burst into loud laughter.

The girl said again, "Mother, mother, look at the eye, it is exactly like my sister's". Everyone scolded her, calling her "silly, silly".

In this way, before the meal was even finished, the boy, disguised as the wife, pretended to go fetch betel leaves and went to the riverbank with a water pitcher. At the river landing, he found a merchant. He told the merchant his whole story. The merchant, taking pity on him, took him onto his boat.

Meanwhile, after the meal, not finding the Ogress's daughter-in-law, everyone came to the riverbank. Seeing the boy in the wife's disguise from a distance, they thought, "There is the daughter-in-law". Before they could get close, the boy got into the merchant's boat and threw off the Ogress's wife's clothes.

The Ogress's party had no trouble understanding what had happened. The Ogress's son, and the wife's mother, father, and sister all began to wail loudly. The Ogress, in a rage, started tearing out her hair.

The merchant's boat set off. The freed boy began to chant a rhyme:

"Ogress, Ogress, howl and whine,

On your son's wife's flesh you dine!"
