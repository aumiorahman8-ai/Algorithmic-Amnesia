A father had five sons and one daughter. One of the sons proposed that they should perform some feat that would bring them renown in society. Together, they decided to cut a ghila creeper in a single stroke of a machete. The father himself made his five sons swear an oath that whoever could not do it in one stroke would be made to live in a hut by the path where tigers roamed. The next day, everyone sharpened their machetes properly. Suddenly, when their father went to answer the call of nature, the five brothers took the opportunity to conspire and blunt the edge of their father's machete by rubbing it with a stone. The five sons went to the ghila creeper cutting competition with their sufficiently sharp machetes. Each of them cut the ghila creeper in a single stroke, but their father could not. He struck the machete again, but still failed. Then the youngest son questioned him, "Father, even if you gave your word, you shouldn't do such a terrible thing." The father said, "A man has one word, not two. Build me a hut by the side of the tiger's path." The five sons built a hut at the edge of the tiger's path, and one afternoon, after leaving their father there, the sons all returned to their own homes. Then, in the evening, with fearsome roaring sounds, the tigers began to arrive.

At that, their father shouted out, "I am the father of five brave sons. My body is shaped like a bottle. My teeth are like the door of a chicken coop. My eyes are as big as a ball of thread. I am that man." As he roared, "Where are you?" the tigers fled. The next morning, his only daughter came with food and called out, "Father, are you there?" The father replied, "Yes, I am. Come in, my daughter." In this way, the daughter would bring food in the morning and evening. The father scared away the tigers in the same manner for two nights, but on the third night, sleep overcame him. Taking this opportunity, the tigers came and killed him in his sleep. In the morning, his daughter called out as she did every day, "Father, Father, are you there? I've brought food." But there was no response from inside the hut. Even after the girl repeatedly called "Father, Father," there was no answer from her father.

She returned home and told her elder brothers everything that had happened. "Listen, brothers, Father is no longer alive." Then the five brothers said, "This cannot be. We must take revenge for our father's murder." To avenge their father, the five brothers prepared their bows and arrows, sharpening them. The next day, they set off towards the tigers' lair. On the way, they passed many villages and neighborhoods, finally reaching the rooster village. A rooster asked, "Where are you going, five brothers?" They replied, asking if they would be able to avenge their father's murder. The rooster said, "As soon as I sit on the ladder and crow, you must shoot me with an arrow from your bow. If you can hit me, you can take my meat as food for your journey." The five brothers, pleased, prepared to shoot with their bows and arrows. As promised, the rooster sat on a high ladder and as soon as it crowed "Ku-ku-ru-ku," the five brothers shot their arrows. But none of them could hit it.

In this way, the five brothers passed through the rooster village and reached the Mala pig village. There too, the same question was asked. "Where are you going, five brothers?" The five brothers gave the same reply: to avenge their father's murder. "Let's see if you can avenge your father or not." The situation was the same as with the rooster; no one could hit the Mala pig with an arrow. Even so, the Mala pig told them in a gentle tone, "Will you be able to avenge your father's murder?" They declared, "We will." In this way, they passed through the cowherd village and the cow village. Everyone had the same question, and the five brothers called out in a soft tone, "Auntie, guests have arrived at the house. Open the door." From inside the house, a Debi replied, "I am sick. I cannot open the door." The guests entered her house via the rooster road and the pig road. It was late afternoon. At the five brothers' humble request, Debi opened the door. She cooked rice for them. As a curry, she also cooked their father's flesh. After eating, the five brothers praised Debi highly for her cooking. The night grew late. It was time to sleep. Debi said to the five brothers, "Sons, there are various mice in my house. Give me your arrows, and I will keep them safe." The five brothers, in simple faith, handed the arrows to Debi. After they fell asleep, Debi destroyed the sharpness of their arrows with her teeth. In the morning, Debi asked the five brothers, "What do you want? The slippery slope of a tree or the slippery slope of high ground?" They said, "The slippery slope of high ground." Then the five brothers hid behind a high mound of earth. Debi then called her pet dogs (tigers). "Where are you, hungry dogs? Come. Come eat the rest of your ginger." With a roar, the hungry tigers came. The five brothers all shot their arrows one by one. They couldn't kill a single tiger. In the end, the five brothers became food for the tigers.

Hearing this terrible news, their helpless younger sister at home did not know what to do. She had lost her father, and in the end, she lost her five brothers as well. With a destitute heart, she only called out to Pakhiyan (the Creator). One day, while drying paddy on a bamboo platform behind the house, she looked up at the sky and once again remembered the Creator. Just then, an amloki fell from the sky. After eating this amloki, the only younger sister of the five brothers became pregnant. A few days later, she gave birth to a son. She couldn't decide what to name the child. An old, widowed midwife said, "Since the child came from eating an amloki, you might as well name him Suanlo Suanlo (like an amloki)." Everyone agreed in unison. The old woman was praised for the name. Suanlo Suanlo began to grow up slowly. His peers were engrossed in various games. He also wanted to play. One day, he went to his mother and said, "Mother, all my friends are playing, I want to play too. Give me a ghila." His mother replied, "Go outside, look up at the sky and say, 'Give me a ghila.'" Following his mother's words, he looked at the sky and asked for a ghila. A stone ghila fell from the sky. But his friends did not want to include him in their game with this ghila. When struck by the stone ghila, the other children's ghilas would break. For this reason, his friends would say various things to him, even taunting him for being a fatherless child. His mother would console him. "Who says you are a fatherless child? Your father is more merciful than anyone. The one we call Pakhiyan (God) is your father. From now on, you must prepare to avenge the murder of your five uncles." She taught him all the tactics and techniques. On the mountains here, a type of mim is cultivated. Mim looks like wheat. This acila mim is very slippery. No one can easily stand on mim. Suanlo Suanlo practiced by running again and again on the mim. Suanlo Suanlo's mother said, "The time has come. Can you avenge your uncles?" "Ask your father in the sky for a bow, arrows, and other necessary things. His things are very strong."

Suanlo Suanlo set off to avenge his uncles. Like them, he too passed through the rooster village, the pig village, the cowherd village, and the cow village. Finally, he arrived at Debi's house. Debi said, "Suanlo, there are many creatures in my house that might damage your things. Give them to me, I will keep them safe." He did not agree. When he went to sleep, he slept with the arrows under his pillow. When the night was deep, Debi secretly got up and tried to steal his arrows and blunt their edges. But she could not, as they were made of stone. The next day, Debi said to Suanlo, "What do you want? The slippery slope of high ground or the slippery slope of a barkless tree?" He said, "The slippery slope of a barkless tree." Suanlo climbed up the barkless tree. Debi called her pet dogs (tigers). "Come, come, finish the rest of your food." The tigers began to approach with roars. At that moment, Suanlo was ready with his bow and arrows and, one by one, he killed all the tigers. Finally, the pregnant chief tigress came at him ferociously; he killed her and split her belly open. The cubs inside scattered into the surrounding jungle; porcupines and other wild animals caught and killed the cubs. At that point, Debi was hiding inside the hole of her rice-husking pedal. Pleading profusely, she somehow managed to save her life. Suanlo took revenge for his five uncles as he wished. To show his mother proof, he cut off the head of that chief tigress and brought it home. He arrived to find his dear mother engaged in an improper act inside the house. Suanlo called to his mother, "Mother, Mother, open the door. I have come after avenging my uncles' murders. Mother, open the door." Even after he called three times like this, his mother did not open the door. With a heart full of sorrow, Suanlo said, "Mother, since you do not want to see my face, I will not stay either. Here is the proof of my revenge for my uncles' murders—the tigress's head and a rooster chick. Look at them when you feel like it." Saying this, Suanlo went outside the house, looked up at the sky, and said, "Father, lower a ladder for me." Then a ladder descended from the sky. Suanlo climbed that ladder and disappeared into the sky. His mother, despite much crying and wailing, could not see him again. She saw the head of the tigress her son had hunted and the rooster chick. Then, while handling the tiger's head, her finger was suddenly cut by a tooth. From the incessant bleeding, Suanlo's mother eventually fell into the arms of death.
