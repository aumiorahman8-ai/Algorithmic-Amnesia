In a certain country, there was a prince. The prince’s beauty lit up the royal palace. People could not say enough about the prince’s virtues.

One day, the prince decided he would travel to foreign lands. The faces of the kingdom’s people grew heavy, the queen gave up food and sleep, only the king said, “Very well, let him go.”

Then the people of the country prepared themselves in droves,
The king provided spies and attendants,
The queen came with platters of gems and jewels.

The prince took no people, no gems and jewels, no spies or attendants with him. Dressed in new clothes, with a new sword hanging by his side, the prince set out to see the world.

On and on and on he went, past many countries, many mountains, many rivers, and the kingdoms of many kings, until the prince arrived in the middle of a forest! He saw that in the forest there was no sound of birds, no sign of tigers or bears! The prince continued to walk.

Walking on and on, after going a great distance, the prince saw that in the middle of the forest there was a royal palace—a palace without end. The prince had never seen such a palace before! Seeing it, the prince stood there in amazement.

The spire of the palace gate touched the sky. The gateway to the palace spanned the entire forest. But no music played from the spire, and there was no guard at the gate.

The prince slowly went inside the royal palace.

Going inside, he saw that the palace was so clean it seemed as if it were washed in milk—gleaming brightly. But in such a palace, there was not a single soul, not a sound of anything could be heard. The palace was still and silent—not a leaf fell, not a speck of dust moved.

The prince was astonished.

The prince looked this way and that, taking in all four sides of the palace. At one place, the prince stopped short! He saw a vast courtyard, and filling the courtyard stood elephants, horses, sepoys, soldiers, gatekeepers, guards, troops, and commanders, all standing in rows!

The prince called out! No one spoke, no one turned to look at him.

Amazed, the prince went closer and saw that the rows upon rows of sepoys and soldiers, the rows upon rows of elephants and horses, had all become statues of stone. No one’s eyes blinked, not a hair on anyone’s body moved. The prince stood there in astonishment.

Then the prince went into the main palace.

Entering one chamber, he saw that it was filled with all kinds of shields and swords, bows and arrows, all hanging by the thousands. The guards were stone statues, the sepoys were stone statues. The prince unbuckled his own sword and slowly walked on.

Entering another chamber, he saw a great royal court. In the court, ghee lamps burned brightly in golden lamps, and gems and jewels sparkled all around. But on the royal throne, the king was a stone statue; in the minister’s seat, the minister was a stone statue; the courtiers, companions, bards, and attendants, the sepoys and soldiers—whoever was wherever, was a stone statue. No one’s eyes blinked, no one’s lips spoke.

The prince saw that the royal canopy over the king’s head was tilted, the fly-whisk in the maidservant’s hand was drooping—there was no sign of life, no sound, all were in a deep, silent slumber. The prince bowed his head and walked on.

Entering another chamber, he saw what looked like hundreds of lamps burning at once—so many kinds of treasures, so many diamonds, so many rubies, so many pearls—the chamber could not hold them all. The prince did not touch anything; after looking, he went on to another chamber.

He had barely entered that chamber when the fragrance of thousands upon thousands of flowers overwhelmed him. Where was such a floral scent coming from? The prince went inside and saw that, with no water or soil in sight, the middle of the chamber was filled with hundreds of thousands of blooming lotus flowers! The fragrance of the lotuses filled the room. The prince slowly approached the flower garden.

Approaching the flower garden, the prince saw that in the garden of flowers was a golden bedstead, on the golden bedstead was a diamond post, from the diamond post a garland of flowers was swaying; and under that garland, on a diamond stem, was a golden lotus, and in the golden lotus, a supremely beautiful princess was sleeping soundly. The sleeping princess’s hands could not be seen, her feet could not be seen, only her moonbeam-like face shimmered within the golden petals of the golden lotus. Leaning on the diamond post with its pearl tassels, the prince gazed in wonder.

As he watched and watched and watched, many years passed. The princess’s sleep did not break, and the prince’s eyes did not blink. The princess slept in a deep slumber, and the prince watched, captivated.

Suddenly one day, the prince saw a golden stick by the princess’s head! The prince slowly picked up the golden stick.

As soon as he picked up the golden stick, he saw on the other side a silver stick. The prince, surprised, picked up the silver stick as well. Holding the two sticks, the prince turned them over and examined them.

While he was looking, the golden stick somehow tapped lightly on the sleeping princess’s head! At once, the lotus garden trembled, the golden bedstead stirred, the golden petals fell away, and the princess’s hands and feet appeared; stretching her limbs and rubbing her eyes, the sleeping princess sat up with a start.

And at that very moment, all around the royal palace, birds began to sing, the guard at the gate shouted his call, elephants and horses in the courtyard trumpeted, and the sepoys’ swords clanged; in the royal court, the king awoke, the minister awoke, the courtiers awoke—from a thousand-year sleep, wherever they were, they awoke. Soldiers and guards, troops and commanders stood ready with their bows and swords.

Everyone was astonished—who had come to the royal palace!

The prince was astonished; the princess stared in amazement.

The king, minister, and all the people of the court came and saw—the prince and princess bowed their heads. All around the palace, drums and pipes began to play!

The king said, “Prince of which fortunate king from which country are you, who has saved us from the clutches of a deathly sleep!”

The people said, “Oh! Which divine prince of which god-king are you—a demon had touched us with a silver stick and put our magnificent golden kingdom to sleep, and you have come and awakened us, saving us.”

The prince bowed his head and remained silent.

The king said, “What do I have, what can I give? I give this princess into your hands, I give this kingdom to you.”

A rain of flowers fell all around, a rain of sandalwood; flowers bloomed, puffed rice was scattered—the thousand drums of the royal palace beat with a ‘dum-dum’ sound.

Then, hundreds of maids ground spices, thousands of servants chopped vegetables;

At every door, an auspicious pot,
A bouquet of five-leafed sprigs and flowers;
Alpana drawings, throngs of married women,
Around the ceremonial seats, the conch shell sounds.

What a sight!—The four courtyards of the palace shimmered and shone. In every courtyard, there were ululations of joy, the royal treasury was thrown open; there was a rush of people, and the long-sleeping palace now trembled with activity, tumultuous with happiness.

After that, in the bright moonlight, with the fire-priest before them, and with betel nut, betel leaf, and the entire kingdom as a dowry, the king placed a five-jeweled crown on the prince’s head and gave the princess to him in marriage.

Cries of celebration rose from all around.

One year, two years, year after year passed—the prince who had gone to see the world still had not returned. Weeping and beating her head, the queen had taken to her bed. Worrying and shedding tears, the king had become blind. The kingdom was dark, filled with despair.

One day, before dawn had broken, drums and pipes sounded at the royal gate. The gate trembled with the calls of elephants, horses, sepoys, and guards!

The queen said, “What, what is it?”
The king said, “Who, who is it?”

The subjects of the kingdom came running. The prince had returned, bringing the princess he had married!!

Trembling, the king came and took the prince in his arms. Stumbling, the queen came and welcomed the princess.

The subjects raised a cheer of joy.

The prince touched the golden stick to the king’s eyes, and the king’s sight was restored. Seeing her son and her son’s wife, the queen’s illness was cured.

Then, with the prince and the princess from the sleeping palace, the king and queen ruled their kingdom in happiness.
