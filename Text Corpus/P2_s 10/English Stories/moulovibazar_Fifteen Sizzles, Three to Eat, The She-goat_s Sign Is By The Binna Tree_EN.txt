A husband and wife were quarreling. The wife was making pithas, and every time she placed one on the griddle, it made a sizzling sound. The husband, listening, counted this sound fifteen times. The wife served her husband three pithas. Seeing the three pithas, the husband said to his wife, "Fifteen sizzles, three to eat."

The wife thought to herself, "I made the pithas in the kitchen; how did my husband know the count was fifteen? He must have become a fortune-teller!" The wife announced to everyone in the neighborhood that her husband was a fortune-teller and could find anything that was lost. A woman in the neighborhood had lost her goat. Unable to find it anywhere, she went to the man's house and said angrily, "Sir! Your wife says you can divine things. My goat is lost and I can't find it. Can't you use your powers to see where my goat is?" The irritated husband thought to himself that this was all his wife's doing. He told the woman to come back in the afternoon. After the woman left, the man went out of his house to search for the goat. After a long search, he found the goat near a binna tree. The man returned home and waited. When the woman arrived in the afternoon, the man said, "Fifteen sizzles, three to eat, the she-goat's sign is by the binna tree." Following the man's words, the woman went to the binna tree and found her black goat. From that day on, the man was known as Gonok Thakur, the Revered Fortune-teller.
