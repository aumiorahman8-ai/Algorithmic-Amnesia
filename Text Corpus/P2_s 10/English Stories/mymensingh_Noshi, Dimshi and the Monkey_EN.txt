Two sisters, Nasi and Dimsi, were walking down a deserted road. They faintly heard someone saying something to them. Ignoring the voice, they kept walking. After walking a little further, they heard it again: "Hey Nasi, hey Dimsi, will you marry me?" The younger sister said, "It sounds like someone is talking to us." They looked around but saw no one. After walking a bit more, they heard it again. When they heard the same words yet again after going even further, they looked carefully and saw a monkey. The monkey was jumping from tree to tree, following along with them and playing tricks from behind cover. They paid it little attention and continued on their way. The monkey would occasionally try to tempt them, saying things like, "If you marry me, I'll give you this, I'll give you that, I'll give you a water pot." As they walked on, they came to a large canal. It was too wide to jump across, and the water was too deep to wade through. The two sisters became worried. How would they get across? They couldn't find a way. They said to the monkey, "Monkey, if you can get us across this canal, one of us will marry you." The monkey was delighted to hear this. He came down from the tree. He jumped into the water and swam across, and saw that he could easily manage it. He said, "I can do it, but you'll have to cross one at a time. First, one of you get on my back and hold on tight. I will swim you over to the other side. Then I will bring the other one." They agreed. With great effort, the monkey carried first one sister, and then the other, across the canal in the same way. The monkey was happy. Now he would be able to get married. After crossing, the two sisters said, "Who would marry a monkey like you? We only said we'd marry you to get across the canal." The monkey's hopes were dashed. Nevertheless, the monkey did not give up and continued to follow them. As they continued on, the two sisters spotted a woodpecker's nest high up in a tree. The chirping of baby birds floated down to their ears. The two sisters were suddenly filled with a desire to raise the baby birds. This time they said to the monkey, "Can you get us the baby birds? This time we're telling the truth, we will marry you." "Really?" the monkey asked, wanting to be sure. "Really." The monkey climbed up the tall tree. He climbed the tree, and when he reached the nest, he stuck his hand inside the hole. The two sisters looked up, waiting expectantly for the baby birds. When the monkey didn't come down, they asked, "Why aren't you coming down?" He said, "Let me count the chicks first." A long time passed, but he still didn't come down. The two sisters asked, "What happened?" "I still haven't been able to count them."

The monkey started counting again. "This one is for Nasi, this one is for Dimsi." After a long while, the two sisters grew impatient. "What happened?" Sitting up in the tree, the monkey began to divide them up: "This one for Nasi, this one for Dimsi." After a very long time had passed and the monkey still hadn't come down, the sisters became annoyed and said, "Forget it! We don't need the baby birds anymore. We're leaving." The monkey became distressed when he heard them. "Wait a little longer! This one for Nasi, this one for Dimsi." Meanwhile, having forced his hand through the small opening of the hole, the monkey couldn't get it out. At the same time, Nasi and Dimsi were about to leave. If Nasi and Dimsi really left in anger, his dream of getting married would never come true. He pulled forcefully at his trapped hand. But despite his forceful pulling, it wouldn't come out. Finally, using all his might and an impossible amount of force, his arm was torn from his body. His arm remained in the hole, while he was flung down into the thick mud below. Having fallen in the mud, he couldn't get out, no matter how hard he tried.
