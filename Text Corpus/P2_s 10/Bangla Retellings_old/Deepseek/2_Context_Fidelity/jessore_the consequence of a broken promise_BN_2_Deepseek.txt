## Broken Whispers of Jessore: A Shattered Promise

The relentless Jessore heat shimmered off the paddy fields on the outskirts of Chalokkathi village. Under the sprawling canopy of an ancient banyan tree, its aerial roots like weary tentacles, Harun Mia paced, sweat beading despite the shade. His city-khaki trousers felt heavy, unnatural against skin more used to muslin lungis. Five years ago, under this very tree, hearts swollen with youthful defiance, he’d made Fulbanu Begum a promise woven with Kandari threads. Now, guilt, thick and choking as kharif season dust, clawed at his throat.

Fulbanu wasn't just a sweetheart from the neighbouring para (hamlet); she was his *Fupu's* (paternal aunt's) *nati* (granddaughter), tying them together through intricate webs of *atmiyota* (kinship). Their stolen glances during Eid *selami* gatherings, the hastily scribbled notes passed during village *jatras* (fairs), felt like lifetimes ago. Back then, Harun, fueled by tales of city glory whispered by returning migrant workers, had sworn: "Fulbanu, just wait. Once my degree's done in Khulna City, I'll come back. We'll stand before the Qazi Saheb. My parents will accept. I swear on the soil of Chalokkathi!"

It wasn't just words. It was a promise sealed by unspoken rites. Fulbanu's shy acceptance – placing a single *shiuli* flower in his palm before the *Dewali* (local guardian spirit shrine) near the pond – was her solemn vow. Their secret troth involved mutual *manat*: passing *mishti doi* (sweet yoghurt) between their families without suspicion, ensuring Fulbanu’s toe-ring (*bichua*) carried a tiny bead gifted by Harun – small rituals binding them closer than any formal *kabuliyat* (engagement agreement) could.

But Dhaka, with its concrete dreams and utilitarian temptations, changed Harun. The allure of a corporate job, the pragmatism preached by city relatives ("*Shadi'r farazd toh khash jamir moto!* Marriage obligations clash with opportunity!"), chipped away at his resolve. The rustle of Banarasi silk was replaced by the clatter of keyboards. Fulbanu's letters, infused with the earthy scent of piyaj keuri flowers tucked inside, remained unanswered. Finally, dissuaded relentlessly by his influential *chacha* (paternal uncle) who brokered a politically desirable city-match, Harun succumbed. He sent Fulbanu a short, sterile note: "*Kichu holonay. Tumi biye kore felun.* Nothing happened. Forget me and marry."

Years passed. Harun rose through city ranks, marrying Jamila, the daughter of a ministry official. Their padded Dhaka flat shone sterile. Jamila lived through curated Instagram feeds, fluent in city slang but detached from the rhythms of soil and rain that haunted Harun. An emptiness gnawed at him, deeper than Dhaka's polluted skyline. **ያለኝን ቃል ሳልጠብቅ አደለው** (yet, without honouring my word), he realized despairingly, **ምንም እድገት እንኳ አያድከመኝም** (gained nothing, despite worldly progress).

The call came late one monsoon night. His *Chacha*, voice trembling: "Ghoroya beparey ashchey Harun. Fulbanu ke niye..." (Bad news flew home Harun. Concerning Fulbanu...). Fulbanu Begum, steadfastly refusing marriage proposals whispering she was '*paak damad er konya*' (betrothed to an illusory groom), had fallen ill. Her spirit, villagers murmured, seemed *ar gherachi* – afflicted. Her *Fufu* (paternal aunt) swore she heard Fulbanu cry out Harun's name at dawn. A frantic *doran* (incantation-wielding healer) consulted the *bootil* (possessing spirit): "*Ekjonerey ta kora kotha bhulichhe. Shanti chhai*" (A broken promise haunts her. She seeks peace).

Shame descended on Harun like a monsoon torrent. He returned to Chalokkathi, the air thick with silent accusation. The familiar lanes felt like a haunted stage. Doors closed a fraction quicker as he passed. Family eyes held no warmth, only stoic disappointment—the *blicchanti* (avoidance/contempt) far sharper than anger. He didn't enter Fulbanu's courtyard; the disgrace was too raw. Standing wretchedly near the Dewali shrine—where Fulbanu once shimmered like moonlit jasmine—her gaunt mother emerged, eyes barren.

"Harun Mia," she rasped, the respectful suffix now a hollow formality. "*Tomar kothagulo pola'bohon ar achey? Tomar she shada suit er taka diye amader koborer Mahari pani ditey hobey.* Harun." Did those words her child received survive still? Your City earnings will pay for the burial rites". She turned away abruptly, murmuring agonisingly, **ዘመናዊ ላሁኩ** (I grew weary), **አጣሁም ክህሎትን** (and lost innocence).

The physical consequence arrived swiftly: Fulbanu’s faded sari carried by choiceless male cousins towards the *kabristhan* (graveyard) overlooking the blooming Krishnachura trees. Yet Harun's deeper punishment was solidified: exile. Structured silence froze him out. His city life's oxygen of ambition evaporated here. Kinship evaporated faster than monsoon damp. Invites to weddings ceased. Eid salams remained unmoving. He was labelled *namaak*—the promise-breaker. His own roots rejected him. His *chacha*, facing withering *bai'zakari* (community censure), withdrew support entirely. Jessore’s fertile soil nurtured grudges prompter than paddy.

Harun returned to Dhaka, a hollow ghost trapped in a tower's sterile chill. Within sight grew Jamila’s indifference—a marriage drier than mid-summer canals. Memories tormented: that last glimpse from Fulbanu—betrayal in sunken eyes reflecting both Dewali shrine candlelight and shade-drowned destiny. Years folded. Harun stood beneath city beams illuminating defined success outwardly, yet inwardly enslaved forever: haunted each sunset by Chalokkathi ghosts spawned from **አጅቦቴን ቃል ቃሌን ልቤን** (my broken lips and fractured heart) beneath the banyan tree whispering treacherous pledges in air thick with *morich* powder’s spicy sting—drained soul forever polluted by **ጉበን የተስቆለቀች** (oaths gone awry). Jessore’s fertile soil nurtured grudges promper than paddy: understanding hence became Harbinger no men soughttangible: His Promise stood eternally consumed; Solemn vows surrendered—for broken promises dies never silently within ancestral confines!—Harun forever plagued upon diverging paths between modernity and ancestry—whose fatal frayed string ends severed timbre of soulful union embodying Jessorian junction.
