A boy, orphaned young and raised by his sorrowful mother in hardship, loved pithas above all. As her means dwindled, on the last day she could afford to make one, she planted it in the courtyard—and miraculously, a tree sprouted, bearing delicious pithas on its branches. The boy thrived, eating happily from the tree.

Soon, an Ogress, disguised as a woman, began visiting. She lure the boy with sweet words, demanding pithas—but when he offered one, she rejected it, insisting he hand it directly, then tried to seize him. One day, she succeeded: she captured him, bound him, stuffed him into a sack, and carried him off. Midway, when she left to relieve herself, he tore the sack open, escaped, and cleverly refilled it with earth, leaves, thorns, and a water-filled bamboo tube before returning home.

The Ogress resumed her walk, mistaking the sack’s movements and sensations for the boy’s resistance—thinking he was urinating, kicking, or pricking her—and grew furious, vowing revenge. Upon opening the sack at her home and finding only tricks instead of the boy, she raged and redoubled her efforts to capture him.

She lured him again, captured him once more, and upon reaching home, ordered her daughter-in-law, Ghutila, to cut and cook him. But the boy, spotting a cleaver, decapitated Ghutila, donned her clothes and veil, and cooked her instead. At the feast, as the Ogress and her in-laws ate, the boy’s sister recognized Ghutila’s hand and eye—but was scolded as silly.

Before Dessert, the boy, pretending to fetch betel leaves, fled with a water pitcher to the riverbank, where he met a merchant. He told his story, and the merchant took him aboard his boat.

The Ogress’s party soon arrived, spotting the disguised boy at a distance—thinking he was Ghutila. Before they could react, he climbed into the merchant’s boat, discarded the disguise, and escaped.

Realizing the deception, the Ogress’s relatives wailed and tore their hair; the Ogress herself tore out her own hair in rage.

As the merchant’s boat sailed away, the freed boy chanted:

> *Ogress, Ogress, howl and whine,*  
> *On your son's wife's flesh you dine!*
