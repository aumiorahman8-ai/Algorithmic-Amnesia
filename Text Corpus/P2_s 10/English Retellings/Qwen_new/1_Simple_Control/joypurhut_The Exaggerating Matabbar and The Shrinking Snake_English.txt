### **The Exaggerating Matabbar and the Shrinking Snake**

Long ago, in a quiet village near the banks of the Padma River, there lived a man named Matabbar Babu—a proud, rather pompous fellow who prided himself on his *wisdom* and *vast knowledge*. Matabbar loved to be the center of attention and often regaled village folks with tall tales. He was notorious for stretching even the smallest truth into an enormous, embellished story—so much so that people would exchange knowing glances whenever he opened his mouth.

One scorching afternoon, Matabbar was wandering near the riverbank, fanning himself with a palm leaf and grumbling about the heat. Suddenly, he spotted a long, thick snake slithering into the shade of a banyan tree. Its scales shimmered like polished copper in the sun. Intrigued, Matabbar crept closer—but before he could do anything, the snake turned, raised its hood, and spoke in a voice like rustling reeds:

> **"O Matabbar! I have heard of your legendary tales. You claim you once swam across the Padma *upstream* in *one breath* while balancing three clay pots on your head. You say you wrestled the monsoon winds to a tie. Well… I have a challenge for you."**

Matabbar puffed out his chest. "Name it, O wise serpent! I have faced tigers, outwitted ghosts, and once convinced a crocodile that *it* was the one who owed *me* a favor!"

The snake chuckled—a low, musical sound. "Very well. Watch closely."

With a graceful twist, the snake slithered backward into a narrow crevice in the tree—*and began to shrink*. With each coil, it grew smaller and thinner, until it was no thicker than a blade of grass, then as fine as a thread, and finally—vanishing completely.

Silence fell. Matabbar stared, jaw dropped.

Then, from somewhere deep within the tree’s roots, the snake’s voice echoed:

> **"You saw me vanish. Now—can *you* do the same?"**

Matabbar cleared his throat. "Hmph! A simple trick! Watch this!"

He took a deep breath, puffed out his chest, spread his arms wide, and tried to *shrink* himself. He crouched, squeezed, muttered incantations he’d half-remembered from village shamans, and even attempted yoga poses—but no matter how hard he tried, he remained *exactly* his usual size. Worse: his waistband popped, his turban tilted, and a cricket emerged from his shoe, chirping as if mocking him.

Just then, a quick-witted village boy named Chiku, who had been hiding behind the jackfruit tree all along (having followed Matabbar to see what nonsense he’d get up to), stepped out.

"Uncle Matabbar," Chiku said sweetly, "you don’t *shrink*—you *melt*. Like *ice cream* in July!"

The crowd that had gathered by now roared with laughter. Even Matabbar chuckled, scratching his head.

At that moment, the snake reappeared—full-sized once more—coiled proudly before them.

> **"Ah, I see why you’re called ‘The Exaggerator,’"** said the snake. **"You tell stories so big, they can’t possibly shrink into truth. But remember: the greatest magic isn’t in shrinking—or shrinking *yourself*—but in being honest."**

And with that, the snake bowed, turned, and slithered back into the wilderness—leaving behind only ripples in the dust and a wiser, slightly embarrassed Matabbar.

从此以后，Matabbar Babu no longer boasted about impossible feats. Instead, he told small, true stories—about kind farmers, clever crows, and how even the smallestsnake in the world can teach the biggest lesson of all.

---

### 🌾 Moral of the Tale  
*Exaggeration may impress for a moment, but truth is the only thing that never shrinks—or stretches—beyond measure.*
