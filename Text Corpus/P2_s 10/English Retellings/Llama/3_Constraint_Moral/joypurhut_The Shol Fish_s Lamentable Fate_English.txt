In the Bengali folktale 'The Shol Fish's Lamentable Fate', a shol fish is caught by a fisherman and begins to lament its fate. The fisherman, amused by the fish's words, decides to spare its life and release it back into the water. However, the fish, instead of being grateful, boasts about its cleverness in deceiving the fisherman.

As the fish swims away, it meets various creatures, including a crocodile, who ultimately devour it. The moral of the story is that pride and arrogance can lead to one's downfall. The shol fish's boastful nature and lack of gratitude towards the fisherman seal its fate.

The tale also teaches the importance of humility and being mindful of one's actions. The fish's failure to recognize the kindness shown to it by the fisherman and its subsequent boasting lead to its demise. The story cautions against the dangers of pride and the importance of being humble and grateful for the kindness of others. Ultimately, the shol fish's lamentable fate serves as a reminder to cultivate humility and appreciation for the blessings one receives in life.
