In the Bengali folktale of 'The Pitha Tree and Rakkhoshi,' a poor farmer plants a pitha tree, which attracts a Rakkhoshi, a female demon. The Rakkhoshi offers to grant the farmer's wishes in exchange for food and shelter. The farmer, blinded by greed, wishes for wealth and luxury, but the Rakkhoshi's gifts come with a terrible price: the farmer's family and friends begin to disappear.

The moral of the story is that greed and materialism can lead to destruction and loss. The farmer's insatiable desires ultimately destroy the very things that truly matter in life: relationships and loved ones. The tale also highlights the importance of contentment and gratitude. The farmer's initial happiness with his simple life is replaced by an unquenchable thirst for more, demonstrating that true happiness comes from within and from appreciating what one already has.

The story also teaches the value of being mindful of one's actions and their consequences. The farmer's reckless wishes have devastating effects, illustrating the need for careful consideration and responsibility in one's decisions. Ultimately, the tale of 'The Pitha Tree and Rakkhoshi' cautions against the dangers of unchecked ambition and the importance of prioritizing what truly brings fulfillment and joy in life.
