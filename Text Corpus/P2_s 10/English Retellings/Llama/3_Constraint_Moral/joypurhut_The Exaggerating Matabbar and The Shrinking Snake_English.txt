In the Bengali folktale "The Exaggerating Matabbar and The Shrinking Snake," a boastful matabbar (village headman) claims to have caught a massive snake, exaggerating its size to impress the villagers. Meanwhile, a snake nearby overhears the matabbar's boasts and, feeling embarrassed for being much smaller, begins to shrink further with each exaggeration.

The moral of the story is that excessive pride and boasting can lead to one's downfall. The matabbar's constant exaggeration not only makes him a laughingstock but also affects the snake, causing it to lose its confidence and shrink away. The tale teaches the importance of humility and honesty, warning against the dangers of inflating one's accomplishments.

The story also highlights the value of self-acceptance and self-esteem. The snake's shrinking serves as a metaphor for how negative self-perception and comparison to others can erode one's confidence. By embracing one's true nature and abilities, individuals can avoid the pitfalls of excessive pride and low self-esteem, leading to a more authentic and fulfilling life. The folktale offers a lighthearted yet thought-provoking lesson on the importance of humility, honesty, and self-acceptance.
