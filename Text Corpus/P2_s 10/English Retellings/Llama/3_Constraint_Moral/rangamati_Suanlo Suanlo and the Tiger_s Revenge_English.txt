In the Bengali folktale 'Suanlo Suanlo and the Tiger's Revenge', a clever hunter named Suanlo outsmarts a tiger by tricking it into believing it's a cat. The tiger, feeling embarrassed, vows to take revenge. However, Suanlo's cleverness ultimately leads to the tiger's downfall.

The story teaches several morals. Firstly, it highlights the importance of intelligence and wit in overcoming challenges. Suanlo's cleverness saves him from the tiger's clutches, demonstrating that brains can be more powerful than brawn.

Secondly, the tale warns against the dangers of pride and revenge. The tiger's obsession with avenging its embarrassment ultimately leads to its own demise, showing that excessive pride and a desire for revenge can be self-destructive.

Lastly, the story promotes the value of humility and forgiveness. If the tiger had let go of its pride and forgiven Suanlo, it might have avoided its fate. The tale encourages readers to prioritize humility and forgiveness over pride and revenge, promoting a more peaceful and harmonious coexistence. Overall, 'Suanlo Suanlo and the Tiger's Revenge' offers valuable lessons on the importance of intelligence, humility, and forgiveness.
