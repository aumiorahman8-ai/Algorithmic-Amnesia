In the verdant countryside of Mymensingh, a region steeped in the rich cultural heritage of Bengal, there lived a young girl named Khuki. She resided with her father, Baba, and her stepmother, Step-Ma, in a small village surrounded by lush green fields and winding rivers. Khuki's mother, Ma, had passed away when she was just a child, and her father had remarried soon after.

Step-Ma, whose name was Jhorna, was a woman of strong will and determination. She had brought her own daughter, Didi, from her previous marriage, and the two of them had settled into their new life with Baba and Khuki. Initially, Jhorna had tried to be kind and affectionate towards Khuki, but as time passed, her behavior became increasingly cold and distant.

Despite this, Khuki continued to address Jhorna as Step-Ma, showing respect and deference to her position as her father's wife. However, Jhorna's actions were motivated by a misguided sense of affection for her own daughter, Didi. She would often favor Didi over Khuki, giving her the best food, clothes, and attention, while neglecting Khuki's needs.

One day, during the festival of Nobanno, which celebrates the new harvest, Jhorna decided to prepare a special meal for the family. She spent hours in the kitchen, cooking a variety of traditional dishes, including pitha, payesh, and shorshe ilish. However, when it was time to serve the food, she deliberately gave Khuki a smaller portion and a less desirable dish, while Didi received the choicest items.

Khuki, feeling hurt and neglected, decided to seek the advice of her wise and aged grandmother, Thakur-Ma, who lived in a nearby village. Thakur-Ma, who was known for her knowledge of traditional rituals and customs, listened to Khuki's story and offered her guidance.

Thakur-Ma told Khuki that she should perform a special puja, a ritual worship, to appease the goddess Lakshmi, who is revered as the embodiment of good fortune and prosperity. Thakur-Ma instructed Khuki to prepare a small altar, adorned with flowers, incense, and a diya, a clay lamp, and to offer prayers and mantras to invoke the goddess's blessings.

Khuki followed Thakur-Ma's instructions and performed the puja with devotion and sincerity. As she prayed, she asked the goddess to grant her the love and affection of her stepmother, and to bring peace and harmony to their family.

Miraculously, Jhorna's behavior towards Khuki began to change after the puja. She started to show more kindness and compassion towards Khuki, and the two of them began to bond over their shared love of traditional Bengali cuisine and rituals. During the festival of Saraswati Puja, which celebrates the goddess of knowledge and learning, Jhorna even helped Khuki prepare a beautiful anjali, an offering of flowers and fruits, to the goddess.

As the days passed, Khuki and Jhorna grew closer, and their relationship became more affectionate and loving. Khuki learned to appreciate Jhorna's strengths and weaknesses, and Jhorna, in turn, learned to balance her love and affection between her two daughters.

The story of Khuki and Jhorna serves as a reminder of the importance of kinship ties and the need to respect and honor the relationships that bind us together as a family. It also highlights the significance of traditional rituals and customs in Bengali culture, which play a vital role in promoting social harmony and cohesion.

In the end, Khuki's love and devotion towards her stepmother, as well as her faith in the divine, helped to transform their relationship and bring peace and prosperity to their family. The story of "The Stepmother's Misguided Affection" remains a beloved folktale in Mymensingh, passed down through generations, and continues to inspire and educate people about the importance of family values, respect, and compassion.
