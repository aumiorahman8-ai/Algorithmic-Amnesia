In the lush hills of Rangamati, where the Kaptai Lake sparkles like a sheet of molten silver, there lived a young boy named Suanlo Suanlo. He was the youngest son of a humble Marma family, who made their living by farming and fishing. Suanlo's parents, Thaing Khyang and Ma Ching, loved him dearly, and his elder siblings, Bhaiya and Didi, would often tease him affectionately, calling him "Suanlo Suanlo" - a term of endearment that meant "little brother" in their native Marma language.

One day, while Suanlo Suanlo was out playing in the forest, he stumbled upon a tiger taking a nap in a clearing. The tiger, whose name was Bagh, was a majestic creature with shimmering golden fur and piercing green eyes. Suanlo Suanlo, being a curious and brave boy, decided to test his courage by teasing the tiger. He poked Bagh with a stick, laughing and shouting, "Bagh, Bagh, wake up! Why are you sleeping so soundly?"

Bagh, startled and annoyed, woke up with a growl and chased Suanlo Suanlo back to his village. The boy ran as fast as his legs could carry him, but Bagh was gaining on him. Just when it seemed like all was lost, Suanlo Suanlo's quick thinking saved him. He remembered that tigers are afraid of fire, so he grabbed a burning torch from a nearby hut and waved it at Bagh, shouting, "Go away, Bagh! Don't come any closer!"

Bagh, frightened by the flames, retreated back into the forest, vowing to take revenge on Suanlo Suanlo one day. From that day on, Suanlo Suanlo's family and friends would often warn him, "Suanlo Suanlo, be careful! Bagh is waiting for you in the forest. Don't go out alone, or he'll surely get you!"

But Suanlo Suanlo, being a brave and confident boy, didn't heed their warnings. He continued to explore the forest, playing with his friends and learning about the ancient rituals and customs of his people. He would often accompany his father, Thaing Khyang, to the sacred grove, where they would perform the traditional "Sangrai" ceremony to appease the forest spirits.

As the days passed, Bagh's anger and resentment towards Suanlo Suanlo only grew stronger. One full moon night, when the villagers were busy preparing for the "Bijhu" festival, Bagh decided to take his revenge. He snuck into the village, his eyes fixed on Suanlo Suanlo's hut, where the boy was fast asleep.

But the villagers, who had been warned about Bagh's intentions, had taken precautions to protect Suanlo Suanlo. They had performed a ritual called "Khrai Khrai," where they would chant and sing to ward off evil spirits and protect their loved ones. The ritual, led by the village shaman, Maung Shwe, involved the burning of incense, the beating of drums, and the recitation of ancient prayers.

As Bagh approached Suanlo Suanlo's hut, he was met with a barrage of noise and light. The villagers, armed with torches and sticks, chased him away, shouting, "Bagh, go back! Leave our Suanlo Suanlo alone!" Bagh, realizing that he was no match for the combined efforts of the villagers, retreated back into the forest, his tail between his legs.

From that day on, Suanlo Suanlo was known as the bravest boy in the village. His family and friends would often gather around him, listening in awe as he recounted his adventures with Bagh. And every year, during the "Bijhu" festival, the villagers would reenact the story of Suanlo Suanlo and the tiger's revenge, reminding themselves of the importance of bravery, community, and the ancient rituals that protected them from harm.

As the story goes, Suanlo Suanlo grew up to be a great leader, respected by all for his courage and wisdom. And Bagh, the tiger, never forgot the lesson he learned that full moon night - that the power of community and tradition can be stronger than even the fiercest of beasts.
