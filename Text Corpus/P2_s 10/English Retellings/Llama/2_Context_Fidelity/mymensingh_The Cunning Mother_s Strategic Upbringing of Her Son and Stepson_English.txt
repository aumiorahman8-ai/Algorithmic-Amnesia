In the heart of Mymensingh, a region in Bangladesh, there lived a wise and cunning mother, known as 'Ma' in Bengali. She was a widow who had a son from her previous marriage, and after remarrying, she had a stepson from her husband's previous marriage. The stepson, referred to as 'Dhonyobad' or 'stepbrother' in Bengali, was a spoiled and lazy boy, while Ma's biological son, 'Chele' or 'son,' was kind and hardworking.

Ma, being a clever and resourceful woman, decided to teach her sons the value of hard work and responsibility through a strategic upbringing. She called both her sons and said, "Chele, you will go to the forest and collect firewood, while Dhonyobad, you will stay home and take care of the household chores." However, Ma had a hidden plan.

As Chele went to the forest, Ma gave him a small amount of food and a bottle of water, saying, "Beta, be careful and don't waste your time. You must return before sunset." Meanwhile, she gave Dhonyobad a large amount of food and instructed him to cook and clean the house. But, unbeknownst to Dhonyobad, Ma had intentionally given him a never-ending list of chores, ensuring that he would be busy all day.

As the day progressed, Chele worked tirelessly in the forest, collecting firewood and facing many challenges. He encountered wild animals, crossed rivers, and climbed hills, but he persevered, remembering his mother's words. On the other hand, Dhonyobad struggled to complete the household chores, growing more and more frustrated as the tasks seemed to multiply.

As the sun began to set, Chele returned home, exhausted but proud of his accomplishments. Ma welcomed him with a warm smile and a glass of fresh water, saying, "Chele, you have done a great job. Now, come and rest." She then served him a delicious meal, prepared with love and care.

Dhonyobad, however, was still struggling to complete his tasks. Ma, with a hint of sarcasm, said, "Dhonyobad, you are so lazy! You can't even finish the household chores. How will you survive in life?" Dhonyobad, feeling ashamed and frustrated, realized that he had been outsmarted by his cunning mother.

The next day, Ma reversed the roles, sending Dhonyobad to the forest and keeping Chele at home. But this time, she gave Dhonyobad a small amount of food and a bottle of water, just like she had given Chele the previous day. As Dhonyobad faced the challenges of the forest, he began to understand the value of hard work and perseverance.

Upon his return, Ma welcomed Dhonyobad with a warm smile and a glass of fresh water, saying, "Dhonyobad, you have learned a valuable lesson today. Hard work and responsibility are essential for success in life." She then served him a delicious meal, prepared with love and care, just like she had done for Chele the previous day.

As the days passed, Ma continued to teach her sons valuable lessons, using her cunning and strategic upbringing to shape them into responsible and hardworking individuals. She would often say, "Chele, Dhonyobad, a mother's love is not just about giving, but also about teaching and guiding. Remember, a good mother is like a 'Guru' or teacher, who shows you the right path in life."

During the Bengali festival of 'Bhai Phonta,' which celebrates the bond between brothers and sisters, Ma would perform the traditional ritual of 'Phonta,' where she would apply a vermilion mark on her sons' foreheads, symbolizing her love and blessings. As they grew older, Chele and Dhonyobad became inseparable brothers, working together and supporting each other in all aspects of life.

Ma's strategic upbringing had paid off, and her sons had become responsible and hardworking individuals, thanks to her cunning and loving guidance. The story of the cunning mother from Mymensingh serves as a reminder of the importance of teaching children valuable life lessons, using a combination of love, discipline, and strategic guidance.
