What a delightful tale to retell! 'Neelkomol Lalkomol' is a beloved Bengali folktale from the revered collection of 'Thakurmar Jhuli' (Grandmother's Bag of Tales). This enchanting story has been passed down through generations, and I'll do my best to preserve its original cultural nuances, kinship terms, and specific rituals.

Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a kind and gentle girl named Neelkomol. She was the daughter of a poor but pious Brahmin family. Neelkomol's mother, Ma, was a devoted wife and mother, who spent her days managing the household chores and taking care of her family. Neelkomol's father, Baba, was a learned man who spent most of his time studying the scriptures and performing puja (worship) at the local temple.

One day, while Neelkomol was out collecting flowers in the forest, she stumbled upon a beautiful and mysterious boy named Lalkomol. He was dressed in fine clothes and had a radiant complexion. As they struck up a conversation, Neelkomol learned that Lalkomol was a prince from a neighboring kingdom, who had been cursed by a wicked sorceress. The curse had turned him into a bird, and he could only regain his human form during the day, when the sun shone bright.

Neelkomol, being a kind and compassionate girl, decided to help Lalkomol break the curse. She invited him to her home, where her Ma and Baba welcomed him with open arms. As they sat down to eat, Neelkomol's Ma served them a traditional Bengali meal of rice, dal, and vegetables, along with some sweet payesh (rice pudding) for dessert.

As the days passed, Neelkomol and Lalkomol grew closer, and their bond became stronger. They would spend hours talking, laughing, and playing together. Neelkomol's Ma and Baba, seeing the love and affection between the two, decided to perform a special puja to help Lalkomol break the curse.

On the day of the puja, Neelkomol's Ma prepared a beautiful altar with flowers, incense, and diyas (earthen lamps). She also cooked a special meal of khichuri (a mixture of rice and lentils) and beguni (fried eggplant), which is traditionally served during pujas. As they performed the puja, Neelkomol's Baba chanted mantras and offered prayers to the gods, seeking their blessings to break the curse.

As the puja came to an end, a bright light filled the room, and Lalkomol was transformed back into his human form. Overjoyed, Neelkomol and Lalkomol hugged each other, and Neelkomol's Ma and Baba blessed their union.

The story ends with Neelkomol and Lalkomol getting married in a traditional Bengali wedding, surrounded by their friends and family. The wedding ceremony, known as 'biye', was a grand affair, with Neelkomol dressed in a beautiful red and white sari, and Lalkomol wearing a traditional dhoti and kurta. The ceremony included rituals like 'gayé holud' (turmeric paste application), 'sindur daan' (vermilion application), and 'saat paak' (seven steps around the fire).

And so, Neelkomol and Lalkomol lived happily ever after, surrounded by the love and blessings of their family and community. This tale, 'Neelkomol Lalkomol', remains a cherished part of Bengali folklore, reminding us of the power of kindness, compassion, and the importance of preserving our cultural heritage.
