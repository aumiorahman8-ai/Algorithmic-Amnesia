A young boy lived with his mother after his father's passing. He loved pithas, a type of food, and his mother made them for him every day. However, as time passed, it became increasingly difficult for her to do so. One day, she planted a pitha in their courtyard, and to their surprise, a tree grew, bearing pithas on its branches. The boy was overjoyed and spent his days eating the delicious pithas.

But their happiness was short-lived, as an Ogress, disguised as a woman, began to visit the tree, trying to trick the boy into giving her a pitha. She would make excuses and try to grab him, but the boy managed to escape each time. One day, the Ogress finally caught the boy, bound him, and put him in a sack. However, the boy cleverly escaped by filling the sack with earth, leaves, and a bamboo tube filled with water, and then fled home.

The Ogress, unaware of the boy's escape, continued on her way, mistaking the water trickling from the bamboo tube for the boy's urine and the thorns and clods of earth for his attempts to hurt her. When she finally opened the sack and found it empty, she became furious.

The Ogress again tried to capture the boy and eventually succeeded. She took him to her home, intending to eat him, but the boy outsmarted her once more. He disguised himself as the Ogress's daughter-in-law, Ghutila, and cooked her instead. The Ogress and her family unknowingly ate Ghutila, and the boy eventually escaped by pretending to fetch betel leaves and fleeing to a merchant's boat.

As the merchant's boat sailed away, the boy revealed his true identity and began to chant a rhyme, mocking the Ogress and her family for eating Ghutila's flesh. The Ogress and her family were left behind, wailing and enraged.
