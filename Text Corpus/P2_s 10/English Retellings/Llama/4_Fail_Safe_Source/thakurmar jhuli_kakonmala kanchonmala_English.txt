A prince and a shepherd were close friends, and the prince promised to make the shepherd his minister when he became king. They spent their days happily together, with the shepherd playing his flute for the prince under a tree. However, when the prince became king, he forgot about his promise and his friend, and instead focused on his new life with his queen, Kanchanmala.

The shepherd, heartbroken, came to the palace to remind the king of his promise, but was turned away. The next day, the king woke up to find his body covered in needles, and he was unable to eat, sleep, or speak. The queen, Kanchanmala, took on the responsibility of running the kingdom, but the king's condition remained a mystery.

One day, a beautiful girl approached the queen at the river ghat and offered to become a handmaiden in exchange for removing the needles from the king. The queen agreed, but the girl, whose name was Kankonmala, had ulterior motives. She tricked the queen into giving her the queen's clothes and jewelry, and then took the queen's place as the ruler of the kingdom.

Kanchanmala was reduced to a handmaiden and was forced to do menial tasks, while Kankonmala ruled with an iron fist. The king remained unaware of the switch, and his condition continued to worsen. Kanchanmala, meanwhile, was filled with sorrow and longing for her former life.

One day, while doing her chores, Kanchanmala met a mysterious person who was collecting needles. The person turned out to be the shepherd, who had been seeking revenge against the king for breaking his promise. The shepherd used his magical thread to expose Kankonmala's true identity and to punish her for her deceit.

In the end, the shepherd's thread threaded itself through the needles on the king's body, and then used the needles to stitch shut Kankonmala's eyes and mouth, killing her. The king was cured, and he reunited with his shepherd friend, making him his minister as promised. The king, the shepherd, and Kanchanmala lived happily ever after, with the shepherd playing his flute for the king under the same tree where they had spent so many happy days together.
