Once upon a time, there was a Brahmani, a wise and hardworking woman, who was married to a foolish Brahmin. The Brahmin did nothing but eat and sit around, leaving all the household chores to his wife. One day, the Brahmin asked his wife to make pitha, a traditional dish, but she was fed up with his demands and chased him away.

The Brahmin went to the forest, where he met a sage and began to learn reading and writing. He studied diligently, although his learning was more about making sounds and reciting nonsense phrases than actual knowledge. Despite this, he became convinced of his own intelligence and decided to return home to show off his newfound wisdom.

When he arrived, he found his wife cooking and claimed to have calculated that she was making 21 fritters. His wife was impressed, and soon the whole village was talking about the Brahmin's amazing knowledge. People came to him for advice, and he would spout nonsense, but they were convinced of his genius.

One day, a washerman named Moti came to the Brahmin, asking for help in finding his lost donkey. The Brahmin pretended to perform a ritual and told Moti that the donkey would be found the next day. That night, the donkey wandered into the Brahmin's courtyard, and he was able to "discover" it, further solidifying his reputation as a wise and powerful scholar.

The Brahmin's fame spread, and he was eventually summoned by the king to find a missing necklace. The Brahmin was terrified, but he stumbled upon the thief, a woman named Jaga, who had taken the necklace and was passing by the room where the Brahmin was being held. She overheard him mentioning the name "Jagadamba" and, thinking he had discovered her secret, confessed to the crime and returned the necklace.

The Brahmin was hailed as a hero and became the chief scholar of the king's court. He was showered with wealth and gifts, and his wife was overjoyed by their newfound prosperity. The Brahmin lived a life of luxury, eating pitha every day and basking in the admiration of his wife and the community. Despite his lack of actual knowledge, he had become a respected and revered figure, and he lived happily ever after with his devoted wife.
