In a forest, three siblings, Arun, Barun, and Kiranmala, lived with a Brahmin who had taken them in. As they grew up, they helped the Brahmin with his daily tasks and learned from him. Kiranmala, the sister, was particularly diligent in keeping the house clean and helping with household chores. The brothers, Arun and Barun, learned the Brahmin's knowledge and helped with tasks such as picking fruits and chasing deer.

One day, the Brahmin called the siblings to him and told them that he was leaving for another kingdom, entrusting them with all his belongings. He departed, and the siblings were left to fend for themselves. The king of the land, who had been away hunting, returned to find his kingdom in darkness and decided to go hunting again. During the hunt, he got separated from his companions and stumbled upon the siblings' home, where they welcomed him and gave him water to drink.

The king was astonished by the siblings' kindness and told them that if they ever needed anything, they should let him know. Kiranmala asked her brothers what kings had, and they replied that they had elephants, horses, and palaces. Kiranmala suggested building a palace, and the brothers agreed. With their hard work, they built a magnificent palace that rivaled the gods' own abodes.

A sanyasi (a wandering ascetic) passed by the palace and was amazed by its beauty. He told the siblings that the palace was missing a few things to make it truly perfect: golden fruits on silver trees, water of falling pearls, and the sweet song of a golden bird in a diamond tree. The siblings decided to embark on a journey to find these things.

Arun, the eldest brother, set out first, but he was turned to stone by the illusions of the Mountain of Illusion. Barun, the second brother, followed, but he too was turned to stone. Kiranmala, determined to save her brothers, dressed as a prince and set out to the Mountain of Illusion. She faced many challenges and dangers but persevered, using her courage and wit to overcome them.

When she reached the diamond tree, the golden bird told her to take the spring water, flowers, and other treasures. Kiranmala struck a drum, and the Mountain of Illusion was stilled. She sprinkled the spring water, and all the princes who had been turned to stone, including her brothers, were restored to life.

The siblings returned to their palace, planted the silver tree and diamond tree, and released the pearl-fall water. The golden bird sang, and the palace was filled with joy and beauty. The king, who had been searching for the siblings, finally found them and was amazed by their palace. The golden bird revealed that the siblings were actually the king's own children, whom his wicked sisters-in-law had lied about and replaced with a puppy, a kitten, and a wooden doll.

The king was overjoyed to be reunited with his children and brought his entire kingdom to the palace. The queen, who had been living in sorrow, was also reunited with her children. The kingdom celebrated for seven days and seven nights, and the king, queen, and their children lived happily ever after, ruling the kingdom with wisdom and kindness. The wicked sisters-in-law were punished, and the kingdom prospered under the rule of the reunited royal family.
