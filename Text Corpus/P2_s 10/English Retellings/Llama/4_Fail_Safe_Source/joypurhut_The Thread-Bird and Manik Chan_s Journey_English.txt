In a certain country, a king ruled over his kingdom. Near the palace lived a blacksmith named Mathur Kamar and a tailor named Abbas Khalifa, both of whom possessed extraordinary skills. The king summoned them to his court and asked them to showcase their abilities. Mathur Kamar claimed he could create 101 iron fish that would come to life and dance in a pond, while the tailor said he could create a bird of thread that could devour all the fish in one swoop.

A competition was held to prove whose power was greater. The blacksmith released his iron fish into a pond, and they began to swim around. The tailor then released his thread-bird, which devoured all the fish. The king was amazed and asked the tailor to perform another trick. The tailor said his bird could perform tricks with a two-and-a-half-day-old infant while flying in the air.

The king borrowed his youngest queen's infant and gave it to the tailor. The tailor's bird took the infant and flew high into the air, performing acrobatics. However, the king asked for more time, despite the tailor's warnings that the bird would go out of control if it exceeded its time limit. The bird eventually flew away with the infant, leaving the kingdom and crossing into another country.

The bird landed in a garden belonging to a flower-seller, who had no children of her own. She and her husband decided to raise the infant as their own and named him Manik Chan. To avoid suspicion, they pretended that the flower-seller was pregnant and eventually announced the birth of a son.

Years passed, and Manik Chan grew up. He wove a threadless garland and accompanied the flower-seller to the palace, where he met the king's daughter, Chandramukhi. They fell in love and got married, and Chandramukhi became pregnant. After the birth of their first son, they decided to visit Manik's parents, but they didn't know the way. They used the thread-bird to fly to their native land.

During the journey, Chandramukhi went into labor, and the bird landed on a river's sandbar. The bird's wing was broken, and it eventually died after saving the newborn child from a python. A ferryman found the two children and took them to his home, raising them as his own.

Meanwhile, Chandramukhi was taken by a merchant who proposed marriage to her. She agreed to marry him after four years, on the condition that she would live in a separate house and accompany him on his trading voyages. A year later, the merchant set out on a trading voyage with Chandramukhi in a separate boat.

Manik Chan, who had been searching for his wife and children, told the king of the country that he believed they had been abducted. The king posted a guard at the sandbar's landing and ordered all merchants to stay overnight and have their boats searched. The ferryman who had taken the two children was assigned to guard duty.

When the merchant returned from his trading voyage, his boat was moored at the sandbar's landing, and the two brothers were stationed as guards. The elder brother sang a mournful song, which Chandramukhi heard and recognized as her sons. She devised a plan to reunite with them by accusing the brothers of making lewd remarks to her.

The king summoned everyone to his court, and the two brothers sang the same song, revealing their true story. Chandramukhi embraced her sons and told the king the entire story. The king rebuked the merchant and ordered him to leave. Manik Chan was summoned to the court, and he recognized his wife. The king arranged for Manik Chan and his family to return to their native land, and they set off on a boat, finally reunited.
