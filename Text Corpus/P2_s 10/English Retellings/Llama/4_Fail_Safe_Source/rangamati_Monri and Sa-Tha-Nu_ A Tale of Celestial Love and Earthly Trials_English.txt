Long ago, Indra, the King of Heaven, had seven beautiful princesses who were identical in appearance. They spent their days playing and laughing in heaven, but they were also curious about the Earth. One day, they asked their father if they could visit the Earth, and after some hesitation, he agreed. The princesses were overjoyed and visited the Earth every day, playing hide-and-seek and bathing in a lake.

However, their fun was short-lived, as a hunter discovered them and sought to catch one of them. With the help of the Naga King, the hunter caught the youngest princess, Monori, using a net. Monori's sisters tried to help her, but they were unable to rescue her and eventually abandoned her. The hunter, however, took pity on Monori and freed her from the net. Monori was so grateful that she accepted the hunter as her sworn brother.

Later, Monori met a prince named Sa-tha-nu, and they fell in love. They got married, and Monori became pregnant. However, their happiness was short-lived, as the prince had to leave for war. Monori was left alone, and the Brahmins, who were jealous of her influence, began to deceive her, telling her that her husband would not return and that she should leave the palace.

Monori became sad and decided to return to heaven. She left her son behind, but she couldn't bear to be away from him and returned to Earth. She then met a devout sage and gave him a ring to give to her husband if he ever came looking for her. The sage told Sa-tha-nu about Monori's whereabouts, and Sa-tha-nu set out to find her.

Sa-tha-nu crossed the ocean and reached the deserted island, where he met two birds who told him about the grand feast in the celestial kingdom. He disguised himself as an ant and flew to heaven on the back of one of the birds. In heaven, he found Monori, who was being bathed by the celestial nymphs. Sa-tha-nu put the ring he had received from the sage into a pitcher, and Monori recognized it as her own.

The King of Heaven, Indra, decided to test Sa-tha-nu to see if he was worthy of Monori. Sa-tha-nu passed the tests, which included identifying Monori's finger among her sisters' and recognizing her in the sky. The King of Heaven was impressed, and Sa-tha-nu and Monori were married again in a grand ceremony.

After the wedding, the King of Heaven ordered his soldiers to take Sa-tha-nu and Monori, along with their palace, and place them on Earth. When they woke up the next morning, they found themselves on Earth and were surprised to see that their son had grown old. However, when Monori took her son in her lap, he turned back into a two-month-old baby.

It was revealed that one day in heaven is equal to one hundred years on Earth, which explained why their son had aged so much. Monori and Sa-tha-nu were reunited with their son, and Monori became even more devoted to Buddhism. It was also revealed that their servant in a past life had prayed to Lord Buddha to reunite them in every life, and that's why they kept finding each other. In this life, the servant was reborn as the hunter who had brought Monori and Sa-tha-nu together.
