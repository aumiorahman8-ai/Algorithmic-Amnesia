Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived an old man and his wife who had a beautiful daughter named Dh'bi. They were happy and content with their life, practicing jhum cultivation and living off the land. However, their happiness was short-lived, as one day the old man's wife was turned into a tortoise by a curse.

The curse occurred when the old man, overcome with thirst, shouted "Dur ho!" (meaning "tortoise" in the Chakma language) at his wife, and in that moment, she was transformed into a tortoise. The old man was filled with regret and grief, and his daughter Dh'bi was heartbroken.

As time passed, the old man remarried, and his new wife, along with her daughter K'bi, treated Dh'bi cruelly. Dh'bi's stepmother was jealous of her beauty and kindness, and she made Dh'bi do all the household chores. Despite her hardships, Dh'bi remained kind and gentle.

One day, the king announced that he would choose a bride for his son, the prince, from among the unmarried girls in the kingdom. Dh'bi's stepmother and stepsister were eager to attend, but Dh'bi was not allowed to go. However, with the help of a banyan tree that had grown from the remains of her tortoise mother, Dh'bi was able to acquire beautiful clothes and jewelry and attend the palace.

At the palace, Dh'bi's beauty and kindness captivated the prince, and he chose her as his bride. However, her stepmother and stepsister were not willing to give up, and they plotted to kill Dh'bi. They dug a pit under the floor of the weaving shed, and when Dh'bi went to retrieve her wedding ring, they poured hot water on her and buried her alive.

Dh'bi's spirit did not rest, and she was transformed into a yellow bird. The bird flew to the palace, where it died, but its spirit continued to haunt the prince's room. The prince discovered that the bird's spirit was actually Dh'bi, and he was overjoyed to have her back.

With Dh'bi's return, the palace was filled with joy once again. The wicked stepmother and stepsister were punished, and Dh'bi and the prince lived happily ever after. The prince became king, and Dh'bi became queen, ruling the kingdom with kindness and wisdom.

According to legend, the yellow bird that can be seen in the country is actually Dh'bi, who still exists in that form, a reminder of the power of love and kindness to overcome even the greatest challenges.
