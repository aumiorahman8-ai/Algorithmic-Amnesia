'Brahman Brahmani' is a classic Bengali folktale that has been passed down through generations. The story revolves around a poor but kind-hearted Brahmin couple who are childless. Here's a retelling of the tale:

Once upon a time, in a small village, there lived a Brahmin couple, Brahman and Brahmani. They were a devoted couple, but despite their devotion to each other and the gods, they were childless. The couple longed for a child, and their desire only grew stronger with each passing day.

One day, while Brahman was out performing his daily rituals, he came across a beautiful lotus flower floating on the surface of a nearby pond. As he picked up the flower, he noticed that it had a small, exquisite child nestled within its petals. The child was no bigger than a thumb and had skin as fair as the lotus.

Brahman was overjoyed and decided to take the child home to his wife, Brahmani. When he presented the child to her, she was overjoyed and welcomed the little one with open arms. The couple named the child 'Kamala' (meaning 'lotus' in Bengali) and raised her as their own.

As Kamala grew up, she proved to be a kind and gentle soul, loved by all who knew her. However, the couple soon realized that Kamala had a unique gift – she could grant any wish to those who treated her with kindness and respect. News of Kamala's gift spread quickly, and people from all over the village would come to visit her, hoping to have their wishes fulfilled.

But not everyone who visited Kamala had pure intentions. A greedy and cunning neighbor, who had always been jealous of the Brahmin couple's good fortune, decided to test Kamala's powers for his own gain. He treated Kamala with kindness and respect, and when she asked him what he desired, he wished for immense wealth and power.

Kamala, being a granting spirit, fulfilled the neighbor's wish, but with a twist. The neighbor's wealth and power came at a great cost – he lost all his friends and family, and his newfound riches brought him only misery and loneliness.

As time passed, the Brahmin couple grew old, and Kamala continued to grant wishes to those who treated her with kindness. But she also taught them valuable lessons about the importance of humility, gratitude, and the consequences of one's desires.

Eventually, the Brahmin couple passed away, surrounded by Kamala's love and care. Before they died, they asked Kamala to return to the lotus flower from which she was born, as her work on earth was done. Kamala, with a heavy heart, bid farewell to the village and returned to the pond, where she disappeared into the lotus flower, leaving behind a legacy of kindness, wisdom, and the power of selfless love.

The story of 'Brahman Brahmani' has been retold and adapted in various forms of Bengali literature and art, and its themes of kindness, generosity, and the consequences of one's desires continue to resonate with audiences to this day.
