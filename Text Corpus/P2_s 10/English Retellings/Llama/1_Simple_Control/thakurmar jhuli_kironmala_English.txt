Kironmala is a classic Bengali folktale that has been passed down for generations. The story revolves around a beautiful and kind-hearted princess named Kironmala, who is known for her exceptional beauty and her love for flowers.

Once upon a time, in a far-off kingdom, there lived a king and queen who had a stunning daughter named Kironmala. She was the apple of their eye, and they loved her more than anything in the world. Kironmala was not only beautiful but also had a heart of gold. She spent most of her time tending to the flowers in the palace garden, which is how she got her name, Kironmala, meaning "garland of flowers."

One day, while Kironmala was out for a walk in the garden, she stumbled upon a magical flower that had the power to grant wishes. As she picked the flower, she wished for a husband who would love her truly and take care of her. Suddenly, a handsome prince appeared before her, and they fell deeply in love.

However, their happiness was short-lived, as the prince was actually a prince from a neighboring kingdom who had been cursed by a wicked sorceress. The sorceress had cast a spell on the prince, turning him into a snake during the day and back into a human at night.

Kironmala, unaware of the curse, married the prince, and they lived happily together at night. But during the day, she would find a snake in her bed, and she couldn't understand why her husband was behaving so strangely.

As time passed, Kironmala discovered the truth about her husband's curse and decided to break it. She went on a journey to find the sorceress and convinced her to lift the curse. The sorceress, impressed by Kironmala's kindness and determination, agreed to lift the curse, but only if Kironmala could answer her three riddles.

Kironmala, being clever and wise, answered the riddles correctly, and the curse was lifted. The prince was transformed back into his human form, and they lived happily ever after.

The story of Kironmala has been retold and adapted in various forms of Bengali literature, theater, and film, and remains a beloved folktale in Bengali culture. It teaches us about the power of love, kindness, and determination, and how these qualities can help us overcome even the most challenging obstacles.
