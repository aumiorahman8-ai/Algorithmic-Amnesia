Ghumonto Puri is a popular Bengali folktale that has been passed down through generations. The story revolves around a young girl named Ghumonto, who lives in a small village with her mother.

Ghumonto's father had passed away when she was very young, and her mother had remarried. However, her stepfather was cruel and treated Ghumonto poorly. He would often scold and beat her, making her life miserable.

One day, Ghumonto's stepfather decided to get rid of her by sending her to live with her aunt in a distant village. Ghumonto's mother was heartbroken, but she couldn't do anything to stop her husband.

As Ghumonto traveled to her aunt's village, she met a wise old man who advised her to be careful and clever. He told her that her aunt was not what she seemed and that Ghumonto would have to use her wits to survive.

When Ghumonto arrived at her aunt's village, she was greeted with a mixture of kindness and cruelty. Her aunt would often praise her and give her gifts, but she would also scold her and make her do excessive household chores.

Ghumonto soon discovered that her aunt was a witch who had a magical oven that could grant wishes. However, the oven would only work if a young girl was locked inside it and cooked alive. Ghumonto's aunt had been using the oven to grant wishes to the villagers, but at a terrible cost.

Ghumonto was horrified and decided to outsmart her aunt. She pretended to be obedient and helpful, but secretly, she was planning her escape. One day, when her aunt asked her to get into the oven to grant a wish, Ghumonto cleverly tricked her aunt and locked her inside the oven instead.

The oven granted Ghumonto's wish, and she became rich and powerful. She returned to her village and reunited with her mother, who had been searching for her everywhere. Ghumonto's stepfather was punished for his cruelty, and Ghumonto lived happily ever after with her mother.

The story of Ghumonto Puri teaches children the importance of cleverness, bravery, and kindness. It also highlights the struggles faced by women in a patriarchal society and the need for them to be strong and independent. The tale has been retold and adapted in various forms of Bengali literature and art, and remains a beloved folktale in Bengali culture.
