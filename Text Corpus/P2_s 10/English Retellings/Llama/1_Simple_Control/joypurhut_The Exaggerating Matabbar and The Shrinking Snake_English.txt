What a delightful tale. Here's my retelling of the Bengali folktale, "The Exaggerating Matabbar and The Shrinking Snake":

In a small village in Bengal, there lived a Matabbar, a village headman, who was notorious for his exaggerations. He would often boast about his bravery, strength, and accomplishments, which were far from the truth. The villagers would often roll their eyes at his tall tales, but the Matabbar didn't care. He loved being the center of attention.

One day, while the Matabbar was out for a stroll, he came across a massive snake. The snake was enormous, with scales as black as coal and eyes that glowed like embers. The Matabbar, being the exaggerator that he was, decided to make the most of the situation. He rushed back to the village and announced, "I have just killed a snake as long as a mountain range! It was so big that it blocked out the sun, and its roar was like thunder!"

The villagers were amused by the Matabbar's tale, but they didn't believe him. They asked him to show them the snake, and the Matabbar, not wanting to be caught in his own lie, said, "Oh, I've already buried it, but I can show you the grave." He took them to a small mound of earth and said, "This is where the giant snake lies."

As the villagers were about to leave, a small, harmless snake poked its head out of the mound. The Matabbar, still trying to save face, said, "Ah, you see, this must be the snake's baby. The mother snake was so big that it had to be buried in a separate grave, which is why you don't see it here."

But, as the villagers watched, the small snake began to shrink before their eyes. It got smaller and smaller, until it was no bigger than a thread. The Matabbar, still trying to exaggerate, said, "You see, this is the snake's magic. It can shrink to any size it wants. I'm just lucky that I was able to kill its giant mother before it could cast its spell on me."

The villagers couldn't help but burst out laughing at the Matabbar's absurd tale. They teased him mercilessly, saying, "Oh, Matabbar, you're so brave that you can kill a snake that's smaller than a thread!" The Matabbar, realizing that he had been caught in his own web of lies, slunk away with his tail between his legs.

From that day on, the villagers would often tease the Matabbar about his exaggerations, and he would smile sheepishly, knowing that he had been caught in his own tall tales. And as for the shrinking snake, it became a legendary creature in the village, symbolizing the dangers of exaggeration and the importance of telling the truth.
