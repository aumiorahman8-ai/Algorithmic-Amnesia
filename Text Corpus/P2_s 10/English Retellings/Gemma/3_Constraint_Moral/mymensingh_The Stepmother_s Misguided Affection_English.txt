Once upon a time, a kind woodcutter had a beautiful daughter, Shona. After his wife died, he remarried a woman who favored her own son above all else. This stepmother, though providing lavishly for her son, constantly found fault with Shona, forcing her to do all the hardest chores and feeding her scraps.

One day, the stepmother sent Shona into the forest to collect firewood in the dead of winter, hoping the cold would harm her. However, Shona stumbled upon a magical golden bird who, touched by her kindness, gifted her a wish. Shona wished for her stepbrother to receive all the misfortune intended for her.

Immediately, the stepbrother fell ill, his lavish life replaced with suffering. The stepmother, realizing her cruelty and the power of Shona’s pure heart, begged for forgiveness. Shona, despite everything, used her second wish to restore her stepbrother to health.

The tale teaches that **malice ultimately harms the perpetrator**. The stepmother’s misguided affection, born of jealousy, brought suffering upon her own child. More importantly, it highlights the **power of compassion and forgiveness**. Shona, despite being wronged, chose kindness, demonstrating that **true strength lies not in revenge, but in empathy and a generous spirit.**
