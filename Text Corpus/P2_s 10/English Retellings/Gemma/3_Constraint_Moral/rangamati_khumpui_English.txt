The tale of Khumpui is a cautionary story from the Chittagong Hill Tracts. Khumpui was a beautiful, vain young woman obsessed with her reflection. She spent all day admiring herself in the water, neglecting her chores and ignoring her mother’s warnings. One day, a spirit disguised as a handsome young man offered her a magical mirror, promising it would show her even greater beauty. 

Enthralled, Khumpui traded her promise to marry him for the mirror. However, the mirror didn't reflect beauty, but her decaying soul, consumed by vanity. Horrified, she tried to break it, but the spirit revealed his true form – a fearsome demon – and dragged her into the mirror world, trapping her forever.

Her mother, heartbroken, searched for her, eventually learning the truth from villagers. The tale ends with a warning: excessive vanity and prioritizing superficial beauty over duty and kindness leads to ruin. Khumpui’s fate serves as a stark reminder that true beauty lies within, in a good heart and fulfilling one’s responsibilities. The story emphasizes the importance of humility, respecting elders, and valuing inner qualities over outward appearance. It’s a lesson about the dangers of making deals with the unknown and the price of unchecked desire.
