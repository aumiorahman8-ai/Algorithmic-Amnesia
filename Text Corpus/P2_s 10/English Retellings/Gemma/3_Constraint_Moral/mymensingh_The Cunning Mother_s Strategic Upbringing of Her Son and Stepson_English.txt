The tale of the Cunning Mother tells of a widow who cleverly raises both her biological son, Haru, and her stepson, Chinu. Knowing Haru is naturally less bright, she deliberately *appears* to favor Chinu, showering him with praise and opportunities while seemingly neglecting Haru. This fuels Haru’s ambition, driving him to excel to earn his mother’s approval.

Meanwhile, Chinu, accustomed to constant praise, grows complacent and arrogant. The mother subtly guides both boys, ensuring Haru learns practical skills and humility through perceived hardship, while Chinu remains focused on superficial achievements.

Eventually, the king seeks a worthy groom for his daughter. Both boys compete, but Haru, hardened by his ‘disadvantage’ and possessing genuine skill, wins the princess. Chinu, despite his initial advantages, fails due to his lack of real ability and inflated ego.

The mother reveals her strategy, explaining she knew Haru needed motivation and Chinu needed a reality check. The tale’s moral isn’t about favoritism, but about **understanding individual needs**. Effective parenting isn’t about equal treatment, but about providing the *right* kind of nurturing to help each child reach their full potential, even if it means appearing unfair. It also warns against the dangers of complacency and the importance of genuine skill over empty praise.
