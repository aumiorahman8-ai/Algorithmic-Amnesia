Once upon a time, a poor woodcutter’s daughter, Chandrabati, was promised in marriage to a wealthy man she disliked. Desperate, she prayed to a deity who sent a golden snake to her. The snake revealed itself as a cursed prince, transformed until he found a wife who would accept him in any form. Chandrabati, kind and selfless, agreed to marry him as a snake.

She endured ridicule and hardship, faithfully tending to her serpentine husband. Eventually, her unwavering devotion broke the curse, and the prince regained his human form, becoming a handsome king. He took Chandrabati as his queen, showering her with riches and respect.

However, Chandrabati, remembering her humble origins, used her newfound power to help the poor and downtrodden, sharing the kingdom’s wealth. She also insisted her father be honored.

The tale of Chandrabati and the snake prince teaches several lessons. **True beauty lies within, and judging by appearances is foolish.** More importantly, **faithfulness, compassion, and selfless love can overcome any obstacle.** Chandrabati’s reward wasn’t just wealth, but the satisfaction of using her position to do good, demonstrating that **true nobility lies in kindness and empathy, not just royal blood.**
