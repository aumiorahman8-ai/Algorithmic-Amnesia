Morar Shakkhi, or the Witness of the Bubbles, tells of Morar, a simple farmer known for his honesty. He’s summoned to court by the King to identify the thief who stole the royal ruby. Everyone else lies, hoping to avoid punishment or gain favour, pointing fingers at innocent villagers. 

Morar, however, truthfully states he *didn’t see* who took the ruby. The King, infuriated by his lack of cooperation, orders Morar flogged. As the lashes fall, bubbles rise from Morar’s wounds, each containing a perfect image of the thief – the King’s own minister!

The minister, exposed, confesses. The King, ashamed, asks Morar why he hadn’t spoken up earlier. Morar explains that truth is precious, and he wouldn’t trade it for safety. He’d rather suffer for speaking honestly than lie to protect a wrongdoer.

The tale powerfully illustrates the importance of **truthfulness, even in the face of adversity.** It warns against the dangers of flattery and false accusations, and highlights that **honesty, though often painful in the short term, ultimately prevails.** Morar’s suffering *became* the evidence, proving that integrity is its own reward and a powerful force for justice.
