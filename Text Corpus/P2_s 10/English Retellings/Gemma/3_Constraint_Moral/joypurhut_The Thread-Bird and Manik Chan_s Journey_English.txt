Manik Chan, a young man blessed with beauty but cursed with laziness, lived a life of ease funded by his mother’s hard work. One day, she tasked him with bringing back a golden apple from the distant King’s garden, hoping to instill some responsibility. 

On his journey, Manik Chan encountered a tiny, wounded Thread-Bird (Shukchara). Despite warnings, his kind heart compelled him to painstakingly remove thorns from its wings. The grateful bird promised to aid him.

Reaching the garden, Manik Chan easily obtained the apple, but boasted of his own cleverness. The Thread-Bird, witnessing his pride, subtly led him astray on the return journey. He wandered for years, facing hardship and realizing the value of humility. 

Finally, humbled and repentant, Manik Chan understood his error. The Thread-Bird reappeared, guiding him home. He returned a changed man, ready to work and appreciate his mother’s sacrifices.

The tale of Manik Chan and the Thread-Bird teaches us that **true success isn't about innate talent, but about humility, gratitude, and hard work.** Pride blinds us to the help we receive and leads to suffering. Kindness, even to the smallest creature, is rewarded, but only when coupled with a genuine and humble heart. It’s a reminder that character, not just capability, defines a person.
