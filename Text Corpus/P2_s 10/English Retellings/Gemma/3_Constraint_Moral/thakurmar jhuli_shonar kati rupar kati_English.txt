Once upon a time, a poor woodcutter had two daughters, Shona (golden) and Rupa (silver). Both were beautiful, but Shona was kind and hardworking, while Rupa was vain and lazy. A magical bird told the woodcutter his daughters would each marry a prince, but only if they remained true to their natures.

True to the bird’s word, two princes arrived. The elder, captivated by Rupa’s beauty, chose her. The younger, seeing Shona’s gentle spirit and diligence, chose her. Rupa, expecting a life of ease, demanded servants and fine clothes, constantly complaining. Shona, content with simple joys, continued her kind deeds, making her husband and kingdom happy.

Soon, the elder prince grew weary of Rupa’s selfishness and divorced her, leaving her penniless. He then saw Shona’s grace and wisdom and, realizing his mistake, asked for *her* hand in marriage. Shona, ever compassionate, forgave him and accepted, uniting both princes with kind, deserving wives.

The tale of ‘Shona Koti Rupar Koti’ teaches that **true beauty lies in character, not just appearance.** Vanity and laziness lead to unhappiness, while kindness, hard work, and a generous spirit bring lasting joy and reward. It emphasizes that inner qualities are far more valuable than outward show, and forgiveness is a virtue.
