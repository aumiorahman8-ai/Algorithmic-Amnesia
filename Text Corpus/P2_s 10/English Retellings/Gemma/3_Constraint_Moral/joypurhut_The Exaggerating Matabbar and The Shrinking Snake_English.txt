Old Matabbar was famed throughout the village for his tall tales. He’d boast of catching fish bigger than boats and wrestling tigers single-handedly. One day, he claimed to have subdued a monstrous snake, initially describing it as thicker than a palm tree and longer than the river! 

A curious young man, Karim, went to see the “defeated” snake. To his surprise, it was a perfectly ordinary, though sizable, python. Each time Karim returned to Matabbar with a truthful measurement, Matabbar, desperate to maintain his reputation, *shrank* the snake in his description. “Oh, it was much larger, I assure you! Now it seems… smaller, perhaps because of the heat.”

This continued until Matabbar claimed the snake was no bigger than a worm! Karim, realizing the absurdity, publicly exposed Matabbar’s lies. The villagers, initially amused, grew disappointed. Matabbar, stripped of his prestige, was left embarrassed and alone.

The tale of the Exaggerating Matabbar and the Shrinking Snake teaches us the dangers of vanity and dishonesty. It shows that **truth, though sometimes less impressive, is far more valuable than fabricated glory.** Trying to inflate one’s importance through lies ultimately leads to ridicule and loss of trust. A small truth is better than a shrinking lie.
