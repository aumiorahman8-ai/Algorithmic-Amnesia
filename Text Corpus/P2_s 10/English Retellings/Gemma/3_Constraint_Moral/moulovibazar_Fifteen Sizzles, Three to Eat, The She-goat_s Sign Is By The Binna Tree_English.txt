Long ago, a poor woodcutter and his wife had fifteen children. Times were desperately hard, and fearing starvation, the woodcutter planned to abandon the youngest fifteen, believing they wouldn't survive. His wife, heartbroken, secretly marked each child with a tiny sizzle on their foreheads, instructing them to follow the path and call out, "Fifteen sizzles, three to eat, the she-goat's sign is by the Binna tree!"

Lost in the forest, the children encountered a kind she-goat who understood their plea. She led them to a hidden village where they were adopted and thrived. Meanwhile, the woodcutter, filled with remorse, eventually found the village. He recognized his children, now healthy and happy, by the faint sizzles.

The tale teaches several lessons. Firstly, **desperation shouldn't lead to abandoning hope or family.** The woodcutter’s initial plan was born of fear, not love. Secondly, **kindness and compassion can appear in unexpected forms** – the she-goat represents this. Most importantly, it highlights **the resilience of children and the power of a mother’s love and foresight.** The sizzles weren't just marks, but a symbol of her hope and a guide for her children’s survival. Ultimately, the story champions faith and the belief that even in the darkest times, good can prevail.
