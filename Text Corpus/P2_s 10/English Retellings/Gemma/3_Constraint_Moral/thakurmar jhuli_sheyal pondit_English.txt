## The Fox Scholar (Sheyal Pondit)

Old Man Ghosh, a simple farmer, found a baby fox and raised it like a son, naming him Rupchand. Rupchand grew clever, learning to read and write, even mimicking a Pandit (scholar)! Word spread of the “Fox Scholar” and people flocked to him with questions. Rupchand, using his cunning, always gave vague, double-meaning answers that pleased everyone, earning him gifts and praise.

One day, the King, skeptical, tested Rupchand with a riddle: which is heavier, a sack of cotton or a sack of iron? Rupchand, fearing a wrong answer, cleverly replied, “It depends on who is carrying it, Your Majesty!” 

The King, amused by the fox’s wit, declared him a true Pandit. However, a wise courtier pointed out Rupchand’s trickery – he avoided a direct answer, prioritizing self-preservation over truth. 

Exposed, Rupchand fled, revealing his true fox nature. Old Man Ghosh, though saddened, understood.

**The moral of the story is clear:** True knowledge isn't about clever manipulation or avoiding difficult questions. It’s about honesty, directness, and a willingness to stand by the truth, even when it’s challenging.  Appearances can be deceiving, and superficial intelligence is no substitute for genuine wisdom and integrity.
