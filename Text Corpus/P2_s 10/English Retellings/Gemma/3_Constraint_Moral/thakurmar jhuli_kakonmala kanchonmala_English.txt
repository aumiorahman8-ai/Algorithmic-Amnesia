Once upon a time, two sisters, Kakonmala (dark-skinned) and Kanchonmala (fair-skinned), lived with their widowed mother. Their father, a merchant, had promised a grand wedding for both, but died before fulfilling his vow. Kanchonmala, favored for her beauty, was betrothed to a wealthy prince. Kakonmala, however, was left with only her mother’s sorrow and a neglected fate.

Driven by jealousy, Kanchonmala’s mother secretly sent Kakonmala to a distant land with a deceitful servant, intending to get rid of her. Kakonmala endured hardship, eventually becoming a palace maid. Through her kindness and skill in crafting beautiful flower garlands, she caught the eye of a neighboring king. He fell in love with her inner beauty and married her, making her a queen.

Meanwhile, Kanchonmala’s marriage proved unhappy. The prince was cruel and vain. When Kakonmala, now a queen, visited her sister, she forgave Kanchonmala’s mother and offered help. 

The tale of Kakonmala and Kanchonmala teaches us that **true beauty lies within**. External appearance fades, but kindness, resilience, and inner strength are enduring qualities. It also highlights the dangers of **jealousy and deceit**, and the importance of **forgiveness**. Kakonmala’s success wasn’t due to her looks, but her character, proving that virtue is its own reward.
