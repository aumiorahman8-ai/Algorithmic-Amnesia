## The Story of the Two Princes

A king had two queens, one of whom was secretly a Rakshasi (demon). They had two sons: Kusum, the gentle son of the human queen, and Ajit, the son of the Rakshasi. The brothers were very close. The Rakshasi-queen, however, desired to eat Kusum, but was frustrated because Ajit always stayed by his brother’s side.

The human queen fell ill and died, and the Rakshasi-queen began to mistreat Kusum, driving away even her own son, Ajit, when he tried to comfort him. Ajit endured this, but Kusum grew weaker and weaker.

One night, a Rakshas attacked the palace and began to devour Kusum while the king was paralyzed with fear. The Rakshasi-queen laughed. Ajit woke up and, seeing the attack, slapped the Rakshas, causing it to vomit a golden lump and flee. The Rakshasi-queen, enraged, then ate her own son, and a lump of iron came from her throat. She went to the roof where a gathering of Rakshasas was taking place and declared she would stay while they returned to their land.  The palace spire broke, and the Rakshasas departed, leaving the queen burning with unrest. She then buried a golden and iron lump and disappeared.

The kingdom was overrun with Rakshasas, and people fled. Eventually, a farmer found the buried lumps inside a bamboo stalk. They turned into two eggs, which hatched into two princes, Neelkamal and Lalkamal, with swords and crowns.

The princes traveled to another kingdom plagued by a Khokkosh (a monster) that devoured people. They volunteered to fight it, and after a clever battle of wits and trickery, they defeated the Khokkosh.  They used their knowledge of the Rakshasi’s nature to outsmart the monster, and ultimately killed it.

However, the Rakshasi-queen, still alive, learned of the Khokkosh’s defeat and sent messengers to destroy Neelkamal. The princes journeyed to the land of the Rakshasas, aided by magical birds, and discovered the source of the evil. They found the Rakshasi-queen and, with the help of magical hornets, defeated her and her army. 

Finally, they returned to their kingdom, where they were reunited with their father, the king, who had recovered from his shock.  The kingdom rejoiced, and the two princes, Neelkamal and Lalkamal, ruled happily ever after with their new wives.
