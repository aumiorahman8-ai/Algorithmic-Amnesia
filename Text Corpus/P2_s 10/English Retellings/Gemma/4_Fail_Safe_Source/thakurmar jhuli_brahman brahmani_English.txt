Once upon a time, there was a Brahmani who did all the work while her Brahmin husband did nothing but eat. She was strong and capable, but burdened by his laziness and constant demands. One day, exasperated, she lashed out at him when he asked for pitha, telling him she had nothing to make it with and ordering him to leave.

The Brahmin, offended, went to the forest and eventually met a sage who taught him to read. He struggled with the lessons, reciting nonsensical sounds, but convinced himself he was gaining great knowledge and would become famous. He left the ashram intending to return home and show off his learning, hoping to escape his wife’s scorn.

When he returned to his village, he found his wife seemingly still working as before. He announced his newfound knowledge, and she scoffed at him, demanding twenty-one fritters as a sign of respect. He boasted he could *know* how many fritters she was making just by calculating, astonishing her. 

Word spread of his supposed wisdom, and he gained fame, reading palms and fortunes. However, his knowledge proved useless when Moti the washerman’s donkey went missing. He pretended to use his skills, but ultimately failed to find it, fearing his charade would be exposed.

That night, a thief (actually the missing donkey) entered his courtyard. Terrified, the Brahmin tried to catch the thief, but ended up falling on the donkey, accidentally causing its death. His wife cleverly explained to the neighbors that he had *calculated* the donkey’s return, saving him from embarrassment.

This led to even greater fame, eventually reaching the king, whose princess’s necklace had been stolen. The Brahmin was summoned and, desperate, was saved by the royal gardener’s wife, Jaga, who confessed to stealing the necklace. He instructed her to hide it in the pond.

He then claimed to have located the necklace through his calculations, and it was indeed found in the pond. The king rewarded him handsomely, making him his chief scholar. 

In the end, the Brahmin lived in luxury, eating pitha every day, while his wife, overjoyed, happily served him. They both prospered from his accidental success and the cleverness of his wife.
