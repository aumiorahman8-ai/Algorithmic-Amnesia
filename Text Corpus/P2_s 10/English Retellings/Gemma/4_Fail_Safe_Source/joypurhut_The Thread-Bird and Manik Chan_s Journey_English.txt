Once, in a certain country, lived a king who summoned two famous men to his court: Mathur Kamar, a blacksmith with impossible powers, and Abbas Khalifa, a tailor with extraordinary skills. The king challenged them to a competition. Mathur Kamar promised to create 101 iron fish that would come to life and swim in a pond. Abbas Khalifa countered that he could make a thread-bird that would devour all the fish.

The competition took place before the king and a crowd. Mathur Kamar’s fish sprang to life, and Abbas Khalifa’s thread-bird swiftly ate them all. The king, amazed, then asked the tailor if the bird could perform other feats. The tailor claimed it could perform tricks with a two-and-a-half-day-old infant. 

Despite protests, the king obtained his youngest queen’s baby, with the condition the child be returned immediately after the performance. The tailor warned the bird could only perform the trick for a limited time, but the king insisted on more. The thread-bird flew with the infant, performing acrobatics, but when the king demanded it continue, the bird ascended higher and higher, flying out of the kingdom.

The queen wailed, and the king consoled her, but the bird continued its flight, eventually landing in the garden of a childless flower-seller. The bird and child were welcomed, and the flower-seller and her husband decided to raise the child as their own, naming him Manik Chan, and cleverly fabricated a story of pregnancy to explain his sudden appearance.

Years later, Manik Chan, now a young man, wove a threadless garland and accompanied his adoptive mother to sell it at the palace. The king’s daughter, Chandramukhi, was captivated by both the garland and Manik Chan, and requested to marry him. They married and had a son.

Later, Chandramukhi became pregnant again. They decided to visit Manik Chan’s birth parents, and flew on the thread-bird. Chandramukhi went into labor mid-flight and demanded they land, despite the tailor’s warning. The bird landed on a sandbar, breaking its wing. While Manik Chan gathered firewood and Chandramukhi washed clothes, a python attacked their newborn. The thread-bird bravely fought the snake, sacrificing itself to save the child, but both perished.

Two ferrymen found the abandoned children and raised them. Meanwhile, Chandramukhi was abducted by a merchant who forced her into a marriage agreement with conditions to protect herself. Manik Chan, distraught, sought the king’s help to find his family. The king ordered searches of passing merchant boats.

The ferrymen, assigned to guard the river landing, were on duty when the merchant’s boat arrived. The elder brother sang a song lamenting the loss of his family, which Chandramukhi recognized. She falsely accused the brothers of impropriety to the merchant, leading to a court hearing. When forced to repeat the song, the brothers sang it again, revealing their identity to Chandramukhi. 

Chandramukhi revealed the whole story to the king, who summoned Manik Chan. They were reunited, the merchant was banished, and Manik Chan, Chandramukhi, and their children returned to their homeland.
