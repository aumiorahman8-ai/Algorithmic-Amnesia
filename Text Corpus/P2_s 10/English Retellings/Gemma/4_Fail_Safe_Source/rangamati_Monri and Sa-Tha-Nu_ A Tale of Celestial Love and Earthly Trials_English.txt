Long ago, Indra, King of Heaven, had seven identical and beautiful daughters who loved to play but were curious about Earth. Despite their father’s initial reluctance, spurred by a dream of a lotus flower being stolen by an ogre, he allowed them to visit the mortal world. 

They enjoyed their time on Earth, but during one visit, a hunter saw them bathing and desired one for himself. He sought help from the Naga King, who provided a net. The youngest princess, Monori, was caught in the net and fell to Earth, abandoned by her sisters.

The hunter, surprisingly kind-hearted, freed Monori. Unable to return home that night, he consulted the kingdom’s prince, Sa-tha-nu, who fell in love with Monori at first sight. They married and lived happily until Sa-tha-nu had to leave for war. While pregnant, Monori was left alone and faced the resentment of the Brahmins, who had lost influence due to her devotion to Buddhism.

The Brahmins plotted against her, convincing the king – through a misinterpreted dream – that Monori’s life was a threat to the prince’s success in war and should be sacrificed. Hearing this, Monori decided to return to heaven, but couldn’t bear to leave her son. She left him with milk and a final kiss, intending to return, but a dove’s call reminded her of her child and she went back. 

Knowing she couldn’t stay, Monori entrusted a ring to a sage, instructing him to give it to Sa-tha-nu if he ever searched for her, and explained how to reach heaven – a perilous journey across a sea guarded by a massive Naga King.

In heaven, her family worried. Eventually, Sa-tha-nu, after a long search, found the sage and learned of Monori’s whereabouts. He braved the dangerous journey to heaven, aided by birds who sheltered him and guided him. He entered heaven disguised as an ant and, through cleverness and Monori’s help, passed tests set by Indra to prove his worthiness. He shot arrows at Monori while she flew with her sisters, bringing her safely down.

They were remarried and sent back to Earth with their palace, only to discover a hundred years had passed. Their son was now an old man, but Monori’s touch restored him to infancy. It was revealed that time flows differently in heaven. Monori continued her devotion to Buddhism, and it was explained that their connection spanned many lifetimes, with the hunter being a devoted servant who had wished for their union in every life.
