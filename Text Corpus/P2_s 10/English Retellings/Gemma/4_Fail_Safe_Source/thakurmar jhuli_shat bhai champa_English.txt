Once upon a time, a king had seven queens. The older six were proud and arrogant, while the youngest was gentle and kind, and the king loved her most. However, the king was deeply saddened by his lack of children.

When the youngest queen became pregnant, the king declared a celebration, opening his treasury to anyone who wanted riches. The elder queens, filled with jealousy, repeatedly tugged on a golden chain the king had tied between himself and the youngest queen, hoping to disrupt his joy and see what was happening during her labor. Each time, the king rushed to the birthing room only to find nothing. Angered, he threatened to behead all the queens if the chain was tugged again before the child was born.

The youngest queen eventually gave birth to seven sons and a daughter, beautiful as moonlight. But the elder queens lied to her, claiming she had only given birth to rats and an elephant. They then secretly hid the babies in earthen pots and buried them in the ash heap, before falsely reporting to the king that only tadpoles and mice had been born. The king, believing their lie, banished the youngest queen from the palace.

The banished queen became a dung-gathering maid, while the elder queens reveled in their supposed victory. However, the kingdom fell into sorrow, and the king found no happiness. One day, the gardener discovered seven Champa flowers and one Parul flower blooming on the ash heap. 

When the gardener tried to pick the flowers, they spoke, refusing to be taken unless the king himself came. Then, they refused to be taken unless each of the elder queens came in turn. Finally, they declared they would only come down for the disgraced, dung-gathering maid.

The king brought the youngest queen back to the ash heap. As she approached, the flowers descended from the sky, revealing the seven princes and the princess hidden within – her children! The king was overjoyed, and the elder queens were punished by being buried in a pit of thorns. The king then welcomed the youngest queen and her children back to the palace, and happiness returned to the kingdom.
