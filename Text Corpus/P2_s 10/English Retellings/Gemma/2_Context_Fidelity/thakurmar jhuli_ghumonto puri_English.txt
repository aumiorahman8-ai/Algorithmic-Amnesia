## The Tale of Ghumonto Puri from Thakurmar Jhuli

Thakurma (grandmother) settled deeper into her rocking chair, the oil lamp casting dancing shadows on the walls. Little ones, come closer, come closer! Tonight, I’ll tell you the story of Ghumonto Puri, a tale of a clever girl, a greedy brother, and the blessings of Lakshmi Thakurani.

Now, there once lived a poor Brahmin, Pandit Hariram, with his two sons, Ramu and Ghanu. Ramu was the elder, strong and boastful, but not blessed with much wit. Ghanu, the younger, was quiet and thoughtful, and possessed a heart full of kindness. Their mother had passed away long ago, leaving Hariram heartbroken and the household running on little.

One day, Pandit Hariram received a summons from the Raja. The Raja, a just but demanding ruler, needed a *purohit* (priest) to perform a special *Puja* (worship) to Lakshmi Thakurani, the Goddess of Wealth, for the prosperity of the kingdom. Hariram, though poor, was renowned for his devotion, and the Raja insisted he undertake the task.

“But Baba,” Ramu protested, puffing out his chest, “Why must *you* go? I am stronger, I can carry the *pujor samagri* (worship materials) and perform the rituals!”

Hariram smiled sadly. “Strength is not enough, Ramu. This requires purity of heart and knowledge of the *mantras* (sacred verses). I must go.”

Before leaving, Hariram called Ghanu. “My son,” he said, “I am leaving you in charge. Look after the house and, most importantly, look after your *Dada-Thakur* (grandfather’s deity – a small household shrine).  I am taking with me a single *pitha* (sweet cake) – a Ghumonto Puri – blessed by your mother. It is a small offering for Lakshmi Thakurani, but it holds great significance. Do *not* let anyone touch it.”

Ramu scoffed at this instruction, but Hariram departed, leaving Ghanu with the responsibility. 

As soon as Hariram was out of sight, Ramu began to pester Ghanu. “What is so special about this Ghumonto Puri? It’s just a cake! I am hungry, let me have a bite.”

Ghanu, remembering his father’s words, firmly refused. “Baba has specifically forbidden it, Dada. It is for Lakshmi Thakurani.”

Ramu, consumed by greed, wouldn’t listen. He waited until Ghanu went to fetch water from the *ghat* (riverbank) and then snatched the Ghumonto Puri. He devoured it in a single gulp, leaving only a few crumbs. 

When Ghanu returned, he was horrified. Ramu, trying to appear innocent, claimed he hadn’t touched it. But Ghanu saw the crumbs on his brother’s face.  He was heartbroken, not for the loss of the cake, but for the disrespect shown to his father and Lakshmi Thakurani.

Meanwhile, Pandit Hariram reached the Raja’s palace and began the *Puja*. But every time he chanted a *mantra*, a strange thing happened. The *dhupa* (incense) wouldn’t light, the *deep* (oil lamp) would flicker and go out, and the flowers would wither. He realized something was terribly wrong.  He understood that the blessing of the Ghumonto Puri, meant to ensure the *Puja’s* success, was missing.

Distraught, Hariram returned home. Ghanu, weeping, confessed everything about Ramu and the stolen *pitha*.  Hariram, though saddened, understood his younger son’s honesty. 

He decided to confront Ramu.  “Ramu,” he said sternly, “You have disobeyed me and disrespected Lakshmi Thakurani.  As punishment, you will become a *Ghumer* (a type of bird known for its constant spinning and aimless flight) and wander the earth until you learn the value of honesty and respect.”

And so, Ramu instantly transformed into a Ghumer bird, forever circling and unable to find rest.

Hariram, with Ghanu by his side, then decided to perform a simple *Puja* at home, offering whatever little they had.  Ghanu, with his pure heart, chanted the *mantras* with devotion.  Suddenly, a beautiful woman appeared before them, radiating light. It was Lakshmi Thakurani herself!

“I am pleased with your devotion and honesty, Ghanu,” she said. “Your brother’s greed angered me, but your sincerity has touched my heart.  I bless this house with prosperity.”

And with that, Lakshmi Thakurani vanished.  The house was filled with the scent of jasmine and the sound of tinkling bells.  Soon, their fortunes turned. They received gifts from the Raja, their home was filled with food, and they lived happily ever after. 

But remember, children,” Thakurma concluded, adjusting her shawl, “Greed and dishonesty always lead to misfortune.  It is *bhakti* (devotion) and *satya* (truth) that truly bring blessings from Lakshmi Thakurani and all the Gods.”



This retelling attempts to capture the essence of a Thakurmar Jhuli tale by:

* **Using Bengali kinship terms:** *Baba* (father), *Dada* (elder brother), *Dada-Thakur* (grandfather’s deity – a respectful term for a household shrine).
* **Incorporating specific rituals:** *Puja*, *mantras*, *dhupa*, *deep*, *pitha*, *ghat*.
* **Maintaining cultural nuances:** The importance of respecting elders, the reverence for Lakshmi Thakurani, the consequences of greed, and the value of honesty.
* **Preserving the storytelling style:** Thakurma’s conversational tone and the moral lesson at the end.
* **Using Bengali words where appropriate:**  to add authenticity and flavour.
