## The Golden Spinner, The Silver Spinner (Shonar Kati Rupar Kati)

This is a classic Bengali folktale, a story about kindness, cleverness, and the rewards of a good heart. Here's a retelling of 'Shonar Kati Rupar Kati':

Once upon a time, in a small village, lived a poor woodcutter named Ramu. He was a kind man, but incredibly unlucky. He struggled to provide for his wife and children. One day, while deep in the forest, he stumbled upon a beautiful clearing. In the center stood two magnificent spinning wheels – one made of pure gold, the other of shimmering silver.

As he approached, a voice echoed through the trees, "These are magical spinning wheels. The golden wheel spins gold thread, and the silver wheel spins silver thread. Take whichever you wish, but heed this warning: never reveal to anyone *how* they work. If you do, their magic will vanish."

Ramu, overjoyed, chose the golden wheel, thinking gold would solve all his problems. He carefully carried it home, hiding it from prying eyes. His wife was astonished! He began to spin, and indeed, beautiful golden thread poured forth. They sold the thread in the market and quickly became wealthy. They built a beautiful house, bought fine clothes, and ate delicious food.

However, prosperity brought unwanted attention. The local Zamindar (landlord), a greedy and arrogant man, noticed Ramu’s sudden wealth. He became suspicious and demanded to know the source of his fortune. Ramu, initially hesitant, eventually succumbed to the Zamindar’s threats and revealed the secret of the golden spinning wheel.

The moment he spoke the method of operation, a terrible thing happened. The golden wheel shuddered, lost its glow, and turned into a worthless piece of wood. Ramu was devastated, his wealth gone in an instant. The Zamindar, furious at being tricked, threw Ramu and his family out of their new home and back into poverty.

Desperate, Ramu remembered the silver wheel. He returned to the clearing, hoping it was still there. To his relief, it was! He took the silver wheel home, vowing to keep its secret safe.  He spun silver thread, and once again, they prospered, though not as lavishly as before.

The Zamindar, still obsessed with wealth, continued to harass Ramu. He sent spies, offered bribes, and even threatened violence, but Ramu remained steadfast. He refused to reveal the secret of the silver wheel, no matter the cost.

Finally, the Zamindar, losing patience, decided to steal the wheel himself. He crept into Ramu’s house one night and made off with the silver spinning wheel.  He rushed home, eager to spin his own fortune. But when he tried to operate the wheel, nothing happened! He fiddled with it, pulled at it, even threatened it, but it remained stubbornly silent.

Meanwhile, Ramu, realizing the wheel was stolen, didn't panic. He knew the magic wasn't in the wheel itself, but in the *way* it was used. He went to the Zamindar’s palace and, with a clever plan, asked for work as a simple servant. 

He observed the Zamindar struggling with the wheel, then casually offered to help.  He pretended to be ignorant, asking questions about how the Zamindar was trying to use it.  As he "guided" the Zamindar, he subtly demonstrated the correct technique – the specific way the thread needed to be started, the rhythm of the spinning, the gentle touch required.

The moment the Zamindar replicated Ramu’s actions, the silver wheel sprang to life, spinning a beautiful stream of silver thread! But as the Zamindar realized he had been tricked, and understood *how* the wheel worked, the magic vanished once more. The wheel turned into ordinary wood.

The Zamindar, enraged, was about to punish Ramu, but the palace guards, witnessing Ramu’s cleverness and the Zamindar’s greed, intervened. They had long been unhappy with the Zamindar’s tyranny. They arrested the Zamindar and declared Ramu the new ruler, recognizing his wisdom and kindness.

Ramu, however, didn't want to be a ruler. He simply wanted to live peacefully with his family. He used his wisdom to govern justly, ensuring the well-being of his people. He never sought wealth again, understanding that true riches lie in honesty, kindness, and keeping one's word.



**The moral of the story:**  The tale emphasizes the importance of keeping promises, the dangers of greed, and the power of cleverness and kindness. It also suggests that true wealth isn't material possessions, but rather a good heart and a peaceful life.
