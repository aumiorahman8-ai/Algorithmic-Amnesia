## Suanlo Suanlo and the Tiger's Revenge: A Bengali Folktale

Long ago, in a small village nestled beside the Sundarbans, the mangrove forest, lived a young man named Suanlo. Now, Suanlo wasn't known for his strength or bravery, but for his incredible wit and a rather boastful tongue. He loved to brag, and his favorite story was about how he’d outsmarted a tiger.

The tale, as he told it, went like this: He was traveling through the forest when he came face to face with a magnificent Royal Bengal Tiger. Instead of being frightened, Suanlo, with a clever trick, convinced the tiger he was a powerful sorcerer. He pretended to cast a spell, making dramatic gestures and chanting nonsense words. The tiger, thoroughly intimidated, bowed its head and fled into the jungle, convinced Suanlo was a force to be reckoned with.

Suanlo repeated this story to anyone who would listen, embellishing it with each telling. He’d puff out his chest, mimic the tiger’s fear, and generally make himself the hero of a very unlikely encounter.  The villagers, though amused, warned him. "Suanlo," they'd say, "Don't boast so much. The forest has ears, and the tiger might not appreciate being made a fool of."

But Suanlo wouldn't listen. He believed his cleverness could overcome anything.

One day, while returning home from the market, Suanlo found himself truly face to face with the very same tiger he’d boasted about. This time, however, the tiger wasn’t fooled. It remembered the charade, the false gestures, and the empty words.  Its eyes burned with fury.

“So, *you* are the one who dared to mock me?” the tiger roared, its voice shaking the trees. “You claimed to be a sorcerer? Let’s see your magic now!”

Suanlo, for the first time in his life, was truly terrified. His boastful words caught in his throat. He tried to repeat his performance, waving his hands and mumbling, but the tiger wasn’t having it.  It lunged, pinning Suanlo to the ground.

Just as the tiger was about to strike, a wise old woman, known throughout the village for her knowledge of the forest and its creatures, happened to be passing by. She saw the situation and quickly intervened.

“Hold, Tiger!” she called out, her voice surprisingly strong. “This young man is foolish, yes, but taking his life won’t solve anything. He learned a harsh lesson about pride.  Instead, let him serve you. Let him be your personal masseur for a month.  Perhaps kneading your muscles will teach him humility.”

The tiger, surprisingly, agreed. It was a more satisfying revenge than a quick kill. 

And so, Suanlo, the boastful storyteller, spent the next month giving the tiger a daily massage. He rubbed its powerful muscles, carefully avoiding its claws and teeth.  He learned to be quiet, respectful, and truly appreciate the tiger’s strength.  He realized how foolish he’d been to lie and brag. 

The experience humbled him completely.  He understood that true strength wasn't in boasting, but in respecting the power of nature and the dignity of all creatures.

When the month was over, the tiger released Suanlo.  He returned to the village a changed man. He never boasted again, and when anyone asked about his encounter with the tiger, he simply told the truth – a tale of foolish pride and a well-deserved lesson. 

And so, the story of Suanlo Suanlo and the Tiger’s Revenge became a cautionary tale, reminding everyone that boasting leads to downfall, and humility is the greatest virtue of all.
