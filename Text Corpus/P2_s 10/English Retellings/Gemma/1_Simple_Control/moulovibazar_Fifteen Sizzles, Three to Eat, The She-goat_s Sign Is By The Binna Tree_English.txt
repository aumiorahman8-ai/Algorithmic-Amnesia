## Fifteen Sizzles, Three to Eat, The She-goat's Sign Is By The Binna Tree: A Retelling

This is a story told in Bengal for generations, a tale of cleverness, greed, and the power of respecting the natural world. It centers around a poor woodcutter named Ramu and his wife, Shanti.

Ramu and Shanti were desperately poor. They toiled all day, but barely had enough to eat. One day, while deep in the forest cutting wood, Ramu stumbled upon a clearing he’d never seen before. In the center stood a magnificent Binna tree (a type of fig tree with large leaves), and beneath it, a shimmering pile of *pitha* – delicious, fried rice cakes. 

But these weren't ordinary *pitha*. As Ramu approached, he heard a voice, faint as the rustling leaves, say, “Fifteen sizzles, three to eat, the she-goat’s sign is by the Binna tree.”

Confused, Ramu looked around. He saw fifteen perfectly golden *pitha* sizzling gently, radiating a mouthwatering aroma. The voice repeated the riddle: “Fifteen sizzles, three to eat, the she-goat’s sign is by the Binna tree.”

Ramu, overcome with hunger and the thought of finally having a feast, understood the first part easily. He could eat three *pitha*! But what did the rest mean? He searched around the Binna tree and finally spotted it – a small, hoof-shaped marking etched into the bark, clearly left by a she-goat. 

He reasoned, “The voice must want me to offer three *pitha* to the she-goat!”  He carefully placed three *pitha* near the hoof-print, hoping to appease whatever spirit guarded this magical food. 

As soon as he did, the voice boomed, “Well done, honest woodcutter! You understood the sign and showed respect. Take all fifteen *pitha* and share them with your wife.”

Ramu, overjoyed, quickly gathered the *pitha* and raced home to Shanti. They feasted like royalty, enjoying the delicious cakes and thanking their good fortune.

Now, Ramu had a greedy brother named Kalu. When Kalu heard about the magical *pitha*, his eyes lit up with avarice. He demanded Ramu take him to the Binna tree.  Ramu, hesitant but unable to refuse his brother, agreed.

They returned to the clearing.  There were fifteen *pitha* sizzling just as before, and the voice repeated the riddle: “Fifteen sizzles, three to eat, the she-goat’s sign is by the Binna tree.”

Kalu, however, was too focused on grabbing all the *pitha* for himself to bother with riddles. He saw the hoof-print, but instead of offering anything, he scoffed, “What nonsense! A she-goat’s sign? This is just a trick to make me leave some behind!”

He immediately began stuffing *pitha* into his bag, ignoring the voice and the sign. He tried to take all fifteen! 

Suddenly, the ground trembled. The Binna tree groaned, and the voice thundered with anger, “You have disrespected the spirit of the forest! You have ignored the sign and succumbed to greed!”

In an instant, all fifteen *pitha* turned into fifteen wriggling, biting snakes! Kalu screamed and dropped his bag, fleeing the clearing in terror, covered in snake bites.

Ramu, who had stood back watching in fear, was unharmed. The voice, now calmer, spoke to him, “Your brother’s greed brought this upon him. Remember, respect the signs of nature and be honest in your dealings.  The forest rewards those who are humble and mindful.”

Ramu returned home, shaken but grateful. He never told anyone about the *pitha* again, and he always remembered the lesson he learned by the Binna tree:  true wealth lies not in taking everything for oneself, but in respecting the world around us and understanding the signs it offers.



This tale is a reminder of the importance of respecting nature, understanding symbolism, and the dangers of unchecked greed. The "she-goat's sign" isn't just a literal hoof-print, but a symbol of the forest's spirit and the need for offering and respect.
