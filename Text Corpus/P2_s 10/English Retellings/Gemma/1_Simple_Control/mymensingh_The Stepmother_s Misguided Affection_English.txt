## The Stepmother's Misguided Affection (Boudi'r Bhul Karun) - A Retelling

This is the story of Roopali, a girl blessed with beauty but cursed with a cruel stepmother. It's a tale told and retold in Bengal, a cautionary story about jealousy and the dangers of misplaced love.

Roopali’s father, a kind but weak man, remarried after her mother’s passing. His new wife, Boudi (meaning ‘elder sister-in-law’, a common term for stepmothers), was a woman consumed by vanity. She had a daughter of her own, Puti, who was plain and unremarkable. Boudi, desperate to see her own daughter shine, couldn’t bear the thought of Roopali’s beauty eclipsing Puti.

From the moment she entered the house, Boudi began to subtly, then not-so-subtly, mistreat Roopali. She made her do all the hardest chores – grinding spices until her hands were raw, fetching water from the distant well, cleaning the entire house single-handedly.  She dressed Puti in fine silks and adorned her with jewelry, while Roopali was given rags and told to stay hidden.

But Roopali’s beauty couldn’t be hidden. It shone through her simple clothes and weary face.  Word of her loveliness reached the ears of the young Prince, who was travelling incognito through the countryside. He was immediately captivated by Roopali when he saw her drawing water at the well, her face reflecting the sunlight.

He proposed marriage, but Roopali, knowing her stepmother’s nature, refused. She feared Boudi would claim the dowry and ruin her life.  The Prince, heartbroken but understanding, left her a beautiful golden anklet as a token of his affection, promising to return.

Boudi, witnessing the encounter, flew into a rage. She demanded to know what the Prince had said. When Roopali confessed, Boudi hatched a wicked plan. She forced Puti to dress in Roopali’s clothes, and then, using soot and ash, she deliberately disfigured Roopali’s face, making her unrecognizable. 

“Now,” Boudi hissed, “when the Prince returns, *you* will go to meet him as Roopali.  He will never know the difference!”

Puti, though not inherently cruel, was easily manipulated by her mother. She reluctantly agreed.  When the Prince returned, Boudi pushed Puti forward, claiming she was Roopali.  The Prince, initially confused by the change in Roopali’s appearance, noticed the golden anklet on Puti’s ankle.  He remembered Roopali had worn it on the same foot.  He asked Puti to lift her foot, and upon seeing the anklet, realized the deception.

He was furious, but more heartbroken. He demanded to see the real Roopali. Boudi, cornered, tried to deny everything, but the truth was undeniable.  The Prince ordered Roopali to be brought forward. 

When Roopali’s face was cleaned, her beauty was even more radiant than before. The Prince was overjoyed. He immediately recognized her and, enraged by Boudi’s cruelty, banished her and Puti from the kingdom.

Roopali and the Prince were married, and she became a beloved Queen.  However, Roopali, remembering her suffering, used her position to help others who were mistreated and oppressed.  

The tale doesn’t end with Boudi and Puti finding redemption. They wandered the countryside, living in poverty and shame, a constant reminder of the destructive power of jealousy and the importance of kindness.



**The moral of the story is clear:**  True beauty lies within, and cruelty, born of envy, will always be exposed.  It also highlights the importance of a kind heart and the dangers of a stepmother’s misguided affection, where love for one’s own child can lead to the suffering of another.
