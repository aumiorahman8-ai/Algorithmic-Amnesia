## The Tale of Brahman and Brahmani: A Bengali Folktale

This is the story of Brahman and Brahmani, a tale about pride, humility, and the power of simple faith. It's a very popular folktale in Bengal, often told to children.

Once upon a time, in a small village in Bengal, lived a Brahman (a Hindu priest) and his wife, Brahmani. They were known throughout the land for their piety and religious observance, but unfortunately, they were also incredibly proud and boastful. They believed themselves to be superior to everyone else, looking down on the villagers and constantly reminding them of their own spiritual standing.

Every day, the Brahman would perform elaborate rituals, chanting complex mantras and making grand offerings. Brahmani, not to be outdone, would meticulously clean their home, prepare elaborate meals for the gods, and loudly proclaim her devotion. They were obsessed with appearances and believed that only *they* were truly righteous.

One day, a travelling sadhu (holy man) came to their village. He was simple in his attire, carrying only a begging bowl and a staff. He went from door to door, humbly asking for alms. When he reached Brahman and Brahmani’s house, they barely acknowledged him. 

“Look at this beggar!” Brahmani scoffed to the Brahman. “He dares to come to *our* door! We are performing sacred rituals, and he expects us to interrupt them for him?”

The Brahman, equally dismissive, said, “He is clearly not worthy of our attention. His appearance is unkempt, and his devotion is likely superficial.” They offered him only a small, stale piece of rice, barely enough to sustain him.

The sadhu accepted the offering with a gentle smile, saying, “May your devotion bring you blessings.” He then continued on his way.

However, unbeknownst to Brahman and Brahmani, this sadhu was actually Lord Vishnu in disguise, testing the sincerity of their faith.  He was deeply saddened by their pride and lack of compassion.

As a consequence, a strange affliction befell the couple.  Whenever they tried to eat, their food would turn into bitter neem leaves!  No matter how delicious the meal, or how carefully Brahmani prepared it, the moment it reached their mouths, it would transform into the unpleasant, bitter leaves. 

They were distraught. They consulted doctors, astrologers, and other Brahmans, but no one could diagnose or cure their ailment. They continued their rituals, hoping to appease the gods, but the bitter leaves persisted.

Finally, in desperation, they sought the advice of an old, wise woman in the village. She listened patiently to their story and then said, “You have offended a holy man. Your pride and lack of hospitality have angered the gods. You must find the sadhu you turned away and beg for his forgiveness.”

Brahman and Brahmani, humbled and ashamed, set out to find the sadhu. They travelled for days, searching every village and town. Finally, they found him sitting under a banyan tree.

They fell at his feet, weeping and begging for forgiveness. “We were foolish and arrogant,” the Brahman confessed. “We judged you by your appearance and failed to recognize your divinity. Please, forgive our transgression and lift this curse from us.”

Brahmani added, “We have learned our lesson. We understand that true devotion lies not in outward show, but in kindness, humility, and compassion.”

The sadhu, revealing his true form as Lord Vishnu, smiled kindly. “I tested your faith, and you have failed. But your repentance is sincere.  Go home, and offer a simple meal of rice and dal to a poor beggar.  Then, and only then, will the curse be lifted.”

Brahman and Brahmani returned home and followed the sadhu’s instructions. They prepared a simple meal and offered it with genuine humility to a hungry beggar.  As the beggar ate, they themselves partook of the same meal.  And to their joy, the food tasted sweet and delicious!

From that day forward, Brahman and Brahmani shed their pride and lived a life of humility and compassion. They learned that true devotion is not about grand rituals or outward appearances, but about kindness, generosity, and recognizing the divine in everyone.



**The moral of the story:**  The tale of Brahman and Brahmani emphasizes the importance of humility, compassion, and genuine devotion over pride and superficial religious observance. It teaches that judging others based on appearance is wrong and that true spirituality lies in kindness and service to others.
