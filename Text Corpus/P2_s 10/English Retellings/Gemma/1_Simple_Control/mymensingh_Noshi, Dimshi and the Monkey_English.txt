## Noshi, Dimshi and the Monkey: A Bengali Folktale

Once upon a time, in a small village nestled beside a lush forest, lived two sisters, Noshi and Dimshi. Noshi was kind and clever, always thinking of others. Dimshi, however, was lazy and selfish, constantly complaining and wanting everything for herself.

One day, their mother sent them into the forest to collect firewood. "Be careful," she warned, "and don't wander too far. And *share* the work, you two!"

Noshi, as always, happily agreed. Dimshi grumbled, but followed her sister into the woods. Soon, they found a good spot and began gathering branches. Noshi worked diligently, piling the wood neatly. Dimshi, however, quickly grew tired and sat down, complaining of thirst.

"Noshi, fetch me some water! I'm too tired to move," she whined.

Noshi, being the kind sister, agreed. She told Dimshi to guard the pile of firewood while she went to a nearby stream.  As soon as Noshi was out of sight, a mischievous monkey swung down from the trees. He was known throughout the forest for his trickery.

The monkey saw the pile of wood and, being playful, began to untie it, scattering the branches everywhere. Dimshi, instead of stopping him, simply watched, thinking, "Let the monkey do the work! It's not *my* job to gather it again."

When Noshi returned with a pot of cool water, she was horrified to see the scattered wood and Dimshi lounging idly by. 

"Dimshi! What happened? Why is the wood all over the place?" she asked, dismayed.

Dimshi, without a shred of guilt, pointed to the monkey. "That naughty monkey did it! He untied the bundle and scattered everything!"

Noshi, though upset, knew they had to fix the situation.  She began to gather the wood again, but the monkey, enjoying the chaos, continued to interfere, untying the bundles as soon as she tied them. 

Noshi, thinking quickly, had an idea. She said to the monkey, "Oh, clever monkey! You are so good at untying knots! I bet you can't tie one yourself!"

The monkey, puffed up with pride, immediately accepted the challenge. "Of course I can! I'm the cleverest creature in the forest!"

Noshi handed him a piece of rope. The monkey struggled and strained, pulling and twisting, but he couldn't manage to tie even a simple knot. He huffed and puffed, getting increasingly frustrated.  He was so focused on tying the knot, he completely forgot to untie the wood.

Meanwhile, Noshi quickly gathered all the branches and tied them into a strong bundle.  She then said to the monkey, "See? Tying a knot is much harder than untying one! You are very good at untying, but you can't tie!"

The monkey, defeated and embarrassed, swung away into the trees, leaving Noshi and Dimshi with a neatly tied bundle of firewood.

Dimshi, surprisingly, didn't apologize for her laziness. She simply said, "Let's go home now."

As they walked back to the village, Noshi thought to herself, "It's good to be clever, but it's even better to be hardworking and honest.  And sometimes, a little trickery is needed to overcome laziness and selfishness."



**The Moral of the story:**  The tale emphasizes the importance of hard work, honesty, and cleverness. It also warns against laziness and selfishness, showing how they can lead to trouble and relying on others.  The monkey represents mischievousness, but also the vanity that can be exploited.
