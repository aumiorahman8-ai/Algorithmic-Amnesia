## The Tale of Kolaboti Rajkonna (Princess Kolaboti)

Once upon a time, in a kingdom far away, lived a beautiful and clever princess named Kolaboti. She was the youngest of seven daughters, and her father, the King, loved her dearly. However, Kolaboti was known for her stubbornness and a peculiar habit: she refused to eat anything but *kolaboti* – a type of sweet coconut confection. 

The King, worried about her health, tried everything. He had the royal cooks prepare *kolaboti* in countless ways – with more sugar, less sugar, different types of coconut, even shaped like animals! But Kolaboti would only eat the *kolaboti* made by her mother, the Queen, who sadly passed away when Kolaboti was very young.  

After the Queen’s death, Kolaboti refused all food except for the last remaining *kolaboti* her mother had made, carefully preserved in a jeweled box. She ate a tiny piece each day, prolonging the inevitable moment when it would be gone.

As the last piece dwindled, the King grew desperate. He announced a proclamation: whoever could make a *kolaboti* that would satisfy Princess Kolaboti would be rewarded with her hand in marriage and half the kingdom!

Princes and cooks from far and wide came to the kingdom, each attempting to recreate the Queen’s recipe. They used the finest ingredients, followed ancient texts, and even consulted with magical beings. But none could replicate the taste that brought a smile to Kolaboti’s face.  She would taste each offering, politely but firmly declare it insufficient, and return to her sorrowful vigil over the last piece of her mother’s *kolaboti*.

Finally, a poor, young woodcutter named Shantanu arrived. He wasn't a prince or a renowned cook. He was simply a kind and observant young man. He didn't boast or offer grand promises. Instead, he asked the King if he could speak to Kolaboti.

The King, weary and desperate, agreed. Shantanu approached the princess not with a confection, but with questions. He didn't ask about ingredients, but about memories. He asked Kolaboti to describe her mother, the kitchen where the *kolaboti* was made, the scent in the air, the feeling of warmth and love. 

Kolaboti, touched by his genuine interest, began to reminisce. She described the way her mother would sing while stirring the coconut, the gentle smile on her face, the feeling of being cherished.  As she spoke, Shantanu listened intently, noticing the subtle details.

He then went to the kitchen and, instead of trying to *copy* the recipe, he tried to *recreate the feeling*. He used simple ingredients, but he stirred the mixture with the same gentle rhythm Kolaboti had described, humming a tune similar to the one she remembered her mother singing. He infused the process with love and care.

When he presented the *kolaboti* to Kolaboti, she hesitated. She took a tiny bite, and her eyes widened.  It wasn't exactly the same as her mother's, but it held the *essence* of it – the love, the warmth, the memory.  A single tear rolled down her cheek, and she ate the entire piece.

Kolaboti had finally found someone who understood her grief and could offer her comfort, not through a perfect imitation, but through genuine empathy and a loving heart. 

The King, overjoyed, kept his promise. Kolaboti married Shantanu, and they ruled the kingdom with wisdom and kindness.  Kolaboti learned that while memories are precious, life must go on, and that love and connection are the sweetest things of all.



The tale of Kolaboti Rajkonna is a beautiful story about grief, memory, and the power of empathy. It teaches us that sometimes, what we truly crave isn't a perfect replica of the past, but a connection that understands and honors our feelings.  It's a reminder that love, not just ingredients, is the key to a truly satisfying life.
