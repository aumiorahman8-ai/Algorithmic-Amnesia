## The Tale of Shat Bhai Champa (The Seven Brothers Champa) - A Retelling

Once upon a time, in a kingdom far away, lived a grieving king. He desperately wanted a son to inherit his throne, but years passed and he remained childless. Finally, the queen gave birth… not to one child, but to *seven*! Seven beautiful boys, all born at once. 

The king was overjoyed, but a wicked astrologer, envious of the king’s happiness, whispered a terrible prophecy. He claimed that the princes, though handsome and intelligent, were destined to bring ruin to the kingdom. He warned that one of them would eventually kill the others and seize the throne.

Terrified, the king ordered his ministers to secretly abandon the seven princes in the forest. He couldn't bear the thought of his sons fulfilling such a dreadful fate.  

The ministers, heartbroken, carried the babies deep into the woods and left them under a magnificent Champa tree (a fragrant magnolia).  As they were leaving, they heard a soft cooing. A mother bird, a *chakshi pakhi* (a bird with human-like eyes, often associated with divine protection in Bengali folklore), descended and began to care for the abandoned infants. 

The chakshi pakhi fed them fruits and berries, sheltered them from the rain, and protected them from wild animals.  As they grew, the princes, named after the Champa tree – Shat Bhai Champa – became strong, skilled, and deeply devoted to each other. They lived a simple life, hunting and gathering, unaware of their royal lineage.

One day, while hunting, the eldest prince, feeling restless, ventured further than usual. He stumbled upon a beautiful city and, captivated by its splendor, entered. He soon learned he was in his father’s kingdom! He discovered the truth about his birth and the prophecy that led to their abandonment.

Filled with a desire to see his father and clear their names, he returned to his brothers and told them everything.  The brothers, though shocked, agreed to go with him.  

When they arrived at the palace, the king was astonished. He couldn't believe his eyes! He had thought his sons were long dead.  However, the wicked astrologer, still lurking in the shadows, quickly reminded the king of his prophecy.

The king, still fearful, devised a test. He ordered each prince to bring him a magical item: a horse that could fly, a sword that could cut through anything, and a ring that granted wishes. He believed this test would reveal which prince was the most ambitious and therefore, the most likely to fulfill the prophecy.

Each of the Shat Bhai Champa, using their individual skills and courage, embarked on their quest. The eldest, known for his strength, found the flying horse. The second, a master swordsman, obtained the invincible sword. The third, clever and resourceful, secured the wish-granting ring.  The remaining four brothers, each possessing unique talents, brought back equally remarkable gifts – a magical shield, a self-playing musical instrument, a healing potion, and a talking parrot.

When they presented their gifts to the king, the astrologer insisted that the eldest prince, having brought the flying horse, was the most dangerous. He argued that the horse symbolized ambition and a desire for power.

But the princes, united in their love and loyalty, refused to let the astrologer sow discord. They declared that they would share all the gifts equally and rule the kingdom together. They proved that their strength lay not in individual ambition, but in their collective wisdom and brotherhood.

The king, witnessing their unwavering unity and selfless love, finally understood the true meaning of the prophecy. It wasn't a prediction of doom, but a test of their character. He realized the astrologer had misinterpreted the signs.  

He embraced his sons, banished the wicked astrologer, and declared them all heirs to the throne. The Shat Bhai Champa ruled the kingdom justly and wisely, bringing peace and prosperity to their people. They proved that love, loyalty, and unity could overcome even the darkest of prophecies.



And so, the tale of Shat Bhai Champa is a beloved story in Bengal, reminding us of the power of brotherhood, the importance of challenging fate, and the triumph of good over evil.  It’s a story often told to children, emphasizing the value of family and cooperation.
