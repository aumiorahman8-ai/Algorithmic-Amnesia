## Morar Shakkhi: The Witness of the Bubbles

The tale of Morar Shakkhi is a poignant Bengali folktale, a story about a simple, honest man, a cruel landlord, and the ephemeral nature of wealth and power. It's a story often told to remind people of the importance of truth and compassion.

Morar, a poor farmer, lived in a village under the oppressive rule of a zamindar (landlord) named Krishnarai. Krishnarai was notorious for his greed and injustice. He squeezed every last paisa from his tenants, often inventing false accusations to confiscate their land and possessions. 

Morar was known throughout the village for his honesty. He was a quiet man, content with his simple life, and always spoke the truth, even when it was difficult. This honesty, unfortunately, caught the attention of Krishnarai.

One day, Krishnarai decided he wanted Morar’s small plot of land. He summoned Morar to his court and falsely accused him of stealing a valuable ruby from the zamindar’s wife.  Morar vehemently denied the accusation, but Krishnarai, fueled by his desire for the land, refused to believe him.

“You will be punished unless you confess!” Krishnarai thundered. “But I will offer you a chance.  If you can truthfully answer one question, I will believe your innocence.  Tell me, what did your wife whisper to you last night as you slept?”

This was a cruel trick.  No man could truthfully reveal the intimate whispers of his wife in public.  Morar was trapped.  He knew confessing to the theft would mean ruin, but revealing his private life was equally unthinkable.

He thought and thought, his mind racing. Finally, with a heavy heart, he answered, “My Lord, last night, as I slept, my wife whispered, ‘Look, Morar, look! Bubbles are rising in the water!’”

Krishnarai and the court erupted in laughter.  “Bubbles? What nonsense is this!  A man of your stature, and your wife speaks of bubbles? Clearly, you are lying, trying to distract me with a foolish tale!”  He ordered Morar imprisoned and prepared to seize his land.

However, Krishnarai’s wife, who was a kind and just woman despite her husband’s cruelty, overheard the exchange.  She was intrigued by Morar’s answer.  That evening, she quietly went to Morar’s home and asked his wife about the previous night.

Morar’s wife, surprised, explained, “It’s true, my Lord.  We were washing clothes by the river.  The water was disturbed, and bubbles were rising to the surface.  I pointed them out to Morar, saying, ‘Look, Morar, look! Bubbles are rising in the water!’ It was a simple thing, but it brought us a moment of joy.”

The zamindar’s wife realized Morar had spoken the absolute truth, even in the face of immense pressure.  She was deeply ashamed of her husband’s injustice.  

She confronted Krishnarai, revealing Morar’s honesty and her husband’s malicious intent.  Krishnarai, exposed and humiliated, was forced to release Morar and return his land.  

But the story doesn’t end there.  Soon after, Krishnarai fell ill.  As he lay on his deathbed, he began to see bubbles rising around him – bubbles representing his wealth, his power, and his life, all fleeting and ephemeral. He realized that all his greed and cruelty had amounted to nothing.  He understood the profound truth in Morar’s simple answer.

The tale of Morar Shakkhi, the witness of the bubbles, serves as a powerful reminder that truth and honesty are more valuable than wealth and power. It teaches us that even the most seemingly insignificant moments can hold profound meaning, and that ultimately, everything in life is as transient as bubbles on the water.
