## The Exaggerating Matabbar and The Shrinking Snake

Old Matabbar was known throughout the village for one thing: his stories. Not for their truthfulness, mind you, but for their *size*. Every tale he told grew and grew with each retelling, becoming more fantastical and unbelievable than the last. He’d caught a fish the size of a boat, wrestled a tiger with one hand, and once, he claimed, had even flown to the moon on the back of a giant goose! People listened, mostly to laugh, and Matabbar thrived on the attention.

One day, while walking through the jungle, Matabbar stumbled upon a truly enormous snake. It was thick as a tree trunk and stretched as far as the eye could see. Now, most people would have been terrified, but Matabbar saw an opportunity. This was a story waiting to be born!

He rushed back to the village, breathless with excitement. “You won’t *believe* what I saw!” he boomed to the gathered villagers. “A snake! A snake so huge, it blocked out the sun! Its scales were like shields, and its eyes… its eyes were like burning coals! It was so long, it circled the entire village *three times*!”

The villagers chuckled. “Three times, Matabbar? Surely you exaggerate!” old Rahim chided.

Matabbar puffed out his chest. “Three times, I tell you! And when it hissed, the leaves fell from the trees! I stood before it, fearless, and it *trembled* at my gaze!”

He continued to embellish the story with each telling. The snake grew longer, thicker, and more terrifying. It now circled the village five times, then seven, then ten! Its hiss caused houses to crumble, and its shadow plunged the entire district into darkness. Matabbar, of course, remained the brave hero, single-handedly facing the monstrous beast.

But something strange began to happen. With each exaggeration of the snake’s size, the snake itself… *shrank*. 

The next day, villagers cautiously ventured into the jungle to see this incredible creature. They found a large snake, yes, but nowhere near the size Matabbar described. It was still impressive, but it only circled the village *once*.

The following day, the snake was even smaller. It barely circled the village at all, just coiled around a large banyan tree. 

As Matabbar’s stories reached new heights of absurdity, the snake continued to diminish. He claimed it was so long it touched the clouds, and the snake became no bigger than a strong rope. He said it could swallow elephants whole, and the snake could barely swallow a rabbit.

Finally, on the seventh day, Matabbar declared, “This snake was so colossal, it held the entire world in its coils! It was a creature of unimaginable power!”

When the villagers went to the jungle, they found… a tiny, harmless garden snake, no thicker than a finger. It blinked at them innocently from a patch of wildflowers.

The villagers realized what had happened. The snake, it seemed, was a magical creature. It thrived on truth, and withered with lies. Matabbar’s constant exaggerations had literally shrunk the snake out of existence!

Matabbar, humbled and ashamed, finally understood the consequences of his boasting. He had not conquered a monster, he had destroyed a wonder with his lies. 

From that day on, Matabbar still told stories, but he tried, with great difficulty, to keep them closer to the truth. He learned that a small, honest tale was far more valuable than a grand, fabricated one. And the villagers, though they still enjoyed his company, always listened with a knowing smile, remembering the tale of the exaggerating Matabbar and the shrinking snake.



This folktale is a charming reminder about the importance of honesty and the power of truth. It also serves as a gentle warning against boasting and exaggeration, showing how even fantastical things can be diminished by falsehoods.
