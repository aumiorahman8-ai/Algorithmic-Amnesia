## The Shol Fish's Lamentable Fate (Shol Machher Dukho) - A Retelling

The Padma River, wide and shimmering, was once home to a particularly beautiful and proud Shol fish. Now, Shol fish are known for their sleek silver bodies and delicious taste, and this one was the finest of them all. He wasn't just handsome, he was arrogant. He believed himself superior to all other fish, constantly boasting about his speed, his strength, and his shimmering scales.

“Look at those dull Katla!” he’d sneer, flicking his tail. “And those clumsy Rohu! They’re nothing compared to the grace of a Shol like me!”

He ignored the warnings of the older, wiser fish. They told him, “Pride comes before a fall, little Shol. The river is full of dangers, and boasting won’t protect you.” But he just laughed, convinced his speed would always save him.

One day, a fisherman named Haru cast his net into the Padma. Haru was a poor man, struggling to feed his family. He needed a good catch, and the Shol fish, with its reputation, was a prize worth hoping for. 

The net descended, and the Shol, as usual, was showing off, darting and weaving through the water. He saw the net, but instead of hiding, he thought, “Ha! I’m too quick for this clumsy thing!” He tried to outswim it, to flaunt his speed.

But Haru was clever. He’d coated the net with a fine, sticky mud. The Shol, in his frantic attempts to escape, only became more entangled. He thrashed and struggled, his beautiful scales becoming coated in the mud, dulling his shine. 

He cried out to the other fish, “Help me! Save me! I’m the fastest, the most beautiful! You must help!”

But the other fish, remembering his constant insults, simply watched. The Katla shook their heads. The Rohu swam away. They had warned him, and now he was facing the consequences of his pride.

Haru hauled the net in, and the Shol fish, covered in mud and utterly defeated, was brought onto the boat. He pleaded with Haru, offering promises of future good luck, even offering to grant wishes. 

“Please, fisherman! I am a magnificent fish! Don’t kill me! I’ll bring you fortune!”

Haru, though a kind man, was desperate. He needed to feed his family. He looked at the struggling fish, then at his empty basket, and sadly said, “Your beauty and speed are of no use to me now. I need food, not boasts.”

And with a heavy heart, he killed the Shol fish.

As the Shol fish lay dying, he finally understood the wisdom of the older fish. His pride had blinded him, and his arrogance had left him with no friends to help him in his time of need.  He lamented his fate, realizing that true worth wasn't in boasting, but in humility and kindness.

The tale of the Shol fish’s lamentable fate is told and retold along the banks of the Padma, a cautionary story about the dangers of pride and the importance of respecting others. It reminds everyone that even the most beautiful and talented among us are vulnerable, and that kindness and humility are far more valuable than arrogance and boastfulness.
