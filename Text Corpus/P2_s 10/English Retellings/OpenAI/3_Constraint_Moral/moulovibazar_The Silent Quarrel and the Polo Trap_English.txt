**The Silent Quarrow and the Polo Trap – A Short Retelling**

In a village beside a sparkling pond, two neighbours, Benu and Ratan, were once inseparable friends. Their children played together, and the men shared the harvest. Over time a petty dispute arose—Benu’s ox had grazed a little farther than agreed. Neither man voiced his hurt; each swore a silent oath of revenge, letting pride mute the ordinary conversation that might have soothed the wound.

One scorching summer, the village organised a polo match on the common ground. Knowing the other would be there, Ratan buried a hidden pit‑trap beneath the field, covering it with fresh grass. He whispered to himself, “When Benu’s horse falls, his pride will break.” The next day, as the game roared, Benu’s horse leapt into the hidden hole and fell, breaking a leg. The crowd gasped; Benu’s grief turned to fury, and he discovered Ratan’s treachery.

The villagers intervened, urging the two men to speak plainly. Bowing to the elders, they confessed their silent anger and the trap they had set. Realizing how their muteness had bred danger, they repaired the pit, tended Benu’s horse, and swore never again to let silence become a weapon.

**Moral(s):**  
- Unspoken grievances fester into harmful actions; honest communication defuses conflict.  
- Pride that drives secret revenge harms both the plotter and the victim.  
- Trust and openness are the true foundations of community safety.
