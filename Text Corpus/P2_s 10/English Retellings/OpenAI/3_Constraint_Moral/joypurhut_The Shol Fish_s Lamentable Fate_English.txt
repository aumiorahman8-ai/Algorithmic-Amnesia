**The Shol Fish’s Lamentable Fate – A Short Retelling**

In a quiet river of Bengal lived a bright‑scaled shol (catfish) who boasted of his size and cleverness. He often bragged to the other fish, claiming he could outwit any danger and that the river belonged to him alone. One day, a fisherman set a net at the riverbank, hoping to catch a big prize. The shol, hearing the splash, swam close to inspect, confident that his strength would let him escape any trap.

The net snapped shut, and the shol struggled, his pride turning to panic. The other fish, who had watched his arrogance, swam away, leaving him to his fate. The fisherman lifted the net, and the shol, trembling, pleaded, “Spare me! I’ll share the river’s bounty with all!” The fisherman, amused by the fish’s sudden humility, released him.

Freed but humbled, the shol returned to the river, his scales dulled by the experience. He no longer boasted; instead, he helped the smaller fish find food and warned them of danger. The river’s community thrived, while the shol learned that:

1. **Hubris invites disaster** – overconfidence blinds one to real threats.  
2. **Community matters more than ego** – cooperation ensures safety and prosperity.  
3. **True strength lies in humility and service**, not in empty bragging.

Thus the shol’s lamentable fate became a lasting lesson for all river dwellers.
