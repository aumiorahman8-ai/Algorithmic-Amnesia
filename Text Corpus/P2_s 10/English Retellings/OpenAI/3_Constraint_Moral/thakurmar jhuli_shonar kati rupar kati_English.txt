**Shonar Kati, Rupor Kati – The Gold Knife and the Silver Knife**

A poor woodcutter named Gopal once found two shining knives buried in a banyan tree: one of pure gold, the other of bright silver. The old forest spirit appeared and said, “The golden knife can cut anything you desire, but it will bring you only the taste of hunger. The silver knife cuts only for those who share what they take, and it fills the heart as well as the belly.”

Gopal first took the golden knife home. With a single slash he cut a mountain of rice, a river of milk, and a heap of jewels. His village marveled, then envied him. He began hoarding, refusing to help the needy, and soon his friends turned away. The gold‑knife’s shine dulled as guilt ate at him; the riches felt hollow, and hunger gnawed inside.

Desperate, Gopal tried the silver knife. The first cut he made was a small piece of fish for a starving child. Instantly, the knife glowed, and the fish multiplied, feeding the whole village. Each act of sharing made the silver blade sharper, and Gopal’s heart grew lighter.

When he finally returned the golden knife to the forest, the spirit praised him: “Greed builds only illusion; generosity builds true wealth.”  

**Lesson:** Possessions that satisfy only the self become burdens, while tools used for the welfare of others multiply blessings. Contentment, kindness, and sharing are the real treasures, far beyond any golden trinket.
