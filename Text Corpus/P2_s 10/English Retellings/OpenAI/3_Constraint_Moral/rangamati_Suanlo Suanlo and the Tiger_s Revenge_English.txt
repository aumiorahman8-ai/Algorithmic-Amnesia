**Suanlo, Suanlo and the Tiger’s Revenge**

Once, in a remote Bengali village, two inseparable friends—Suanlo and Suanlo—prided themselves on daring feats. When a ferocious tiger began terrorising the hamlet, the pair decided to trap it. They laid a trail of cooked rice and meat straight to a deep pit, then covered the opening with a flimsy bamboo mat. The tiger, drawn by the scent, fell in. The villagers cheered; Suanlo and Suanlo basked in fame.

The tiger, humbled but furious, swore revenge. While the friends celebrated, the tiger whispered to the forest spirits, who taught him a lesson in humility. The next morning, the tiger emerged, wearing a man’s loin‑cloth and pretending to be a wandering sage. He approached the friends, warning them that a “dangerous beast” lurked nearby and offered to lead them to safety—for a price. Remembering how easily they had fooled the tiger once, Suanlo and Suanlo laughed, refused his aid, and set a simple snare along the path. The disguised tiger stepped into it, and the trap snapped shut, imprisoning him once more.

The villagers learned three lasting lessons:

1. **Cleverness outweighs brute strength** – wit can tame even the mightiest foe.  
2. **Unity and caution safeguard success** – together they saw the tiger’s ruse.  
3. **Respect the consequences of pride** – triumph should never breed arrogance, lest revenge find a clever way back.

Thus, the tale endures as a reminder that humility, teamwork, and sharp minds are the true weapons against danger.
