**The Cunning Mother’s Strategic Upbringing**

A widow, once a devoted wife, remarried a widower who had a grown son, Raju. The new mother, Shanti, loved her own son, Binu, far more. To keep Binu from being eclipsed, she taught him to be clever, hardworking, and never to boast; she made him share his food and gifts with the step‑son, but always reminded him that the world rewards those who hide their brilliance behind modesty.

Raju, unaware of the subtle rivalry, grew proud and lazy, relying on his father’s name. Shanti, seeing his complacency, gave him a seemingly impossible task: to bring back a “golden mango” from the forest. While Raju fumed, Binu set out quietly, using his mother’s lessons—he observed the forest, befriended a hermit, and learned that the “golden mango” was a metaphor for wisdom. He returned with a simple, ripe mango and a story about patience and humility.

When the father judged the sons, he praised Raju for daring to venture out, yet he admired Binu for his thoughtful return. The father realized the true worth lay not in loud bravado but in quiet skill and self‑control. He rewarded both boys, but Binu’s modesty earned him greater respect.

**Morals**

1. **Humility masks true talent** – Quiet competence outlasts boastful show.
2. **Strategic nurture** – Parents can shape character by teaching restraint and cleverness.
3. **Self‑reliance over entitlement** – Hard work and observation bring genuine success.
4. **Equality through subtle guidance** – A caring step‑parent can uplift a child without overt favoritism.
