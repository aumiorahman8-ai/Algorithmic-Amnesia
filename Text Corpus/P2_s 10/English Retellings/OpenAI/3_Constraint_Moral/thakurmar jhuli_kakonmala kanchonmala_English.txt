**Kakonmala and Kanchonmala – A Bengali Folktale in Brief**

In a village by the river Brahmaputra lived two sisters, Kakonmala and Kanchonmala. Both were celebrated for their beauty, but their hearts were different. Kakonmala was modest, caring, and always helped the poor; Kanchonmala, though equally pretty, was proud, selfish and coveted wealth above all.

One monsoon night a wandering sage, disguised as a wandering minst — a *bhakta*—appeared at their doorstep. Sensing Kakonmala’s humility, he gifted her a single, shining *kakoli* (a white lotus) and said, “Plant it with kindness and it will bloom a thousand golden fruits for the good you do.” He warned that if greed or jealousy touched the flower, it would wither and bring misfortune.

Kakonmala planted the lotus in her modest garden and, each day, gave the first fruits to starving neighbours, the sick, and travelers. The lotus multiplied, and the village prospered. Her sister, observing the miracle, demanded the flower for herself. She stole the lotus, intending to hoard its riches. The moment Kanchonmala touched it with covetous hands, the petals turned black and the plant died, bringing famine to her own house.

The sisters learned that:

1. **Virtue outweighs vanity** – generosity nurtures abundance; selfishness rots it.
2. **Respect for gifts** – blessings are entrusted, not owned, and must be used responsibly.
3. **Humility invites divine favor** – pride repels it.
4. **Community over self** – true prosperity comes from sharing, not hoarding.

Kakonmala’s garden flourished forever, while Kanchonmala lived in regret, reminding all that the richest harvest is the one sown with compassion.
