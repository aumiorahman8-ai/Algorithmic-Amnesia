**The Stepmother’s Misguided Affection – A Short Retelling**

In a village by the Hooghly lived a widower with his two young daughters, Riya and Mina. After his death, a new wife entered the house. She was beautiful and charismatic, but she had never known a child’s love. When Riya, the elder, fell ill, the step‑mother tended to her with meticulous care, yet she praised only the child’s good looks and cleverness, never her kindness.

Mina, shy and quiet, spent her days weaving mats and sharing food with the hungry neighbors. The step‑mother dismissed her as “plain” and kept her chores heavy, believing that affection should be given to the child who appeared most worthy.

One monsoon night a fierce storm uprooted the thatched roof. The house trembled, and the children were trapped beneath debris. Mina, remembering the old woman’s warning that “help comes to those who help others,” slipped out, found a sturdy branch, and dragged her sister to safety. The step‑mother, frozen by fear, could do nothing.

When the floodwater receded, the villagers praised Mina for her bravery and compassion. The step‑mother, humbled, finally saw that love that is given for selfish reasons withers, while love rooted in selflessness endures.

**Moral(s):**  
- True affection is earned through kindness, not by appearance or status.  
- Favoritism blinds us to genuine virtue; equal care nurtures a harmonious family.  
- Self‑less deeds protect and uplift us in crisis, whereas selfish love leaves us vulnerable.  
- Humility and gratitude are the foundations of a caring heart.
