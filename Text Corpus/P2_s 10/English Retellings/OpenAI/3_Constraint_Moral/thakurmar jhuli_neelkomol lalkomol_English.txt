**Neelkomol and Lalkomol – A Brief Retelling and Its Lessons**

In a tranquil village beside a wide river lived two sisters, Neelkomol and Lalkomol. Their names meant “blue lotus” and “red lotus,” reflecting their opposite natures. Neelkomol was calm, humble and content with simple meals; Lalkomol was proud, greedy and always craved the finest things.

One summer the river flooded, covering the fields. The villagers were forced to share the scant food that survived. Neelkomol offered the little rice she had to her neighbors without hesitation. Lalkomol, however, hoarded a few grains, hoping a later bargain would make her richer.

When the flood receded, the village elders announced a festival to thank the river gods. They asked each family to bring a lotus—blue for humility, red for courage. Neelkomol, having lived with modesty, found a perfect blue lotus floating gently downstream. Lalkomol searched desperately for a red lotus, but none would come; the river, remembering her selfishness, kept the red blossoms hidden.

At the festival, the priest praised Neelkomol’s lotus as a symbol of *sattva*—pure, self‑less spirit. Lalkomol left empty‑handed, realizing that greed repels blessings.

**Moral(s):**  
1. **Humility and generosity attract true wealth**, while selfishness drives it away.  
2. **Contentment outweighs craving;** a modest heart discovers beauty where the arrogant sees none.  
3. **Our deeds ripple like water**—what we give returns to us, and what we withhold is withheld.  

Thus, the tale of Neelkomol and Lalkomol teaches that the real lotus of life blooms from a humble, giving spirit.
