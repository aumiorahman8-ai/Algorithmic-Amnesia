**Monri and Sa‑Tha‑Nu: A Celestial Love, an Earthly Test**

In the heavens lived Monri, the moon‑kissed daughter of the sky‑king, whose laughter made the night bloom with flowers. On earth dwelt Sa‑Tha‑Nu, a humble potter whose hands shaped clay but whose heart yearned for something higher. One night, while Sa‑Tha‑Nu worked by a river, Monri slipped down on a silver wisp, drawn by the potter’s sincere song. They fell in love, and the heavens granted Monri a thin veil of mortality so she could walk beside Sa‑Tha‑Nu.

Their union, however, invited the jealous wind‑spirit, who warned Monri that a mortal’s world was fraught with hunger, disease, and death. The warning came true: Sa‑Tha‑Nu fell ill, his workshop burned, and the village accused them of sorcery. Monri, remembering her celestial gifts, called upon the stars for aid, but every miracle demanded a price—her life force. She chose instead to teach Sa‑Tha‑Nu patience, humility, and the power of community. Together they tended the sick, rebuilt the kiln, and shared their bounty with neighbors.

When the final test arrived—a famine that threatened the whole valley—Monri’s tears turned to dew, irrigating the fields. The harvest returned, and the wind‑spirit, humbled, granted Monri back her immortal glow, on the condition that she would return to the heavens each full moon.

**Lessons**

1. **Love demands sacrifice, not surrender.** True partnership means sharing burdens and growing together.  
2. **Humility and service turn hardship into strength.** By helping others, Sa‑Tha‑Nu regained his own hope.  
3. **Balance between the divine and the mortal is possible through duty, not escape.** Monri kept her celestial nature alive by honoring earthly responsibilities.  
4. **Community is the cushion against tragedy.** The couple’s survival hinged on the village’s willingness to receive their gifts.  

Thus the tale reminds us that love, when rooted in compassion and responsibility, can transcend even the gap between heaven and earth.
