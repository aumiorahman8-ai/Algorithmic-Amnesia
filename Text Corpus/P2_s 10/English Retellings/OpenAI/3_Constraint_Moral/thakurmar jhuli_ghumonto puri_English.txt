**The Tale of Ghumonto Puri – A Bengali Folktale (250 words)**  

In a quiet village by the Hooghly lived Puri, a cobbler whose greatest talent was falling asleep. From dawn till dusk he could be found with his head on a pillow of straw, his tools untouched, while his wife, Lakshmi, toiled to keep the hearth warm.  

One scorching summer day a travelling merchant stopped at the village square, his cart laden with fresh rice‑pulao. He asked Puri to repair a cracked sandal for a modest fee. Puri, half‑asleep, promised the work “tomorrow.” The merchant, trusting the man's word, left his cart under a mango tree and went on his way.  

When Puri finally awoke, the cart was gone—stolen by a band of thieves who had taken advantage of his slumber. News of the loss spread, and the villagers, who had once praised Puri’s skill, now whispered about his idleness. Desperate, Puri begged Lakshmi for forgiveness and vowed to change.  

From that night onward, Puri worked from sunrise to sunset, mending shoes for everyone who needed them. He saved a small coin each day, and when a flood threatened the hamlet, he used his savings to help rebuild homes. The merchant, hearing of Puri’s transformation, returned, offering a permanent steady job.  

**Moral(s):**  
- **Laziness steals more than time; it steals trust and livelihood.**  
- **Honour your promises; a single neglected duty can ruin many.**  
- **Diligence and humility restore reputation and invite new blessings.**  
- **A community rewards those who turn effort into service.**  

Thus the legend of Ghumonto Puri endures as a reminder that waking up to one’s responsibilities is the first step toward a respected, prosperous life.
