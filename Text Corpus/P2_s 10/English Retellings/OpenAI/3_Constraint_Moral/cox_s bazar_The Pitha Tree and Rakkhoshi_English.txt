**The Pitha Tree and the Rakkhoshi**

In a quiet village, a humble woman named Lila tended a small garden. One night she dreamed of a magical pitha‑shaped tree that bore sweet rice cakes all year round. When she awoke, she planted a seed she found by the riverbank, hoping the dream would become real.

Weeks passed, and a slender sapling sprouted, soon bearing golden‑brown pithas that smelled of coconut and jaggery. The whole village gathered to taste the miracle, praising Lila’s generosity. Yet, a jealous rakkhoshi (demoness) who lived in the nearby forest coveted the tree’s bounty. Disguised as a weary traveler, she begged Lila for a single pitha. Lila, kind‑hearted, offered the whole basket.

The rakkhoshi’s greed swelled; she demanded the tree itself. Lila refused, saying the tree was a gift for all. Angered, the rakkhoshi tried to uproot the tree with her dark powers, but the tree’s roots, nourished by the villagers’ gratitude, glowed and repelled her. Defeated, the rakkhoshi fled, never to return.

**Morals**

1. **Generosity multiplies blessings** – Lila’s selfless sharing turned a miracle into a communal treasure.  
2. **Greed destroys** – The rakkhoshi’s insatiable desire led to her downfall.  
3. **Community gratitude protects** – The collective appreciation of the villagers gave the tree strength beyond any single power.  

Thus, the tale reminds us that kindness nurtures lasting wealth, while selfishness invites loss.
