**The Exaggerating Matabbar and the Shrinking Snake**  

In a quiet Bengali village lived Matabbar, a man famed for his wild bragging. He boasted that his strength could lift a buffalo, his wisdom could outwit a king, and his generosity could feed an entire town. One day, while strolling near a paddy field, Matabbar saw a long, glossy snake coiled on a stone.  

“Ah, a tiny creature!” he declared, “I could swallow it whole and still have room for a mango!” He lunged, but the snake slipped away, leaving only a faint hiss. Embarrassed, Matabbar tried to save face by claiming the snake had been a monstrous beast that he had merely frightened away.  

The next morning, the village chief announced a contest: whoever could bring the most impressive tale would win a sack of rice. Matabbar, eager to prove himself, exaggerated further, describing how he had wrestled a tiger, out‑sang a nightingale, and turned a storm into sunshine with his voice.  

When the chief heard the story, he smiled and said, “Matabbar, your tongue is longer than any rope, but truth is the only rope that can bind respect.” He then asked Matabbar to fetch water from the well. The well was dry; the only water left was a tiny puddle that shrank as Matabbar tried to scoop it, mirroring his own shrinking credibility.  

**Morals**  
1. **Honesty outweighs hyperbole** – Boasting may win momentary attention, but truth builds lasting respect.  
2. **Pride precedes a fall** – Exaggerating one’s deeds invites ridicule and loss of trust.  
3. **Actions speak louder than words** – Genuine deeds, however modest, are more valuable than grandiose tales.  

Thus, the village learned that humility and sincerity are the true measures of a person’s worth.
