**The Brahman and the Brahmani**

A learned Brahman and his clever wife, the Brahmani, lived modestly in a village. One day a travelling merchant showed them a glittering pot of gold, claiming it was a gift for anyone who could answer his riddle. The couple, convinced of their wit, boasted they would solve it and claim the treasure.

The riddle was simple: “What does a man own that he can give away, yet still keep?” The Brahman, proud of his learning, answered quickly, “My shadow.” The merchant smiled, poured the gold into their hands, and vanished.

Ecstatic, they rushed home, dreaming of grand houses and fine clothes. Yet, on the way, a hungry child begged for a morsel. The Brahmani, remembering a proverb her mother taught—*“Wealth that is hoarded is like a river that never reaches the sea”*—gave half her gold to the child. The Brahman, abashed, did the same.

When they arrived, their house was unchanged, but the joy of giving warmed their hearts. That night, a soft voice whispered: *“True wealth lies in generosity, humility, and contentment.”* The couple learned that boasting and hoarding bring only emptiness, while sharing even a small portion turns riches into lasting blessings.

**Morals**

1. **Humility over pride** – Boasting blinds us to true wisdom.  
2. **Generosity multiplies wealth** – Giving enriches the giver more than hoarding.  
3. **Contentment beats greed** – Satisfaction with what we have yields lasting peace.  
4. **Wisdom is practical, not just scholarly** – True knowledge is shown in how we treat others.
