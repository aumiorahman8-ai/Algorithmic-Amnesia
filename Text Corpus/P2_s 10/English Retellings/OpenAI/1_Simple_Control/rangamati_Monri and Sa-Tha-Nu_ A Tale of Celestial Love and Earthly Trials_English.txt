**Monri and Sa‑Tha‑Nu: A Tale of Celestial Love and Earthly Trials**  
*(A Bengali folktale retold for today’s listeners)*  

---

### 1. The Birth of Two Stars  

In the time when the night sky was still being “sewn” by the weaver‑god **Chitrakar**, two bright sparks fell from the heavens and took shape among the constellations.  

* **Monri** – a moon‑daughter, born of the silvery curve of *Chandradipa*. She glittered like a full moon reflected on the water of the **Padma** river. Though a celestial being, Monri possessed a tender heart that fluttered at the scent of earth‑bound jasmine and the sound of a village’s *bhat*‑boiling kettle.  
* **Sa‑Tha‑Nu** – a young star‑prince, the youngest son of the fiery constellation **Rig‑Vijñāna**. His light was a golden amber, warm enough to melt the night‑frost on the paddy fields. He was bold, curious, and harboured a secret longing to walk among mortals.

The two never met in the heavens; their courses were different—Monri glided in the cool, calm lanes of the moon, while Sa‑Tha‑Nu raced the swift, restless paths of the sun‑streaked sky. Yet the ancient **Pothik‑Mayer** (the old weaver‑woman of fate) wove a single golden thread between them, foretelling that they would one day be drawn together by a mortal’s longing.

---

### 2. The Falling to Earth  

Long ago, in a sleepy village called **Balijore**, a poor potter named **Gopal** toiled by the riverbank, shaping earthen pots for the upcoming **Durga Puja**. Each night he would look up, praying for a boon that would lift his family from poverty. One moonlit night, as Gopal sang a plaintive *bhajan* to the moon, a silver tear fell from Monri’s cheek—she had felt the pang of human yearning. The tear became a tiny pearl that slipped into the river and rolled onto Gopal’s workshop floor.

When Gopal lifted the pearl, it glowed with a soft lunar hue. He placed it in a simple clay pot and, unknowingly, offered it as *prasad* to the deity. The next morning, the river swelled, the fields blossomed, and Gopal’s pots sold for gold. Word spread, and the village rejoiced.

But the pearl’s brilliance also attracted the attention of Sa‑Tha‑Nu, who had descended from the sky as a shooting star, chasing the same sigh of longing he sensed in the mortal world. He landed beside the river, his ember‑like footstep turning the water a faint amber. From the rippling surface, he glimpsed the shining pearl tucked inside the pot.

Sa‑Tha‑Nu, unable to stay a mere spark, transformed his fiery essence into a handsome young man, cloaked in a **dhoti** of woven starlight. He entered the village, pretending to be a wandering *baul* minstrel, and sang songs of distant constellations. The villagers, entranced by his voice, invited him to Gopal’s home, where the pearl lay.

When Sa‑Tha‑Nu touched the pearl, a burst of radiant light filled the room. In that instant, the veils between heaven and earth fell away, and Monri, still in her moon‑maid form, stepped out of the glow, her hair streaming like silver lotus petals.

The villagers gasped, but Monri’s eyes fell upon Sa‑Tha‑Nu, and a smile like the first crescent of dawn lit her face. The two celestial lovers recognized each other at once, for the golden thread of destiny had braided their souls long before their mortal forms ever touched.

### 3. The First Trial – The Test of **Bhramar** (The Bumblebee)  

The heavens are never quick to bless love that crosses their borders. The **Brahma‑Kalpādikshi**, the great all‑seeing owl of the night, swooped down and declared:

> “If your love is true, you must each endure a trial on earth. Until the trial is passed, the heavens will not permit you to remain together.”
