**The Princess, the Python, and the Four Brothers**  
*An old Bengali folktale, retold*

---

### 1. The Kingdom and the Princess

In a lush kingdom by the banks of the Ganges, a wise king ruled with kindness. He had a beautiful daughter named **Princess Rani**, whose voice was as sweet as the nightingale’s song and whose heart was as pure as the river’s water. The king loved her dearly and promised that she would marry a man of great virtue.

One day, while the princess was strolling through the palace garden, a **great python**—as long as a cart and as thick as a banyan tree trunk—slithered out from the underbrush. The serpent’s eyes glittered like polished jade, and its tongue flicked with a hiss that sent shivers through the garden.

The python spoke in a deep, resonant voice:

> “Princess, I have been cursed by a jealous sorcerer. Only a brave soul can free me, and in return I shall grant a boon to the one who helps me.”

Princess Rani, though frightened, felt compassion for the creature. She promised to find a way to break the curse, for she believed that kindness could tame even the fiercest of beasts.

---

### 2. The Four Brothers

In a nearby village lived **four brothers**—**Bikram, Chandra, Gopal, and Hari**—who were known throughout the land for their strength, wit, and unity. Though each had a different talent, they always worked together as a team.

- **Bikram** was the strongest, able to lift a bull with one hand.  
- **Chandra** possessed a clever mind and could solve riddles in a heartbeat.  
- **Gopal** was a master of music; his flute could calm even a storm.  
- **Hari** was swift and agile, moving like a deer through the forest.

When news of the princess’s plight reached the village, the brothers decided to help. They rode their sturdy oxen to the palace, where the king explained the python’s curse and the promise of a boon.

The king warned them: “The python is guarded by a fierce guardian spirit. Only those who are pure of heart and united in purpose may approach it.”

The brothers bowed, clasped hands, and set out together toward the forest where the python rested.

---

### 3. The Journey Through the Forest

The forest was thick with tangled vines and echoing calls of unseen creatures. As they ventured deeper, they encountered three challenges, each testing a different brother’s gift.

#### **First Challenge – The River of Mirrors**

A wide river blocked their path, its surface shimmering like a thousand tiny mirrors. In its depths swam a monstrous fish that would devour anyone who tried to cross.

- **Bikram** stepped forward, his muscles bulging. He tried to wade through, but the water roared back.  
- **Chandra** examined the river and noticed that the fish was attracted to bright reflections. He instructed his brothers to cover their faces with dark cloth and to walk quietly.  
- **Gopal** lifted his flute and played a soft, soothing melody. The fish, entranced, rose to the surface and swam in circles, allowing the brothers to cross safely.  

#### **Second Challenge – The Hill of Whispering Winds**

A steep hill rose before them, its summit shrouded in a swirling vortex of wind that whispered doubts and fears into the ears of travelers.

- **Hari** sprinted up the hill, but the wind tried to push him back, shouting, “You cannot succeed!”  
- **Chandra** reminded his brothers that the wind only spoke lies. He whispered a counter‑spell of confidence, and the wind’s voice faded.  
- **Bikram** used his strength to anchor a sturdy pole, while **Gopal** sang a steady chant that steadied their steps. Together they reached the top, the wind calming into a gentle breeze.

#### **Third Challenge – The Garden of Illusions**

At the hill’s crest lay a garden of fragrant flowers, each petal glowing with an otherworldly light. The garden was an illusion; those who lingered would become lost forever in its beauty.

- **Gopal** recognized the pattern of the flowers and played a tune that revealed the true path.  
- **Hari** darted ahead, testing each step, and signaled the safe route.  
- **Bikram** and **Chandra** followed, never looking back, until they emerged from the garden onto a clearing where the great python lay coiled around a massive stone.

---

### 4. The Confrontation with the Python

The python’s eyes glowed with a sorrowful light. “You have come far, brave brothers,” it hissed. “But to break my curse, you must each give something of yourself.”

- **Bikram** offered his mighty strength, promising to give up his power for a day.  
- **Chandra** offered his cleverness, agreeing to solve any riddle the python might pose.  
- **Gopal** offered his music, willing to silence his flute forever if needed.  
- **Hari** offered his swiftness, ready to stay still for a whole night.

The python coiled tighter and spoke a riddle:

> “I have a heart that never beats, a home without doors, and I travel without moving. What am I?”

The brothers looked at each other. **Chandra** smiled, whispered the answer, and said, “A river.” The python’s eyes softened, and the stone began to crack.

A bright light burst forth, and the curse shattered. The python’s massive body shrank, its scales turning into golden threads that floated upward, forming a radiant crown. The serpent’s true form—a **prince** named **Ranjit**, cursed by a jealous sorceress—was revealed. He bowed deeply to the brothers and the princess, thanking them for their bravery and compassion.

---

### 5. The Boon and the Celebration

True to his word, the freed prince offered a boon. He asked only for one thing: that the four brothers be honored as protectors of the kingdom, and that Princess Rani be allowed to choose her own destiny.

The king, overjoyed, declared a grand feast. The brothers were given lands and titles, and the princess, moved by the brothers’ selflessness, chose to marry **Prince Ranjit**, whose heart was now pure and grateful.

The kingdom celebrated for days, with Gopal’s flute echoing through the streets, Hari’s swift dances, Bikram’s feats of strength, and Chandra’s riddles that entertained all. The curse of the python became a story told to children, reminding them that **courage, unity, and kindness can break even the darkest spells**.

---

### 6. The Moral

- **Unity is Strength:** The brothers succeeded because they combined their unique gifts and worked as one.  
- **Compassion Breaks Curses:** Princess Rani’s willingness to help a suffering creature set the whole adventure in motion.  
- **True Power Lies in Self‑Sacrifice:** Each brother offered a part of himself, showing that generosity can transform fate.

And so, the kingdom lived in peace, the river sang its gentle song, and the tale of the Princess, the Python, and the Four Brothers was whispered from generation to generation, a shining example of bravery, love, and the magic that lives in a kind heart.
