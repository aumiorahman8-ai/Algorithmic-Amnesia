**The Exaggerating Matabbar and the Shrinking Snake**  
*(A Bengali folk tale, retold)*  

---

### 1. The Village and Its Characters  

In a quiet village tucked between the emerald fields of Bengal, lived a man named **Matabbar**. He was known far and wide for one peculiar habit: whatever he saw, he would **exaggerate**. A tiny mango became a “giant golden fruit,” a short rainstorm turned into a “monsoon that drowned the world.” People loved him for his humor, but they also kept a wary eye on his tall tales.

Near the same village, winding through the paddy fields, slithered a **snake** named **Nokh**. Nokh was no ordinary serpent; he possessed a magical gift—**he could shrink himself at will**. When frightened, he could become as thin as a blade of grass; when confident, he could swell to the size of a thick rope. He kept this secret to himself, fearing that the villagers would kill him if they discovered his power.

---

### 2. The First Encounter  

One scorching afternoon, Matabbar set out to fetch water from the well. As he walked, he sang loudly, “The sun is a blazing furnace that will melt the earth!” He reached the well and, peering into its dark depths, he saw a flash of movement. A slender snake was coiled around a stone, its head flicking lazily.

Matabbar, ever the dramatist, shouted, “A monstrous dragon has taken residence in my well! It will devour anyone who dares to draw water!” He ran back to the village, breathless, and gathered a crowd.

The villagers, half‑amused and half‑concerned, followed him to the well. When they saw the tiny, harmless snake, they laughed. “Matabbar, you’ve turned a garden‑variety snake into a fire‑breathing beast!” they chided.

Nokh, hearing the commotion, felt a pang of embarrassment. He slithered away, shrinking himself until he was no larger than a blade of grass, and hid among the reeds.

---

### 3. The Snake’s Plan  

Nokh thought, *If this man can make a tiny snake seem monstrous, perhaps I can turn his exaggerations against him.* He decided to teach Matabbar a gentle lesson. Using his shrinking gift, he would appear in different sizes at the right moments, letting Matabbar’s own stories spiral out of control.

The next day, Matabbar set out to the market, boasting to anyone who would listen, “I will bring back a sack of gold!” He imagined the sack so heavy that the cart would creak under its weight.

As he passed a mango tree, a **tiny snake**—still Nokh, now shrunk to the size of a thin twig—slithered across his path. Matabbar, eyes wide, exclaimed, “Look! A gigantic serpent! It must be the guardian of the treasure I shall bring!” He stopped, stared, and then, with a theatrical gasp, declared, “I shall tame this beast and it shall lead me to riches!”

He tried to grab the snake, but it slipped away, shrinking further until it was invisible. Matabbar, now flustered, shouted, “It’s a phantom! A ghost‑snake that can vanish!” The villagers, hearing his cries, gathered again, this time more curious than before.

---

### 4. The Exaggeration Escalates  

Word spread through the village: *Matabbar has encountered a magical snake that can disappear and reappear.* Children whispered, “Maybe it can swallow whole houses!” Elders chuckled, “Perhaps it will swallow the whole village!” The story grew taller with each retelling, just as Matabbar liked it.

Nokh, watching from his hidden burrow, realized his plan was working—Matabbar’s exaggerations were now the talk of the town, and the villagers were beginning to treat the snake with a mixture of awe and fear.

But Nokh also felt a twinge of guilt. He had never meant to frighten anyone; he only wanted Matabbar to understand the power of words.

---

### 5. The Confrontation  

One evening, as the sun painted the sky amber, Matabbar sat by the river, still bragging about his “gold‑laden sack” and the “vanishing serpent.” He boasted, “When I return, I shall bring a treasure so great that the moon itself will envy us!”

From the reeds, a **soft hissing** rose. It was Nokh, now at his normal size—neither huge nor tiny, just a modest snake. He slithered forward, his eyes calm.

Matabbar, startled, leapt up, his hands flailing. “Aha! The great dragon appears! I shall wrestle it!” he shouted, his voice echoing over the water.

Nokh raised his head and spoke, his voice a gentle whisper that only Matabbar could hear:  

> “Matabbar, I am not a dragon, nor a phantom. I am a snake who can shrink and grow, but I have no desire to frighten you. I have watched you turn the smallest things into giants. Let us make a pact: you will speak the truth, and I will keep my secret safe.”

Matabbar, eyes wide with a mixture of fear and curiosity, stammered, “What… what do you want from me?”

> “Only this,” replied Nokh. “When you tell a story, let it be as it truly is. If you must embellish, do it with a smile, not to frighten or deceive. In return, I will never shrink to hide, nor grow to cause panic. I will simply be the snake I am.”

Matabbar, humbled, lowered his arms. “I have been foolish,” he admitted. “My words have become louder than my heart. I will try to be honest.”

Nokh nodded, and with a flick of his tail, he **shrunk** just enough to slip into the grass, disappearing from sight.

---

### 6. The Lesson Takes Root  

From that day forward, Matabbar’s stories changed. He still loved a good tale, but he kept his exaggerations light, using them to entertain rather than to alarm. When he spoke of the mango tree, he said, “The mangoes are big enough to feed a family,” instead of “giant golden fruits that could feed a kingdom.” The villagers laughed, but they also trusted him more.

Nokh, meanwhile, lived peacefully among the reeds, occasionally shrinking to avoid danger, but never using his gift to cause trouble. He and Matabbar shared a quiet respect; the villagers would sometimes spot a glint of scales in the grass and smile, remembering the day a snake taught a man the value of modesty.

---

### 7. The Moral  

The tale of **The Exaggerating Matabbar and the Shrinking Snake** reminds us that words have power. A small truth, when blown up, can become a frightening monster; a modest truth, spoken with humility, can bring harmony. And sometimes, the quietest creatures—like a snake that can shrink—hold the wisdom to set us straight.

---  

*And so, in the fields of Bengal, the story lives on, whispered by the wind, teaching each new generation the balance between imagination and honesty.*
