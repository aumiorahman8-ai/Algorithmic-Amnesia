**Neelkomol – Lalkomol: A Bengali Folktale Retold**

*In the shade of the mango trees that sway over the salty banks of the Hooghly, there once lived a modest family in a thatched‑roofed cottage on the edge of the village of Chandrapur.*  

The mother, **Madhabi**, was a skilled weaver; her hands could turn a skein of raw cotton into a shimmery dhoti or a bright saree that glimmered like sunrise on water.  Her two daughters were as different as the two flowers after which they were named:  

* **Neelkomol** – “the blue lotus” – a child of soft speech, calm eyes, and a heart that never balked at a stranger’s plea.  
* **Lalkomol** – “the red lotus” – a fiery spirit, quick to laugh, quick to take, and quick to think only of herself.

Their father had died when they were still infants, and the three of them survived on Madhabi’s weaving and the little plot of rice they tended.  Yet the seasons were fickle: a bad monsoon could wipe out a harvest, a fever could sap strength, and the world beyond the village seemed ever‑larger and harsher.
