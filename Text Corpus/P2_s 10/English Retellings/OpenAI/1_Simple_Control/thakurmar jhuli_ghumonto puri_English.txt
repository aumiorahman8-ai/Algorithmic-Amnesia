### The Tale of **Ghumonto Puri** – The Ever‑Sleeping Villager  

*In the winding lanes of a small Bengal village, where rice paddies stretch like green ribbons and the scent of jasmine slips out of every open window, there lived a man who was famous for one thing alone: he could sleep through anything.*

His name, as the story goes, was **Puri**—and because he was never seen awake, folk began calling him **Ghumonto Puri** (the Sleeping Puri).  
The tale has been told around clay‑lamp flickers for generations, each teller adding a pinch of humor, a dash of exaggeration, but the heart of the story stays the same.

---

## 1. The Village and Its Unusual Son  

Puri was the only son of **Mohan Babu**, a modest weaver who stitched cotton dhotis for the market. While other boys ran after crickets or helped pull the plough, Puri’s favourite pastime was to curl up on a *jori* (woven mat) under the shade of a tamarind tree and drift into dreamland.

*Even the call of the *bela* (the harvest festival) couldn’t rouse him.*  
The village priest would shout, “Puri! Come to the puja!”  
Puri’s eyes opened just enough to blink, then closed again, as if the world outside were a distant echo.

Because of his habit, the villagers began to treat him like a living legend. “If you ever need a secret kept, whisper it to Ghumonto Puri—he’ll never remember it!” they’d joke.

---

## 2. The Royal Invitation  

One scorching summer, the **King of Burdwan** announced a grand feast to celebrate the arrival of a caravan of silks from the north. The invitation reached every hamlet, and the village council decided that *Puri* should go, just for the novelty of seeing a man who slept through a royal banquet.

Mohan Babu, though reluctant, tied a small bundle of *jute* sandals, a *lota* of water, and a sack of *panta bhat* (fermented rice) for his wandering son and sent him off.

Puri set out, not because he wanted to, but because his mother **Sakhi** gave him a gentle push. He walked lazily, his head bobbing like a rice plant in the wind, until he reached the palace gates.

---

## 3. The King’s Dilemma  

When the guards saw the rag‑clad villager, they were bemused. “Who is this?” they asked the king.  

The king, amused by the sight of a man who could sleep through a royal proclamation, ordered a seat for him on the banquet’s farthest side. He thought, *“Let him nap; the feast will be fuller without his chatter.”*

But as the banquet began, a **troublesome monkey**—the palace’s notorious mischief‑maker—leaped onto the table, scattering plates, knocking over the golden *pankha* (decorative fan), and stealing the king’s prized ruby‑set necklace.

The court erupted into panic. Guards chased the monkey, but the animal was too swift, slipping through corridors and hiding in the palace’s many secret passages. The king, his face turning as pale as the moon, declared: *“If the thief cannot be caught by my best men, I shall forfeit a portion of the kingdom’s grain to the hungry foragers of the forest!”*

The assembled nobles gasped. The kingdom’s food stores were already low after a season of drought. The king’s resolve was firm—find the monkey, or lose a great portion of their livelihood.

---

## 4. Ghumonto Puri Saves the Day (Without Even Waking Up)  

While the palace swarmed like a hive, Ghumonto Puri remained seated, his chin resting on his folded hands, eyes closed. A low, steady snore escaped his lips—soft, rhythmic, like a distant drum.

A **young palace cook**, who had been tasked to fetch water, paused beside Puri. The cook, a clever girl named **Mali**, had heard the story of the sleeping villager and wondered whether his “sleep” might be a special kind of awareness.

She whispered, “Sir, the monkey has hidden the necklace somewhere. Perhaps if we… speak to you while you sleep, you’ll hear us.”

Mali leaned close to Puri’s ear and described the situation in a hushed, urgent tone, describing every detail: the monkey’s silver tail, the glittering jewels, the hidden chamber near the west wing. She repeated it thrice, as if chanting a spell.
