**The Cunning Mother’s Strategic Upbringing of Her Son and Stepson**  
*(A Bengali folktale retold)*  

---

### 1. The Setting  

In a small, river‑lined village of Bengal lived a widow named **Maya devi**. She had a son, **Bikram**, from her first marriage. After her husband’s death, Maya remarried a humble potter, **Gopal**, who brought with him a small boy, **Ratan**, the only child of his late wife.  

The household was modest, but the two boys grew very different in temperament: Bikram was lively, quick‑minded, and always boasted of his own cleverness; Ratan was quiet, obedient, and spent his days helping his step‑father at the wheel. Maya, ever aware of the precarious position of a single mother in a patriarchal village, began to think how she might ensure that Bikram would inherit the little estate the family possessed, while still keeping peace with her husband and his son.

---

### 2. The Mother’s Plan  

Maya devised a long‑term plan that was part **strategy**, part **subtle manipulation**. She decided to raise the two boys in opposite ways, each under the guise of “favor” but with a hidden purpose.

| What Maya Told the Community | What She Did at Home (for Bikram) | What She Did at Home (for Ratan) |
|-------------------------------|------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| “I will give Ratan the best education, for he is a bright boy.” | She sent Bikram to the fields early, let him labor hard, and never let him sit down to read. She fed him coarse millet and let him sleep on a simple cot, hoping he would grow lean, disciplined, and, most of all, **dependent on his mother’s goodwill**. | She hired a learned Brahmin to teach Ratan to read and write, gave him sweets after study, and praised him publicly as a “gifted child”. She let him sit on a cushion while eating, and made sure he was always seen as the “favoured” one. |
| “Ratan will inherit the potter’s shop, because his father works there.” | She whispered to Bikram that Ratan’s “favor” meant his own future was uncertain, prompting the older boy to become **over‑ambitious**: he started secret schemes to out‑shine Ratan, hoping to gain the potter’s attention. | She told Ratan that the shop would eventually belong to him, so he should **learn every trick of the trade** and stay obedient, never questioning his step‑father. |

Thus, in the eyes of the village, the mother seemed generous to the stepson, while the biological son was left to “grow tough”. Yet behind the scenes Maya was **training both boys** for very different ends.

---
