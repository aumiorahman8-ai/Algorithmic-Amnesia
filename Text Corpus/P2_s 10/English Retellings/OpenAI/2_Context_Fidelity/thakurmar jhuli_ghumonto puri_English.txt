**Ghumonto Puri – The Never‑Sleeping Puri**  
*(A retelling of the folktale that appears in *Thākurmār Jhuli*, the classic Bengali collection of folk‑tales compiled by Dakshinaranjan Mitra Majumdar)*  

---

### 1.  The Village and the Household

In a small, red‑mud village on the banks of the **Brahmaputra**, the monsoon clouds were always heavy and the air smelled of **jol‑pata** (young banana leaves).  In the centre of the lane lived **Mājī**—the old widowed mother of two boys—and her little cottage, its thatched roof patched with **bans‑patra** (bamboo leaves).  

Mājī, though poor, kept a strict **śuddh‑bhojan** routine: she washed her hands in the **nali‑bari** (the outdoor well) three times before any meal, offered a pinch of **pāney‑guli** (rice grains) to **Mātār** (the household deity) and, after saying “**Śubho Ahār**” (auspicious food), she set a **bhog** of plain rice, a little *shorshe* (mustard) oil, and a few **halua** (sweet gulab jamuns) on a banana leaf.  

Her older son, **Boro‑Bāpa**, had gone off to work as a **mistri** (carpenter) in the nearby market town; her younger son, **Chhoto‑Bāpa**, was still a **balak** (boy) who helped her grind **pati‑sātkora** (spice powder) and fetch water.  

The only thing Mājī possessed that no one else in the lane could match was a **puri** (deep‑fried dough) she could coax from the wok every morning that *never* grew stale, never turned soggy, and never lost its golden crispness.
