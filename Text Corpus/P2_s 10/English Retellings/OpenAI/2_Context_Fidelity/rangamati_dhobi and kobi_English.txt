**The Tale of the Dhobī and the Kobī**  
*as it is told in the winding valleys of Rangamati, the Chittagong Hill Tracts*

---

### 1. The Setting – Village Life on the Rangamati Lake‑shore  

When the mist lifts off **Mongla Bagan** each early‑morning, the whole **Pahar‑gaon** awakens to the sound of the **dhola ta** (the communal washing‑platform built of bamboo and thatch on the bank of the **Lama River**).  

Women in their **sari‑punj** (hand‑woven, indigo‑dyed saris) gather with their children, chatter about the **bengali new‑year** *Baisābī* that will be celebrated in a few weeks, and pour **pithe‑sugar water** as an offering to **Matsya‑devi**, the river‑spirit who guards the clean water.  

Among them sit the **dhobī**, *Bhrigudā*—a stout, good‑natured man whose beard is always flecked with suds. He spins the wet cloth on a **jhuri** (a wooden spindle) while humming the old **bhatu‑geet** (work‑song) his mother taught him.  

On the opposite side of the river, under a shady **chorang** (banyan‑like **sangāng** tree), rests the **kobī**—*Rashikāla*—the village’s wandering poet. He is thin, with ink‑stained fingers, a bamboo **khan‑pipe** (reed flute) at his side, and a satchel of **patal‑pata** (bamboo‑leaf paper) on which he writes verses that spin the village’s history into rhythm.

Both are respected: the dhobī for his honest labour that keeps the families clothed, the kobī for the verses that keep the memory of the hills alive.

---

### 2. The Boast – “Ēi Rāgra Nām”  

One **Shashti** (full‑moon night) after the harvest, the villagers gathered for **Bhairab Puja**, a night‑long feast where each caste tells a story of its craft.  

Bhrigudā, swigging a cup of **darā (rice‑wine)**, rose on his **chuli (stool)** and proclaimed:

> “_Āmi dhobī, the water of the Lama River obeys my hand.
