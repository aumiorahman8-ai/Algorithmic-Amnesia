**The Tale of *Neelkomol* and *Lalkomol***  
*(as it appears in *Thākurmār Jhuli*, the beloved collection of Bengali folk‑tales compiled by Dakśinaranjan Mitra Majumdar)*  

---

### 1. The Village and the Family  

In a little *pāṅti* tucked among the emerald paddies of Bengal lived a humble farmer, *Bābā* Lāla, and his devoted wife, *Mā* Gopi.  Their thatched‑roofed *bahāl* swayed to the rhythm of the monsoon wind, and the scent of *jhalmuri* and *panta bhāt* drifted from the kitchen every evening.  

From the union of Bābā and Mā were born two daughters as bright as sunrise:

* **Neelkomol** – her hair was bound with a garland of deep‑blue *neel* (indigo) flowers. She was gentle, obedient, and quick to share the *prasad* pressed from the sweet mangoes that fell in the courtyard.  

* **Lalkomol** – a matching rose‑red *lal* garland crowned her braids. Though beautiful, she was proud, quick to claim what was not hers, and delighted in flaunting her *shok* (new finery) before the neighbours.
