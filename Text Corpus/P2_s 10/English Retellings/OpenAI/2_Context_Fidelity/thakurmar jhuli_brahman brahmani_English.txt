**Brahman‑Brahmani (ব্রাহ্মণ‑ব্রাহ্মণী)**  
*From the beloved collection *Thākurmār Jhuli* (ঠাকুরমার ঝুলি)*  

---

### 1. The House of the Brahman

In a sleepy village on the banks of the **Gomti**, lived a learned **Brahman** named **Śivapād** and his devoted **Brahmani**, **Mṛtā**.  Their mud‑walled kutcha house stood beneath a spreading **bat**‑tree; the thatch roof was always scented with the incense of **sulab** (frankincense) that Mṛtā burned each evening while chanting the **Pañcāśṭaka**.  

The couple were well‑liked. The **pitr̥̄s** (elders) of the village—*Thākur* (the village priest), *Dāidu* (the old weaver), and *Jāmuna* the water‑lady—called them **Baba** and **Ma**, and the children of the neighbours called the Brahman “*Dāḍā‑Bābā*” and the Brahmani “*Māmā*”.

---

### 2. The Longing for a Child

Though they were rich in knowledge and reverence, Mṛtā’s heart ached for a **son**.  One night, while the moon floated like a silver **durjek** over the river, she slipped a handful of **kapāle** (puffed rice) into the fire and whispered the **Yajña‑Mantra** of **Devī‑Śrī**:

> “*Śrī‑Kāṇṭhī, answer my devotion; grant us a child who will keep the Vedas alive.*”

Śivapād, seeing the devotion of his wife, resolved to perform a **Vratā** (fast‑day) of three days, staying up through the **sandhā** (evening), reciting the **Gayatri** from the **śruti**, and offering **kākālī** (sacred rice) on a **kumbha** (pot) filled with *tulsi* water.  He also requested the **guru‑pujā** of the village’s *Thāpur* (animistic lord of the banyan).

---
