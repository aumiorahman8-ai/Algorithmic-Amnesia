**Monri and Sa‑Tha‑Nu: A Tale of Celestial Love and Earthly Trials**  
*as it is told in the rice‑field villages and bamboo groves of Rangamati*  

---

### 1. The Dawn of the Stars  

In the time when the **chara**‑clad sky was still being woven by the ancient weavers of fate, there lived a bright, lilac‑skinned goddess named **Monri**. She was the daughter of **Nabonnora**, the Sun‑mother, and **Bodhu‑Bora**, the Moon‑father. In the villages of Rangamati the children call her *Chandnari* and paint her face on **pithas** during **Bôshonto Utsab**, for she is the one who sprinkles the first dew on the **jhum** fields.

Far below, on the earth, dwelt a mortal hunter called **Sa‑Tha‑Nu**, son of **Baba Boro** (the village chief) and **Maa Lata** (the herbalist). Sa‑Tha‑Nu was known for his soft‑spoken *baul* songs, which he sang while beating his **dhol** near the **haor** (wetland). He was called *bhai* by his younger **cousins** (his mother’s brother’s children) and **dadi** (grandmother) would feed him **pitha** made of **boro** (sticky rice) and **jaggery** while telling him tales of the **shonar** (golden) heavens.

---

### 2. The Night When Two Worlds Met  

One moon‑lit night, during **Pôtrô Mela** (the harvest fair), the village gathered under the great **bamboo** canopy for **Kalbazar**, a ritual of offering **Jatra** songs to the river goddess **Matsya Devi**. Monri, delighted by the rhythm of drums and the scent of **tilar pitha**, slipped down a silver thread of moon‑light to watch. Sa‑Tha‑Nu, who was lighting a **diyas** (clay lamp) for the **Lakshmi Puja** in the village square, caught sight of her shimmering veil.

Their eyes met, and a **shobdo** (sound) of *“O O O‑e”* passed between them—an ancient greeting that in the Chakma‑Bengali dialect means “the heart recognizes the soul.” Monri whispered, *“Tumi ki chao, bāprā — a boddhokar bhalobasha?”* (Do you wish, mortal, a love that bends the world?). Sa‑Tha‑Nu bowed low, touching the **dirt‑wet** earth of his **shoa** (home), and answered, *“Āmi chai tōmār bāsi, mon chāyā, kintu amar gārhē chāi bhāi‑bāi‑bāi.”* (I wish to take you into my heart, but my home calls for brotherhood).

Thus began their **celestial‑earthly love**, a thread woven through the **purobindo** (north wind) and the **south‑east** (the direction of sunrise).

---

### 3. The First Trial: The River’s Demand  

The next morning, **Matsya Devi** rose angry. She had seen Monri step upon the river’s surface without asking permission. The goddess sent a flood of **kāl‑dhara** (black water) that threatened to drown the entire **khashi‑pata** (oak forest).  

The village elders—**Dada Babu** (grandfather), **Pishi Muni** (aunt), and **Nani Shona** (great‑grandmother)—convened under the old **Bara Dura** (big banyan).
