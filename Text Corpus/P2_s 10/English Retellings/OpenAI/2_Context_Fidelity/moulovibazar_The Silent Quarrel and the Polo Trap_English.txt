**The Silent Quarrel and the Polo‑Trap**  
*An oral tale from the tea‑groves of Moulvibazar, retold in English while keeping the Bengali words, kin‑terms and rituals that color the original telling.*

---

### 1. The Setting

In the rolling foothills of the Khasi‑Pahari range, where tea bushes sway like a sea of green, lay the village of **Shah‑Bazar**. Here the houses are built of bamboo and thatch, the roofs puffed with thatch called *tala* that whistles in monsoon winds. The people are a mixture of Bengali‐speaking folk and the indigenous **Khasis**, and the everyday language is peppered with words like *bari*, *pukur*, *ghor*, *dighi* and the affectionate kin‑terms *baba*, *ma*, *dada*, *bhai*, *boudi*, *nana*, *mashi* and *khala*.

Every year, on the full moon of **Bhadra**, the village celebrates **Panchami Puja**, a five‑day festival honoring the goddess of the harvest, **Lakshmi**. Women gather in the *mandap* to sing *bhajan* while men clean the *pukur* and prepare the famous **polo**—a sweet, sticky rice cake made of *jaggery* and *poppy seeds* that is later fried and offered to the deity.

It is during this festive season that the tale of a silent quarrel and a clever *polo trap* unfolds.

### 3. The Whispered Dispute

When the *Panchami* festivities began, *Moshareef* asked his two children to prepare **polo** for the *prasad* (offering) that would be placed on the *shikhor* (altar) on the night of *Nabanna*. Rashid, being the elder, claimed he would make the *biggest* and *sweetest* batch, while Meher insisted that her *soft‑centered* version would please Lakshmi more.

The argument was not shouted; it was a **silent quarrel**—each kept their lips sealed, eyes a‑glint with defiance. In Bengali folklore, a *nishabda* (silent) dispute is considered **more dangerous** than a loud one, because the unsaid words fester like *shorosh*
