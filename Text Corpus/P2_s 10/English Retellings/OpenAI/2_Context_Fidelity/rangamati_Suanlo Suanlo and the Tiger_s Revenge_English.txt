**Suanlo Suanlo and the Tiger’s Revenge – a Rangamati folktale**  

*The version below follows the oral rendition that is still told around the fire‑pit ( *bong‑bong* ) in the villages of the Rangamati district, especially among the Chakma and Marma peoples.  I have kept the original kinship terms, ritual words and place‑names that the storytellers use, because they are the texture of the tale.*  

---

### 1. The Setting  

In a deep‐fold of the *Chandranath* range, beside the winding *Rong* river, lay the hamlet of **Kachchhura**.  The houses, thatched with *mor‑kali* (palm leaves), clustered around a common courtyard where the villagers gathered for the weekly ***badhukhri*—the evening storytelling circle**.  The forest that hugged the village was regarded as the abode of the great tiger spirit **Rong Biyal**, protector of the jungle and of the *Bong‑Bong* rituals that kept the human world in balance with the wild.

The families were organized by the matrilineal kinship system, so the principal line of descent ran through the women.  The elder woman of the house—*“* *Naga*”* (grand‑mother)—was the spiritual head; her son (*“* *Bapa*”*) handled the fields, while the uncle (*“* *Mama*”*), the husband’s brother, often acted as the village’s ***Tal‑Pahri* (forest guardian)**.

---

### 2. The Characters  

| Name (in Chakma) | Meaning / Role | English gloss |
|------------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Suanlo** | The clever girl, daughter of Naga | “wise one” |
| **Suanlo** (the twin) | Her identical sister, equally sly | same |
| **Rong Biyal** | The white‑spotted forest tiger | “Tiger” |
| **Bapa** | Father of the twins | Father |
| **Mama** | Uncle, Tal‑Pahri | Uncle/Forest guardian |
| **Naga** | Grand‑mother, matriarch | Grandmother |
| **Pipra** | The village hunter | Hunter |
| **Rong‑Mala** | The river spirit, sister of the tiger | River deity |

The twins are always called **Suanlo Suanlo** (literally “the two wise ones”).  The name is never altered in the telling, because the duplication signals that they act as a single clever unit.

---

### 3. The Tale  

#### **The First Hunt**

One bright monsoon morning, the village gathered for the ***Rong Biyal Puja*—the annual offering to the forest tiger**.  The villagers brought *hut‑pitha* (rice cakes), *shik‑bhat* (boiled rice), and a bundle of *til‑ghur* (sesame leaves), which they placed on a bamboo altar beside the *sacred stone* (**Chongkrong*).  The *Tal‑Pahri* *Mama* chanted the old verses:

> “Rong Biyal, Rong Biyal, gom‑dok a kor ma,  
>  Bolo go kachu ni sam‑ra, aji tui a na‑ri.”  

(The tiger’s spirit hear us; we ask for protection.)

After the offering, **Pipra** the hunter, eager for meat to celebrate the upcoming **Bair‑Bhat* (harvest feast), slipped into the forest with his spear.  He found a tiger cub hidden under a *bau‑bush* and, thinking it a prize, killed it.
