A village headman died, and his only son, eager for his father’s power and prestige, tried to take over the position. Lacking the knowledge and intelligence needed for the role, he kept a distant nephew close by, using him as a sycophant. Whenever anything occurred in the village, the son‑turned‑headman would seize the chance to assert his authority, and he constantly boasted, telling wildly unrealistic stories.

The nephew warned him that if he kept spinning such impossible tales, people would see how foolish he was and would stop respecting him. They agreed on a signal: whenever the headman began to exaggerate, the nephew would make a “hmm” sound to remind him to check himself.

During a village council meeting, the new headman launched into another tall tale. He declared, “Yesterday, a massive snake appeared in my bamboo grove! It was 100 cubits long!” Someone from the side shouted, “That’s impossible.” The nephew made the “hmm” sound. Realizing his mistake, the headman revised his claim: “Well, it couldn’t have been less than 90 cubits.” The nephew sounded “hmm” again. The headman then said, “Even if it wasn’t 90 cubits, it was definitely 80.” Another voice objected, “That’s also impossible,” and the nephew gave the signal once more.

The headman, now flustered, exclaimed, “Then it must have been 70 cubits—absolutely no less than that!” The nephew signaled again. Angered, the headman shouted, “Nephew, no matter how much you haggle, I’m not going any lower than 60 cubits!” At this point, everyone present recognized the absurdity of his “wisdom” and burst into loud laughter. The assembly reprimanded him, and the headman left the meeting. From that day forward, he was never seen again at any village council or judgment.
