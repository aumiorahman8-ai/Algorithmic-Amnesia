In a certain land a king had a daughter. One day a giant python seized the princess and carried her out to sea, placing her on the creature’s head. Distressed, the king sent a proclamation throughout his kingdom, beating drums to announce that whoever rescued his daughter from the sea would receive half the kingdom and her hand in marriage.

Four brothers from one family answered the call. The eldest was a carpenter, the second a boatman, the third a hunter, and the youngest a robber. They went to the king and declared their intention to save the princess. The king accepted them and set the condition that they must rescue her from the sea.

Each brother used his own skill: the carpenter built a boat, the boatman rowed it out to the python, the robber climbed onto the serpent’s head, seized the princess, and the hunter killed the python. Together they brought the princess safely back to shore.

Because the robber was the one who first touched the princess and carried her away, the king awarded him the princess in marriage and gave him half of his wealth and property, as promised. The other three brothers were regarded as helpers and accepted the king’s decision. Thus the robber brother married the princess and received his reward, while the carpenter, the boatman, and the hunter returned home satisfied with the outcome.
