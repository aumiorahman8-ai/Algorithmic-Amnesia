During the monsoon the rainwater rose and flooded the fields. Young boys took their hooks to the water, bobbing their baited lines and striking at any flicker of movement. One boy’s hook caught a Shol fish. He brought the catch home and gave it to his mother to cook. She scraped the fish’s scales with ash, then cut and cleaned its two gills. While she was finishing, she had to step away to the bathroom.

Above the scene a kite was soaring. It saw the fish in the mother’s hands and, driven by sudden desire, swooped down, seized the fish and flew away. The kite dropped the fish from its beak into a nearby pond. The Shol fish, now in the pond, swam around its edge.

On the bank, more boys were still bobbing their hooks, hoping to catch fish. The Shol fish addressed them, reciting:

*“I broke through twelve bites and thirteen pokes,
In the splashing fish trap, my head was left bare.
Rolled in the dust, with both my gills cut,
That rascal kite carried me across three worlds.
He who does not know the wound of the bobbing bait,
Go and bob your hook near him.”*
