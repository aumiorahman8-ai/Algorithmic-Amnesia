A king had two queens; one of his queens was a Rakshasi! But no one knew this.
The two queens had two sons—the gentle, human queen's son was Kusum, and the Rakshasi-queen's son was Ajit. Ajit and Kusum were two brothers, thick as thieves.
The Rakshasi-queen's heart was black, her tongue dripped red. Could the Rakshasi bear to see this? When would she get to cook and eat her co-wife's son's tender bones and flesh in a savory stew! But her own wicked son would not leave his step-brother's side. In rage, the Rakshasi's teeth gnashed, her very soul stirred.
Finding no opportunity, the Rakshasi looked for excuses, draining her co-wife's blood with her gaze.
Before long, the gentle queen took to her bed. Then, from behind her veil, the Rakshasi's tongue flickered as she peeked from every nook and cranny. Within two days, the gentle queen's time came. The kingdom was flooded with grief. No one understood a thing.
To Ajit, it was "Get away, get away," and to Kusum, "Drop dead, drop dead"—the Rakshasi hurled countless insults at her step-son and drove away her own son with a flick of her finger. Ajit would take his elder brother to a secluded place and wipe his tears— "Brother, let it be, we won't go near Mother anymore. No one goes near the Rakshasi-mother!" Ajit, with a heart of iron, endured everything; Kusum, with a heart of gold, began to break. Day by day, Kusum started to waste away.
The queen saw it—what! Her own son, born of her womb, had become her enemy! The fire in the queen's heart blazed up.

One night, the elephants in the king's stable died, the horses in the horse-stable died, the cows in the cowshed died;—the king was in a fix.
The next night, a wailing sound came from the room. Startled, the king got up with his sword. Ajit and Kusum were sleeping on a golden bed; a huge Rakshas had seized Kusum. In the Rakshas's hands, Kusum was like a stick puppet! It rushed over, tore out a lock of its hair, and threw it at the king;—his hands wouldn't move, his feet wouldn't move, the king was stupefied.
Before the king's very eyes, the Rakshas began to eat Kusum. The king was flooded with tears, but could not wipe them away. The king's body trembled violently; he could not sit down. The queen burst into peals of laughter.
Ajit's sleep broke;—
The night felt like a stupor,
The heart felt like poison,
Why wasn't his brother beside him?
Ajit sat up with a start and saw the room was eerily silent, the queen's bangles and bracelets were jingling;—a Rakshas was eating his brother! With his hair standing on end and his eyes wide, Ajit ran and slapped the Rakshas on its head. The Rakshas, crying "Aain! Aain!", spun around, fell, vomited a golden lump, and fled!
The queen saw that the world had turned upside down—the son of her womb had become her enemy! Losing all sense in the fire of her heart, the queen crunched and chewed and ate her own son! A lump of iron rolled out of the queen's throat and fell.
The queen's feet were restless, her eyes were wild; taking the golden lump and the iron lump, the queen went up to the roof.
On the roof was a market of Rakshasas. From one side they said—
Hum im tham—we'll eat more.
And from the other side they said—
Gum gum gam—we'll go to our land.
The queen said—
"Gob gob gum thom thom kha!
I will stay here, you all go to your land."
The spire of the royal palace broke and fell, the king's heart trembled;—twisting trees and stones, making the river water swell, the horde of Rakshasas rushed to their land.
Inside, the queen's body burned, her feet burned; the queen found no peace. Outside, the queen's mind was restless, her heart ached; the night would not end.
Unable to bear it, the queen took out her 'rest-stick' and 'repose-stick' and burned them. After that, rising on a magic cloud, she went to a bamboo grove by the river, buried the golden lump and the iron lump under it, and the Rakshasi-queen returned, relieved.
The crow that cawed before the bamboo, the jackal that cried behind the bush, the queen did not hear them.

(3)
The next day, there was chaos in the kingdom. Human bones in every house, heaps of bones on every path! The country was overrun with Rakshasas; there was no escape. When everyone heard that they had eaten the princes too, the living people fled the kingdom in droves.
The king remained stupefied; his kingdom was overrun by Rakshasas.
________________


(4)
By the river, the bamboo grove played in the breeze, swaying in the wind. A farmer cut some bamboo from that grove. Splitting a bamboo, he saw two large, round eggs inside. Snake eggs, or what kind of eggs? The farmer threw the eggs away.
Instantly, the eggs broke, and from the red and blue eggs emerged a red and a blue prince. With crowns on their heads and drawn swords in their hands, the pair of princes swiftly left the kingdom! The farmer fainted in fear.
When he came to, the farmer saw that the shell of the red egg had turned to gold and the shell of the blue egg had turned to iron! Then, with the iron, the farmer forged a sickle; with the gold, he had bracelets and armlets made for his son's wife.
Walking on and on, the pair of princes came to another king's realm. In that kingdom, there was a great fear of Khokkosh. Every day the king appointed a minister, and the Khokkosh would eat that minister and a household of subjects. The king had made a rule: whichever pair of princes could kill the Khokkosh would get the pair of fairy-like princesses and his kingdom. Many pairs of princes had come and ended up in the bellies of the Khokkosh. No one could kill the Khokkosh; no one had won the princesses.
The pair of princes, Neelkamal and Lalkamal, went to the king and said— "We have come to kill the Khokkosh!"
Hope and despair flickered in the king's mind; finally, he said— "Very well."
Neelkamal and Lalkamal went into a chamber, drew their swords, and sat waiting.
________________


(5)
A few hours of the night passed, no one came. A few more hours went by, no one came. The first watch of the night passed, still no one came.
Finally, it was midnight; still, no one came. The two brothers became very sleepy. Neel told Lal— "Brother! I will sleep; wake me later and you can sleep." He added— "If the Khokkosh ask for our names, say my name first; do not say your name first." Lalkamal leaned on his sword and sat up, alert.
As soon as the Khokkosh arrived—perhaps they couldn't see well in the light?—they said— "Put out the light."
Lalkamal said— "No!"
The biggest of the Khokkosh growled in anger— "Indeed! Who is awake in the room?" All the Khokkosh chattered— "Who's awake, who's awake?"
Lalkamal replied—
Neelkamal is awake, Lalkamal is awake,
And the sword is awake.
Hearing Neelkamal's name, the Khokkosh backed away three paces in fear! Neelkamal had been born from the other Rakshasi-queen, so did he not have Rakshas blood in his body! The Khokkosh knew this. They all said— "Alright, let's test if it is Neelkamal." Rakshas-Khokkosh use all sorts of tricks and deceptions. The biggest Khokkosh began. He said— "Let me see the tips of your fingernails."
Lal pushed out Neel's crown with the tip of his sword. Taking it in their hands, the Khokkosh muttered amongst themselves— "Good heavens! If these are the tips of his fingernails, I wonder what he himself is like!"
Then he said again— "Let's see what your spit is like." Lalkamal heated the ghee from the lamp with his sword and sprinkled it out. The Khokkosh's hair singed, filling the room with the smell; the Khokkosh came back and said— "We want to see your tongues."
Lal extended Neel's sword through the crack in the door. The big Khokkosh grabbed the sword with both hands and said to all the other Khokkosh— "This time I'll pull out the tongue and tear it off, you all hold me and pull hard!"
They all pulled with great force, and the razor-sharp naked sword sliced off both the big Khokkosh's hands, unleashing a flood of black blood! Screaming and shouting, the big Khokkosh scrambled over all the others and fled!
A long time later, the big Khokkosh came running back from somewhere and said— "Who's awake, who's awake?"
The Khokkosh hadn't come for a while, and Lalkamal was getting sleepy; by mistake, Lalkamal blurted out—
Lalkamal is awake, and—
The words were still on his lips—when the door burst open and all the Khokkosh fell upon Lalkamal. The ghee lamp overturned, and the crown fell from Lal's head.
Lal called out— "Brother!"
Neelkamal woke up and saw—Khokkosh! Stretching his body, Neel said—
Aramkathi, Jiramkathi, who is awake?
See who breaks my sleep at the door!
At the sound of Neelkamal's voice, all the Khokkosh, big and small, became half-dead. Neelkamal got up, lit the ghee lamp, and cut down all the Khokkosh. The biggest Khokkosh, falling into Neelkamal's hands, was like a baby chameleon!
Having killed the Khokkosh, the two brothers washed their hands and faces and went to sleep peacefully. The next day, the king came and saw the two princes sleeping, entwined like a pair of red hibiscus flowers; all around them were piles of dead Khokkosh. Seeing this, the king praised them greatly. The king's kingdom and the pair of princesses belonged to the two brothers.

(6)
And that Rakshasi-queen? She was still stationed in the king's palace, wasn't she? Ai-Rakshas and Kai-Rakshas, her two messengers, brought her the news of the Khokkosh's death. Hearing this, the Rakshasi-queen's face grew grim, and striking her chest three times she said— "Oh Ai! Oh Kai! I am finished!
That poison pill from my wretched womb,
My mortal enemy for seven lifetimes,
Go and destroy him, root and branch!"
At once, Ai and Kai, taking the form of two soldiers, went to Neelkamal and Lalkamal's court and said— "A cramp in the chest, a cramp in the back, our king's illness cannot be cured without oil from a Rakshas's head."
Lalkamal and Neelkamal said— "Very well, we will bring the oil."
Sharpening a new sword, the two brothers set off for the land of the Rakshasas.
Journeying on, the two brothers arrived in a forest. There was a very large Ashwattha tree. Exhausted, the two brothers sat down at the foot of the tree.
On that Ashwattha tree was the nest of the Bengama-Bengami birds. The Bengami was saying to the Bengama—
"Ah, who are these kind souls who will give two drops of blood to let my children see!"
Hearing this, Lal and Neel said— "Who speaks up in the tree?— We can give blood."
The Bengami cried "Ah, aha." The Bengama came down.
The two brothers bit their fingers and gave blood. The Bengama took the blood to the nest; a little later, two Bengama-chicks came whizzing down and said— "Who are you, princes, who have given us sight? Tell us what we can do for you."
Neel and Lal said— "Ah, may you live long; we have no need of anything right now."
The Bengama-chicks said— "Very well, but where are you going? Come, we will carry you on our backs and drop you there."
In no time, crossing over lands and fields, rivers and streams, hills and mountains— clouds, sky, moon, sun, everything— with the two princes on their backs, the chicks flew whooshing into the void.

(7)
Flying through the void for seven days and seven nights, on the eighth day the chicks landed on a mountain. Below the mountain was a plain, and beyond the plain was the land of the Rakshasas. Neelkamal picked up a few peas and, putting them in Lalkamal's sash, said— "If they tell you to chew iron peas, chew these peas instead!"
Neel and Lal started walking again.
The two brothers had just crossed the plain, when—
"Hau! Mau! Kau!
I smell a human!!
Catch 'em and eat 'em!!!"
—chanting this, hordes of Rakshasas, in tens of thousands, came running. Neelkamal shouted— "Ayi Ma! Ayi Ma! It is us—your Neelkamal, take me in your arms!"
"Indeed, indeed, stop, stop!" she said, stopping the Rakshasas, and then, flinging her long arms and legs, shaking her matted hair, panting, the "Jotbijoti" Ayi-Buri came and took Neelkamal in her arms— "My Neel! My grandson!" she said, caressing him. The smell of the Ayi made Neelu's stomach turn. Seeing Lal, the Ayi-Buri said— "And who is that with you?"
Neelu said— "Oh, that's my brother, Ayi-ma, my brother!"
The old woman said—
"Then why do I smell a human?
If you are my grandson, then chew and eat
these iron peas."
—saying this, the old woman snorted and produced twenty iron peas from her nose and gave them to her grandson Lal to eat.
Lal already knew what to do;— he secretly put the iron peas in his sash and crunched loudly on the real peas from his sash! The old woman saw, it's true, he is indeed her bright red grandson. The old woman was then overjoyed,— taking both grandsons in her arms, she rocked them, swayed them, and said—
"Aiya maiya natur
Lalu Neelu katur
May all harm to my grandsons go away!"
—but the human smell on Lalkamal's body—her sunken eyes watered, her tongue repeatedly felt dry, seven pitchers of saliva dripped from the Ayi's mouth! But a grandson? Can one eat him? The old woman, on the verge of eating the tasty morsel, did not eat him. In the end, the Ayi took her grandsons home.

(8)
What a city!— It spanned a kingdom. That "Ochin Odhin" city was teeming with Rakshasas. All the Rakshasas had scoured the earth, killed living creatures, and filled the city with them. Lal and Neel rode on the Rakshasas' shoulders and saw— piles and piles of the dead, piles and piles of the decaying! Rotting, melting, the city was a thick, sludgy mess— the stench would make a good ghost flee, gods and demons feared it! Seeing this, Lal said— "Brother, the earth is truly desolate."
Neel remained silent— "No, the earth cannot survive!" Then, in the dead of night, when all the nocturnal Rakshasas had gone to lay waste to the kingdoms on this side of the seven seas; not even a single child was in the city; Neelkamal got up, took Lalkamal, and went to the southern well of the city. There, Neel said— "Brother, hold my clothes."
Giving him his clothes, Lal climbed down the well and brought up a sword and a golden casket. As soon as the casket was opened, the Jiyonkathi and Moronkathi, two hornets, emerged.
As the wind touched the bodies of the Jiyonkathi-Moronkathi hornets, a sharp pain shot through their heads and chests, and a realization dawned on the Rakshasas; in the foolish king's country, the Rakshasi-queen dozed off in her sleep.
A jolt in the head, a shock in the chest; with their long, long legs, the Rakshasas bypassed rivers and mountains, rushing back! Seeing this, Neelkamal tore off the Jiyonkathi's legs. The legs of all the Rakshasas fell off.
Supporting themselves on their two hands, the Rakshasas still came rushing— Neelkamal tore off the Jiyonkathi's other four legs. The hands of all the Rakshasas fell off.
No hands, no feet, yet the Rakshasas—
"Hau! Mau! Kau!
We'll eat our mortal enemies!!"
—shouting this, they came rolling and rolling. Holding it by the edge of the sword, Neelkamal cut off the Jiyonkathi's head. And the heads of all the Rakshasas rolled off. The Ayi-Buri's head— flew off and tried to catch and swallow Neel and Lal.
Then the Rakshas-city was desolate;— who was left? Neelkamal and Lalkamal wrapped the Ayi-Buri's head in a new cloth, took the golden casket with the Moronkathi hornet, and called out, "Bengam, Bengam!"

(9)
After three months and thirteen nights, the two brothers set foot in their country. Everyone in the country hailed them with cries of victory!
Neelkamal and Lalkamal said— "Where are the soldiers? Take the medicine!"
Were the soldiers there? Ai and Kai were Rakshasas! They had died that very day. Neelkamal and Lalkamal to their own soldiers' chests...
"Oh-ma!!"—upon seeing the head, the queen—assumed her true form—
"I'll eat raw, I'll eat hot,
I'll crunch on bones!
Hum dhom dhom, the fire of the pyre,
Only then will the burning in my chest go away!!"
Saying this, the Rakshasi-queen took on a hideous form and ran and ran until she reached the kingdom of Neelkamal and Lalkamal.
At the outer door— "I'll eat! I'll eat!!"
Lal said— "Stop, stop." Lalkamal brought the Moronkathi hornet— and opened the casket. Her body swelling like a drum, her gaze turning cloudy, upon seeing the Moronkathi, the Rakshasi fell down dead!
Everyone came and saw— what is this thing! Is it the Khokkosh's grandmother? Has she come to our kingdom for a feast? Everyone burst out laughing, "Ho-ho-ho!"
The executioners came and disposed of the dead Rakshasi.
________________


(10)
The queen died, and the stupefied king's affliction was cured! Having recovered, the king had drums beaten throughout the kingdom.
The subjects came and said— "Alas! Where are our golden princes, Ajit and Kusum?"
The king sighed and said— "Alas! Where are Ajit and Kusum?"
Just then, the sound of drums came from the royal palace. The king said— "Go see what it is."
Two princes, arm in arm, came and bowed at the king's feet. The king said— "Are you my Ajit and Kusum?"
All the subjects said— "These are our Ajit and Kusum!"
Then the two kingdoms became one; Neelkamal and Lalkamal, with Ilabati and Lilabati, the two kings, began to live happily.
