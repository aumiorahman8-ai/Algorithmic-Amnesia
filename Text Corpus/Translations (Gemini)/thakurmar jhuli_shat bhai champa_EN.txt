A king had seven queens. The elder queens were so arrogant their feet barely touched the ground. The youngest queen was very gentle. For this reason, the king loved the youngest queen more than all the others.

But for a long time, the king had no children. Who would enjoy such a vast kingdom? The king lived in sorrow.

Days passed in this way. After a long time, the youngest queen was to have a child. The king's joy knew no bounds; calling his guards and messengers, the king had it proclaimed throughout the kingdom that he had opened the royal treasury—sweets, confections, gems, and jewels, whoever could take as much as they wanted, could come and get it.

The elder queens began to burn with jealousy.

The king tied one end of a golden chain to his own waist and the other to the youngest queen's, saying, "When the child is born, give this chain a tug, and I will come to see the baby!" With these words, the king went to his royal court.

The youngest queen was about to give birth; who would go to the birthing room? The elder queens said, "Oh, the youngest queen is having a child, why should we send someone else? We ourselves will go."

As soon as they entered the birthing room, the elder queens tugged on the chain. At once, the king adjourned his court and, with the music of drums and pipes, with priests carrying gems and jewels, he arrived—only to see that there was nothing!

The king went back.

No sooner had the king sat down in his court than the chain was tugged again.

The king rushed back again. Arriving, he saw that once again, there was nothing. In his distress, the king said angrily, "If the chain is tugged again before a child is born, I will have all the queens beheaded." Saying this, the king left.

One by one, the youngest queen gave birth to seven sons and one daughter. Oh, the children were like dolls made of moonlight, like flower buds. They waved their little hands and feet, and the birthing room was filled with light.

The youngest queen said softly, "Sisters, won't you show me what kind of child was born!"

The elder queens gestured mockingly before the youngest queen's face, flaring their nose rings, and exclaimed, "Not a child, an elephant has been born—as if she could have a child! A few rats and a few crabs have been born."

Hearing this, the youngest queen fell into a faint.

The cruel elder queens did not tug the chain again. They secretly brought earthen pots and lids, stuffed the children inside them, and went and buried them in the ash heap. After coming back, they pulled the chain.

The king again arrived with the music of drums and pipes, with priests carrying gems and jewels; the elder queens, wiping their hands and faces, hurriedly brought out some tadpoles and baby mice to show him.

Seeing this, the king became a ball of fire and drove the youngest queen out of the palace.

The elder queens could not contain their smiles; the jingling of their anklets did not cease. The thorn in their side was removed; setting the palace ablaze with quarrels and conflicts, the six queens began to live their lives in happiness.

The sorrow of the ill-fated youngest queen could make trees and stones crack and rivers and canals dry up. The youngest queen became a dung-gathering maid, wandering from street to street.

Days passed like this. There was no happiness in the king's heart, no happiness in his kingdom. The palace felt desolate, flowers did not bloom in the king's garden, and the king's daily worship was not performed.

One day, the gardener came and said, "Your Majesty, I cannot find flowers for the daily worship. But today, on the ash heap, on a seven-Champa tree and a Parul tree, seven perfect Champa flowers and one Parul flower have bloomed."

The king said, "Then bring those flowers, I will perform the worship."

The gardener went to get the flowers.

Seeing the gardener, the Parul flower on the Parul tree called out to the Champa flowers,

"Seven Champa brothers, wake up!"

At once, the seven Champa flowers stirred and responded,

"Why, sister Parul, do you call?"

The Parul said,

"The king's gardener has come,
Will you give your flowers or not?"

The seven Champas swiftly rose higher and, shaking their heads, began to say,

"We will not, will not give our flowers, we will rise a hundred leagues higher,
Let the king come first, then we will give our flowers!"

Seeing and hearing this, the gardener was astonished. Dropping his flower basket, he ran to the king and gave him the news.

Amazed, the king and everyone in the royal court came to the spot.

When the king came and went to pick the flowers, the Parul flower immediately called out to the Champa flowers,

"Seven Champa brothers, wake up!"

The Champas answered,

"Why, sister Parul, do you call?"

The Parul said,

"The king himself has come,
Will you give your flowers or not?"

The Champas said,

"We will not, will not give our flowers, we will rise a hundred leagues higher,
Let the king's eldest queen come first,
Then we will give our flowers."

Saying this, the Champa flowers rose even higher.

The king summoned the eldest queen. The eldest queen, her anklets jingling, came and went to pick the flowers. The Champa flowers said,

"We will not, will not give our flowers, we will rise a hundred leagues higher,
Let the king's second queen come first, then we will give our flowers."

After that, the second queen came, the third queen came, the fourth, the fifth, the sixth—none could get the flowers. The flowers went and bloomed in the sky like stars.

The king sat down on the ground, his head in his hands.

Finally, the disgraced queen was mentioned; then the flowers said,

"We will not, will not give our flowers, we will rise a hundred leagues higher,
Only if the king's dung-gathering maid comes, then we will give our flowers."

A search immediately began. The king sent a palanquin, and the guards and bearers took the palanquin to the fields and brought back the dung-gathering maid, the youngest queen.

Her hands and feet were smeared with cow dung, her clothes were torn. In this state, she went to pick the flowers. At once, the Champa flowers glided down from the sky, and the Parul flower joined them; from within the flowers, seven handsome, moon-like princes and one princess emerged, calling out "Mother, Mother!" and jumped into the arms and onto the lap of the dung-gathering maid, the youngest queen.

Everyone was stunned! Tears streamed down the king's face. The elder queens trembled with fear.

The king immediately ordered the elder queens to be buried in a pit of thorns with more thorns piled on top, and then he took the seven princes, his daughter Parul, and the youngest queen to the royal palace.

The drums of victory sounded in the royal palace.
