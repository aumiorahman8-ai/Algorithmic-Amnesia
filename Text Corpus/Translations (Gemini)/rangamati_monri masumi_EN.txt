Long, long ago, Indra, the King of Heaven, had seven exceptionally beautiful princesses. No one as beautiful existed not just in heaven, but in all the three worlds. The seven princesses looked identical. It was impossible to tell who was older or younger. The seven sisters were very lively, cheerful, and spent their days playing and laughing joyfully in heaven. However, they sometimes liked to think about the Earth and were curious to know various things about it. So, one day, they all went to their father and revealed their hearts' desire.

Their father thought about this for a few days. Suddenly, one night, he had a dream about it: seven beautiful lotus flowers were blooming splendidly in a lake. All at once, a hideous ogre appeared from nowhere and plucked one of the flowers. In the dream, the king felt as if the seven flowers were his seven daughters, and the ogre was like misfortune, snatching one of them away. The king suddenly woke up. His heart trembled at the thought of his daughters being in danger. The next day, he forbade his daughters from going anywhere. But the girls repeatedly pleaded with their father to let them go to Earth. In the end, the king's heart melted at his daughters' pleas, and he gave them permission to go to Earth.

Who could then contain the girls' joy? Dancing with delight, they left the celestial realm the next day to visit the mortal world. Upon arriving on Earth, they liked everything. Everything in the mortal world seemed colorful to them. They looked at everything with interest and curiosity. As time passed and the sun set, painting the western sky red, they spread their wings and returned to heaven.

From then on, they would come to visit the mortal realm every day and play hide-and-seek all day long. After their games, they would all go to a secluded meadow and bathe in a lake. Their days were passing in such joy and comfort.

It was on one of these happy days that a disaster befell them. One day, a hunter appeared from somewhere and, hiding behind a bush, secretly watched them bathe. Seeing them, he was on the verge of going mad. He thought to himself, such beautiful girls are not meant to be on Earth! Where did they come from? As he was thinking this, the celestial princesses spread their wings and flew up to heaven. To see this sight again, the hunter came and hid there the next day as well, and he saw the same thing happen. The hunter then thought to himself that if he could catch one of them, he would marry her. But how could they be caught? As he pondered this, he suddenly remembered his friend, the Naga King. He went to the Naga King and told him everything. Hearing it all, the Naga King laughed and said, "Friend, why do you worry so much when I am here? I will give you a net. In the evening, when the celestial beauties spread their wings to fly, throw this net at them. That's it, the job will be done. Someone or other will surely be caught in this net." Happy to hear his friend the Naga King's words, the hunter returned home, not forgetting to take the net given by the Naga King.

The next day, the hunter took the net and hid in a bush near the lake in the secluded meadow. That day too, the celestial princesses came to bathe in that lake. After bathing, just as the seven sisters were about to fly into the sky, the hunter threw the net at them. The net fell on the little finger of the left hand of the youngest sister, 'Monori'. The poor celestial princess, caught in the net, began to tumble towards the ground. Crying, she said to her sisters, "Sisters, I can't fly, please help me." Seeing their youngest sister in trouble, the six sisters came forward to help her. But Princess Monori's fate was ill. Despite trying hard, they could not rescue their youngest sister. Instead, in trying to help her, they themselves were about to face danger. Finally, in despair, her older sisters abandoned her and fled. The youngest princess's name was 'Monori'. Poor Monori, weeping, fell to the ground under the weight of the net.

Meanwhile, seeing the princess fall to the ground, the hunter ran towards her with a joyful heart. Seeing the hunter, Princess Monori cried and said, "Brother, please save me! Rescue me from this net." The hunter was not a bad person. Seeing Monori's plight and her pleading, his heart melted. He freed Monori from the net. And Monori joyfully accepted the hunter as her sworn brother. A little later, night fell, so Monori could not return home. The hunter, not knowing what to do, went to consult with the prince of the kingdom. Hearing the whole story, the prince, out of curiosity, rode his horse to that secluded meadow. Arriving there, he was mesmerized by the princess's exquisite beauty. Princess Monori was also captivated by Sa-tha-nu's appearance. It was beyond her imagination that such a handsome man could exist on Earth. They both stared at each other with unblinking eyes. After a while, Sa-tha-nu asked the princess for her identity. Princess Monori then revealed who she was. The prince was very happy to learn Monori's identity and, in that moonlit night, took her on his horse to the royal palace. Then, at an auspicious moment, they were married with great pomp and ceremony. After the wedding, they began to spend their days happily, laughing and playing in great joy.

On such a happy day, sorrow suddenly descended upon their lives. An enemy king attacked their kingdom. The prince then had to leave Monori alone and set off for war. Poor Monori was pregnant at the time. But her husband had to go to war. So, she dressed him in his battle attire and bid him farewell with teary eyes. Then began the sorrowful nights of her life. In her husband's absence, her life felt like a desert. But happiness comes after sorrow. That is the way of the world. So, laughter returned to Monori's life as well. Two months after her husband left, she gave birth to a beautiful baby boy, as radiant as the moon. Finding him, Monori forgot all the sorrows of her life. She called upon Buddhist monks and devoted herself to religious activities. And the Buddhist monks blessed her wholeheartedly. However, the Brahmins did not look favorably upon Princess Monori's devotion to Buddhism. This was because before Monori's arrival at the palace, they were held in high esteem. After Monori came, no one respected or revered the Brahmins anymore. So, they were secretly very angry with Princess Monori and began to think of ways to harm her.

An opportunity also arose. When the prince was very busy with the war, the Brahmins began to deceive Monori with various words. Some Brahmins said, "The prince has gone to war. No one who goes to war ever returns. You should go back to your father's house." Other Brahmins said, "Your Highness! There is nothing called happiness on Earth. Here, there is only sorrow. Happiness exists only in heaven. You should go to heaven."

Hearing these things every day, Princess Monori became sad. And at this time, her father-in-law had a strange dream. The dream was this: a white elephant came out of his stomach, circled the entire palace seven times, and then re-entered his stomach. The king became very worried about what this strange dream could mean. He summoned the Brahmins to find the meaning of his dream. How could the Brahmins let such a golden opportunity pass? They were overjoyed. They all conspired and told the king, "Your Majesty! A day of great peril awaits our crown prince. One cannot say what will happen in the war."

The king was already consumed with worry after sending his son to war. Hearing the Brahmins' words, his anxiety grew even more. His heart became restless. He requested the Brahmins to tell him how to avert this danger. Then the Brahmins, seizing the opportunity, said to the king, "Your Majesty! If you grant us protection, we can tell you the remedy." The king granted them protection. Then the Brahmins said, "Your Majesty! We have found only one way, and that is to sacrifice your beloved daughter-in-law, Princess Monori. Otherwise, a grave danger awaits the prince on the battlefield." Hearing these words from the Brahmins, the king was startled with fear. He said, "No! No! It is not possible for me to do this."

The wicked Brahmins tried hard to convince the king, but the king remained silent. Gradually, this news spread throughout the palace and also reached Princess Monori's ears. Seeing all these activities of the wicked Brahmins, Princess Monori's heart grew even sadder. With deep sorrow, she began to think about leaving the mortal world for heaven. Then one day, she prepared to leave the attachments of the mortal world and go to heaven. But the poor woman's heart ached for her son. With great sadness, she breastfed her son and put him to sleep in his cradle. Thinking that her son might wake up and look for her, tears rolled down her two eyes. Princess Monori poured some milk into a bottle for her son and placed it beside him. Then, she lovingly gave her sleeping son a final kiss on the forehead, spread her wings, and set off for heaven. As she went, her heart yearned again and again to return to Earth. Her heart constantly wept thinking of her son, and under the weight of her sorrow, even her wings seemed to want to descend back to Earth. Still, she flew on and on, covering a great distance. Just then, far away, a dove suddenly cooed. As its mournful call reached her ears, she felt as if her son was crying for her. Monori could bear it no longer. She quickly returned to Earth. Upon arriving, she saw her son sleeping soundly. Then, Monori woke her son and breastfed him once more. After the boy fell asleep again, many past events and memories came to Monori's mind. She thought, if her husband returns home victorious from the war, he might look for her. At this thought, a few teardrops fell from her eyes to the ground. At that moment, she suddenly remembered a devout sage. That sage lived in a deep forest. He was very fond of Monori. As she thought of him, Monori spread her wings and flew to him. She told the sage everything. Then, taking off the ring from her left hand, she gave it to the sage and said, "O great sage! If my husband ever comes here looking for me, please give him this ring as a memento from me and tell him to come to heaven to bring me back."

She also told the sage how to get to heaven. To go from Earth to heaven, one must cross a vast, boundless ocean. In the middle of the ocean, there is a deserted island. First, one must go to that island. Then, from that island, one must fly to heaven. Between Earth and the island, there is a terrifying ocean current. The water of that current is so hot that anyone who falls into it will instantly be burned to ashes. However, there is only one way to reach that island, which is that a great Naga King lives in that hot sea. That Naga King is so enormous and long that if he rests his head on Earth, his tail will reach that island.

In the morning, the Naga King's tail floats on the water towards the Earth. At noon, the tail sinks into the water, and the middle part of his body floats on the ocean's surface like a mountain. In the afternoon, that middle part also sinks, and only his head remains floating on the water. To go to heaven, one must first travel over the Naga's back to that deserted island and then, somehow, fly to heaven from there. After saying these words, Monori took leave of the sage and flew away to heaven.

Meanwhile, in heaven, everyone was extremely worried for her in her absence. Her sisters, in particular, often used to cry for her. Her father wondered how to bring his daughter back to heaven from Earth. At that moment, Monori arrived in heaven. Everyone was overjoyed to have Monori back. Her sisters embraced her in the joy of her return. But her father was not so happy. He could supposedly smell the scent of a human on Monori's body, and he could not tolerate it. He called his servants and ordered them to bathe Monori. Following the king's order, thousands of servants brought pitcher after pitcher of water from the pond near the palace and bathed Monori day after day. This went on for two weeks, yet there was no sign of it stopping.

On the other hand, at that time, her husband returned to the country, victorious in the war. But upon returning, not seeing his wife, the prince became like a madman. All his joy of victory turned to dust. He began to search for his wife from one land to another. Days passed, nights passed, yet his search did not end. Searching and searching, he finally reached that sage one day. The sage then told him everything and gave him the ring Monori had left. The next day, taking leave of the sage, Prince Sa-tha-nu crossed many forests and thickets and reached the celestial ocean. Arriving there, he saw the great Naga King, just as Princess Monori had described, and bravely began to cross the hot sea on the Naga King's back. The storms and tempests in that sea were so fierce that he was saved from falling into the sea by a gust of wind only by a hair's breadth. Later, after much hardship and suffering, he reached that deserted island in the evening. As soon as he reached the island, the Naga King's enormous body dived into the ocean with a tremendous stir.

Night was falling all around. With extreme hunger and the hardship of the journey, he fell asleep under a large tree in a deep forest, overcome with boundless fatigue. In the dead of night, he was awakened by the conversation of two birds. On the tree under which he was sleeping lived two large birds. The queen bird was saying to the king bird, "Well, today we managed to find food after much wandering. What will happen tomorrow?" The king bird then said, "My dear, do not worry about tomorrow. Tomorrow, there is a very big feast in the kingdom of the King of Heaven, Do-ma-ray. If we go there tomorrow, we can get a lot of food and drink." The queen bird, surprised, asked, "What is the reason for such a big feast in the celestial kingdom all of a sudden?" The king bird said, "That's a long story, my dear! It can't be finished tonight. You should sleep now, I will tell you about it tomorrow." Hearing this, the queen bird's curiosity doubled. She repeatedly pleaded to hear the story. Finally, melting at her plea, the king bird said, "My dear! The King of Heaven has seven daughters. The youngest among them is named Monori. Monori went to the mortal world some time ago and married a prince there. Then she returned to heaven. Tomorrow, she will be ritually bathed and made a resident of the celestial kingdom again. So, on this occasion, the King of Heaven has arranged a grand feast." After saying this much, both the king bird and the queen bird fell asleep.

While the king bird and queen bird were talking, Prince Sa-tha-nu, sitting under that tree, was listening to their words with interest. When the two birds fell asleep, the prince, in that interval, used a spell to turn himself into a small ant, climbed the tree, and slipped under one of the queen bird's wings to wait for dawn. The next morning, the king bird and queen bird spread their wings and set off towards heaven. Along with them, the prince, disguised as an ant, flew to heaven on the queen bird's wing. After flying over the ocean for a long time, the queen bird said to the king bird, "My love! I don't understand why my body feels so heavy today. It feels like some other creature is riding on my body. Should I throw it off?" Hearing this, the prince's eyes widened in terror, because the tremendous roar of the ocean could still be heard from below. The prince waited anxiously to hear what the king bird would say! Hearing her, the king bird said, "My dear! Don't flap your wings too much! If there is any creature with you, the poor thing will fall into the sea and die. Instead, do one thing: rest the wing that feels heavy on my wing. The creature will be saved. This will bring us great merit." The queen bird did so, and the two of them flew towards heaven. And with them, the prince also flew to heaven in the guise of an ant. Finally, upon reaching heaven, the two birds landed under a large palm tree near the pond of the celestial kingdom. They heaved a sigh of relief upon landing.

In that interval, the prince quickly descended to the ground and resumed his own form. He sincerely thanked the king bird for saving him. The king bird then laughed and said to the queen bird, "See, my dear! I told you then that there was probably some creature with you." After this, they happily went off in search of food. After they left, Prince Sa-tha-nu saw a surprising scene at the pond's edge! One after another, celestial nymphs were coming from the celestial palace, filling their pitchers with water from the pond, and leaving. Seeing this, he understood that Princess Monori was surely being bathed with this water. Many nymphs came to the pond's edge, took water, and slowly, everyone began to return. Only one nymph was left behind. All this time, the prince had been watching this scene from behind the palm tree. Now, from a distance, he aimed a magic arrow at the last girl's pitcher. As a result, despite trying hard, the girl could no longer lift the pitcher. Then she looked around for help. At that moment, she saw the prince and asked for his help.

Then the prince, seizing the opportunity, placed the ring given by Monori inside the pitcher and lifted it for her. And he said to himself, "If Monori is still truly my wife, may this ring surely fall into her hands." After the nymph went to the palace, the prince waited for Monori. Meanwhile, upon reaching the palace, as soon as the nymph poured water on Monori, the ring from inside the pitcher plopped right into Monori's hand. As soon as she saw it, she recognized it; it was the ring from the ring finger of her left hand. She had given this ring to the devout sage to give to her husband. She understood that her husband had arrived in heaven in search of her. At this thought, the princess's heart danced with joy. She told the maids, "Stop pouring water, I am feeling cold." Then she went and told her sisters everything. Hearing all of Monori's story, her sisters were overjoyed. They went and told their father, the King of Heaven, everything. Hearing all this, the King of Heaven said, "Alright. First, I must test whether my son-in-law is worthy of my daughter. If he passes the tests, only then will I give my daughter's hand to him. And if he cannot pass the tests, he will be cut into pieces." Hearing this decision of the King of Gods, Princess Monori and her sisters became worried. Everyone began to think about how to help their youngest sister, Monori.

Meanwhile, the prince, sitting by the pond, was thinking about how to enter the royal palace. At that time, the girls of the celestial kingdom came to him, laughing and making merry. They took him to the palace with great care. At the palace, after showing him considerable hospitality, the King of Heaven said, "Prince! If you wish to have my youngest daughter Monori as your wife, you will have to pass several difficult tests. Only if you can pass those tests will you get my daughter as your wife. And if you cannot, you will be cut into pieces. Now think it over! Will you take the tests? Or will you return to your country?" Prince Sa-tha-nu, after some thought, decided to take the tests.

A specific day was set for the tests. The first test would be this: seven holes would be made in a wall. The little fingers of the left hands of the King of Heaven's seven daughters would be inserted into these seven holes. The prince would then have to choose his wife Monori's finger from those seven little fingers. The prince fell into great worry. He could not figure out how he would choose his wife's finger from among seven identical fingers! While he was worrying about this, Princess Monori secretly came to him at one point and said, "My love! I have found a way." The prince asked, "What way?" The princess said, "When you take the test, I will send a fly. That fly will buzz around you. While flying, it will eventually land on my finger. Then you will know that is my finger." After giving this advice to the prince, Monori went back to the inner quarters.

On the appointed day, the test began. Everyone watched the prince's first test with interest. From behind a wall, the seven sisters inserted their fingers into seven holes. On the opposite side stood the King of Heaven, Do-ma-ray, Prince Sa-tha-nu, and other spectators. At this time, a fly came and started buzzing around the prince. After flying for a while, unnoticed by everyone, it landed on Princess Monori's finger. The prince's gaze was fixed on the fly. The prince understood that this was Monori's finger. The others did not even notice. He held up that finger in front of everyone. Seeing his success, everyone present was astonished.

Next was the turn of the second test. The second test was that the King of Heaven's seven daughters would wear identical clothes and fly in the sky. The prince would then have to identify which one was Princess Monori. This test was truly very difficult because the seven sisters looked identical. It was very hard to tell them apart. This time too, the prince fell into great worry. And again, Princess Monori secretly came and said, "My love! There is no reason to worry. When we are flying in the sky, I will fly a little separately from my sisters. And if they fly too high, I will separate from them and fly a little lower. Besides, while flying, I will let my scarf hang a bit longer, and the hem of my dress will also be longer than theirs and fly in the wind. From this, you will be able to recognize me easily." Hearing Princess Monori's words, the prince was relieved of his anxiety.

Again, on the appointed day, the second test began. The seven sisters, wearing beautiful clothes, began to fly in the sky. They looked very beautiful then. They first flew downwards, then flew higher and higher up. At that point, it was truly a difficult task to recognize them. Even the King of Heaven himself could not say who was who. Everyone in heaven was enjoying this unprecedented sight. Now it was the prince's turn to be tested. The king said, "Prince! Now tell me, which one is Monori?" At this time, everyone present was looking at the prince with bated breath. The prince looked into the distance and unhesitatingly pointed his finger towards Princess Monori, saying, "That is my wife, Monori. She is flying a little lower than her six sisters. And her scarf is also flying in the wind, slightly longer than the others'."

Everyone waited with bated breath for the result. When the seven sisters came down, it was seen that the prince had indeed pointed his finger at his wife, Monori. The seven princesses of the celestial kingdom came down, circling over the prince's head. And the prince extended his hand and helped Monori to the ground. Then, a joyous uproar erupted among the spectators. The king, too, was very happy in his heart. After this, the King of Heaven said to the prince, "Prince! I will not test you anymore. You have passed my tests. Now, you show a test of your own!"

Then the prince said to the king, "Your Majesty! Let your daughters fly in the sky again. I will shoot arrows at my wife Monori among them and bring her down to the ground in such a way that the arrows will strike her clothes but will not pierce her body in any way." As per the prince's words, the seven sisters began to fly in the sky again. The prince got ready with his bow and arrows. Then, carefully aiming at Monori, he shot seven arrows one after another. Everyone watched with bated breath, wondering what would happen. Just then, Princess Monori came down to the ground, floating on the seven arrows. Seeing that, everyone in heaven was overwhelmed with joy, because everyone in heaven loved her very much. The princesses too were all happy and began to embrace one another.

The King of Heaven himself was also very happy. He married Monori to the prince again with great pomp and ceremony, according to the customs of heaven. On the wedding night, the entire celestial city went wild with joy. On the occasion of the wedding, the king gifted them a new royal palace. The mortal prince and the celestial princess were filled with great joy. After a long time, they had found each other again. So their hearts yearned to be close to one another. They talked all night and, late in the night, fell into a deep sleep. At this time, the king ordered his soldiers to take Princess Monori and her husband, the prince, along with the palace, and place them on Earth. Following the king's order, the soldiers took Princess Monori and Prince Sa-tha-nu, along with the new palace, and placed them on the plains of Earth. As Princess Monori and her husband were in a deep sleep, they did not know anything about this.

The next day, they were awakened by the crowing of a rooster. Waking up in the morning and finding themselves on Earth, they were surprised. Then, after finishing their morning chores and having food and drink, they went out to look for their son. After searching for a long time, they found their old royal palace. But they were both surprised to see the state of the house. The reason was that they had gone to heaven just the other day. At that time, the palace was brand new, sparkling and shiny. And the one they had found now was a dilapidated, old royal palace. Be that as it may, they went to look for the old king. But the gatekeepers, hearing their words, looked as if they had fallen from the sky. They said, "The king you are talking about passed away almost a hundred years ago." Hearing this, the two of them were surprised and introduced themselves to the gatekeepers. Seeing them, the gatekeepers' eyes nearly popped out of their heads! They said, "You went to heaven from the mortal world almost a hundred years ago. Yet you are still as youthful as ever. And here, your son, meaning our king, has become a hundred-year-old man."

The prince and Monori now went to see their son. Seeing their son, their eyes widened in disbelief. Instead of seeing a two-month-old baby before them, they saw a decrepit old man! All his hair had turned white, his teeth had fallen out, and he could no longer see well with his eyes. Their old son, hearing their voices, came groping his way towards them. Seeing their son's miserable condition, they could not figure out what to do. As soon as Princess Monori quickly took her old son in her lap, a strange thing happened: the old man turned back into a two-month-old baby. And he began to drink his mother's breast milk.

Everyone present, seeing this strange sight, could not understand whether they were dreaming or awake. Later, after hearing the whole story from Princess Monori and Prince Sa-tha-nu, they understood that one day in heaven is equal to one hundred years on Earth. After returning to Earth, the celestial princess Monori became even more devoted to Buddhism than before. It was not just for this reason; in past lives as well, she had always been devoted to Buddhism, and Prince Sa-tha-nu had always been her husband. In one life, for some reason, she got angry with her husband and prayed to Lord Buddha, "Lord! May I never get such a husband in any future life." Hearing her prayer, their servant was very heartbroken, because in that life, they were his masters. One day, he was bringing food for his master and mistress. After the servant arrived, when the master went outside for a while, the servant decided to set aside the master's share of the food and donate his own share to Lord Buddha. When the master returned, he took his share of the food, went to Lord Buddha, donated the food, and said, "Lord! May I, in every life, have my master and his wife as husband and wife. And may I be the reason for their union in every life."

Lord Buddha probably accepted this offering from the hungry devotee with pleasure. That is why, in life after life, he was born as the reason for the union between his beloved master and mistress. In this life, when 'Monori' was born as the daughter of the King of Heaven, Do-ma-ray, and Sa-tha-nu as a mortal prince, he was born as a hunter to be the cause of the union between his beloved master and mistress.
