At the confluence of the Mathabhanga river, there was a prevailing custom for any merchant vessel passing by to offer a portion of its goods to the goddess. Whatever the cargo boats carried—be it eggplants, radishes, jackfruits, or wood apples—a part of it was offered to the goddess. Once, a merchant not only gave nothing to the goddess but also made some insulting remarks towards her. Consequently, his cargo-laden boat sank into the river's abyss. Divers were brought in to recover the boat. First, they lowered ropes to measure the river's depth. In an instant, the rope ran out, but they could not find the riverbed. More rope was let out, but still, the bottom could not be found. Even more rope was released. Everyone was astonished that even with so much rope, it was impossible to measure the river's depth. No diver dared to descend. Finally, a brave man offered to go down into the river. He was outfitted like a diver with ropes and the necessary equipment.

Descending into the river's depths, he saw a golden boat. On its deck, two fairies were seated on either side. They were pulling on the rope. The rope had accumulated into a massive heap. The man said fearfully,
-Our boat has sunk, so I have come looking for it. Please forgive me, mother.
The fairies replied,
-You will get your boat back, but on one condition. You must not tell anyone about this; if you do, you will vomit blood and die. And listen, go to the Kazis' ghat, and you will find the boat there.

The boat was found at the Kazis' ghat. But the man could not stop himself from telling his wife what had happened. This utterance sealed his fate. The man vomited blood and died.
